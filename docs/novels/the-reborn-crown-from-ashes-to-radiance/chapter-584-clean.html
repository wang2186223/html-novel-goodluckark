Chapter 584

"Look, now that Alistair’s gone, we’re keeping an eye on Callie," one of the neighbors barked.

"Damn right," another threw in. "She’s basically my own kid."

"She's family, period."

The crowd closed in on Elara and Julian, shoulders tight, sending a crystal-clear message. This wasn't small talk. They were laying down the law for Julian. *Mess with her, and you mess with all of us.* Sure, her grandpa was gone, but this town was her shield. If Julian ever stepped out of line, he’d have to fight his way through the whole zip code.

A lump hit Elara’s throat. She blinked back tears.

Julian scanned the room, giving a slow, respectful nod. He got it. Back with the Jewell family, Elara was a ghost—pushed aside, stepped on, invisible. But here? She was gold. These people loved her, no strings attached.

Neil clapped his hands, breaking the huddle. "Alright, let's eat!"

The community hall was already buzzing. Word had spread fast that Elara and Julian were back, and the locals had whipped up a massive potluck in record time. On the walk over, Neil fell into step beside Elara, filling her in on the local gossip.

"Seriously, Callie, we’re only standing on our own two feet because of you and your granddad. We owe you guys everything."

Elara laughed, waving it off. "Come on, Neil. You’re the mayor of the century. We just got lucky."

"Nah," Neil insisted, smiling. "Having you two here was our lucky break."

They traded jokes all the way to the new community center. Elara stared. The building was brand new, glowing with light and packed with noise. It hit her then—this place had exploded, and she and Alistair had built the foundation. Even after she packed up and left, she’d secretly wired money and greenlit local projects to keep the town moving.

They grabbed a table, and a line formed to hug Elara. She pulled Julian in, introducing him to every face. Julian didn't play the big-city hotshot. He gripped hands, flashed a genuine grin, and fit right in. The locals ate it up. Soon, the room was a wall of laughter.

Over plates of food, Elara and Neil talked shop about the town’s new tourism push. Things were finally looking up. Julian leaned in, listening close, and dropped a casual hint about cutting a check to fund the next phase.

Right as everyone was digging into the fried chicken, mashed potatoes, and homemade pies, the vibe shattered.

The double doors banged open. A guy about Elara's age stumbled in, chest heaving. His shirt was smeared with bright red blood.

"Neil!" he gasped.

The chatter died instantly. You could hear a pin drop.

"Finn? What the hell, man?" someone called out. "Sit down, grab a plate! Callie’s back. Can this wait?"

Finn didn't look at them. His eyes locked onto Neil, wild and sweating. He tried to force a laugh, but it came out as a wheeze. "It's... nothing. Seriously. Just need a quick second with the mayor."

Elara stood up, her stomach dropping. "Finn, is that blood? Are you hurt? What happened?"

Finn was her childhood buddy. The guy was usually a rock, never rattled. Seeing him shake like a leaf meant one thing: something had gone horribly wrong.
