Chapter 804

Rex scanned the grassy slope. Oak trees threw messy shadows over the wild weeds. "So, you picked this spot," he muttered. "Yeah. It’s actually pretty nice."

Elara managed a tiny smile. "You dig it, right?"

Rex choked up, staring straight at the stone. "I'll be back. Like, a lot. Promise."

Elara stepped closer. She looked at the fresh dirt. "Hey, Declan. Don't stress. I've got Rex's back. I swear."

The crowd stuck around forever. People hugged, cried, and talked about old times. Then, the silence hit. One by one, they drifted off. Soon, it was just Elara, Julian, and Rex standing there among the old graves.

...

They drove back to Rex's place. Elara and Julian kept pushing him to pack a bag. "Come stay at the manor," they begged. "Just for a bit. You shouldn't be alone."

Rex shook his head. "Nah, guys. I'm good. This is my spot. I need to be home."

Elara pulled Julian into the hall. They whispered for a second. "I'm staying here with him," she said.

Julian frowned. He hated the idea of sleeping alone, but he got it. You don't just snap out of it when your person dies. He planted a quick kiss on her forehead. "Call me. Seriously. Anything you need." Then he walked out.

Elara pulled an all-nighter with Rex. He just kept talking. He rambled about Declan, then switched to random, pointless stuff. Honestly, he was weirdly chill. Usually, Rex was a total drama king and stubborn as hell. Tonight? Total silence. He wasn't faking it, but he wasn't crying his eyes out either. He just spun stories.

Finally, his eyes got heavy. "I'm crashing," he said. "Get some sleep, okay? Don't worry about me. I'm fine."

Grief is a bitch. Elara knew that. It’s been years since her granddad died, but she still sees him in her dreams. She still gets that sharp stab in her chest when she spots something he would've liked. The pain doesn't leave. It just gets quieter, blending into the background.

Rex had a brutal road ahead. Living without Declan was going to suck. But he'd make it. Declan left him with a ton of memories and a lot of love. It had to be enough.

The funeral had completely drained her. The second her head touched the pillow, she was out cold.

...

Next morning, she dragged herself to the kitchen, rubbing her eyes. Rex was already up, fully dressed, and looking totally bright. Like his whole world hadn't just shattered. The place smelled amazing. He’d whipped up a whole spread: scrambled eggs, crispy bacon, toast, and sliced fruit.

"Morning! Look who's awake!" Rex yelled, looking back with a huge grin. "You gotta be starving. Sit, eat!" He brandished a spatula like a sword. "Seriously, my cooking game is next level now. You'll totally regret missing out on this!"

Sun streamed through the glass, making his blond hair glow. For a split second, Elara saw a ghost. Declan was sitting right there at the table, smirking up at Rex with that goofy, loving look, probably ready to talk some trash.

Rex blinked at her. "Uh, you okay? Still in a fog?"

"Yeah, pretty much," Elara yawned, stretching her arms. "But hey, if you're hyping this food up so much, I gotta test it." She slid into a chair and stared at the food. "Looks pretty, I'll give you that. Let's see if it actually tastes good."

She grabbed her fork and gave him a playful, judgy look.

Rex laughed and sat down opposite her. "Trust me, it's fire. Declan always said I learn fast. Even with kitchen stuff."

His smile slipped for a microsecond. A shadow crossed his eyes. But then he squared his shoulders, ready to take on the day.
