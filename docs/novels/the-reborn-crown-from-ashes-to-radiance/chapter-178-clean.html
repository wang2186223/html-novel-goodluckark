Chapter 178

Blair Sutton stood in the huddle, rubbing hot tears from her eyes, absolutely livid. Right next to her, Rowan Grey balled her hands into tight, white-knuckled fists. God, they knew. They knew the hellhole Elara had survived for years. They felt every single jab Beatrix threw, knowing exactly how deep those words cut.

Back then, Elara was just a kid begging for a scrap of love. She’d busted her ass trying to make them proud, trying to get them to notice her. But they had their favorites. They didn't give a crap about her feelings. Not one bit.

Milo tensed up, ready to throw hands right there, but Rhys clamped a heavy hand onto his shoulder. "Whoa, easy," Rhys's grip said, pulling him back.

The room started buzzing. Sure, okay, maybe Coraline had the better resume, but would the Jewells actually dump their own flesh and blood? No way. DNA is DNA. An adopted kid just doesn't hold a candle to the real deal.

"Man, I feel bad for Elara," someone whispered. "Like, if they actually treated her right, she wouldn't be trying this hard to run, you know?"

"Please. She’s probably just throwing a tantrum."

"Still, she’s their actual kid. They wouldn't really kick her out, right?"

"I don't know, the Jewells totally baby Coraline. Elara’s got nobody. It’s honestly kind of sad."

"Or maybe Elara’s just a brat? If she actually behaved, they wouldn't hate her so much."

"What if Coraline is just a total snake, though?"

"Who knows, honestly."

The room split down the middle. Team Coraline versus Team Elara. Nobody actually knew the truth, so they started placing mental bets. Would Elara really cut the cord? And what the hell would she do next?

Half the room figured she’d crash and burn without the family money. The other half reckoned she had enough swagger to make it on her own. Plus, hey, she had Julian. Her husband had her back.

But then the haters chimed in. "Julian doesn't love her. He's just paying off some old debt. He'll get bored and dump her." Without the Jewell name, she was way out of his league anyway.

"Look, I'm being reasonable here," Beatrix said, her eyes narrowing into slits. "Just hand over a million bucks, and we're square!"

"Babe, stop," Walter groaned, his forehead creasing. "Seriously, don't start a scene here. And Callie, cut it out. This isn't a joke."

"Let her walk," Cassian sneered, rolling his eyes. "The world isn't gonna stop spinning without her. She's the one who's gonna suffer, not us. Hey, Elara—you want out so bad? Fine. Just don't come begging on your knees when you fail."

Elara let out a dry laugh, looking right at Cassian. "Beg you? In your dreams. When have I ever needed you anyway? What have you ever actually done for me? Seriously, why would I want you around?"

It was honestly hilarious to her. She’d always paid her own way. She hadn't touched a single dime of the Jewell fortune. Her granddad had set her up with a fat inheritance, and these idiots didn't even have a clue. They'd never given her a damn thing.

"I bought my own food," she said, her voice dropping to absolute zero. "Yeah, I lived under your roof, but you shoved me in the storage closet. A massive mansion, and I got the literal worst room. What, am I supposed to thank you for shelter? Newsflash: that's just basic parenting."

Cassian scowled, his temper flaring. "Whatever. Just don't ask us for a lifeline later. You wanted this! Besides, where are you even gonna get a million bucks?"

"A measly million? Why the hell wouldn't she have it?" A smooth, deep voice sliced through the tension, making every head in the room snap toward the door.
