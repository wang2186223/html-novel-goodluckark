Chapter 1035

Elara was grinding on set. Meanwhile, Rowan kicked back at the Sutton place. Sure, Rowan’s own mom wasn't exactly Mother of the Year, but the rest of the clan? Pure gold. They smothered her with love. Plus, the family cook whipped up these insane, soul-healing meals. It showed, too. Rowan filled out fast. Color rushed back into her cheeks. She didn't look like a walking corpse anymore.

No job. No stress. Rowan was living her best life. The only glitch in her matrix? Rhys Stewart. The guy just wouldn't stop trying to crash her peace.

Rhys couldn't wrap his head around her weird-ass memory loss. Like, how do you forget *only* him? Every single time he showed up, Rowan played her part. She stared right through him like he was some random guy on the street. After a few rounds of this game, Rhys was about to snap.

Then came another one of his drop-bys. Blair Sutton spotted him from the porch and rolled her eyes so hard it hurt.

She crossed her arms. "Seriously, Mr. Stewart? This is pathetic. Rowan already told you—she doesn't care if her memory comes back or not. She knows her past, she's cool with it, and she's moving on. Without you. Why can't you just let it go and live your life?"

"She can't just erase me, Blair!" Rhys snapped, his voice cracking with pure frustration. "She remembers literally everyone else. Why am I the only guy left out in the cold?"

It was eating him alive. Rowan's icy shoulder was driving him crazy. He'd tried to tell himself he could walk away and let her heal. But now that she actually was healing? He couldn't take it. He needed her to remember him. Bad.

Rhys clenched his jaw. "Look, if she gets her memory back and still tells me to get lost, fine. I'll walk. But she has to remember me first. Blair, please. Just get her out here. I want to take her out."

"She's not going anywhere with you, Mr. Stewart." Blair stood in the yard, shaking her head. "You're just making a mess of things. She's shut you down a dozen times. How many hints do you need? You're just being stubborn now, and honestly? It's getting old."

Blair let out a heavy sigh. "You guys belong in the friend zone. She was your student, Rhys. That's it. You never wanted her like that. She loved you, and now she doesn't even know who you are. Isn't that exactly what you wanted? Why the hell are you trying to force her to remember? Just so you can break her heart all over again?"

She locked eyes with him. "If you actually give a damn about her, let her be happy. Isn't that what a good mentor does?"

"No, you don't get it—I wouldn't leave her hanging this time!" Rhys blurted out.

Blair raised an eyebrow, intrigued. "Oh, really? So when she was crazy about you, you didn't give a shit. But now that she's wiped you from her brain, you suddenly realize she's the one? That's your move?"

Rhys flushed red. He looked down at his shoes, totally speechless.

Blair shrugged, giving him a look of pure pity. "Too little, too late, Mr. Stewart."

"She still needs to remember," Rhys mumbled, his jaw locking up again. "Once she does, we'll figure it out."

Blair stared at him, recognizing that stupid, stubborn look. The guy wasn't going to back down. If she told him Rowan wouldn't see him, he'd just camp out on their porch or wander around the house until someone cracked. He was getting worse by the day—more stubborn, more annoying. Rowan could hide all she wanted, but Rhys would just hang around the front gate or crash their dinner table, refusing to budge until he got his eyes on her.
