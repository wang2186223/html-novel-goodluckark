Chapter 1040

"And cut the crap about us liking each other," Rowan said, her voice dead steady. "We’re teacher and student. Period. We cross that line, and we lose everything. It’ll just get weird." She locked eyes with Rhys, letting him see she wasn't budging.

"Rowan, seriously?" Rhys snapped, frustration boiling over. "Why are you doing this now? It took me forever to get my head straight about us, and you’re already packing your bags? What the hell is so great about some average Joe anyway? With me, none of that class bullshit matters."

"Well, it matters to me. I want normal. I want a normal guy," Rowan shot back, digging her heels in.

Sure, he could ignore the class divide, but his family wouldn't. Plus, her own messy baggage would trigger a never-ending nightmare of drama if they tried to make it work. She refused to pull him into her quicksand. Back in the day, she used to think love solved everything—just be bold and chase the guy. But reality had kicked that naive dream out of her. Feelings didn't guarantee a happy ending. Wanting someone didn't mean you got to keep them.

"Rowan!" Rhys scowled, his face falling. He’d pictured this talk going totally differently—maybe finally bridging the gap between them. He’d completely miscalculated. She was shutting him out, leaving zero room to breathe. A year ago, he would’ve cheered at this rejection. Not anymore. He was hooked on her, genuinely hooked. Now, her words felt like a physical weight crushing his chest.

"But you still want me, don't you?" he begged. "Stop overthinking and just feel it."

Rowan shook her head. "Whatever this is, it’s not romance. We have history, that's all. Besides, I haven't exactly dated around."

"Once this mess is over, I’m going to put myself out there. I'll find a guy who actually fits my life," she murmured. "I mean, isn't that what you wanted from the start?"

"I've got my head straight now. I'm moving on. So do me a favor—let me go. Stop dragging me back." She stood up, scraping her chair back.

She scanned the room, her voice dropping. "Nice place. Different, though. Look how much those roses grew."

"So, we're back to teacher and student. Just like old times. That's our lane." She stared him down, dead serious. "I'll keep learning from you, sure. But that is the hard limit. Got it?"

"I won't mess with your arranged marriage or whatever corporate merger your family is planning. Just give me the same space. Let's live our lives and stay out of each other's business. Deal?"

"My Uber's outside. I'm out of here," she said, shutting down the conversation. She spun on her heel and walked toward the exit.

Rhys sat frozen, jaw clenched, feeling like he’d just taken a hard left hook to the ribs. His brain screamed at him to chase her, but his legs were lead. He just watched her walk away.

Was he seriously going to let her leave? God, the regret was eating him alive—why hadn't he just said yes when she wanted him? Why didn't he take the leap? Was he actually going to marry some girl his parents picked out? Were they really going to end up as total strangers, pretending they never existed to each other?

A massive fist squeezed his chest, cutting off his air. Then, something snapped. Rhys bolted upright, tore through the door, and burst outside just as Rowan was sliding into the backseat of a sedan.

He lunged forward, his throat tight, trying to yell her name—but the driver hit the gas and the car tore away before he could get close.

Just like that, she was gone, swallowed by the traffic. Rhys stood there, a hollow crater where his heart used to be, feeling nothing but the biting wind and a deafening, empty silence.
