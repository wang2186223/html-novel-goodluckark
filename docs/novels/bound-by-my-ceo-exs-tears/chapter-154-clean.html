Chapter 156

I bolt upright.

"Yo, what's wrong?" Orion asks.

My brain is a scrambled mess. Goddammit, they had one job: keep Lyra safe. Why the hell is she in the ER? This is exactly why I wanted her locked down at the compound. Safe. Sound. No variables.

"Edmund," I spit out, my jaw aching from how hard I'm grinding my teeth. "Lyra's in the hospital."

Panic and pure rage fight for control inside my chest. Losing her again? Not an option. Not after I finally got her back.

"Move. I'm driving," Orion says, grabbing his keys.

I jerk my head in a nod and storm out.

"Clear my schedule. Everything," I bark at my secretary as I blow past her desk toward the elevators.

My face must look like murder. People in the corridor scramble to get out of my way, parting like the freaking Red Sea.

The elevator drops toward the basement garage, my chest thumping hard. My brain keeps looping the last time she ended up in the ER. Over and over. A sick, twisted replay. Terror claws at my throat, painting worst-case scenarios in my head. Fuck. I need to see her. Now.

We throw ourselves into the car, and Orion hits the gas, tires screeching as we peel out.

"Feels like a sick joke, doesn't it?" I mutter, a heavy wave of déjà vu hitting me square in the chest.

That day. The agony. The sheer terror that ripped my soul into tiny, jagged shards. I want to erase that memory, but it's burned into my brain like cheap ink.

"Hey, she's tough. She'll make it," Orion says, cutting through my spiral.

I shut my mouth. Words won't fix this. Only seeing her breathing will.

The streets flash by in a blur. Next thing I know, we're slamming to a halt in the ER drop-off zone. The second the engine dies, I'm out the door, sprinting for the entrance.

I burst through the sliding doors. A nurse spots me and immediately waves me over. They all know my face here. Not because of my name or my money, but because I basically lived here when Lyra was in that coma.

"The doctors are in there with her," she says, leading the way. "Her folks are waiting in the lounge."

I throw the waiting room door open. Edmund and Beatrice snap upright. Edmund's face is a mask of cold stone; Beatrice is a red-eyed, weeping mess. She's clutching baby Aurora, who is screaming her lungs out.

Instinct takes over. I snatch the baby from Beatrice's arms and tuck her against my chest.

"Shh, it's okay, baby girl. I got you," I murmur, rocking her to kill the panic. Her screams die down to ragged whimpers, her tiny fingers clutching my shirt.

"Let me grab her," the nurse says, reaching out. "She's likely starving, poor thing."

I nod and hand her over. The nurse slips out just as Orion jogs into the room.

I turn on her parents, keeping my voice to a deadly, low hiss. "What the hell happened? I trusted you guys to watch her."

"We did!" Edmund shoots back. "I swear, we don't know. She went to use the restroom. Next thing, Aurora runs out looking completely freaked. She told us Lyra passed out on the floor."

Beatrice chimes in, her voice shaking. "We didn't stop to ask questions or figure out why she was in there with Lyra. We just needed to get her to the ER."

"Wait, where's Aurora now?" Orion asks, looking around.

"No clue," Edmund says. "She vanished right after she told us."

Dammit. My blood is boiling. I need to smash a wall, or someone's face.

Right then, the doctor steps out.

"Declan," he starts, giving me a hard, disapproving look. "When I let Lyra go, I assumed you'd keep her safe. I didn't expect to see her back in my ER two weeks later."

I throw a nasty glare at her parents, then lock eyes with the doc. "Is she okay?"

"She's stable," he says. "Still out cold, but she should come to in a couple of hours."

My chest loosens a fraction, but the knot is still there. I'm not breathing easy until she opens her eyes.

"Did she take a hit to the head when she went down?" the doctor asks.

"I don't think so," Beatrice stammers. "Why?"

"She's got a nasty gash on the back of her skull. We patched it up and stopped the bleeding, but I need the details for the chart."

"She was on her side when we got to her," Edmund says, shaking his head. "No way she hit her head falling like that."

The realization hits me, and my anger turns into a raging wildfire.

Someone did this to her. And knowing how batshit crazy Aurora is, I'd bet my entire damn bank account she's responsible.

"That's weird," the doctor mutters, scratching his chin.

"So she's still out?" I ask.

He nods.

"Perfect. Back in a second." I turn to Orion. "Keys. Now."

He hesitates, staring at me, but finally drops the fob into my palm. "Hey, man, where are you going?"

I don't say a damn word.

I spin on my heel and storm out of the waiting room, leaving them all behind. My blood is pure fire.

Time to go pay Aurora a little visit. Nobody touches my girl and gets away with it.
