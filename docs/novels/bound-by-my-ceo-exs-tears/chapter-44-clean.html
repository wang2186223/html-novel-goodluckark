Chapter 44

"No way. You're pulling my leg, right?"

Chloe didn't laugh. She just looked miserable and shoved her phone into my face.

The headline practically screamed at me: *AVA SHARP’S HOUSE BURNS DOWN HOURS AFTER REVEALING IDENTITY AS HOPE FOUNDATION FOUNDER.*

I stared. Read it once. Read it twice. *Come on, this is some sick joke.* I swiped down. A video loaded. Orange flames eating a roof.

Holy shit. That was my house. No denying it.

My stomach dropped. I slammed the phone onto the table, scraped my chair back, and bolted.

"Lyra, hold on!" Chloe yelled.

I didn't hear her. I didn't see anyone. I was just a streak of panic tearing through the hallway and bursting out the front doors.

I threw myself into the driver's seat, cranked the engine, and slammed the gas. Tires screeched. In my rearview mirror, Chloe ran out, waving her arms like a maniac. I ignored her and hit the road.

My brain was a total mess. Fear and fury fighting for control.

*Did I leave the stove on? Did I?* I couldn't remember. I literally couldn't think.

My phone buzzed. Declan. I swiped decline. No way in hell was I dealing with him right now.

Then Adrian called. Then Piper. Corbin. My mom. Buzz, buzz, buzz. I ignored every single one. I just needed to see the wreckage.

"Watch where you're going, you crazy bitch!" some guy shrieked, slamming his brakes. I'd almost clipped him. He threw up a middle finger.

"Sorry!" I yelled out the window, not even slowing down. He kept shouting, but his voice faded as I sped off.

Twenty minutes later, I hit my street.

I crawled. The road was blocked by cruisers, a massive red fire engine, and a crowd of nosey neighbors. I parked on the curb, jumped out, and ran the rest of the way.

I stopped dead.

It was a nightmare. Actual firemen were spraying hoses, but the roof was already gone. The whole place was just a giant bonfire.

Something cracked inside my chest. It wasn't a mansion, sure, but it was home. Asher and I had built a life there these past few months. Good times. Real memories. Gone.

My eyes stung. *Seriously? Why me? Haven't I paid enough dues?*

"Lyra."

That deep voice. I spun around. Declan was standing right there, looking at me with this pitying, puppy-dog expression. God, I hated that look.

"What are you doing here?" I snapped, wiping my face real quick.

At least the fire hadn't spread to the neighbors. Thank god. I couldn't live with myself if someone else got hurt because of my drama.

"You weren't picking up," he said, taking a step closer. "I got worried."

Man, I wanted a hug. I wanted to just collapse into someone. But not him. Never him.

"I'm fine," I said. I took one last, miserable look at the ashes, then turned on my heel. Nothing left to do here.

I walked fast. I could hear his heavy boots crunching behind me. I had to find a place to sleep tonight, and I needed to figure it out now.

I unlocked my car, hopped in, and went to pull the door shut.

Nope. Declan grabbed the handle, swung the passenger door open, and shoved his massive frame right into the seat.

"What the hell?" I gasped. "Get out!"

"You're a wreck," he said, clicking his seatbelt. "I'm not letting you drive alone. Period."

"Get out of my car, Declan."

He didn't move.

"Seriously, get the fuck out."

He gave me this deadpan, icy stare. "Either you drive, or we sit here all day. Your call, babe."

I glared. He glared back. Two minutes of pure silent warfare. I broke first. I couldn't deal.

"What about your ride?" I muttered, twisting the key. Declan didn't do cabs; he definitely had a driver or his own car.

"Dennis'll bring my car back. I'll call him later," he grunted.

I threw the car into reverse and backed out.

"Home," I whispered, my throat tight. "I don't even have one."

"We'll fix it," he said.

"Will we?" I muttered. I had this horrible gut feeling that things were about to get way worse.

I grabbed my phone and dialed my realtor. He picked up instantly.

"Oh, Lyra, I am so incredibly sorry. I just saw the news," he squeaked.

"Yeah, thanks," I sighed. "Look, tell me you've got something for me. Anything. I'm literally on the street."

A hotel sounded depressing. If he said no, I was going to lose it.

"Actually, yeah. I have one place. It's perfect. You'll love it."

I let out a breath I didn't know I was holding. "Send me the address. I'll go look now. If it works, I'll sign."

We hung up.

"So, a new place?" Declan's voice cut through the quiet. "You know I have empty properties. You could've just asked. I'd give you the keys."

I jumped. I'd honestly forgotten he was sitting right next to me.

"No thanks," I snapped. "I can buy my own damn house. I don't need your charity."

He mumbled something under his breath. I didn't catch it, and I didn't care.

The silence in the car got thick and awkward.

"How'd you get the cash anyway?" Declan asked, breaking the ice. "Last I checked, your dad cut you off completely."

I wanted to ignore him, but then I realized what he was doing. He was trying to keep my mind off the smoking pile of ash we just left.

"I knew our marriage wouldn't last," I said, staring at the road. "One of us was going to tap out. I figured it'd be you the second Aurora came back."

He winced at her name. Took a sharp breath.

I didn't care what went down between them yesterday. Not my business.

"I didn't want a dime of your money. Just Asher's child support. But I couldn't give him the life he deserved on a teacher's salary. So, four years into our marriage, I took my savings and dumped them into a startup."

I paused, checking my mirrors.

"The bank guys told me I was crazy. Said the risk was too high, that I'd lose every cent. But Tom’s Logistics blew up. Now I'm a silent partner."

I couldn't help but smile. It was a massive company now.

"Once the dividends started rolling in, I took some business classes. Learned the ropes. Then I started angel investing. Startups that couldn't get bank loans came to me. If their pitch was good, I wrote the check."

"Every single one paid off. Eventually, they buy me out, or I keep a seat on the board."

I glanced at him. His jaw was slightly slack. He looked genuinely blown away.

"And you've been doing this for five years?" he asked softly.

"Yep. When I made my first million, I was so hyped. I couldn't wait to tell you. I wanted you to be proud of me, you know? To see I wasn't just some useless housewife."

The memory hit me like a physical blow.

"I waited up all night. You didn't show. So I waited till morning. I sat next to you at the kitchen island, super excited, and told you I had big news."

I swallowed hard, trying to clear the lump in my throat.

"You didn't even look at me. Just gave me this freezing glare and said you didn't care. You said my life was irrelevant to you. Then you told me I could drop dead right there and you wouldn't give a shit. Told me to stop ruining your morning and go bother someone else."

The silence in the car was deafening. I saw his throat muscle twitch as he swallowed.

"Lyra..." he started, his voice rough.

"So, I shut up," I cut him off. "I never brought it up again. You made it clear you didn't care, so I kept my life to myself."

It still stung, but like everything with Declan, I was numb to it now. Eventually, the pain would fade completely.

We sat in silence. I reached to turn on the radio, but his phone buzzed. He picked up.

"Yeah, she's with me," he said.

He listened for a second. "Got it. We're on our way."

He hung up and looked at me. "The police chief wants you at the station."

"Why?"

"It's about the fire. He's got a meeting soon, so he wants to talk now. You'll have to push back the house viewing."

I didn't even argue. I just whipped the wheel and headed toward the precinct.

"Why did he call *you*?"

"Because I made sure I was involved," Declan said.

"I told you to butt out," I groaned. "Seriously, stay out of my business."

Why was he so dense? We were over.

"You're the mother of my kid, Lyra. Someone is targeting you. I'm not sitting on my hands."

The station was close. We pulled up, and Declan practically jumped out before the car stopped. I scrambled out and caught up to him as we walked into Chief Garrett's office.

"Have a seat," Garrett said.

"What do you have?" Declan demanded, leaning over the desk.

Garrett sighed. "Look, there's no easy way to say this. We're treating this as arson."

My brain stalled. "What?"

"Someone set that fire on purpose, Lyra," Garrett said slowly. "And we think they thought you were inside. This was an attempted murder."

Ice water flooded my veins.

If I hadn't met Chloe early this morning... I would've been in that bed. I would've burned alive.

I cheated death. Again. But how many times could I get lucky?
