Chapter 222

Boom. Just like that, my brain clicked. Everything came rushing back. Calling me pissed off? That’s a joke. I was fucking nuclear.

"You lied to me!" I screamed, slamming my palm right into Declan’s chest. Might as well have hit a concrete wall, but I didn't care. "You lying piece of shit. For months, Declan. You kept this up for fucking months!"

He froze. The realization hit him like a physical blow—he knew I remembered. For a second, actual fear flickered in his eyes. I’d never seen him look scared before, but then again, he’d never seen me this goddamn furious.

"I’m out of here," I muttered to the room, not even looking at him. I scanned the place, spotted my keys on the counter, and snatched them up. I made a break for the door, but Declan lunged and grabbed my wrist.

"Whoa, hold on, you can't go," he pleaded, his eyes going all soft and desperate. "You passed out, Lyra. I need to get you to an ER. You need a doctor, okay?"

"Get your hands off me, Declan." I yanked my arm, but his grip only locked tighter. "I swear to God, I am not telling you twice."

"Please, baby," he begged. But I was so over his shit.

I pivoted my hips, wound up my right fist, and drove it straight into his face. *Crack.* The sound of his nose snapping gave me a sick, beautiful rush of dopamine. He stumbled back, totally blindsided, his hands flying to his bloody face in pure shock.

"We are not done," I spat, my voice cold as ice. I turned on my heel. As I stormed out, I caught Silas standing by the bed out of the corner of my eye. I didn't say a damn word to him either. He was in on it too. The whole fucking lot of them had played me, but Declan? Declan was the one who deserved to burn.

I threw myself into the driver's seat, slammed the key into the ignition, and tore out of the driveway, tires screaming.

Inside my skull, my old life and my new life were crashing together, fusing into one chaotic mess. My head throbbed like crazy, but I had to push through. I needed to handle this right fucking now.

Every single lie he’d fed me started unraveling, fueling the fire in my chest. Honestly, why was I even shocked? This was classic Declan. The guy was a master manipulator, always twisting every situation to get exactly what he wanted. I should’ve known. But hey, hindsight is a real bitch, isn't it?

I’d walked straight into his trap like a total idiot. How did I miss the red flags? Actually, scratch that. I didn't miss them. I knew he was full of shit. I knew he was hiding things, but I chose to play dumb because I wanted to believe in the stupid, perfect fairytale he was selling me.

Right now, I didn't know whether to burst into tears or applaud the sheer, psycho dedication it took for him to keep me.

I don't even remember the drive to the police station. I was so blinded by rage and panic, it’s a miracle I didn’t wrap my car around a telephone pole.

I forced myself to numb out. I had a job to do. Declan and his web of lies could wait until I handled this. Once I was done here, though? He was dead meat.

"Where's the chief?" I demanded, cornering the first cop I saw.

The guy frowned, giving me a once-over. I probably looked like a total trainwreck, but who cares? "Uh, no, ma'am," he said, trying to be polite. "He's off today. Not scheduled to come in."

Like hell he was. "Call him," I snapped. "I need him. Now."

"I can't do that, ma'am. Like I said, it's his day off."

Every second I stood there, my blood boiled hotter. I was already at my limit, and this rent-a-cop was pushing me right over the edge.

"Tell him it's Willow. Trust me, he'll want to take this call."

"Sorry, ma'am, but rules are—" That was it. I snapped. I slammed both hands down on his desk with a loud *BANG*, making half the precinct freeze and stare.

"You call him right fucking now," I hissed, leaning in close, my jaw locked tight. "Or I swear to God, I will tear this entire precinct apart. Do not play with me today. I have had the worst fucking day of my life, and you do *not* want to be the one who pushes me over. Get the chief on the phone before I lose whatever sanity I have left."

He took one look at my face, paled, and practically dove for the receiver. I left him to dial Garrett and slumped into a plastic chair nearby, rubbing my temples. My brain felt like it was about to explode.

I sat there, bouncing my heel against the linoleum floor, counting the agonizing seconds.

To his credit, Garrett got there fast. When he walked through the double doors, I tried to stand up, but the room spun, and I stumbled. He rushed over, catching my arm to steady me. "Whoa, Lyra, are you okay?"

"Yeah, fine... just a hell of a day," I muttered, shaking him off. "We need to talk. Privately."

He nodded, his face serious, and gestured toward his office.

Once the door clicked shut and we sat down, he leaned forward. "Alright, what's going on?"

"Let Aurora go," I said, cutting straight to the chase.

Garrett stared at me, his jaw dropping. He looked like I’d just spoken in alien.

"Why the hell would I do that, Lyra?" he asked, totally baffled.

I took a deep, shaky breath. "Because I remember everything now. Right before I went down, I saw the shooter. And it wasn't Aurora."
