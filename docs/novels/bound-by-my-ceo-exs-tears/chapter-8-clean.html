NovelVibe

Chapter 8

Her face goes dead. Just like that, the warmth vanishes, leaving me freezing.

"What do you want, Declan?" she asks. Her voice is a flat line. I’m already pushing past her into the entryway.

It’s the tone you use on a telemarketer. Like I’m a nobody. I just stand there, dumbfounded. Ten years under the same roof, and I can't find a single damn word to say.

My eyes drop to her arm, still strapped in that sling. I only came to check on her. Plus, it’s Friday. My weekend with Asher.

Then I remember the guy I just saw walking out. That smile she had? Yeah, it was for him.

My jaw locks. I try to swallow the sudden, stupid spike of anger in my throat. "Who was that?" I demand.

Sure, he’s a cop. Sure, he saved her life. But he’s crossing a line. I hate the guy. I don't want him anywhere near her.

"None of your business," she snaps.

"It is my business when you're hosting guys at the crack of dawn," I fire back. "With my son in the house. Did he crash here? Is that why he's just leaving?"

The thought tastes like battery acid. We’ve been split up for five minutes, and she’s already bringing random guys around Asher? It’s trashy. It’s not a good look.

She lets out this dry, humorless bark of a laugh.

"Are you kidding me right now? It’s none of your damn business, Declan. Do I spy on you and Aurora? No. So cut the hypocritical judge act."

I glare. "Aurora is different."

"Oh, really?" She plays dumb, then snaps her fingers like she just solved a puzzle. Pure sarcasm. "Right. Forgot. She’s the love of your life. My bad."

I grind my teeth. She’s begging for a fight. What is her problem lately? It’s like she hates my guts.

"I’d never do anything to hurt Asher," she says, stepping closer. "But I’m single now. I can sleep with whoever I want. I’m going to date, Declan. I’m not spending the rest of my life alone."

My hands ball into fists. Something hot flares up in my chest. I want to punch a wall.

She spins on her heel and walks off. I stand in the hall, taking a slow breath to keep from losing it. Kitchen sounds—pots clattering—echo down the corridor. I look around.

I’ve never actually been inside this place. It’s nothing like our old house. It’s so different it throws me off.

I shake it off and follow the noise. She’s in the kitchen, aggressively wiping down the island.

She looks up, eyes flashing. "Still here? I was hoping you’d take the hint and get lost."

Jesus. Why is everything she says lately laced with poison? It’s like her only goal is to rip me and my family apart.

"It’s the weekend, Lyra," I growl. "I’m here to pick up Asher."

"He’s asleep. You could’ve just honked from the driveway like you always do. No need to come inside."

I pull out a barstool and sit. She scowls, opening her mouth to launch another insult. I cut her off before she can start. "I’ll wait. We need to talk anyway."

Her scowl deepens. She squeezes the dish towel in her fist.

"We have nothing to talk about, Declan. We stick to the custody schedule, and we pretend the other person doesn't exist. That's the deal."

I sigh, rubbing my temples. Since when did she get this stubborn? It’s driving me crazy. Where did the quiet, easygoing woman go?

"Isn't this what you wanted?" she asks. "Me out of your hair?"

I pinch the bridge of my nose. "I’m not the one who filed for divorce."

I’m already regretting stepping foot in here. She just wants to fight, and I don’t have the energy. I have a meeting with Kanta later.

"Sure," she says, her voice dripping bitter. "But you wanted it. And hey, perfect timing. Aurora’s back in town. Now you finally get to be with your dream girl."

It’s a cheap shot, but I never lied to her. I never led her on. She always knew Aurora had my heart. If we hadn't made that mistake nine years ago, Aurora would be my wife.

"What do you want me to say?" I ask. "I never lied to you. You knew how I felt about her."

She slams the dish towel onto the counter. "Didn't stop you from using my body, did it? God, I hate you. I can't believe I wasted so many years on you."

My jaw tightens. That stings. Yeah, we slept together. We were married. It was physical, an itch to scratch. But I took vows. I didn't cheat on her, even if I didn't love her.

"I'm not doing this," I say, shutting it down. "I'm here for Asher. Let's talk about him."

We're just running in circles. I need to say my piece and get out before I lose my temper.

Mentioning Asher works. She stops. She reaches into a cabinet, grabs a prescription bottle, and pops two pills with one hand.

I read the label. Painkillers.

"How's the arm?" I ask.

"Just say what you came to say, Declan. We both know you don't give a damn about me, so drop the act."

My hand clenches. "Dammit, Lyra!"

"What? It's the truth. Talk, or get out. I'll text you when Asher's up."

She turns to walk away. I reach out and grab her good wrist. She yanks it back instantly, like my hand is a hot stove.

"Don't touch me!" she spits.

I throw my hands up. Seriously? Is this how it's going to be now? Just constant screaming matches? We have a kid together.

"This childish attitude is exactly why I always preferred Aurora," I snap, my temper finally redlining. "Why I fell for her."

Her face goes completely blank. Her eyes turn ice-cold.

"Say what you came to say, then get the hell out of my house. I'm not letting you insult me and compare me to her. My life is none of your business anymore. We're divorced. Go lecture your precious Aurora if you want to play therapist."

We just stare at each other. Neither of us blinks. She’s actually standing up to me now.

"Look," I mutter, rubbing my face. "I'm sorry."

She's right. I shouldn't have said that. Comparing them was a low blow. We can't fight like this. It'll mess Asher up, and I won't let that happen.

"Take your apology and shove it—"

"Lyra," I warn.

"Fine," she rolls her eyes, a nasty smirk on her face. "What is it? Hurry up. I've got men to entertain, remember?"

I groan but let it slide.

"Your mom and my parents are going into witness protection. It's because of the gang case. I want Asher to go with them."

Her smirk vanishes. "Why?"

"Because I helped put them away. I'm getting threats. They know Asher is my weak spot."

"And Aurora," she cuts in, her voice dripping with sarcasm. "Can't forget your little princess."

I run a hand through my hair. God, this morning is a nightmare, and she is pushing every single button I have.

I ignore the jab. "Your dad is already dead because of these people, Lyra. I'm not risking our son."

"Why didn't you tell me this yesterday?"

"You stormed out before we could finish the conversation."

"How long will he be gone?"

"Until they catch them and the threat is gone."

She nods slowly, swallowing hard. "When does he leave?"

"The day after tomorrow."

I can see the pain in her face. She doesn't want to let him go. But she knows it's the only way to keep him safe. At least we agree on that—we'd both die for him.

"Fine," she says quietly. "I'll pack his bags. You take him today, I get him tomorrow. That's fair."

"Deal," I say.

She turns and heads down the hall to wake him up. Standing there alone, it finally hits me.

The look she used to give me—the warmth, the love—it's completely gone.

There's nothing left. Now, she looks at me the exact same way I used to look at her. Like I'm nothing but a mistake.
