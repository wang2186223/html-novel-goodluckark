Chapter 86

Two days. That’s how long it’s been since some bastards grabbed Aurora and me. The cops swarmed the city looking for Kaelen, but the guy ghosted. Again. The few grunts they actually locked up? Tight-lipped. Not a damn word. Now, I’m jumpy. Every little noise makes me flinch. I can’t live like this, looking over my shoulder, getting dragged into drama that isn’t even mine.

"Hey, Mom? Can I hop on the Xbox?"

Asher’s voice snaps me out of it. Thank God. I’ve been manic-cleaning all morning just to keep my brain from short-circuiting. Right now, I’m folding laundry. Once this basket is empty, I’m out of distractions.

"Yeah, go ahead," I say. "When’s Jasper coming over?"

Those two are practically glued together lately. Best friends at school, best friends at home. It’s sweet. Honestly, it reminds me of how Declan, Orion, and Corbin used to be back in the day.

"Like, three o'clock," Asher says.

"Cool. I'll whip up some snacks for you guys."

He plants a quick, sticky kiss on my cheek. "You rock, Mom."

Then he’s gone, and the silence hits me. Right back to the mental hamster wheel. Seriously, my head is a mess. For forty-eight hours, my brain hasn't shut up. It’s exhausting. Even my dreams are stressed out.

And Declan? Don't even get me started. He keeps ringing my phone, but I just shove it at Asher. I can't. I literally do not have the bandwidth to decode whatever new, caring-guy persona he’s trying to pull off.

It makes zero sense. For nine years, I was invisible to him. Now, suddenly, he cares? What flipped the switch? Nothing changed. I’m still Lyra. Still the same girl whose heart he trampled on for a decade.

A few years ago, I would’ve crawled through glass for this. Now? Too much bad blood. Too many nasty words you can't unsay, too many cheap shots you can't take back.

*But isn't this the dream?* a tiny voice whispers. *To have him actually look at you? To want you?*

Sure. I won't lie. I used to pray for the day Declan would crave me. But that ship sailed, hit an iceberg, and sank. I killed those fantasies years ago. That was little-girl stuff. Naive, desperate-for-love stuff. The woman I am today knows better. Some things are just dead on arrival.

I’ve learned the hard way that pain hits way harder than love ever could. The people you love can shatter you into a million tiny, sharp pieces. Love doesn't fix everything. You can't beg someone to love you back. And once trust is busted? It’s done. You don't glue a shattered heart back together and pretend it's brand new.

If I fall for Declan's new routine, I’m an idiot. He wrecked me once. Never again. I am not going back to looking in the mirror and wondering who the hell is staring back at me. No way.

My kids and my peace of mind. That’s all that matters now. It has to be. I don’t need a boyfriend to feel whole.

The whole Prince Charming thing? Dead and buried. Declan and Adrian made sure of that. They proved that fairy tales are just pretty lies we tell kids.

A sharp knock on the front door jerks me back to reality. I check my phone. Shoot, it’s almost three. I’ve been sitting here staring into space for ninety minutes. I need to get a grip. This mental spiral is getting ridiculous.

I push myself up with a sigh. Probably Jasper. I’ll feed the kids, then crash for a much-needed nap.

I swing the door open. My jaw drops.

It’s Jasper, yeah. But his dad is standing right behind him.

"Oh. Hey," I mumble, hugging Jasper.

The kid doesn't wait. He slips past me and bolts straight up the stairs.

Which leaves Silas and me. Just standing there. Staring. Awkward as hell.

I haven't laid eyes on the guy since his last visit. Jasper basically lives at my place, and Asher hangs out at theirs, but Silas? Ghost mode. It felt like he was actively dodging me.

He’s shifting his weight, looking like he wants to bolt.

"Uh, you wanna come in?" I ask.

"Yeah. If it's cool."

I step back. He hesitates for a second, then finally steps over the threshold.

I lead him into the kitchen, pointing to a stool while I start grabbing stuff for the kids' snacks.

"Heard about what happened," Silas says quietly. "Just wanted to check on you."

Right. The kidnapping. It was all over the local news by Tuesday night. Thank God nobody knows I’m a Howell—I can’t deal with that media circus. Everyone still thinks I’m a Sharp, so the tabloids are having a field day guessing why someone would snatch the Sharp sisters.

"Oh. Thanks," I say, keeping it brief. The tension in the room is thick enough to cut with a knife.

"How's the face looking?"

"Better."

The swelling is mostly gone, but I’ve still got a nasty, deep purple bruise painted across my cheekbone.

Silence stretches. Neither of us knows what to say. Honestly, I wish he’d just go. Awkward small talk makes me break out in a cold sweat.

"Look, I'm sorry," Silas blurts out, breaking the quiet.

I turn around, blinking. "For what?"

"For being a jerk to you. You've been great to me and Jasper, and I've been... cold. It's not right."

I just stare. My brain stalls. I definitely didn't see a heartfelt apology coming when I let him in.

"I've had some really bad runs with women," he mutters, rubbing the back of his neck. "Trusting people... it's hard for me."

I swallow hard. Oh. I totally misread him.

"So... Jasper's mom," I say softly. "She didn't just pass away, did she? She really messed you up."

His face falls, confirming everything. Whoever she is, she put him through hell, and he’s still burning. The guy is drowning in his own head. If he doesn't snap out of it, it's going to eat him alive.

"Yeah," he whispers, his voice cracking. He tries to play it cool, but he's hurting bad. "But I don't wanna go there. It's too fucking painful."

My heart aches for him. He looks so damn lost. Maybe I’m projecting, or maybe I just recognize a fellow member of the broken-hearts club. Either way, I want to throw him a lifeline. You can't always cure the pain—mine is still throbbing—but you can learn to carry it without sinking. Silas is just treading water. He’s surviving, not living. And that’s no way to exist, especially when you’ve got a kid looking up to you.

"I get it, Silas," I say, looking him dead in the eye. "Seriously, fuck, do I get it. Apology accepted. So, how about we just be friends?"

He looks skeptical, like he's waiting for the catch, but then he gives a slow, hesitant nod.

He’s a lone wolf, that much is obvious. No backup, no squad. He needs people to drag him out of his cave and remind him how to breathe. That’s what Piper and Adrian did for me. Yeah, Adrian turned out to be a massive prick, but I’ll still thank him for proving that life isn't just one long tragedy.

"Friends?" I ask, sticking my hand out.

"Friends," he says, his lips twitching into a tiny smile as he takes my hand.

But as our fingers lock, a weird chill hits my chest. I don't think he's a bad guy, but something tells me Silas moving next door wasn't an accident. The guy is carrying some heavy baggage, and when those bags finally open, everything is going to blow up.
