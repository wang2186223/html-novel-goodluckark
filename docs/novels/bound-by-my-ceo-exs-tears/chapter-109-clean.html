Chapter 110

That damn note had been living rent-free in my head for days. I just needed to catch the creep behind it so I could finally get some peace.

Now? I was a twitchy, paranoid mess, and I hated it. Even Asher noticed I was off. Every time he asked, I played it cool and lied through my teeth.

God, life with Declan used to be so simple. Back then, my biggest dramas were whether he’d show up for dinner or if he’d ever actually love me. Sure, it hurt like hell, but emotional bruising beat getting murdered any day of the week.

Ever since the papers were signed, my life had been a literal action movie. Three near-death experiences. A car bomb, a house fire, and two freaking kidnappings. I figured letting Kaelen in would finally quiet things down. Nope. New psycho, same old story.

"Hey, Mom, can I head over to Jasper’s?" Asher’s voice made me jump out of my skin.

I clutched my chest, trying to slow my racing pulse. Case in point: total basket case.

He gave me a weird look. I brushed it off, plastering on a fake, shaky smile. "Sure, sweetie. But use the back door, okay?"

He nodded, and I scrambled up. No way was I letting him cross over to Silas’s place without watching him walk through their door.

"We're doing video games today. I crushed him last time, so Jasper's hoping for a comeback," he chattered as we walked.

"Awesome, buddy," I said.

Silas had been a ghost since that kitchen incident. Total radio silence. Was he embarrassed? Hurt because I shut him down? Probably both. I kept my distance, too. The last thing we needed was more awkward tension.

"Later, Mom!" Asher called, jogging over to Cal’s yard.

I waited until the door clicked shut behind him before turning back to my own house.

I barely stepped inside when a knock rattled the front door. My stomach did a flip. Please, God, let it not be another creepy letter.

I pulled it open. Declan. Shit. Our scheduled chat had completely slipped my mind. With everything going on, our little "talk" was at the bottom of my list.

"Hey," he muttered, shoving his hands deep into his pockets. He looked incredibly anxious—a look that definitely didn't fit the Declan I knew.

"Get inside," I said, scanning the street. I didn't need some stalker dropping off a fresh threat while my ex-husband was standing on the porch.

He raised an eyebrow.

"What?" I snapped.

"Honestly? I expected you to slam the door in my face."

"Are you coming in or what?" I gritted out.

He threw me one more look and stepped past me. I slammed the door shut behind him, convinced there were eyes on me.

This paranoia was eating me alive. I needed to wrap this conversation up fast before I lost my mind completely.

I sank into my recliner. I'd bought the thing a few weeks ago for my aching back—right around the time I had to swap out my mattress because it was killing my spine.

"Alright, what's so urgent?" I asked, locking eyes with him. "You made it sound like a big deal. Is something wrong with Asher?"

He let out a long, heavy breath. "No. It's about us, Lyra."

I stared. Was he out of his mind? What the hell was he even playing at?

"There is no 'us,' Declan. You keep forgetting that." I really didn't have the bandwidth for this. Not today.

"Just... please, hear me out," he begged, his voice dropping to a whisper.

The raw desperation in his tone caught me off guard. I didn't want to overanalyze it, so I just shut up and let him speak.

He hesitated, searching for the words. The silence stretched, tense and heavy, until he finally blurted, "I love you."

My jaw hit the floor. I stared at him, totally speechless. Since he was right there, I reached out and slapped a hand onto his forehead. "Are you high? Burning up? Or did my ears just fail me?"

He shot me a quick glare, but his face softened almost instantly. He caught my hand, flipped it over, and pressed a soft, lingering kiss right into my palm.

"I don't know when it happened, Lyra, but I'm in love with you. I was too bitter, too angry back then to see what I had. These past few months without you? Pure hell. Seeing you hurt absolutely guts me. It took me too long to figure it out, but I'm here now. Begging. Just give me a shot to love you the way you always deserved."

I watched, totally paralyzed, as he slid out of his seat and dropped to his knees right in front of me. It felt surreal, like I'd slipped into some weird parallel universe.

"Oh, Declan," I stammered, trying to force my brain to work. "You don't love me. You never have. Aurora is your soulmate, remember? She's the one who owns your heart."

A flash of pure agony crossed his face. I felt a twinge of pity, but come on—he had to be confused. It made zero sense. How do you go from hating someone to loving them?

"You're not hearing me, Lyra," he said, his pain sharpening into frustration.

"I am hearing you! You're the one who's mixed up. Love me now? Seriously? You loathed me until five minutes ago. You spent years proving how little I mattered to you, tearing me down to punish me for 'ruining' things with Aurora. You obsessed over her for a decade, and now I'm just supposed to believe you love me?"

That old, familiar ache flared up in my chest, but I shoved it right back down. I didn't have the energy for another heartbreak.

"I know. You'll never know how much I hate myself for what I did," he whispered, his voice cracking as he stared up at me. "But please. Just one chance. I swear I'll never hurt you again. Let me fix what I broke."

"Declan, saying the words doesn't make them real. We had nine years, and you never gave me a single shot. I loved you with every piece of my soul, and you shattered me. How am I supposed to just get over that? Why should I give you a chance when you never gave me one?"

I looked away, unable to stomach the guilt and misery swimming in his eyes. It was just too damn much.

"Please," he breathed.

"Why now, Declan? Seriously, tell me. If this isn't some joke, why do you suddenly love me now when you couldn't for nine years?"

He stared at me, then dropped his gaze to the floor. Silence. He didn't have an answer, because there wasn't one that made any sense.

I let out a heavy sigh. "Look, you have to see how crazy this sounds. I'm sorry, but no. You spent nine years breathing Aurora. You can't expect me to believe all that magically vanished in a couple of months."

His shoulders slumped. If my ankles weren't throbbing, I'd be pacing the room right now.

But then, the defeat in his eyes hardened into pure grit. He leaned in, cupping my cheek with his hand.

"I get it. It's a lot, and you don't trust me. But I'm not backing down, Lyra. I will prove I love you, even if it takes me a lifetime. I'm not stopping until you believe me," he vowed, his voice dropping an octave, dead serious.
