Chapter 247

"Hell no!" The words rip out of my mouth before I can stop them. I actually scare myself with how loud I say it.

Orion just stares. His eyes do this weird, unreadable flicker, and then—bam. Total stone. His face goes completely dead.

My throat goes dry. The air in the room suddenly feels heavy, like a storm's coming. Yeah, this is the guy I remember. The real Orion. The control freak who gets nasty the second he doesn't get his way.

"Oh, really? You won't even hear me out? Not even a pitch?" He sounds totally chill, but it's a total lie. Under that designer suit, he's a predator. A straight-up shark. He'll rip your throat out before you even realize you're bleeding.

"Nope," I say, keeping my voice steady. "Whatever game you're playing, count me out."

You don't sign contracts with the devil unless you're suicidal, and I'm not that dumb. I finally got my life back on track. No Orion, no drama. I like it that way.

Getting mixed up with him again would be brain-dead. The guy literally shredded my self-esteem and treated me like garbage. Even if I had zero responsibilities, I'd run the other way. One step closer to him means another trip to therapy. My chest still aches from the scars he left the first time around.

"Come on, babe. Sure you don't want to think about it? I'm pretty good at getting what I want." He sinks back into the couch, throws one leg over the other, and flashes that smug, punchable grin.

I sit up straight, squaring my shoulders. "Positive."

"You sure about that? Because I've got a little carrot to dangle."

My ears perk up. Dammit. I force the curiosity down, burying it deep. Curiosity kills, and I'm not trying to die today.

I look away, staring at the wall. "Doubt it, Orion. You don't have anything I want. Period."

Unless he can raise the dead, he's got nothing. And even then, I wouldn't trust a miracle if it came from his mouth.

"How about Unity Ventures?" he says, totally casual. My head whips back to him so fast my neck pops.

He's practically glowing with smugness. He thinks he's got me. He thinks the family name is my leash and I'm about to crawl back to him. "Nice try," I spit out. "Unity went under years ago. Or did you forget I had a front-row seat to the bankruptcy?"

Does he think I forgot Edward killing himself trying to save that sinking ship? I watched my brother bleed himself dry for it. It still went down, and we ended up flat broke.

A dull ache hits my chest, and I press my hand against it. Grandpa's pride and joy, gone. Mom cared about it too, but she cared about Dad more. If she hadn't handed him the keys to the kingdom and let him run it into the ground, maybe things would be different.

"Never say never, sweetheart," he says, and my stomach does a sick flip. "See, our marriage contract had a little clause. If anything happened to your brother, the company fell to me. Edward was too busy dying to notice that Unity was actually salvageable."

Ice water floods my veins. Edward got his diagnosis when I was eighteen. He fought for three years before it took him—right around our third anniversary. Orion didn't even wait for the dirt to settle on my brother's grave before he handed me the divorce papers.
