Chapter 67

I twisted the key in the deadbolt. Click.

"Hey, Mom, where are we going anyway?" Asher tugged my sleeve.

Honestly? I hadn't planned this road trip. But I had to do it. Beatrice and Edmund's missed calls had been piling up for days. I’d been freezing them out, playing defense. Time to stop. How could I ever know if their love was real if I kept building walls? Plus, let's be real—I needed some decent people in my corner.

"You're meeting some people," I said, grabbing his small hand and guiding him down the driveway.

Halfway to the SUV, a massive yellow truck caught my eye. It was parked just three doors down.

"Huh. Looks like we're getting a new neighbor," I muttered, pointing.

That place had been a ghost town for months. It was basically a carbon copy of my house, just with a slightly bigger footprint and a heftier price tag.

"Man, I seriously hope they have a kid my age," Asher piped up, eyes wide. "Don't hate me, Mom, but this neighborhood is literally a retirement home."

I snorted. He wasn't wrong. When I signed the lease, I wanted peace, quiet, and a massive yard. I didn't realize I was buying into a nosy-grandma syndicate. The only other kids around here lived fifteen houses down.

"Fingers crossed, kiddo." I hoisted him into his booster seat and clicked the belt.

I climbed behind the wheel, twisted the ignition, and felt my stomach do a slow flip. We hadn't even cleared the driveway, and my nerves were already shot. When your life is a series of trainwrecks, you get paranoid. Anyone trying to get close feels like a threat. I took a deep breath, shoved the anxiety down, and shifted into drive.

"So, seriously, who is it?" Asher bounced, his seatbelt strap vibrating.

The kid loathed suspense. Total carbon copy of his dad. Most kids love a mystery, but not Asher. He needed a blueprint, a schedule, zero surprises. Weird trait for a seven-year-old, but there it was.

"Zip it and wait," I teased, throwing him a quick grin. "They're some new friends of mine. And they've been begging to meet you."

His forehead puckered into a deep, dramatic scowl. God, he looked so much like Declan in that light. It was uncanny. The face, the attitude, the stubborn set of his jaw—all Declan.

"Come on, just tell me! You know I hate surprises," he whined.

See? Told you. The kid hated being out of the loop.

"I swear, it's a good one," I promised.

He was going to lose his mind when he found out he had two brand-new grandparents. More grandparents meant a double jackpot of toys and attention. Sure, he wanted a baby brother or sister eventually, but right now? He loved being the undisputed king of the family tree.

"Mom? Is Dad gonna marry Aunt Aurora?"

The question hit me like a physical blow. My lungs locked up.

"Yeah. Probably," I forced out, keeping my voice flat.

The old, familiar ache flared up in my chest, but it didn't shatter me this time. I'd processed this grief ages ago. Declan was never mine to keep. He belonged to Aurora—hook, line, and sinker. Sure, they were in a tailspin right now because of her lies, but they'd patch it up. Ten years of history doesn't just vanish. They'd fix the cracks, kiss, and make up. It was just a matter of time.

"I don't want that," he whispered, staring at his sneakers. "I don't want a stepmom."

My heart cracked for him, but my hands were tied. Declan and I were a mistake from day one. We never should've walked down that aisle. I'll always kick myself for not fighting harder when he pushed for the wedding. If I'd just said no, life would be so much simpler. Instead, we played house, faking a happy marriage for Asher's benefit. And now? The poor kid was stuck trying to process his dad moving on.

"Asher, baby..."

"I want you and Dad back together," he muttered, his lower lip trembling. "You guys were happy. You loved each other. Why can't we just go back to how it was?"

How do you tell a kid his entire childhood was a lie? That his parents were miserable. That we were just actors putting on a show for him. How do I explain that Declan's heart always belonged to Aurora, and that my own toxic obsession was the wedge that drove them apart?

"You're a little too young to get it right now, bud. But when you're older, I promise I'll explain. You'll see why your dad and I just don't work."

It sucked having to crush his hopes, but I couldn't feed him fairy tales. Declan and I were nothing. Zero spark, zero future. Our ship had sunk before it even left the harbor.

"I'm not giving up, Mom. I want you and Dad together. And I always get what I want." The sheer grit in his voice was terrifying.

I sighed, steering the car. "Not this time, sweetie."

The cabin went quiet. We drove in silence until the sprawling, ultra-rich estates of my biological parents' neighborhood loomed ahead.

I rolled up to the massive security gates. I leaned out, punched the code into the sleek touchscreen, and watched the iron gates swing open. Edmund had texted me the code, a standing invitation I'd finally accepted. We rolled down a winding, tree-lined private road. It took a solid five minutes of driving just to reach the front steps of the colossal mansion.

"Whoa. This place is insane," Asher gasped, nose pressed to the glass. "It's way bigger than Grandma and Grandpa's place." He meant Declan's parents.

I smirked. Oh, he had no idea. Just wait until he found out who owned this palace.

I killed the engine right by the perfectly manicured hedges. We hopped out, I grabbed his hand, and we walked up the stone path. Asher's head was on a swivel, trying to drink in every single detail. The kid was completely starstruck.

Before my finger even touched the brass doorbell, the massive double doors swung inward. A butler stood there, hands clasped behind his back.

"Welcome, ma'am, young sir," he said with a crisp nod. "Follow me, please. They're expecting you."

We trailed him through the foyer. The interior was pure luxury—jaw-droppingly gorgeous. He led us straight into a massive living room where Beatrice and Edmund were waiting.

The second Beatrice spotted us, she bolted upright. She threw her arms around me in a tight hug, then immediately lunged to wrap Asher in a bear hug. The poor kid went stiff as a board.

"Let him breathe, Beatrice, sweetheart," Edmund chuckled, rising from his chair. "You're going to smother him."
