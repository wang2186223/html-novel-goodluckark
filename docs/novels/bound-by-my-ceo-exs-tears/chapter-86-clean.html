Chapter 87

God, I was losing my mind. Just totally, utterly bored. Sure, weekends with Asher were fine, but weekdays? Pure torture.

Piper and Audrey were grinding at work all day. My parents too. Plus, I’d put Chloe in charge of running The Hope Foundation day-to-day. Going into the office was pointless—I’d just be sitting there waiting to scribble my signature on a few papers.

Silas and I had gotten pretty close over the last week. Turns out, he runs his own construction firm. Launched it two years back, and he says business is booming. Translation: he’s booked solid during daylight hours too.

I actually started craving my old job. I’m only five months pregnant, so it’s not like the baby is popping out tomorrow. But instead of job hunting, I grabbed my phone and punched in a number.

"Hey, Mrs. Albright! How's it going?" I said.

She’s a neighbor who reached out last week. Her high schooler was totally bombing biology, and she’d begged me to tutor the kid.

"Oh, I'm wonderful, sweetie! So good to hear from you. How are things?" Her bubbly tone instantly made me grin.

"Good, thanks," I said, pausing a beat. "Hey, so... do you still need that tutor?"

Fingers crossed she hadn't hired someone else. Though, honestly, I had a backup list of other parents begging for help.

"Oh, absolutely!" she gasped. "I've been losing sleep over this. I was literally praying you'd change your mind."

When she first asked, I’d just started my maternity leave and said no. Big mistake. I had no clue staying home would feel this mind-numbing.

"Awesome," I cheered. "How about right after school? She can just swing by my place."

Afternoons worked great. It still left me time to secretly help out with our adult novelty business. I couldn't have my name on the brand, sure, but I could definitely pull some strings behind the scenes while Piper and Audrey handled the front-end stuff.

"Works for me!" she chirped. "So, what's your rate? Weekly or per session?"

"Actually, it's free," I replied. "I'm on a super long leave and just need to kill some time."

Dead silence. I thought the call dropped.

"Wait, seriously?" she finally asked.

"Yep. Send her over today. We can start right away."

I didn't need the dough. Between my bank accounts and investments, my great-great-grandkids could live like royalty. So yeah, cash wasn't the goal. Teaching was my jam, not a paycheck.

"O-oh, wow. Thank you so much, honey! She'll head over the second the bell rings," she stuttered, clearly blown away by the freebie.

"No problem at all."

I ended the call and dialed three other parents who'd hit me up before. Same reaction. Total shock when I offered my services for zero dollars. They kept asking, "Are you for real?"

By the third call, it hit me: these folks only knew me as a regular teacher. My neighborhood was strictly middle-class. Unlike the rich snobs who live for drama, these people were busting their asses to pay bills. They didn't have the time or energy to waste on trashy gossip rags.

Honestly? I loved that. I just wanted to be normal. No special treatment just because I had a fat bank account.

I slumped onto the couch, grinning. Finally, a productive day. The tension in my shoulders melted. For the first time since my life imploded, I felt like things might actually be okay.

I literally wanted to do a little jig. Just pure, unadulterated excitement buzzing in my veins. No clue why, but the vibe was immaculate.

I headed to the bedroom to wash off and get dressed.

I blasted the hot water, letting the steam work its magic. I stood under the spray for a good while before finally stepping out.

I scanned my closet. I'd purged everything Declan ever bought me and replaced it with a brand-new wardrobe.

I grabbed a cream-colored, body-con dress and slipped it on. I checked my reflection. Damn, I looked hot. This pregnancy was doing serious wonders for my curves, especially my backside. I gave myself a little approving nod.

Time for some makeup. I went with a soft, natural look, then curled my hair instead of leaving it flat. Looking good. I slipped into some cute ballet flats.

I was grabbing my keys when my phone buzzed.

No caller ID. I swiped answer anyway.

"Hello?"

"Hey, Lyra. It's Adrian." That deep, gravelly voice. I didn't even need the name drop to know it was him.

I'd burned that voice into my brain back when I was stupidly in love with him. Back when he sounded so warm and caring, making me feel like I actually mattered. Too bad it was all a sick game.

I shook my head, forcing the bad memories back into their box.

"Hey, Adrian. How are you?" I kept my voice totally flat.

I hadn't gone back to see him. Our only contact was through letters, mostly me giving him basic updates on the baby.

"I'm okay," he said. Then, silence.

God, the tension was thick. I used to tell this guy everything. Now? We were practically strangers.

"You talked to Mom and Dad lately?" I asked, desperate to kill the quiet.

Calling them "Mom and Dad" to him felt bizarre. They raised him, they were his parents—but now he and I were having a kid. Talk about messed up.

"No," he clipped.

"Why not? They're literally begging to hear from you, Adrian. Why are you freezing them out?"

My parents put on a brave face, but they were wrecked. Sure, they were furious about his betrayal, but they still loved him. You don't just switch off twenty-plus years of parental love.

He sighed. "You know why, Lyra."

"Yeah, and your excuse is total bullshit!" I snapped, his stubbornness pushing my buttons. "You're breaking their hearts. Haven't you done enough damage? Do you even care about them? Why do this?"

Silence on his end. I kept pushing.

"Just call them. Please. I've never begged you for a single favor, but I'm begging now. I hate watching them fake a smile while telling me you rejected them again."

Another heavy sigh. "You know I can't say no to you, Lyra." A pause. "Fine. I'll call them."

"Thanks," I said, keeping my voice cool even though I was beaming. Maybe they could actually fix this.

"That's not why I called, though."

"Okay..." I dragged the word out.

"I heard about what went down. Just wanted to make sure you and the baby are okay." His voice went soft, dragging up all those old feelings I wanted buried.
