Chapter 109

"What's up, Lyra?" Kaelen’s voice crackles through the speaker.

We don't do phone calls. Usually, it's just a dry text exchange. He pings me when a package ships; I text back a quick "thanks." That's the whole relationship.

But right now? I'm desperate. Calling him is a massive gamble, but my back is against the wall. That second threat note? Yeah, it absolutely paralyzed me.

"I need a favor, Kaelen," I say, cutting straight to the chase. No "how's the weather." He hates small talk anyway, and honestly, so do I.

It took me an hour of staring at my phone to actually hit dial. I kept weighing my options: the cops or Kaelen. In the end, cold logic won. The police choked the last time I was in deep shit. Kaelen, though? He always had his eyes on the board.

If anyone can hunt down this psycho, it’s him.

"Alright, shoot. What's going on?" He sounds intrigued. Makes sense—I've never asked him for a damn thing before.

"Someone's stalking me."

"Oh. Is this about those creepy letters you're getting?"

My jaw drops. But at the same time, I'm not even surprised. See? This is exactly why I called him. The guy is always three moves ahead.

"Yeah," I breathe. "How the hell do you know about that? Who is it?"

I squeeze my eyes shut and cross my fingers. God, please let him have this handled already. He did promise to keep me safe, right?

"I've got eyes everywhere," Kaelen says. "Don't know the name yet. But my guy says the person dropping them off wears a mask and a long black coat. He's pretty sure it's a chick."

A woman. I rack my brain, trying to match a face to the description. Nothing. I've pissed off plenty of people lately—Aurora being the latest disaster—but who hates me enough to want me dead? It's a long list, and they all have motives.

"Why didn't your guy grab her?" I snap.

I mean, seriously. You see a freak in a mask leaving death threats, you tackle them.

"He didn't think it was a big deal until the second note," Kaelen explains. "That's when he flagged it."

Fair enough. I didn't panic until the second one either.

"What do they actually say?" he asks.

"Hang on. Sending a photo."

I line up the two notes on the table, snap a quick pic, and hit send.

Silence stretches over the line while he checks them out. My stomach does a nervous flip. If the cops find out I'm talking to him, I'm screwed.

Kaelen is a wanted man. The feds are actively hunting him. I'm literally harboring a fugitive here. If a jury gets wind of this, "we barely text" isn't going to keep me out of an orange jumpsuit.

"Yeah, this person definitely wants you dead," Kaelen finally grunts.

"No shit, Sherlock," I roll my eyes. "The whole 'say goodbye to your family' part kind of gave it away."

I sound bitchy, but my nerves are shot. Knowing someone is plotting my murder is terrifying. Knowing I'm pregnant makes it a million times worse. If I go down, the baby goes down with me. No way in hell am I letting that happen.

"That's all I've got for now," Kaelen sighs. "Who's got a beef with you? Who wants payback? Give me some names."

"Honestly? I don't even know. You're the one who watches my life like a hawk, so you know my enemy list is a mile long."

"Fine. Write down everyone you've ticked off and send it over. I'll start digging, okay?"

I nod, then realize he can't see me. "Yeah. Okay."

"Hang tight, Lyra. We'll nail this asshole. I won't let them touch—"

The call ends.

I slump back onto the sofa. The house is a mess, but I don't have the energy to lift a finger. My brain is spinning too fast to focus on chores anyway.

I lose track of time until keys rattle in the lock. The front door swings open. Heavy footsteps echo in the hallway.

My parents walk in, holding hands.

I can't help but smile. Those two are so disgustingly in love, they're practically glued together. It's sweet, honestly.

"Hey guys," I say, pushing myself up.

Mom slides onto the cushion next to me. Dad takes the armchair opposite us.

"Hey, sweetie," Dad beams.

"Hi, my darling," Mom says.

I'll never get tired of how they look at me. Pure, unconditional love. No mind games, no guessing. It's right there on their faces.

"So, what brings you two over?" I tease. "Not that I'm kicking you out."

They trade a quick, loaded look. Mom turns to face me, taking my hand.

"We actually have a couple of things to talk about," she says. I nod, waiting.

"First... I just wanted to say thank you, baby." Her eyes well up with tears. "Adrian called us yesterday. I know he did some horrible things to you, but hearing his voice... you have no idea what that meant to me. He told us you're the one who convinced him to reach out. He realized he needs his family, and we are his family."

I force a smile. Adrian and I mostly write letters. He barely ever calls. I haven't visited him since that first prison visit, but I know I'll have to once the baby is born. The kid deserves to know their dad, even if he's locked up.

"It's fine, Mom. Don't worry about it."

"No, it's not just 'fine,'" she insists, wiping a tear. "Thank you. I really missed my son."

A shudder runs down my spine. Hearing her call him her "son" is so incredibly messed up, considering I used to sleep with him. That taboo is never going to feel normal.

I lean in, giving her a quick hug and a peck on the cheek. "I'd do anything for you guys."

We hold each other for a second. Dad waits patiently until Mom dabs her eyes dry.

"Alright, what's the second thing?" I ask.

Dad clears his throat, sitting up straighter. "Right. We want to officially introduce you to society. As our daughter and sole heir."

I knew this was coming, but my jaw still hits the floor.

"Are... are you guys sure?" I stammer.

Honestly, I'm the one who's unsure. I've been dragging my feet on this for months. It's not that I'm embarrassed of them—I just dread the media circus and the gossip that comes with their last name.

"Absolutely," Mom says, her face lighting up. "It's time. I want everyone to know you're ours. Finding you was a miracle, and we want to celebrate that."

I chew my lip, thinking it over. What's the point of hiding it anyway? The rumors are already flying since Asher and I are always seen out with them.

"Okay," I finally say. "Let's do it."

Mom squeals, throwing her arms around my neck. "Oh my god, thank you! I'm starting the planning today. Maybe we go old-school—a massive debutante ball in your honor!"

I just smile and nod. For the next hour, she rambles on about themes and venues while Dad and I just listen.

I want to feel the excitement. I really do. But with a faceless killer stalking me from the shadows, I'm just numb.
