Chapter 11

I wedged the phone between my ear and shoulder, scrubbing the counter with my good arm. Thank God my shoulder wasn't screaming today. "Hey, sweetie, how's it going?" I asked Noal.

"Oh my god, Mom, it's so awesome!" His voice blasted through the speaker, nearly blowing my eardrum. "We just stuffed our faces with ice cream, and now we're heading to the giant slides! They literally drop you straight into the ocean!"

Hearing him bubble over like that instantly cured my bad mood. If he was happy and safe, I was golden.

"See? Told you you'd have a blast, baby."

I tossed the sponge into the sink and slumped onto the couch. Cleaning could wait; my kid deserved my full attention.

"What about you, Mommy? Having a fun weekend?"

Yeah, right. My eight-year-old was living the dream while I was rotting at home. No plans. No friends. Total loser status.

My coworkers used to throw invites my way, but I blew them off so many times they just stopped asking. Honestly, those invites were pity offers anyway. Just polite background noise because I happened to be standing in the breakroom.

"Uh, you know. Just the usual. Doing some deep cleaning," I mumbled.

A heavy sigh came through the line. "Mom, seriously? You need to get out. Go do something fun. Just because I'm gone doesn't mean you have to sit home alone."

Ouch. Since when did my kid become my life coach?

"I will, I promise. Just gotta finish these chores first," I lied.

My actual post-cleaning itinerary? Binge-watching trash TV, face-planting into a bag of chips, or sleeping for twelve hours. Honestly, all of them sounded like heaven.

"Okay, gotta run, Mom! Pa's yelling for me."

"Love you, baby. Call me tonight, okay?"

"Bye! Oh, and Grandma says hi!"

Just to keep things straight: Asher calls my mom 'Grandma' and my dad 'Grandpa.' But Declan's parents? They're 'Pa' and 'Ma.'

"Got it. Watch your step on those slides," I said, completely blowing past the mention of my mother.

He hung up before he could call me out on the snub. Thank god for the slides. Usually, Asher has this freaky, FBI-level radar for tension—totally inherited that from his dad—but today he was too hyped to notice.

I tossed the phone onto the cushion with a smile. I knocked out the rest of the chores in record time, but instead of queuing up Netflix, I grabbed a stack of biology essays.

Man, my students were crushing it. Not to toot my own horn, but I'm a kickass teacher. My class consistently pulls the highest test scores in the entire school district.

I was grading the last paper when my screen lit up. My heart did a stupid little flip. *Adrian.*

No way. Why was he calling me?

"Hey," I answered, keeping my voice steady. Half of me expected him to say, *Oops, butt dial.*

"Hey, Lyra. You busy right now?"

Okay, definitely not a butt dial.

"Not really. What's up?"

"Well, I heard you weren't down at the precinct's shooting range with everyone else. Thought maybe I could swing by and we could drive over together."

I blinked. "Wait, what? Why on earth would I be at a shooting range?"

"Because your whole family is there. The chief recommended they all get some target practice and grab their firearm licenses. You know, just in case things get ugly."

Huh. News to me. But yeah, makes sense. Packing heat is probably a smart move when you're dealing with a psycho gang.

A flash of anger hit me—nobody bothered to send me the memo—but I shut it down. Whatever. They could all go jump off a cliff.

"So, can I come grab you?" Adrian's voice cut through my mental rant.

I mean, my schedule was wide open. Plus, shooting things sounded therapeutic. And hey, more face time with Adrian.

"Sure. Why not?"

"Awesome. See you in ten."

The line went dead. I bolted to the bedroom, tearing through my closet for something decent. Since we were shooting, I threw on some flat boots. Right on cue, Adrian pulled up, and we hit the road.

"So," I said, turning in my seat to look at him. The vibe in the car was incredibly chill. I hadn't felt this relaxed around another adult in forever. "What made you want to wear the badge?"

He shrugged, keeping his eyes on the road. "A cop killed my dad."

My jaw dropped a little. "Uh. Usually, that makes people hate cops."

"Yeah, well, my dad was a piece of work. Terrible guy, even worse father. Honestly, when the police gunned him down for trafficking illegal weapons, it felt like a massive weight lifted off my shoulders. Watching those guys take out trash who thought he was untouchable? It made me want to do the same. Clean up the streets."

He trailed off, but the cold edge in his voice spoke volumes. His dad wasn't just bad; he was a monster.

I've seen kids like that in my classroom—bruised, neglected, terrified. I always try to step in. No kid deserves that kind of hell, whether the scars are physical or mental.

"What about you?" he asked. "Why teaching?"

Normally, I keep my guard up, but with him, the words just spilled out.

"My parents were pretty much nonexistent. Total neglect. But when I was nine, I got placed in this amazing teacher's class. Grace. She was the mother I never had. She actually cared. She praised me, hugged me when I was down, backed me up. She was just... good. To everyone. I never forgot her. I wanted to be that safe haven for some other kid. Plus, the job just clicked."

Grace had been my lifeline. Her classroom was my only sanctuary. Even now, years later, I still get choked up thinking about how she saved a lonely, miserable nine-year-old girl.

"Wow," Adrian murmured, his voice soft with genuine respect.

The silence stretched between us, comfortable but loaded. Then he dropped a bomb.

"So, I know you've got a son... but are you seeing anyone?"

I blinked, wondering if he was just making polite small talk. "Divorced."

A slow smirk spread across his face. "Perfect. Guess that means I can shoot my shot."

I let out a laugh, assuming he was just teasing. There was no way a guy like him was actually interested. And even if he was, that would evaporate the second he laid eyes on Aurora.

If my family was at the range, history was bound to repeat itself. Just like every guy in high school, he’d take one look at my sister and forget I even existed.

We pulled up to the range, hopped out, and walked inside. Adrian nodded to a few colleagues, introducing me as we made our way to the firing line.

And there they were. The whole happy family. Declan, Aurora, Corbin, and Orion.

Corbin and Orion were busy blasting targets. Aurora was lounging on a bench, while Declan stood in front of her. The second we walked in, Declan turned and scowled.

I rolled my eyes. Here we go.

"Who invited you?" Declan demanded, marching right up to Adrian and me.

"Not you, clearly," I shot back.

"I tried calling you, Lyra," he snapped, his jaw tight. "You didn't answer."

"Then you didn't try hard enough."

I hadn't even been in the room for two minutes and my blood pressure was already spiking. Coming here was a massive mistake. I should've just asked Adrian to hang out another time.

Before Declan could bark out another word, Adrian grabbed my arm and gently pulled me away. "Come on. Let's go get your gear."

I glanced back over my shoulder. Aurora was staring at me, her eyes shooting literal daggers. If looks could kill, I'd be six feet under.

I let out a heavy sigh. God, what a headache. I seriously should have stayed in bed.
