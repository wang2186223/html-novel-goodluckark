Chapter 104

My brain was absolute mush.

I’d been staring at the wall for a solid hour since Silas walked out. He’d agreed to let Asher crash at his place tonight, thank God.

My head was still spinning from the massive truth bombs dropped today. It was way too much, way too fast. I had zero clue how to process any of it.

Then my phone started buzzing. I almost ignored it, but, ugh, what if it was an emergency?

I swiped the screen without even looking. Pressed the cold glass to my ear and kept my mouth shut. My mind was a total blank slate, so I just waited for the other side to break the silence.

"Lyra?" a voice gasped. "Oh, thank God. You okay? Corbin just filled me in on the whole shitshow."

Piper. I knew that voice instantly.

"Honestly? I don’t even know," I whispered back.

I still couldn't wrap my head around Aurora's sheer cruelty toward Silas and Jasper. Sure, she’d always wanted Declan’s babies, but dumping her own flesh and blood just because the kid didn't share Declan's DNA? That’s next-level psycho.

"Corbin said you’re the one who blew the whistle and wrecked Aurora's little fairy tale," Piper said.

I knew exactly what it felt like to be unwanted by a parent, so Jasper's agony hit me right in the chest. His pain became my pain because, god, he was basically a mirror of my younger self.

He didn't ask for a monster like Aurora as a mother, yet she discarded him like trash. It made me want to scream and cry all at once.

"Yeah. Right place, right time, I guess," I muttered.

"But how? How did you actually connect the dots?" Her voice sounded completely blown away.

I couldn't tell if she was reeling from the fact that Aurora had a secret kid, or if she just couldn't believe I was the one who cracked the case.

"It was his smile," I breathed. "I’d seen him smile a dozen times, but today it just clicked. A memory from when she was young flashed in my head. His smile was a carbon copy of hers."

"And Cal never dropped a hint?"

"Never. But the second I threw out Aurora’s name, the guy froze solid. Dead giveaway. Plus, he didn't even try to deny it."

The whole thing still felt like a fever dream. I couldn't picture my life without Asher, so how the hell did Aurora just go on living like her own son didn't exist?

"Give me the dirty details. All of them," Piper demanded. So I spilled. I dragged her through the whole messy timeline, from the very first clue to the current disaster.

By the time I finished, the waterworks were back. I tried not to let my own old baggage bleed into it, but it was a losing battle.

"I didn't even realize where I was driving until I pulled up to Eleanor's place," I admitted. I’d stopped calling her 'Mom' months ago. "I was just so damn angry for him—and for myself. I wanted to do something. Something nobody ever bothered to do for me when I was a kid getting treated like garbage. I wanted to stand up, look Aurora in the eye, and call her out."

That was the exact miracle I’d prayed for growing up. Other adults saw how Eleanor and Frederick treated me, but they just looked the other way. Hell, even Declan’s parents kept their mouths shut and went along with the abuse. Nobody had my back. Nobody stood up for what was right. So, yeah, I did it for Jasper.

"I hear you, babe," Piper said softly. "No kid should ever go through the hell Eleanor and Frederick put you through, or what Aurora did to Jasper. You did the right thing by exposing that bitch."

A wave of relief washed over me. Part of me had been second-guessing the whole thing, thinking maybe I should’ve kept my mouth shut or handled it better. But I’d been so blinded by rage I couldn't think straight. All I could think about while driving was: *like mother, like daughter.*

Just as I was about to speak, the doorbell chimed.

"Someone’s at the door, Piper. Gotta run."

I felt completely wiped out. Physically, emotionally, just totally empty.

"Go. We’ll talk tomorrow. You’ve had a hell of a day."

We traded quick goodnights and hung up. I seriously considered ignoring the knock. Like I said, I was running on empty. I didn't want to deal with anyone.

But I dragged myself up anyway and yanked the door open.

"Declan? What the hell are you doing here?" I blurted.

Honestly, my jaw nearly hit the floor. I figured he’d be glued to Aurora’s side, playing the comforting hero. Seeing him on my porch was a total shock.

"Can I come in?" he asked, dodging the question.

I must have lost my mind, because I stepped back and let him pass. He flashed me a tight, tired smile as he crossed the threshold.

"Is Asher out cold?" he asked, slipping off his jacket.

"Probably. But he’s not here. He’s crashing at Silas’s tonight."

A flash of pure fury crossed his face at the mention of Silas. For a second, I braced myself for a blowup over Cal, but he literally forced his shoulders to drop and swallowed his anger. I almost clapped at the sudden display of self-control.

"Jesus. Today was a total trainwreck," he muttered, pausing. "How are you holding up?"

Look, I’d noticed some major shifts in him lately, but right now, it was hitting me like a freight train. The old Declan wouldn't have given a shit. He would’ve been screaming in my face for hurting his precious Aurora. Was it actually possible the guy had changed?

I shook my head to clear the cobwebs. No. Don't be stupid. He had nine whole years to change and didn't. People don't just magically grow a conscience overnight.

"I'm fine," I said. Then I shook my head. "Actually, scratch that. I'm a total wreck. I feel like I’ve been dragged through gravel."

He closed the distance between us and pulled me against him. His body heat hit me instantly. I felt every single inch of him—all hard muscle pressing against my soft edges.

I should’ve shoved him away. I knew that. But my brain was completely fried from exhaustion. If I had even an ounce of sanity left, I would’ve kicked him out.

"What can I do to make it better?" he murmured. "I know this is tearing you apart, and it’s not just about Jasper."

My heart did a little flip. I was stunned that he actually got it—that he realized this wasn't just about Jasper, but about my own buried scars.

I stared down at his shoes, but he gently cupped my jaw, forcing me to look up at him.

"I don't know, Declan," I whispered.

His thumb traced my cheekbone, slow and soft, before sliding down my neck. He tucked a stray piece of hair behind my ear.

Those grey eyes locked onto mine. He was looking at me the exact way I used to dream about. Like he actually wanted me. Like I was his entire world. Which was total fantasy, right?

"I know one way to take your mind off things," he murmured, his voice dropping an octave. "If I lean down and kiss those pretty lips, are you going to stop me?"

My heart started hammering against my ribs. I just stared at him.

*Tell him no,* my brain screamed. *Say no, Lyra. Push him back.*

I knew I had to, but my body wasn't listening. My mouth wouldn't form the words.

His face drifted down in slow motion. Then, his lips—firm but surprisingly soft—pressed against mine.

I must have completely lost my mind, because my lips parted, letting his tongue slide past. I closed my eyes as a wave of pure heat rushed through me.

I felt him in every single nerve ending as our mouths locked and our tongues tangled. My knees turned to jelly. I would’ve hit the floor if his arm hadn't wrapped around my waist, holding me tight.

I used to wonder what romance novels meant by an "earth-shattering kiss." This was it.

My common sense had officially left the building. I wrapped my hands around the back of his neck, pulling him closer, deepening the kiss. I couldn't get enough. I wanted more. Even Adrian’s kisses had never felt like this.

I completely lost myself as he devoured my mouth. This was the dream. I’d spent years wanting Declan to kiss me like he actually craved me. I’d wanted this every time he came home from work, every morning before he left, every single time we slept together. But we never had this. Not because I didn't try, but because he never wanted me.

*"You were just a pathetic lay, and honestly, you weren't even good at it. Every time I touched you, I wished you were Aurora. I closed my eyes and pictured her. You were nothing special, just an easy, cheap substitute. I used you like the trash you are."*

The brutal words he’d spat at me months ago slammed into my brain like a wrecking ball. I ripped myself out of his grip, the memory looping in my head, torturing me all over again.

I stood there, chest heaving, staring at him.
