Chapter 70

The house was dead quiet. Asher was off at school, leaving me with zero plans and a whole lot of couch time. Honestly, after my brain basically short-circuited last week, I had to call a timeout on teaching. The kids at school threw a fit, sure, but they knew I’d been a total zombie lately. I’ll go back after the baby pops. Right now, it’s just about my boys and the Hope Foundation. I’m still spinning from the recent drama. Everyone’s acting so weirdly nice now. Well, except Aurora. She’s still a grade-A bitch, totally consistent. The rest of them? Overnight personality transplants.

Ugh, whatever. I shake off the bad vibes, grab my phone, and buzz my mom. Ring one, and boom, she answers.

"Hey, Mom," I say. Still feels a little weird rolling off my tongue, but I’m working on it.

"Lyra!" she shrieks. Classic Mom, always hyped to hear from me. "Edmund, babe! Our girl is on the line!"

Some rustling noises follow, then that hollow, echoey sound. Yep, speakerphone.

"Hey, Dad," I call out.

Seriously, those two blow my mind. High school sweethearts since age thirteen, and still crazy about each other thirty-five years later. If I actually gave a crap about romance anymore, that’s the dream. But nope. I’m done. I’ve fully embraced the solo-for-life plan.

"Hey, sweetie, how’s life?" Dad asks.

"Good, you know. Just chilling. Wanted to see how you guys were doing."

I still haven't dropped the pregnancy bomb on them. My belly is definitely popping, but giant tees do a solid job hiding it. I know my keeping secrets hurts them, even if they pretend it doesn't.

Then there’s Adrian. He’s totally freezing them out, which is messed up, but I get his head space. The guy is drowning in guilt. He thinks he’s too dirty for their affection after his screw-ups, so he’s just ghosting them. I actually texted him my latest ultrasound pic. He sent back a super sweet thank-you, raving about how he can't wait to hold the kid. He's totally cool with keeping the gender a surprise until delivery day, too.

"Lyra? You there?" Mom’s voice snaps me out of my head.

"Oh, sorry. Brain fart," I mumble, feeling stupid.

"No worries, hon. Just checking if you're still coming over this weekend?"

"Yeah, definitely. But Asher’s staying behind. It's his dad's weekend, so just me."

Back in the day, the Woods and the Sharps threw these massive monthly block parties. Half the time, my invite got 'lost.' When I did show up, everyone treated me like a ghost. A total outcast. Asher was literally the only person who’d even look at me. I usually ended up locking myself in the bathroom crying or just slipping out early. Those parties stopped months ago, but now that the parents are back in town, I guess the tradition is back on.

"Sucks that the kiddo can't make it, but we're thrilled to see you," Dad says.

I start to reply, but a noise cuts me off. A sharp yip, then a scratching sound. Weird. There are zero dogs around here. I figure I'm hearing things, but then—there it is again.

"Hey, Dad, let me buzz you back. I gotta go check something," I say, already distracted.

"Sure thing, sweetie."

I click end-call and follow the noise. I head out to the backyard, and—holy crap. It’s a total disaster zone. My jaw literally hits the floor.

"Are you freaking kidding me? Do you have any idea how long those took to grow? You shredded everything!" I yell.

The culprit is flopped on the grass, giving me the ultimate guilty puppy-dog eyes. Nice try, buddy.

"I wanted fresh, organic veggies, which is why I busted my ass on this stupid garden! It took forever!"

The dog lets out a pathetic whine, but I’m too pissed to care.

"Oh, hell no. Don't you dare give me those cute eyes. It won't work." I shake my head, staring at him—pretty sure it’s a boy. "How are you gonna pay me back for this, huh?"

But damn it, the longer I look, the faster my anger evaporates. He’s this massive, fluffy, gorgeous beast. Thick black-and-white fur, and these striking grey eyes. For some crazy reason, those eyes instantly make me think of Declan and Asher.

"Wow. Never seen anyone go from furious to soft that fast. Usually, people just want to kick his butt."

The deep, smooth voice startles me. I whip around so fast I nearly give myself whiplash.

Holy hell. Up close, this guy is a walking thirst trap. Jet-black hair, piercing green eyes, a jawline sharp enough to cut glass, and a mouth that practically screams bad decisions. His body is built for sin, and the worst part? He definitely knows it.

I know, I know. *Easy, Lyra.* You already fell for a pretty face once and paid the price. Don't touch.

And I won't. I'm completely done with guys and relationships. But hey, I'm not dead. I can still appreciate a masterpiece when it's standing right in front of me.

"Wait, do I know you?" The question slips out before my brain can stop it. "You look super familiar."

He studies me for a beat. "Yeah. Same high school. You were two grades below me."

I rack my brain, but nothing clicks. Honestly, back then, my Declan-obsession was so bad I had major tunnel vision.

"Silas Garcia," he says, seeing my blank stare.

The second the name leaves his lips, the puzzle pieces slam together.

"Oh my god! You're Nerdy Cal!" I literally screech.

He winces, hating the old nickname. He shoots me a glare, but I just grin.

"Holy shit, talk about a major glow-up," I gawk, totally shameless.

The Cal in my memory was a scrawny kid with oversized glasses sliding down his nose, metal braces, and a face full of acne. A total library geek who hit the books while everyone else was hooking up in the janitor's closet. Back then, he was basically invisible.

Now? He is absolute eye candy. Good for him, seriously. He transformed into a total ten—right up there with Declan.

"Yeah, uh, thanks," he mumbles, rubbing the back of his neck.

"Hey, didn't you have a massive thing for Aurora back then? How'd that play out?" I tease. I'm not trying to be a bitch, just playful.

When he wasn't studying, he used to follow her around like a sad puppy, just like every other guy in school. But Aurora only wanted Declan. Once they hooked up, they were the golden couple. Every guy—minus Orion and Corbin—wanted to be him, and every girl wanted to be her.

"And you were obsessed with Declan. How'd that work out for ya?" he snaps back, clearly annoyed.

"Touché," I mutter.
