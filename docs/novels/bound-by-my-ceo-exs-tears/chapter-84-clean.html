Chapter 85

"Wait, what?" I choke out. My jaw hits the floor. I stare at Declan. No way I heard that right. The Declan I knew would've thrown me to the wolves for Aurora. My chest thumps like crazy. He just stands there, totally expressionless.

"You heard me, Lyra," he says. His voice is dead serious. "If it came down to a choice, I'd let her die. In a heartbeat. Just to keep you breathing."

My first thought? Bullshit. He's just trying to make me feel better. Who wants to hear that their partner of ten years would ditch them for another woman? But I look at his eyes. Damn. He's not bluffing. It's written all over his face. Besides, Declan doesn't do sugarcoating. He's always been a brutal truth-teller. Why start lying now?

I exhale hard and yank my hands back. Too close. Way too close. My brain is already a chaotic mess, and his warm touch is only scrambling my signals.

"Cut the crap," I mutter after a beat. "You've lusted after Aurora since you learned what a crush was. Years, Declan. You've loved her forever. How do you just casually say you'd let her die for me?"

His forehead creases. He opens his mouth, but I shut him down.

"I'm the nobody here. Remember? You hated my guts, Declan. For years. So what's the play? How do I suddenly rank higher than your precious Aurora? It doesn't add up. You don't just flip a switch and stop hating someone."

I flop back onto the pillow, totally drained. It's the truth, and we both know it. Nine years of pure resentment, bitterness, and cold shoulders don't just vanish overnight.

People don't just wake up cured. Heavy feelings don't dissolve. You can't bury them that easily. Trust me, I know. I spent ages trying to kill my feelings for Declan, trying to scrape them out of my chest.

He starts to speak again, but I raise a hand. Done. I'm so done. No pretty lies, please. No sudden personality makeovers. Honestly? I just want the old status quo back. Back when they all openly despised me.

Sounds crazy, right? Who begs for the hate? Isn't this the dream—having them crawl back, begging for a do-over? But here's the kicker: I'm used to their cruelty. It's my comfort zone. This new, nice version of them? It freaks me out. Part of me wants to let go, sure. But the other part? It smells a rat.

Call it trauma from a lifetime of rejection, but I can't buy this sudden 180. It feels like a trap. Like I'm just waiting for the punchline.

"No, Declan," I snap, cutting him off. "Thanks for saving my life, seriously. But get out. Go to Aurora. That's your spot. You've waited a decade for her, so don't screw it up now. What we had? Nothing. A massive mistake. We spent nine years miserable. Let's just find our own happy endings. Yours is with her. Mine... mine is with my kids."

He wants to fight me on this. His grey eyes go dark, like a storm rolling in. I brace myself, waiting for the explosion. But nothing happens.

Instead, he slumps back in his chair and lets out a heavy, defeated sigh. I blink, shocked.

"Fine. I'll back off... for now," he grunts, pushing himself up.

I expect him to bolt, but he doesn't. He leans down, presses a soft, lingering kiss to my forehead, and slips out before I can even process it. The door clicks shut behind him.

I stare at the empty doorway, brain short-circuiting. What the hell was that? I'm glad he's gone, obviously. But Declan usually does whatever the hell he wants. Seeing him actually listen and walk away—even when he clearly wanted to stay—is bizarre.

As my strength trickles back, his words loop in my head. It's impossible to believe. This is Declan. The guy who literally told me I'd never hold a candle to Aurora.

What is his deal? I ask myself, but get zero answers.

If any other guy said he'd dump his dream girl for me, I'd buy it. But not him. Never Declan.

How does he expect me to just swallow this? For nearly a decade, he and everyone else treated me like trash. I was nothing. Now he wants to flip the script and expects me to just nod and smile?

My head is spinning with questions, but there are no answers. It's giving me a headache, so I shut it down. Whatever Declan is going through, it's not my circus, not my monkeys. We're done. I'm not wasting energy figuring him out. My eyelids grow heavy, and I drift off.

I wake up to see my parents sitting by the bed. They look completely wiped out. Each of them is holding one of my hands. Right then, a wave of pure warmth hits me.

This exact feeling—this unconditional love—is what I'd starved for with Declan and the Sharps. Having it now makes my throat tight.

I make a small noise, and their heads snap up.

"Lyra," Mom chokes out, her voice cracking. "How do you feel, sweetie?"

Tears flood my eyes. I blink fast, but they spill over anyway.

"I love you guys so much. Thanks for... just being here. For being real parents." The words barely squeeze past the lump in my throat.

"Oh, sweetheart. You have no idea how long I've waited to hear that," Mom says, tears glistening on her cheeks.

"We love you, kiddo. So damn much," Dad says, squeezing my hand.

I just let the tears flow, sinking into their hugs. It feels safe.

The door clicks open, and we pull apart as a nurse steps in.

"Ready to get out of here, Lyra?" she asks with a warm smile.

Relief washes over me again. The doctors said the baby is totally fine—the smoke or whatever drugs I breathed in didn't do any damage. Thank god.

"Hell yes. I need to hold my boy," I say, beaming back at her.

Declan said the baby was with his folks. I'm grabbing him the second I leave. I'm not letting him out of my sight. Today could've ended so differently. I almost didn't make it back to him.

"Great. Swing by the front desk when you're changed," she says, heading out.

Mom brought me some fresh clothes. She helps me get changed in the tiny bathroom while Dad waits outside. Once I'm decent, we head out.

My parents refuse to let me pay. They handle the discharge paperwork while telling me to just sit tight.

While waiting, I spot them down the hall—the Sharps, Orion, and Declan. I take a deep breath, pumping myself up, and walk over. I don't know why I'm doing it, but I need to know if Aurora made it.

Their heads snap toward me as I approach. Great. It feels exactly like high school—the awkward loser walking up to the cool kids' table.

"Hey. Just wanted to check on Aurora," I say, shifting my weight nervously.

Silence. Then, out of nowhere, Eleanor stands up and throws her arms around me. I go stiff as a board. She's never touched me like this. It feels completely wrong.

My arms hang dead at my sides. No way I'm hugging back. I don't want to give her any false hope.

"She's fine. Just got out of the OR," Corbin says, breaking the tension.

I just nod. Not like there's anything else to say.

Eleanor pulls back when she realizes I'm acting like a mannequin. She stares at me, eyes welling up.

She reaches out to touch my face, but I take a quick step back. Nope. Don't touch me.

"I'm just so glad you're okay, Lyra. Thank God both my girls are safe," she says, her voice cracking.

I want to snap at her. Tell her I'm not her daughter, that she lost that title years ago. But I don't get the words out.

"Sweetheart?" Mom's voice calls out from behind me. "Ready to go home, honey?"

Eleanor flinches at the word 'Mom'. I don't give a shit.

I look at the group one last time, my eyes stopping on Declan. I quickly look away.

"Good to hear she's okay. Tell her thanks for... everything. For what she did." That's all I've got. I turn on my heel and walk back to my parents.

Dad wraps an arm around my shoulder, and Mom grabs my hand. I can feel eyes burning into my back. I don't need to turn around to know who's watching.

My brain is a total mess, but I keep my eyes glued forward. I refuse to look back at Declan. I don't want to see whatever intense emotion is burning in his eyes right now. I'm probably imagining it anyway. There's no way he looks at me with anything resembling love. Declan only loves one girl. And her name is Aurora.
