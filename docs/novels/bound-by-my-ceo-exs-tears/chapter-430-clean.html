NovelVibe

Chapter 439

Two days since Asher and I blew up. Am I good? Hell no. I'm still spitting mad, and honestly? It hurts like a bitch.

Look, I didn't expect him to throw a party over this positive test. But telling me to flush it? To just get an abortion? Yeah, that killed whatever was left of us. It smashed my little fantasy to pieces.

I used to put Asher on this massive pedestal. Thought he was the good guy. Even when he treated me like garbage, I made excuses. *Oh, he just doesn't like me, that doesn't make him a monster.*

But ordering me to erase our baby? Zero hesitation, just "get rid of it"? That's not a cold guy. That's a straight-up villain.

I shouldn't compare. I know he despises me for even thinking it. But what if it was Seraphina? What if she was still breathing, carrying kid number three?

Yeah, she's dead. I know that. But this nasty, toxic green monster still claws at my ribs. It suffocates me. I feel like a total piece of trash for being jealous of a ghost.

*Buzz.* My phone lights up. Uber's outside.

I sigh, give Shadow a quick scratch behind the ears, grab my purse, and bolt.

"Hey," I mumble, sliding into the backseat.

"Morning, ma'am. How's it going?" the driver asks.

I can't just ignore him. Some people do, but it feels too rude. Even when I'm dying inside, I talk.

"Honestly? Pretty crappy," I say, clicking my seatbelt into place.

I actually dragged myself to work the day after the bomb dropped. Asked for some PTO, grabbed my car, and parked it in the garage. It's sitting there right now. I just couldn't face the road today.

It's one of those days where you just want to curl up, have your mom hug you, and sip her special tea.

"Hang in there. It always works out," the driver says, pulling into traffic.

Nice guy. I want to believe him, I really do. But I can't.

Sure, I'm keeping this baby. No question. But right now, I'm stuck in a dark hole, and there's zero light at the end of the tunnel.

"Thanks," I mutter, staring out the glass.

The city is just a blur of gray. My brain is too fried to care.

I keep seeing Asher's face. That sneer. The pure venom dripping from his mouth. The way he talked about my baby—like it was some disgusting, filthy mistake. Like its heartbeat was a personal insult to him.

It's all I've chewed on for forty-eight hours. That fight. His cruel, bitter voice on loop.

I never got how a parent could hate their own flesh and blood. Maybe because my family actually loved me, so the concept seemed totally alien.

Seeing Asher? I still don't get it. I didn't expect him to be thrilled, but to actively loathe a tiny, unborn life?

My hand goes to my chest. I rub the ache right over my heart, trying to stop the bleeding.

I knew he hated me. But hearing him spit those words? It wrecked me.

Why? What did I ever do to him? I never hurt the guy. Why the hell does he hate me so much?

I let out a dry, pathetic laugh. Well, he sure shattered my illusions. He fell hard off that pedestal. Now I see him clearly. He's just a bitter, hateful prick.

"We're here, miss," the driver's voice cuts through the fog.

I blink. Right. Mom's house.

I scramble through my bag, yank out a wad of cash, and shove it at him. "Keep it. Thanks."

I climb out, totally out of it. I probably just paid him triple the fare, but whatever. I just need my mom.

"Have a good one!" he calls out. "Hope things look up!"

I throw a weak smile over my shoulder and head up the walkway. I fumble with my keys, turn the lock, and push the door open.

"Mom?" I call out, kicking off my flats and shutting the door.

The smell of warm vanilla and baking hits me. Instant safety net. This place always catches me when I'm free-falling.

"Si?" her voice drifts from the kitchen.

I don't even think. I sprint down the hall and crash straight into her. She catches me, and I just bury myself in her.

"Mommy."

The second she holds me, the dam breaks. I lose it. Tears just flood out. I can't fight the pain anymore. I can't hold it in.

A sob rips right out of my throat, and I hold onto her like she's the only thing keeping me from drowning.
