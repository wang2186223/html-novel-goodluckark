Chapter 120

Some guy inside dropped the bomb on me: Lyra got shot. Man, it felt like a sledgehammer slammed straight through my ribs. The guy had zero details, but the yard talk was already calling her dead. Nobody walks away from a hit like that, right? Plus, her folks went totally dark. No press releases, no nothing.

I love that girl. And our kid? Jesus. Thinking they both got wiped out nearly snapped my brain.

So I sat there, sweating bullets. Just waiting for my folks to call and drop the hammer. But night fell, and my phone stayed dead. That was it. The rumors had to be real. Why else would they freeze me out?

I didn't sleep a wink. Just tossed and turned, my mind spinning with the worst fucking thoughts imaginable.

At some point, I actually started begging God. Me, the guy who doesn't believe in shit. But right then? I'd have worshipped a rock if it told me they were still breathing.

Even the guards and the other cons were giving me these sad, pathetic looks. I looked like absolute hell, and honestly, I felt like I was rotting from the inside out.

By sunrise, I couldn't even choke down my breakfast. All I could see, on loop, was a pair of coffins. One normal-sized. One tiny. It was driving me fucking crazy.

Then my name got called. Warden's office. My heart did a violent flip. I walked in and saw my family's lawyer standing there. I couldn't even speak.

He told me I had a temporary furlough. A few hours of freedom. Suddenly, this tiny spark lit up in my chest. We got in the car, and Arthur—our lawyer—turned to me. "They're alive, Adrian. Both of them. But they're in the ICU." That spark exploded.

I didn't care who was listening; I thanked every god in the universe. They were breathing. That was all that mattered.

"Adrian? Hey. You okay?"

My mom’s voice snapped me back to reality.

I blinked at her. God, how did I get so lucky with her? Any other mother would've kicked my sorry ass to the curb by now. But she didn't. Neither did Dad.

I looked around the waiting room for him. Found him a few feet away, his hand heavy on Declan’s shoulder.

Declan was staring at me like he wanted to rip my throat out. Pure, unadulterated hate. I didn't give a shit. The feeling was totally mutual, especially after how he used to treat Lyra.

He turned on his heel with Asher, and they both trailed after a nurse.

"Just... relieved, Mom," I choked out, my eyes stinging.

I wanted to scream with joy. They weren't gone. There was still a shot.

"I love you, honey," she whispered, tears spilling over.

Man, I hate seeing her cry. It tears me apart. I could see the terror in her eyes—the thought of losing Ava was absolutely destroying her.

Since they'd unhooked my cuffs, I just pulled her in close.

"Love you too, Mom," I mumbled into her hair. "Hey, don't sweat it. Lyra and the baby? They're tough. They're gonna make it. You'll see."

"You have a daughter," Dad's voice broke in.

Mom and I stepped back.

"Wait, what?" I stammered.

They both beamed at me, real smiles. "A baby girl. Her name is Aurora."

And yeah, those tears I was trying to hold back? They just flooded out.

"Who picked the name?" I asked, suddenly worried Lyra would be pissed we named her without her say-so.

"Asher did," Mom said. "Turns out they were tossing names around a while back. They picked Aurora for a girl, and Kaden if it was a boy."

My lungs locked up. My parents saw it instantly.

"What's up? You don't like it?" Dad asked.

"No, no. Aurora is gorgeous," I said, swallowing hard. "It's just... I told her once that I loved the name Kaden."

Mom gave me a soft, teary smile, and Dad squeezed my shoulder.

I remembered that day. We were tangled up in bed, just talking. I'd casually mentioned that if I ever had a son, I'd name him Kaden. The fact that she actually remembered that, that she wanted to use a name I loved... damn. It meant everything to me.

Before we could say anything else, a nurse called my name.

"Go," Mom said, pushing me gently. "We pulled some strings. You're her dad. You need to see her."

I nodded, numb, and followed the nurse. I threw on the hospital scrubs, and she led me straight into the NICU, right up to my little girl.

Seeing her there, so incredibly tiny, hooked up to all those tubes and wires... my knees literally gave out.

"Is she... is she gonna be okay?" I croaked.

I didn't think it was possible to love someone this much. It was terrifying, totally consuming. Right then and there, Aurora became my entire universe. Nobody else would ever come close.

"Yeah," the nurse said with a warm smile. "She's fighting hard. We're really confident she's going to make it."

Look, I know every dad says this, but seriously—she was the most beautiful thing I'd ever seen.

I reached out and brushed her tiny cheek. I couldn't believe Lyra made something so perfect. She had me wrapped around her little finger already.

I whispered a thank you to Lyra. If she hadn't talked me out of my panic when she first told me she was pregnant, I would've missed out on the greatest thing in my life.

Sure, I had a few more years behind bars. But I'd do whatever it took to keep her safe. I'd be a model prisoner, get that early parole, just for her.

I got a few more minutes with her before the nurse, Chloe, told me my time was up.

It fucking killed me to walk away, but I had to. Still, those few minutes? Best of my life, hands down.

Next up was Lyra. God, I love her. Seeing her lying there, pale and broken, cut me deep. I'll always hate myself for screwing things up between us. But maybe everything happens for a reason. Even if it makes zero sense right now.

I was holding her limp hand when Mom tapped my shoulder. Time's up. I let out a heavy sigh and nodded.

"I'll always love you, Lyra. Don't forget that," I whispered to her quiet face.

Leaving that room took every ounce of strength I had, but I kept telling myself: I promised Aurora I'd get out early.

I walked out and hugged my folks. The guards were stepping up with the cuffs when a voice called out from behind me.

"Adrian?"

I turned. It was Piper. We'd gotten close while I was with Lyra—she was basically the sister I never had.

"Look, I'm still mad at you," she said, "but I'm glad you're okay. Sorry I haven't visited."

"Don't worry about it, Piper. I get it."

"Can I get a hug? I missed my friend."

I nodded, and she threw her arms around me. I held her tight, feeling a tiny bit of the weight lift, but not all of it.

"Take care of yourself," I muttered.

"You too, Adrian," she whispered back. "I'll come see you in prison once the dust settles."

"I'd like that."

I let go, only to catch Corbin glaring daggers at me. I glared right back. I hated the guy for how he'd treated Lyra in the past. Honestly, I still didn't get why Piper stayed with him.

I gave my parents one last quick hug, then held out my wrists for the guards to slap the cuffs back on.

As they marched me back to the transport van, I prayed for one more miracle. Just let Lyra wake up, and let Aurora keep fighting.
