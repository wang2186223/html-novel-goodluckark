Chapter 64

I shoved a forkful of cake into my mouth, tucked away in a corner booth. No kid tonight. Asher was over at Declan's, leaving me totally off the clock. Honestly? I felt great. It was weird, but I rolled with it and hit up this diner for some serious comfort food. Now I was face-planting into sugar like I hadn't eaten in a week.

That prison visit? Total shitshow. I went in expecting Adrian to reject the baby outright. Instead, he threw a massive curveball. He dropped the L-word, and it just left me feeling totally numb. Like, dude, read the room. It’s over. There's no universe where we get back together. The guy literally tried to murder me. What kind of doormat would take him back?

Look, I'm not a monster. I won't block his parental rights. But face-to-face visits? Hard pass. I'll have Beatrice handle the baby drop-offs. That's my absolute limit. I'm not doing a single thing more for Adrian. Anything else is just begging for trouble. Actually, scratch that—I'm done with guys altogether. Love is a scam. I chased it twice, and all I got was a wrecked heart and a ton of trauma.

I'm throwing in the towel. Love clearly hates my guts, and honestly, I'm fine with that now. Time to focus on me. I'm going to be the best damn mom and live my best life. I stabbed another chunk of cake, slid it past my lips, and let out a soft groan. God, it was pure heaven. The chocolate practically melted on my tongue.

"Aren't you worried about blowing up? I mean, you're already huge, but this is just sad."

That screechy voice ruined my vibe. I opened my eyes and groaned. Anita stood there, wearing her signature bitch-face. Her work squad hovered behind her, giggling at the jab. Whatever. I didn't give a shit. They were literally background noise to me.

She handled Declan's money, and yeah, she despised me. I never got why she and her little clique hated me so much. Sure, I scored the ring with Declan, the guy they all drooled over. But come on, anyone with half a brain knew Declan couldn't stand me.

"Hey! I'm talking to you, fat bitch!" she barked when I kept ignoring her.

"Keep barking," I said, taking a slow sip of my juice. "Doesn't mean I'm listening to your bullshit."

During my marriage, I would've swallowed the insult. I used to crave Declan's approval so bad that I let his employees treat me like a doormat. I tried telling myself he didn't notice the bullying, but who was I kidding? He definitely knew.

"Oh, so you think you're hot shit now because you got some cash?" she sneered. "Let me guess. You made it on your back."

I actually laughed. Classic. Girls like her are so insecure they assume every successful woman got there by spreading her legs.

Once I caught my breath, I looked her dead in the eye. "I'm not you, Anita."

"You bitch! How dare you!" She lunged forward, but I stood up fast, locking my eyes onto hers. No more playing nice with these losers.

"Take one more step and see what happens," I hissed, narrowing my eyes. "The old Lyra is dead. I'm not taking your shit anymore. You push me, I will shove you back twice as hard. Trust me, you won't like it."

She glanced back at her crew, then glared at me with those green eyes. She’d brought her cheer squad to watch her trash me, but the joke was on her.

"Is that a threat?" She stepped closer, trying to look tough.

I shrugged. "Sure. Take it however you want."

"One call and Declan will ruin your life. We've played this game, Lyra. All I have to do is tell him you were mean to me, and he'll rip you a new one."

In the past, I would've caved. I used to walk on eggshells to avoid Declan's wrath, letting her walk all over me while she smirked, drunk on her petty power.

I flashed her a grin. "Do it. I don't give a shit. Seriously, pull out your phone and call him right now. I dare you."

"You think I'm bluffing?"

"I'm literally begging you to do it," I smirked.

Piece by piece, the confidence Adrian shattered was locking back into place. No man was ever going to drag me down to hell again.

"What's the issue here?" a sharp voice cut in.

I looked up. Audrey was standing there, eyeing us both. We weren't close—just business associates. Her dad's construction company built my Hope Houses. She owned this restaurant outright, though she also held shares in her family's firm.

"This psycho is causing a scene. Kick her out," Anita chimed in instantly. "We were just minding our own business, and she started screaming insults at me."

I crossed my arms and smirked, watching her dig her own grave. Everyone in town knew Audrey, but nobody knew we had a connection. I waited for the fireworks. Unlike Declan, Audrey wasn't an idiot.

"Really?" Audrey's voice went cold.

I'd always kept my distance from her. She'd tried to be friends, but back then, I felt like too much of a trainwreck to deserve friends. So I kept things strictly professional.

Anita nodded eagerly, gesturing to her friends, who all chimed in like trained seals. Total morons.

"Do you think I'm blind? Or that my restaurant doesn't have security cameras?" Audrey snapped. "I watched the whole thing, and you're lying straight to my face."

I watched the exact second Anita's brain short-circuited. She swallowed hard, her face turning pale.

"It's... it's just a misunderstanding," she stammered, desperate.

"Get out."

"I said get the fuck out of my restaurant! I'm not letting some classless bitch trash my friend under my roof." Audrey was practically vibrating with rage.

My brain snagged on the word *friend*, but I shook it off.

"Are you walking out, or do I need security to throw you out?" Audrey demanded.

"Fine. The food here sucks anyway," Anita huffed.

Total lie. This place was a local hotspot, and everyone knew it.

As Anita spun around, I realized the whole dining room was staring. But before she could escape, Audrey called out.

"Hey, Anita?"

"What?" she snapped back.

"You're banned. From all my spots. Your friends too."

I grinned as Anita's jaw hit the floor. She stomped out, her friends trailing behind, furiously whispering curses at her for getting them blacklisted.

"Thanks, Audrey. She was seriously testing my patience," I sighed.

"Don't sweat it. No big deal," Audrey said, offering a tight smile.

I opened my mouth to say something else, but a voice cut through the air.

"Lyra?"

I froze. I knew that voice.

I turned around. Declan's mother was standing there. Right next to her, eyes pooling with tears, was the woman I used to call Mom. The woman who claimed me as her daughter but treated me like absolute garbage.
