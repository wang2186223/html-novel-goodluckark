NovelVibe

Chapter 4

My chest feels like a garbage disposal switched on high, grinding my heart to mush. Seriously, if I could just reach in, yank the damn thing out, and toss it in the trash, I would. Anything to stop this agonizing burn. I want to bolt. I want to look away. But my eyes won't budge. They're locked onto the two of them, glued to the perfect little picture they're making.

They pull apart, slow. Declan's face goes all soft—that look he only has for his absolute favorite person. He cups her cheeks, pulling her in close. No kiss. Just forehead to forehead. He looks so damn peaceful. Like he finally made it back home. Like he's whole again. His lips move. *I've missed you.* I read the words clear as day.

What if they'd met like this while we were still married? Would he have cheated? Part of me wants to say no way, but who am I kidding? This is Aurora. Declan would literally walk through fire for her.

I can't take another second. I spin around and bolt outside.

The second the cold air hits my face, I boil over. Tears just start spilling. God, it hurts. It hurts so bad and I have no clue how to turn the pain off. But hey, who's to blame here? Me. I'm the idiot who fell for a guy who was never mine to begin with.

"Please," I whisper to the empty sky. "Just make it stop. Please."

Nothing. No magic fix. No relief. My hands fly to my collarbone. My lungs are squeezing shut. I'm gasping, trying to suck in air, but nothing's coming. It feels like I'm literally fading out. Dying, inch by inch.

"That's what you get for chasing a guy who isn't yours." Corbin's voice cuts right through my panic.

"What do you want, Corbin?" I snap, wiping my face and throwing my walls right back up. "If you're just here to gloat or tell me to stay away from your precious sister, save it. Go back to the hospital with the rest of your family. You've got nothing to say to me."

He blinks, totally thrown off. Guess he didn't expect me to bite back.

"Look, I'm just making sure you get the memo," he says, crossing his arms. "Declan was always Aurora's. You got greedy and stole him, but now they're back on track. Just don't mess this up for them. They've waited long enough."

I let out a sharp, ugly laugh.

"Oh, don't worry. I'm done playing roadblock. After today, none of you ever have to look at my face again."

He frowns, his eyebrows knitting together. "Wait, what's that supposed to mean?"

I'm too exhausted for this. I just want to crawl into bed, cry myself to sleep, and pretend today never existed.

"Tell Mom I'll help with the funeral stuff—if she even wants me there. And tell your sister I said hi."

I turn on my heel and march toward my car. Corbin yells my name, but I don't even pause. I just want to get home and fall apart in private.

I hop in the driver's seat and hit the gas. Declan said Asher was staying with his mom. Good. I can't handle another person who despises my existence today. He's safe there, so I'll just grab him tomorrow. I make it home in record time. But stepping into the quiet house just hits me with how incredibly alone I am. No shoulder to cry on. No one waiting up. Nobody loves me. It's just me and Asher against the world.

The waterworks start up again. I'm so sick of crying, but the tears won't stop. If I could just build a time machine and fix my mistakes, maybe I'd be married to someone who actually gives a damn about me. But yeah, that's the past for you. Once it's done, it's set in stone.

Three days pass. Dad's death has everyone spiraling. It was so sudden, and since he was such a big deal around here, the whole town is reeling. I haven't seen Declan once. He's blown up my phone, sure, but I ignored every single call. He's probably wrapped up in Aurora's arms anyway. She's probably already moved her stuff into his place. I don't need him rubbing my nose in it.

I shake my head to clear the venom and struggle with the zipper of my black dress.

"Mommy?" Asher's little voice pipes up behind me.

I turn around. His eyes are swimming with tears. I drop to my knees so we're eye-to-eye.

"Hey, baby, what's wrong?"

"I miss him," he whimpers, his voice cracking. "We were supposed to go fishing this Saturday."

My heart literally snaps. Alistair was a crappy excuse for a father to me, but he was a damn good grandpa to my kid. I pull Asher tight against my chest, whispering nonsense into his hair while his tears soak right through my shoulder.

"I know, sweetie. I know you miss him. But he's with the angels now, watching over you. And he's never really gone, because he's right here," I say, tapping his chest, "and right here," I touch his forehead. "Plus, he wouldn't want you crying. We don't want to make him sad, right?"

He shakes his head, sniffing.

"Good. So here's the plan. Instead of crying, we're going to talk about all the awesome times you had with him, okay?"

I don't have many good memories of my own, but Asher has tons. I'm going to make sure he holds onto them.

"Okay," he whispers.

I wipe his cheeks, grab my purse, and hold out my hand. He takes it, looking up at me.

"Alright. Let's go say a proper goodbye to Grandpa."

He gives me a tiny, brave smile, and we head out the door. Time to face the music.
