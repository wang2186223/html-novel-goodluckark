Chapter 95

Silas buzzed back the second he saw my missed call. "Sorry," he blurted, sounding completely out of breath. "Phone was on silent. Boardroom hell." He was ready to ditch the meeting and sprint straight home, but I stopped him. "Hey, relax. It's handled. Jasper's good. No more tears, no more moping." Seriously, that was all I cared about. He grumbled a bit, hating to stay, but with his meeting dragging on forever, he finally gave in.

So, there I was, standing at the stove, whipping up dinner while my brain ran a marathon. Seriously, can my mind just shut up for five minutes? It’s a non-stop noise machine in here, and now Jasper's drama is taking up prime real estate.

"Hey Mom, what's on the menu?" Asher slid onto a barstool.

"Yeah, seriously, I'm starving," Jasper chimed in, flashing me a grin.

Ugh. That smile. Again. Don't get me wrong, I didn't hate it—how could I ever hate anything that kid does? But damn, it bugged the hell out of me. It was like a puzzle piece that just wouldn't fit, and I couldn't figure out why.

"No clue yet," I said, turning back to the fridge. "Let's see what my weird-ass cravings decide tonight."

Asher smirked, nudging Jasper. "Watch out. Mom's cravings are wild. Sometimes it's heaven, sometimes it's straight-up biohazard."

Jasper chuckled. "Can I help cook?"

"Sure thing, bud. Want to flour the chicken?"

Now, Asher in a kitchen is a certified disaster zone. The kid has negative culinary skills, so he usually gets benched unless it's a foolproof task. I grabbed the marinated chicken breasts and a Ziploc stuffed with seasoned flour, handing them to Jasper. He beamed, taking them like they were a prize. The kid seriously loves food—and cooking it. Some lucky girl is going to hit the jackpot with him in the future.

"And you, mister," I pointed a spatula at Asher, "are on dessert duty."

"Perfect. Even I can't screw that up," he grinned. Jasper and I cracked up. At least the kid knew his limits. I loved that about him.

"Heads up, Jasper," I warned, gesturing to the bowl. "If you get that buttermilk on your hands, keep them away from your face. There's Tabasco and salt in there. It'll burn like hell."

"Got it."

We fell into an easy rhythm, gossiping and laughing. It felt amazing to give him this moment. Just pure, simple joy. I couldn't erase his baggage, but I could damn well make sure he had a good night.

He was such a sweet kid. A little quiet at first, but once he warms up, he's pure gold. It made zero sense to me how his mother could just walk away. From the scraps of info I'd pieced together, she wanted out. No contact. Whether she chose to bail or got forced out, it was tearing Jasper apart. God, the last thing I wanted was for him to end up as damaged as I am.

"I used to pray for this exact thing," Jasper murmured, his quiet voice snapping me out of my head.

"Huh? What's that?" Asher asked.

Jasper took a slow, shaky breath. "Just... this. Having a mom around. Helping her in the kitchen, hanging out. Don't get me wrong, my dad is the best. But he's a guy. He can't be a mom."

Oof. My heart literally cracked in half right there. I wanted to hand this kid the moon on a silver platter, but life doesn't work that way. Some things are just broken beyond repair. Kind of like Asher's delusional dream of me and Declan playing happy family again.

"You doing okay, sweetie?" I asked softly.

He looked up, giving me a brave little smile. "Yeah. I've got you, Asher, and Dad. That's plenty. You might not be my biological mom, but you're basically my mom anyway."

I dropped my spoon, pulled him into a tight hug, and pressed a kiss to his forehead.

"Damn," Asher muttered.

"Watch your mouth!" I snapped, and he instantly shut up.

"Sorry, Mom," Asher said quietly after a beat. "I've been nagging you about getting back with Dad. I didn't realize how good I have it just having you both around. Are you mad at me?"

I opened my arms, and he dove right in. I squeezed him tight, planting a kiss on his forehead too.

"We're good, kiddo," I whispered, letting him go.

"I'm still gonna hope for it," he mumbled, "but I'll stop being a pain about it."

"Thanks, honey."

We got back to business and finished up the food. The boys helped set the table, and we finally dug in. The three of us—well, four, counting the baby bump—practically licked the plates clean. But I made sure to save a portion. Silas was going to be dead on his feet and starving when he got back, and the last thing he'd want to do is cook.

Once dinner was done, I shoved them both into the shower and tucked them into bed.

It wasn't until the house went dead silent that the lightbulb went off. My place has five bedrooms. Even with the nursery, I had a spare room collecting dust. Why not turn it into Jasper's room? A spot that was 100% his whenever he stayed over—his own little sanctuary. The idea sent a rush of adrenaline through me. I grabbed a notepad and started frantically listing decor ideas. I'd need to clear it with Silas first, but there was no way he'd say no. Hopefully. Besides, he'd be the perfect co-designer since he knows exactly what his kid loves.

I was just putting the finishing touches on the shopping list when the doorbell buzzed. I scrambled up and headed to the door, already knowing who it was. Yep. Silas.

"Hey," I said.

"Hey yourself," he sighed, offering an exhausted smile. I stepped back, letting him slide past me into the living room.

"Quiet house. Kids out cold?" he asked.

"Yeah, completely."

I hesitated. A million questions were burning a hole in my tongue, but I didn't want to cross any lines. "So... about earlier," I began, testing the waters. "Jasper was pretty upset. It was about his mom."

The second her name came up, his expression turned to ice.

"Don't bring that bitch up. Seriously," he spat, his voice dropping to a freezing register. "All she does is wreck us. What kind of mother just walks away from her own kid? Fine, she doesn't love me. I get it. I've swallowed that pill. But abandoning Jasper? That's unforgivable. I'll never let that go." His chest was heaving, pure venom dripping from every word.

Loving someone who doesn't love you back. God, it's the absolute worst. I've been drowning in that exact hell since the day I fell for Declan. I hated seeing my friend stuck in the same quicksand, but there he was.

"Every time I call her and she blows him off, it kills me a little more," Silas muttered, his eyes raw with pain. "He's a great kid, and she treats him like trash. I tried to shield him, but he's not stupid. He's growing up. He knows his own mother doesn't want him. God, I hate her. I hate her so much, but... fuck, I still love her."

"I'd give him the damn moon if I could, but the one thing he actually wants is the one thing I can't buy. I wish I'd never met her. I wish I'd never fallen for her. But if I regret her, I have to regret having Jasper. And I can't do that. Not in a million years."

Talk about deja vu. Those exact words had come out of my own mouth. I'd told Declan the same damn thing—that I wanted to regret him, but I couldn't, because doing that meant wishing Asher didn't exist.

"I don't have some magic advice," I said softly. "Honestly, we're in the exact same boat, just different storms. Just... keep showing up for him. Love him so hard that his mother's absence doesn't even register. Make sure he knows he's enough, and that she's the one missing out."

It was all I had. I wanted to fix it, but some things are just too broken. He just nodded, and we let the quiet settle over us.

"Thanks, Lyra," he said quietly after a long silence. "For everything. For being there for both of us."

"Anytime," I replied.

A few minutes later, he gathered up a sleeping Jasper, grabbed the takeout container of leftovers, and slipped out into the night.

Long after the door clicked shut, long after I crawled under the covers, I couldn't sleep. Jasper's smile kept flashing behind my eyelids. That smile, and the ghost of his mother, kept spinning in my head. I was missing something big. There was a connection here, some hidden puzzle piece. If I could just put it together
