Chapter 336

"I'm falling for you."

Those five words. Orion said them, and now they’re stuck on a loop in my head. All damn day. We sat through back-to-back pitches with big-money investors, but my brain was totally checked out. I couldn't focus on a single slide. Just those words, over and over. Look, I’m a chronic overthinker. It’s my superpower, and it drives me absolutely nuts. Today was a prime example.

What’s his play here? Is he actually catching feelings, or is this some sick mind game? Can I even trust a word out of his mouth? And if he’s legit... what the hell do I do now? I wanted to grab him and demand answers, but no way was I going to look needy. Or desperate. God, I was right from the start. Playing the happy wife again was already screwing with my head.

"Hey. You okay?" Orion’s voice cuts through. He’s staring at me, brows knit together.

"Yeah," I mumble, trying to shake the fog. If I keep spiraling, I’m going to trigger a killer migraine. No thanks.

"We don't have to go out," he says, watching me closely. "We can just order room service."

I force a quick smile and pull myself together. "No, really. It’s fine."

It’s dinner time anyway. Orion’s taking me out. Well, technically just downstairs to the hotel restaurant, but whatever. He grabs my hand, pulls me out the door, and we step into the elevator.

I didn't bother glamming up tonight. Not like our first date. Just a basic black dress, heels, and a quick slap of makeup. Honestly, my brain was so fried from a day of mental gymnastics that I didn't have the energy to give a crap about my look. I just wanted the noise in my head to stop. No more spinning. No more running scenarios. Just... peace. I didn't have the answers, and fine, I could live with that. I just needed to take it one day at a time and stop obsessing over every little thing he did.

The restaurant is packed and loud. People laughing, glasses clinking, some low-key music playing in the background. Everyone looks like they're having a blast as we weave through the tables.

"Nice place," I say, sliding into a cozy, private booth in the corner.

Orion just cocks an eyebrow. Classic him. He locks his eyes on me—that heavy, intense stare that makes my skin tingle. I squirm a little, looking anywhere but at his face.

Thank god, the waiter shows up. He gives a polite little nod. "Good evening. Some wine to start?"

"Yes, please," I say.

He pours, and I take a sip. Holy hell. It’s like liquid heaven. Way better than the cheap, boxed stuff I usually buy. But hey, this is a five-star joint. They probably don't even stock bottles under four figures. Exactly what the doctor ordered. I just want to numb out and forget everything for a few hours.

"So, tell me about your ex-husband."

I almost choke. Of all the things I expected him to bring up over appetizers, Owen Hayes was definitely not on the list. I swallow the wine—it glides down smooth—and look Orion dead in the eye.

"What do you want to know?"

"Did you love him?" Orion’s jaw is tight. The words sound like they’re being dragged out of him with pliers.

I don't rush to answer. I take another slow sip of my wine, tilting my head as I study him. Wait. Is that... jealousy? Is Orion actually pissed at the thought of me loving Owen? I can't help but smile.

"No," I say. "I loved him, sure. But I wasn't *in* love with him."

Orion leans across the table, his jealousy instantly morphing into total confusion. "I don't get it. Why marry him then? Did he know how you felt?"

"Yeah, he knew. Look, my heart was already taken, and so was his. We got hitched because I was knocked up, broke, and terrified of how I'd survive alone with a baby. I had zero dollars to my name, and Owen was the first real friend I made when I moved. We liked hanging out. We were comfortable. He’s actually the one who suggested the whole marriage thing."

See, Owen was still hung up on his ex. He knew he’d never love anyone else like that. He was lonely, but he hated the dating scene and didn't want some random girl trying to replace his lost love.

So, we basically started as friends with benefits. Sort of. He was a guy, he was lonely, and he had needs. But he didn't want to just sleep around. My pregnancy hormones were driving me absolutely wild, but even though I wanted it, we never crossed that line while I was carrying Clara. It just felt wrong sleeping with Owen while Orion's baby was growing inside me. We did other stuff, though. Then, after my six-week postpartum checkup, we finally had sex. That was when he—

Thinking about it now, I actually laugh out loud.

"What's so funny?" Orion asks. Before I can answer, he slides out of his seat and slips into my side of the booth. Now we're shoulder-to-shoulder.

I raise an eyebrow at him. He doesn't budge, so I just shrug and keep talking.

"I just realized my marriage to Owen was basically a business deal, too. We married for convenience. Companionship, friendship, and sex. No messy feelings, no risk of getting our hearts broken."

"I don't like hearing you talk about another guy and sex in the same breath," Orion growls, his face darkening.

I scoff and take another sip. "Oh, please. You've probably slept with half the zip code back home. You don't see me throwing a tantrum about it."

He completely ignores my dig. "Did you ever fall for him? Over time?" he demands.

"No. Like I said, I loved Owen, and I always will. But I was never *in* love with him," I say, putting my glass down. "Now, why the third degree? Why do you care so much about my feelings for Owen?"

"Because," Orion says, his hand suddenly wrapping around the back of my neck, pulling me close. "I need to know if someone else took my place. I need to know if I still have a shot at winning you back."

Maybe it’s the wine talking, maybe it’s not. But when his lips hit mine this time, I don't fight it. I'm just too damn tired of holding back.
