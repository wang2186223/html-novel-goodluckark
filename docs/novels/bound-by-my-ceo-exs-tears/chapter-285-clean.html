Chapter 291

"You're seriously doing this?" Clara's eyes locked onto mine, full of worry. "Like, one hundred percent sure?"

Sure? God, no. Not even close. I had zero clue what was coming, but sitting on my hands wasn't an option anymore. "Yep," I said, throwing my shoulders back.

I screwed up. Big time. This whole disaster? My own damn fault. Karma was finally biting me in the ass, but I couldn't let it paralyze me. No more pity parties.

I threw on the white sundress with the blue floral print. Simple. Sweet. I needed to look approachable, like someone you could actually talk to. Sundresses are great for faking that whole "girl-next-door" vibe.

"You know he might just slam the door in your face, right?" Clara bounced on my mattress like a hyperactive kid.

Honestly, that was all I'd been chewing on. How to fix things with Jasper. It was going to be an uphill battle. I'd blown him off so many times over the years that his love for me was probably dead and buried.

It cut deep knowing he ran to Lyra instead of me. Mom kept reminding me how much time he spent over at Declan and Lyra's place. It was a total gut punch, but hey, I brought this on myself.

Every single morning, the guilt just eats me alive. The pain hits the second I open my eyes. I wake up and immediately remember the wreckage I left behind.

My chest tightened. I was drowning in my own dark head space. I just wanted to know my kid. And honestly, I just wanted the hurting to stop for five seconds.

"Earth to Aurora? You in there?" Clara's voice snapped me back from the ledge.

"Yeah, totally," I lied, sliding over to the vanity.

I felt like a ghost lately. Nothing mattered anymore. Some days, the dark thoughts got so loud I figured it'd be easier to just end the whole show.

I used to live for my job. Loved it. Loved the parties, the charity galas, the casual hangouts. Now? I could barely drag myself out of bed.

I grabbed my brush and stared at the wreck in the mirror. Just pure regret staring back. This out-of-control feeling was like a slow-growing cancer. I shoved the thoughts down, brushed my hair, and grabbed the concealer. My skin was usually fine, so I just needed to cover the dark circles under my eyes.

Clara sighed, walked over, and squeezed my shoulder. "Did you think about what we talked about? You need a therapist, babe."

"And what's that gonna fix?" I muttered, dabbing the concealer on.

"I'm watching you fade away, Aurora. Every day, a little more. You've got too much baggage. The guilt, the self-hate—it's eating you up. Talk to a pro before you completely break."

No way. I couldn't even dump this on my best friend, let alone some stranger. How do you even start that conversation? *Hey, I'm drowning and honestly, checking out permanently sounds pretty good right now?*

"I don't need it," I mumbled, tossing the concealer back on the counter.

I swiped some balm over my dry, cracked lips. Done.

"Maybe not right now, but you will. Just let me book a couple of sessions. If it sucks, you quit. Deal?" Her voice softened. "But you can't move on until you forgive yourself, Aurora. It's keeping you locked up."

I nodded, stood up, and forced a smile. I did a quick spin. "Well?"

She smiled back. "Better."

Hey, I'll take it. As long as I didn't look like a walking corpse, we were good.

It was Sunday, which meant Silas and Jasper were home. They always spent Sundays just chilling together.

Silas called it 'boy's day.' He used to try and tell me about their little adventures, but I always shut him down. Now, I'd give anything to know those stupid little details about my own kid.

"Gotta go," I whispered, scanning the room for my clutch. I grabbed it, turned to Clara, and said, "Wish me luck."

"Good luck."

I hugged her and walked out. My mom, Corbin, Clara—they'd been carrying me. But they couldn't do it forever. I had to stand on my own two feet.

"Need a ride, ma'am?" Alfred, our driver, asked as I stepped outside. He was putting mats in the car.

"No, I got it, Alfred," I said softly.

He nodded and tossed me the keys. I climbed in, cranked the engine, and backed out of the driveway.

It was a short drive. Like, five minutes. But my chest was already tightening up. I could barely breathe.

Now that I was actually on the road, panic set in. What was I thinking? Silas told me to stay the hell away. Maybe I should just U-turn and go home.

No. I had to do this.

I don't even remember the drive, my head was spinning so fast. I parked, took one massive breath, and got out.

I walked up the path, fists clenched, trying to slow my racing heart.

I reached the porch and hit the buzzer. Footsteps echoed inside. The door swung open.

Silas stood there. His eyes went wide with shock, then instantly turned to ice.

"What the fuck are you doing here?"
