Chapter 384

Aurora.

I drag myself out of the driver's seat, totally wiped. My feet are screaming in these heels. Seriously, all I want is to kick them off and crash on the nearest flat surface—bed, couch, whatever.

Day one on the job, and holy crap, it was a circus. I’d totally blocked out what the lawyer life actually demands. The sheer madness of it. You’re either pacing the floor or glued to a chair, drowning in mountains of mind-numbing paperwork.

Staring at client files and dissecting evidence for hours? Yeah, by five o'clock, my brain was absolute mush. I felt like I was losing my freaking mind.

But honestly? Even with the exhaustion, this weird, electric buzz was humming through my veins. For two long years, I’d been numb. Now? I felt human again. Like I’d been plugged back in. The broken, empty parts of me were finally clicking back together.

God, it felt amazing to be back in the game. I’d missed it like crazy. I hadn't even realized the massive void it left until I stepped back into the office today.

I click the key fob to lock the sedan—thanks, Mom, for shipping it over the day after I unpacked. I trudge up the steps, digging blindly through my messy purse. My fingers finally wrap around the brass keyring. I slide the key in, twist, and shove the door open, practically crawling inside.

I faceplant straight onto the sofa. A massive, blissful sigh escapes my lips. Seriously, when did just sitting down become the ultimate luxury?

I yank off my heels—totally forgot to leave them by the entryway—and toss them aside. I swing my legs up onto the coffee table, sink deep into the cushions, and let my eyelids drop.

Somewhere in my bag, my phone chimed. Nope. Not moving. Whoever it is can wait until I regain consciousness. Probably Mom, dying for a play-by-play of my first day.

I’m just starting to drift off when this weird scratching noise cuts through the quiet. I ignore it, burrowing deeper into the soft cushions. But a minute later—*scratch, scratch*. Then again, sixty seconds after that.

My eyes pop open. I frown, listening hard. The noise is definitely coming from the kitchen. Ugh, seriously? I groan, dragging my sore body off the couch, and head over to investigate.

I scan the counters and floor. Nothing. I turn to head back, but then—there it is again. I freeze, tilting my ear toward the sound. Ah. It’s coming from right outside the window.

Great, probably a raccoon. I sigh, making a detour to the back door.

I flip the deadbolt and peer outside. My jaw drops. Standing there is this massive, gorgeous Siberian Husky. He was mid-dig, but the sound of the door makes him freeze. He stares at me, looking completely busted. We just stand there, locked in a mutual "what the hell?" stare. Total shock on both sides.

"Shoo! Go on, get!" I snap out of my daze, flapping my hands at him.

No clue who owns this beast, and I really don't need any drama today. Plus, who knows if this guy is actually friendly or about to bite my face off?

But he doesn't budge. Instead, he just cocks his head, giving me this look like I'm the crazy one. Honestly, the look on his face says he thinks *I'm* the one trespassing on *his* turf. Like I should be the one packing my bags.

"Seriously, buddy, please go home," I plead, totally out of my depth.

Zero reaction. He plops his furry butt down, and then, just to show me who's running the show, he stretches out on the grass.

I let out a frustrated sigh, staring at the stubborn furball, completely clueless about my next move.

"Koda." That smooth, familiar voice hits me, and I freeze. My spine goes instantly stiff.

It’s been a whole week since Silas packed up and walked out. Seven days of radio silence from both of them. Suddenly, my hands start shaking, and a wave of pure panic hits my chest.
