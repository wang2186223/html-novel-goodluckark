Chapter 117

"No freaking way. Aurora wouldn't pull a stunt like this," Corbin snapped, jumping to his sister's defense. Yeah, he was pissed at her, but blood is blood. She was still his little sister, and he'd fight tooth and nail to protect her.

"Kid, in my line of work, nothing surprises me," Garrett said, his voice flat. He pulled out a notepad, scribbled a few quick lines, and then glanced back up at us.

"I'm telling you, she wouldn't lay a hand on her. Sure, they hate each other's guts, but she's no killer," Corbin pleaded. The rest of the room stayed dead silent.

I wished I could buy his story, but my faith in Aurora was shot. The person who showed up a few months back was a total stranger compared to the girl who left years ago. She was just a ball of pure rage and venom when it came to Lyra. Hell, she'd even threatened a kid and spun wild lies just to screw Lyra over.

"Look, I'm not pinning it on her yet. She's just on the list," Garrett shrugged. "Hate and payback are pretty damn good reasons to want someone dead. And let's not ignore the timing—Lyra got shot barely three weeks after blowing the lid off Aurora's dirty little secret. The one Aurora spent forever trying to bury."

When you laid it all out, the finger pointed straight at Aurora. She was the only enemy Lyra had made lately. With how toxic things were between them, it wasn't hard to picture Aurora snapping and paying some thug to pull the trigger.

I glanced over at Corbin. The guy looked like he'd just been hit by a freight train. Panic flared in his eyes. He knew he'd screwed up by talking, realizing he might have just thrown his own sister under the bus.

"What about Kaelen?" I chimed in, remembering how he'd gone after both girls before.

"Yeah, he crossed my mind, but my gut says he's clean on this one. His grudge is with you, man. Sure, he grabbed Lyra and Aurora, but the guys we locked up that day admitted he was just playing mind games with you. He never wanted Lyra dead. In fact, he warned his crew that if anyone touched a hair on her head, he'd personally slit their throats."

That threw me for a loop, but I kept my face blank. "Well, that's weird," I muttered, my forehead creasing.

"Totally. And it gets weirder—the guy who actually took the shot at Lyra ended up dead in his cell. Shady as hell," he said. "But like I said, you're his target. Always have been. If he wants payback, he's coming to put you in the ground or destroy your company."

Orion and I let out a dry laugh at the same time. Good luck with that. Plenty of assholes have tried to tank my business. Hell, even the Howells gave it a shot and got wrecked. And if he wants to try and kill me? Well, let him bring it.

"So you really think he's innocent?" Beatrice whispered, her voice shaking.

"Yeah. Whoever pulled that trigger wanted her, specifically. It was personal. You can tell by how they shot her. Kaelen doesn't have that kind of beef with Lyra, so he wouldn't have gone all out like this."

He was just saying what I already knew. This wasn't a warning; it was an execution. They pumped multiple rounds into her to make damn sure she wouldn't survive.

"Did you get the plates on that SUV?" Edmund asked. The guy looked like he'd aged ten years in just a few hours.

"Dead end. They ditched the plates, so all we have is 'black SUV,'" Garrett sighed. "There are probably a million of those rolling around the city."

The room went quiet again. Everyone was drowning in their own heads. My brain was spinning so fast I could barely process a single thought.

I shoved the noise aside. Right now, Lyra surviving was the only thing that mattered. I'd hunt down the shooter once she was out of the woods.

"Alright, I'm heading back to the precinct," Garrett said, cutting through the silence. "Keep her survival under wraps. We don't want the shooter finding out she's still breathing and coming back to finish her off. I'll station two cops outside her door just to be safe."

I gave him a nod, and Orion escorted him to the exit.

"I'll hit up my people, you hit up yours," Edmund muttered. "We need total radio silence on her condition until she's safe."

"Got it," I said. I pulled out my phone and started dialing. Damn, this was going to be a brutal night.

A tap on my shoulder jolted me awake. Shit. I'd passed out in the worst position possible, and now my neck and spine were screaming in pain.

I blinked against the morning light. An older nurse was standing over me, wearing a kind smile. "Morning, Mr. Woods. Just wanted to let you know I'm taking care of Lyra today."

I tried to force my face into a smile, though it probably looked like a grimace. "Just call me Declan."

I glanced toward the door. The two cops were still on guard. Sometime during the night, they'd moved us down to the ICU waiting area.

"Alright, Declan. I'm Grace," she said warmly. "Can I grab you anything?"

"No, I'm good, thanks." The thought of food made my stomach turn. "When do we get to see Lyra and the baby?"

She checked her wrist. "About two hours. That's when visiting starts."

I nodded and thanked her. As she walked off, the others started stirring. They looked dazed for a second, until the reality of last night slammed back into them.

"What did she want?" Beatrice asked, rubbing her eyes.

"Just introducing herself. She's on Lyra's shift," I mumbled.

My brain was mush, and I was running on empty. I probably should've asked her for a coffee or some food, considering I hadn't eaten a damn thing since yesterday.

"Did she say when we can go in?" Beatrice asked.

I opened my mouth to reply, but a figure walking into the waiting room stopped me cold. My sleep-deprived brain was lagging hard.

Then, his face clicked. My expression went dead cold. You have got to be shitting me. What the hell was Kaelen doing in this hospital?
