NovelVibe

Chapter 48

Back and forth. My living room floor is getting a workout. I dial his number again. Nothing. Just voicemail. Ever since he torched Lyra’s place, the guy’s gone ghost. That’s what’s eating at my gut. A silent psycho is a wild card, and I hate wild cards.

No intel means no backup plan. If this lunatic screws up like Black Serpent did, I’m caught with my pants down.

I swipe my screen and hit speed dial.

Blake picks up before the first ring finishes. "Yeah, boss?"

"Tell me you found him," I snap.

Panic isn't my style. Usually, I'm ice-cold. But right now? My chest is tight. There’s this nasty, greasy dread pooling in my stomach, whispering that the hammer is about to drop.

"Nothing, man," Blake mutters. "Dude went completely dark. My guys are hitting dead ends everywhere."

"Goddammit," I growl.

I think back to Black Serpent. The second the cops put cuffs on him, his death warrant was signed. I couldn't risk him talking. So, I put a bullet through his skull. Clean, quick, necessary. No breadcrumbs leading back to my door.

It was the right call. The feds were breathing down his neck. Good thing I beat them to the punch. I know how guys like him tick because I'm wired the exact same way. Face a lifetime behind bars, and you'll sing whatever tune keeps you out of a cage. Loyalty is a fairy tale.

He would've sold me out in a heartbeat to save his own skin. No way in hell was I letting him ruin everything. I built this empire brick by bloody brick. In the end, it worked out. He’s six feet under, the cops are clueless, and I hired a new triggerman.

"Boss? You there?" Blake's voice cracks through the speaker, dragging me back.

"Yeah. What?" My brain is spinning gears. This paranoia is driving me crazy. I hate not being in control.

"We're still shaking trees," Blake says. "I'll buzz you the second we get a hit."

I grunt. "Just find the bastard. Now. I need eyes on him yesterday."

I kill the call. Back to pacing. My skin feels too tight for my body. This whole setup is going sideways. The script got flipped, and now the clock is ticking down. I can practically feel the rope tightening around my throat.

"Fuck!" My fist smashes into the drywall. Dust flies.

The house of cards is shaking. Every dirty secret, every cover-up, every single lie—it's all bubbling to the surface. When it blows, the fallout is going to bury me. And honestly? I don't know if I can survive it.

I grab the phone again. There's only one person who can talk me down from the ledge. The one woman who’s always had my back, no matter what. I drop onto the sofa, forcing my breathing to slow. "Hey, Mom."

"Oh, sweetie!" her voice beams, full of warmth. "So good to hear your voice! I've missed you like crazy."

Just like that, the knot in my shoulders unties. I slump back, letting her voice wash over me.

"Missed you too, Mom. You have no idea," I mumble. Suddenly, I'm ten years old again. The monsters under the bed vanish.

"Not that I'm complaining, honey, but what's up? Aren't you supposed to be at the office?" she asks.

How do I even answer that? *Hey Mom, I called because my life is about to explode and I needed to hear you before I go to hell.* How do I tell her I need her hug now, because once she learns what a monster her son is, she'll never look at me again?

"Nothing major. Just... wanted to hear your voice." My throat tightens on the last word.

"Are you okay, baby?" The worry in her tone is instant.

I manage a weak smile at the old childhood nickname. "Yeah, I'm fine. Just drowning in work stuff. The usual."

"You're a workaholic," she teases. "Take a trip. Go to the beach. It's not like you're broke." I hear clattering in the background—metal on metal. She's in the kitchen. Ten to one she's baking. That woman lives to bake.

"Once this project wraps up, I'll take off. Promise. Just a lot on my plate right now." A half-truth. Deep down, I know a vacation isn't in my future. I'm a bad guy, I get it. But a sinner can still pray for a miracle, right?

"Alright, alright," she sighs. "But at least take the weekend. Unplug. It'll clear the cobwebs out of your head. Sometimes you just need a fresh set of eyes on a problem."

God, I love this woman. She always knows exactly what to say. Sharp as a tack, and she doesn't hide it.

She's right, too. Staring at the walls won't bring the hitman back. A quick breather might keep me from losing my mind. "Thanks, Mom. Seriously. I needed that."

I'd die for her. No contest. She's my whole world.

"Anytime, sweetie. Love you."

"Love you too, Mom," I mumble, my chest aching with sudden, heavy emotion.

I open my mouth to say more, but my screen lights up. Call waiting. Blake.

"Hey, Mom, I gotta run. Love you, tell the family I said hi." I need to hear what Blake has. Now.

"Will do, baby. Stay safe. Call me more often!" I can hear her smiling.

I promise I will, click off, and instantly swap lines to Blake.

"Talk to me," I growl. The warmth vanishes, replaced by cold steel.

I need Hawk. That's his handle. The psycho crashed Lyra's party, rattled her cage, and then reduced her house to ashes the next morning.

When I asked him why, he laughed. The cops thought it was an attempted hit, but Hawk knew she wasn't home. He just wanted to terrorize her. He's a cat-and-mouse guy. Likes to watch them squirm, smell the fear, before he finally snaps their necks.

Then he went dark. No updates, no plan, just vanished into thin air a week ago. I'm completely blind here, and it's driving me insane.

"Speak!" I bark when Blake stays quiet too long.

"We got a ping," Blake blurts out. "Looks like he grabbed Lyra. He's got her."

My breath hitches. I freeze, then let out a long, slow exhale. I stand up, grabbing my Glock from the table.

"Send the address," I say, and kill the connection.

Time to put this dog down.
