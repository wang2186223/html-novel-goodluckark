NovelVibe

Chapter 15

"Declan? How is she?" Eleanor’s voice cracks through the speaker. She’s fighting like hell not to sob. I get it. The past forty-eight hours have been pure, unadulterated torture. We almost lost Lyra. I’m still reeling from it.

"She was up for a second yesterday," I tell her, keeping my voice level. "Just a few minutes, then she drifted off again. Don't panic. The doc says it’s totally normal with head trauma."

A heavy, shaky breath blows through the receiver. Eleanor’s been a different person since her husband passed. Now she’s desperate to play mommy, but Lyra? Lyra shut the door on her family. Hell, she shut the door on all of us.

"Is she gonna make it? Like, fully recover?"

"Yeah. The medical team is optimistic, but they aren't making any promises yet. Too early to call. Brain injuries are tricky. Things can go sideways fast."

That thought scares the absolute shit out of me. Honestly? I just need her to breathe. I need her okay.

"Look, Lyra’s tough. She’ll fight through this. I gotta run, but I’ll buzz you when she opens her eyes. Last time she woke up, she was asking for Asher."

"Okay. Please, Declan. Keep me posted. Make sure she has the best doctors money can buy."

"On it."

I kill the call.

I slide the phone into my pocket and just stare at her. She looks so damn peaceful. Gorgeous, even. Why the hell did I never notice her skin before? It’s flawless. Or those thick, natural lashes? Or how plump and red her lips are?

*Because you didn't want to,* my brain sneers. *You were too busy hating her guts, blaming her for everything, to see anything good.*

I let out a rough sigh. Bullseye. I wanted to despise her, so I built a wall. It worked, too. Until now. This new Lyra? She doesn't ask for attention—she demands it.

I reach out and slide my hand into hers. Tiny. Soft. I’ve been doing this on repeat. Tracing her wrist, counting the steady thrum of her pulse. Just proving to myself she’s still breathing. Proving that the blast didn't take her.

Every time my mind drifts back to that Sunday, my chest tightens so hard I can't breathe. I’d called her to talk trash, to tell her to back off Aurora. Aurora had come to me crying, claiming Lyra had ripped into her.

Then came the boom. The screams. My lungs seized. I was just screaming her name into a dead line, panicking like a lunatic.

Then the follow-up call. *Lyra’s car blew up. She’s critical.* My heart flatlined. I can’t even put a name to the feeling—it wasn't just terror. It was something deeper, darker, something I couldn't handle.

I shake my head to clear the noise and look around the room.

It’s overflowing. We had to haul half the flowers and cards to her apartment because they wouldn't fit in here. Most of it’s from her students. Those kids worship the ground she walks on. No joke. They’ve been showing up in shifts every single day, pestering the nurses for updates.

I used to write off her job. Thought she was just playing teacher because she couldn't do anything else. But seeing this? Seeing these kids pour their hearts out? It flips a switch in me. You don't get this kind of love unless you're the real deal. Her colleagues are the same way—nothing but glowing praise. It makes me realize how little I actually knew about her life.

"Declan."

A soft voice breaks my trance. I turn. Aurora’s standing there, Corbin right behind her. Unlike her, Corbin looks like absolute garbage.

"She wake up yet?" Corbin asks, his voice barely a whisper.

The kid is wrecked. Honestly, we all are. He almost lost his sister, and the reality is finally hitting him like a freight train.

"Nope," I say.

"You need to go home, Declan," Aurora says, stepping closer. "Shower. Change. Come back when you don't look like a walking corpse."

"I'm staying right here, Aurora," I snap.

No way in hell am I leaving. What if she wakes up? What if something goes wrong and I'm not here?

"You're useless to her if you pass out from exhaustion," Aurora counters. "Just go. It’ll take you an hour to look human again."

Corbin chimes in, "She's right, man. Go. I swear we won't leave her alone for a second."

I look back at Lyra. Still out cold. Doesn't look like she's waking up anytime soon. Fine. Maybe a quick shower is a good call.

"Fine," I growl, standing up. "But do not leave this room. Seriously."

Someone tried to kill her. Who’s to say they won't come back to finish the job?

Corbin nods, his eyes glued to his sister. Aurora just stares at me with those sharp blue eyes of hers.

I turn to head out, but Aurora grabs my forearm, stopping me in my tracks.

"She’ll pull through," she says, flashing a small smile. "Lyra’s too damn stubborn to die." Then she leans in and presses her lips to mine.

I nod, stepping back, and walk out. My head is spinning. That’s a first. Since we decided to give us another shot, it’s been nothing but safe pecks on the cheek or forehead. An actual kiss? Never. I’ve spent nine years dreaming about this, but right now? It feels totally off.

Her lips feel wrong. The touch feels wrong. The whole damn kiss feels like a mistake. Why the hell am I thinking this when Aurora is the girl I’ve been starving for since forever?

I haul ass home and take the fastest shower in human history. I’m pulling on clean clothes when my phone buzzes. Corbin. *Still asleep.* I’m running on empty, so I decide to close my eyes for a quick twenty. I haven't slept in days.

I bolt upright, gasping. Shit. I meant to sleep for thirty minutes. I check my phone. Three hours. Damn it.

I throw my shoes on and sprint out the door. Corbin sent another text: *Lyra woke up an hour ago.* I’m furious at myself for missing it.

I drive like a maniac, blowing through red lights, and make it to the hospital in forty minutes flat. I’m about to storm into her room when I spot Aurora and Corbin sitting in the hallway chairs.

"What the hell?" I growl, the anger boiling over. "I told you not to leave her damn side!"

"She kicked us out," Aurora says, her voice completely flat.

"What do you mean, she kicked you out?"

No way. Lyra might hate her family, but she wouldn't be that cold. Not while she's lying in a hospital bed.

"Exactly what she said," Corbin mutters. He’s trying to play it cool, but his voice is shaking. "She told the nurse she wanted us gone. The nurse made us leave."

Just then, a soft laugh drifts from inside the room, followed by a sharp groan of pain.

"Who’s in there with her?" I demand.

Aurora looks up, her eyes drilling into mine. I can't tell what she's looking for.

"Adrian," she says. "He showed up right after we got booted. He hasn't left."

My jaw locks. My face goes hot. That arrogant prick. What the hell is he doing here?

I don't say another word. I just push the door open and walk right in.

Adrian’s sitting on the right side of her bed, surrounded by get-well cards. Lyra’s propped up against the pillows. Even with the bandage wrapped around her head and the cuts on her face, she’s smiling. It completely lights up her face.

Then she looks at me. The smile vanishes instantly.

"Get out," she spits.

And just like that, the ice queen is back.

"Not happening, Lyra," I say, keeping my voice dead calm as I pull up a chair on her left.

Her face twists in pure rage. Her eyes are practically shooting sparks. She was fine yesterday. What changed?

"I don't want you here," she fumes. "Get the hell out. And take those two in the hallway with you. I don't need any of you."

She means Aurora and Corbin. Something went down. Yesterday she was civil. Today she’s venomous. I shouldn't have left her alone with them.

"Hey, take it easy, Lyra," Adrian cuts in, his voice soft. "You're hurt. Don't stress yourself out."

He reaches over and squeezes her hand. Lyra looks down at their joined fingers, then up at him. Her eyes soften. The anger just drains right out of her.

I clench my fists so hard my knuckles turn white. I want to rip his hand away from hers. Seriously, what is wrong with me? Shouldn't I be thrilled she’s latching onto some other guy? I should be relieved her obsession with me is dead. So why am I losing my mind? Why does it piss me off so much to see her look at Adrian like that?

Before I can spiral any further, the door swings open. The police chief walks in.

Adrian lets go of Lyra’s hand and stands up.

"Chief," Adrian says.

"Good to see you, son," the chief says, patting his back before turning to the bed. "Miss Sharp, I need to ask you a few things."

Lyra nods. Adrian sits back down and immediately grabs her hand again.

"Do you remember anything weird about the day of the attack?" the chief asks, pulling out a notepad and a pen.

"No, not really," she says. "It was just a normal Sunday. I woke up, ate breakfast, went to church."

"What about the day before? See anyone hanging around who didn't belong?"

Lyra shakes her head, wincing as she tries to shift her weight.

"Do you have enemies? Anyone who wants you dead?"

"I keep to myself," she says. "I don't know anyone who'd want to kill me."

"That's not what I asked. Do you have enemies?"

Lyra rubs her temples, her forehead creasing.

"Look, there are people who don't like me. My family, for starters. But I don't think they'd bomb my car."

The chief stares at her for a second, then scribbles something down. Something about his tone feels off.

"Looks like the bomb was planted while you were in church," the chief says. "It went off when you hit the key fob. Did you hear anything weird before you unlocked the door?"

Lyra shakes her head. "No. I was... distracted."

Guilt hits me like a punch to the gut. She was distracted because of me. Because I called her just to scream at her.

"Why are you asking all this?" I interject. "I thought this was about the local gang."

"That was our first guess after the shooting," the chief says, turning to me. "But after Sunday? We think Miss Sharp is the actual target. We just don't know if it's the gang behind it, or someone else entirely."

The room goes dead silent.
