Chapter 83

"Fuck! It hurts!" Aurora's scream rips through the air. The sound snaps me out of my daze. I look up. The guy is already raising his piece.

My hands scramble on the dirt, clawing for my dropped gun. I grab it. Aim. Fire. He hits the dirt hard. I scramble up and lunge toward Aurora, who's twisting in pain on the floor.

I don't even look to see if he's breathing. Who gives a shit? My veins are pumping pure adrenaline, and Aurora is bleeding out right in front of me.

"Am I... am I gonna die?" she whimpers, eyes swimming with tears.

Normally, I'd tell her to quit whining. Not today. Not after she literally shoved me out of the way and ate a bullet meant for my chest.

"No way. You're fine," I say, checking her over.

The bullet caught her shoulder. Blood is pouring out fast, and panic starts creeping in. She could bleed out right here, and we're sitting ducks. It's only a matter of time before someone smells blood.

"Liar!" she gasps, flinching as I press down on the wound. "If I'm okay, why does it feel like my whole body is on fucking fire?"

I shut up and work. Teacher training required basic first aid, so I try to remember the steps. The slug is still buried deep in her shoulder—digging it out without knowing what's torn inside is a death sentence. I grab the hem of my dress, rip a thick strip off, and bind the wound tight.

"Dammit. I should've stayed in that goddamn room," she mutters, shooting me a glare. But her eyes twitch—she's hurting bad, no matter how tough she's trying to act.

"Up. We gotta move," I tell her, hauling her up as gently as possible. We start dragging our feet forward.

Shit. Declan is going to murder me. I dragged her into this escape mess, and now she's got a bullet hole in her. If we actually make it out of here alive, I have no idea how I'm going to look him in the eye.

Yeah, technically she chose to jump in front of me. I didn't beg her to play hero. But Declan won't care about technicalities. He'll see his precious Aurora bleeding, and he'll pin the whole damn thing on me.

I let out a breath and hoist her up. She's leaning heavy against my side, practically dead weight. At this snail's pace, we aren't going to make it far.

Time loses all meaning. Five minutes? An hour? Feels like forever. Carrying both of us is draining my battery fast. My thighs are burning, my fingers are trembling, and my skull is throbbing. Aurora is sagging forward now, completely drained from the blood loss.

"Need... a break," I wheeze.

"Yeah... sounds... perfect," she slurs, her knees buckling. We both almost eat the dirt.

I spot a semi-hidden nook and slide her down as easy as I can. Then I collapse right next to her, resting my head against the rusty frame of an old car.

This whole layout is a total maze. Pretty sure we've been walking in circles since we busted out of that room. Honestly, I'm so exhausted and starving that I barely care if we get caught. Just give me some Advil, a sandwich, and a bed.

"Hey, Aurora... I don't think we're making it out," I mutter. Nothing.

I turn my head. Her eyes are shut, her mouth hanging open a bit.

"Aurora?" I nudge her. Still nothing.

My chest goes into overdrive. Panic hits me like a truck. I shake her shoulder, and she just tilts sideways. I grab her before she hits the concrete, pulling her so her head rests on my lap. "Aurora?" I whisper. She's totally out.

My fingers are shaking so bad I can barely feel my own skin. I press them to her neck, terrified of finding nothing. Then—thump. Weak, but there. Thank god. I let out a massive breath. If there was no pulse, I would've completely lost my shit.

The tears finally spill over. We're totally trapped. She's bleeding out, I'm running on empty, and we're sitting right in the heart of the bad guys' territory.

I let the tears run. I'm just so fucking done. Why does this shit always happen to me? All I wanted was a quiet life, but no. I hate this. I hate every single second of this.

I keep my fingers glued to her neck, just needing that steady beat to keep me sane. We're never going to be best friends, sure, but I don't want her dead.

"Well, look what we have here," a voice sneers from the shadows. "Tired of running? Or is your little friend already dead?" The guy steps out, looking like pure trouble. For a split second, my entire life flashes right before my eyes.

Remember that thought about not caring if they caught us? Yeah, scratch that. Total lie. I am absolutely not ready to die today.

"Please," I choke out, watching him raise his barrel.

Logically, I know he won't pull the trigger. Kaelen needs us both breathing for his little plan to work. But logic doesn't stop my stomach from doing backflips.

He clicks the safety off and cocks the hammer.

"The boss wants you alive," he says, proving me right. "But he didn't say nothing about leaving you pretty. You put us through a lot of trouble, sweetheart."

A nasty grin spreads across his face. Looking at him, I get it. This guy didn't take this gig for the paycheck or because he had no other options. He's just a straight-up psychopath who gets off on hurting people.

There's no way out of this. I squeeze my eyes shut and brace myself, waiting for the blow.

A loud bang echoes. But nothing hurts. No bullet hits me. I frown, cracking my eyes open. Holy shit. Declan is standing right there like some pissed-off guardian angel. The bad guy is face-down on the ground, a fresh bullet hole right through his forehead.

"Declan," I breathe, a wave of relief washing over me. But it vanishes instantly when his gaze drops from me to Aurora's limp body.

Shit. I totally forgot about her for a second.

Honestly, I don't know who's scarier right now—Damien or Declan.

"We need a medic. Now," I whisper, my voice shaking. "She took a bullet. She's bleeding bad and passed out a couple minutes ago."

He doesn't say a word. His face is pure stone, which is way more terrifying than him screaming. One thing is for damn sure: the second Aurora wakes up and tells him she took a bullet meant for me, Declan is going to make me pay.
