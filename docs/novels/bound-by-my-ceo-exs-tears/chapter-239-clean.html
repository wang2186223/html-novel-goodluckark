Chapter 244

My eyes glazed over the papers in my grip. God, the last month had been a total shitshow. The board wouldn't get off my back, and I was losing my mind. Seriously, did these old bastards have nothing better to do than micromanage my life? Well, except my dad, I guess. But the rest? Total control freaks. They actually canned my smoking-hot assistant and replaced her with a dude. Their rule? No hot girls in my office until I put a ring on someone.

It got worse. The suits threatened to strip my title. One single rumor about me and some random chick, and boom—I'm out on the street. Dad tried to play peacemaker since he runs the board, but they shut him down. It was an ultimatum: play the family man, or get booted from my own family's legacy. The very empire my bloodline built from scratch.

I’ve been a walking nightmare ever since. Ever since Dad dropped that bomb, I’ve been biting my employees' heads off. I'm toxic right now. Usually, Declan can talk me down, but he’s off on his honeymoon. Supposed to be back this week, but word is they’re extending the trip. Great. I exhaled a sharp breath, shoved my chair back, and started pacing the floor.

"Hey, Mr. Wood. Lunchtime. You hungry?" Arthur popped his head in. No knock, of course. The guy was gay, totally boundary-blind, and always in my space.

"Not unless you can fetch me a warm, willing girl," I muttered, staring out the window.

Arthur rolled his eyes. "Yeah, no can do, boss. And honestly? With the board watching, you aren't getting any action until you're wearing a wedding band."

My hands clenched into fists. I wanted to punch a wall, and he was standing right there. Look, Arthur’s great at his job, no hate. But he’s not a woman. He can't slide onto my desk to blow off my steam when the pressure gets too high. I missed the rush of a quick, risky hookup right here in the office, hearing her gasp while people walked down the hall. Sex is my reset button. Without it, I'm a ticking time bomb. This dry spell was setting a personal record, and not the good kind.

Monogamy isn't my thing. That's Declan's department, not mine. I like options, but the board wanted to put me in a cage.

"Look," Arthur said, leaning against the doorframe. "I get it. Playing the playboy is your brand. But you love this firm. Giving up the bachelor life sucks, but losing your empire sucks more. It's like that TikTok trend—choose your hard, man. Which pill is easier to swallow?"

I let his words sink in. God, I hated this. Forced into another sham marriage. The first time was pure misery. Being trapped, suffocated... I practically threw a party when I finally divorced her and threw her out. I thought I was free forever. Yet, here I am, right back at square one.

"Just order whatever," I sighed, turning away. "Surprise me." Arthur nodded, muttered a quick "on it," and slipped out.

I slumped back into my leather chair, staring blankly at the wall. Time blurred. Then, a light tap on the wood broke the silence. "Enter." Definitely not Chris. Chris doesn't do knocking unless the building is on fire.

The door opened. "Afternoon, Mr. Wood."

I gave a stiff nod. "Please tell me you've got news."

I’d put a private eye on Willow's trail, but the guy had been chasing ghosts for weeks. Dead end after dead end. When the divorce finalized, I cut her off completely—not a single cent. I didn't care if she starved, as long as she got the hell out of my city. I threatened her to make sure she'd never show her face again. Years passed without a peep, and I was glad. Until today.

"I located her," the PI said flatly.

"And?" I snapped, my patience wearing thin. "Give me the details."

"She fled the country after the split. Went back to school, got engaged pretty fast."

"So she's got a husband?"

"Had. She's a widow now. The guy kicked the bucket two years ago."

Call me cold, but that dead husband was a stroke of luck. Willow wouldn't be clingy. We could make a deal. Five years of playing nice—just long enough for those old farts on the board to retire—and then we split for good. "Perfect. Drop me her address."

He sat down, raising an eyebrow. "You really think she'll just jump back into your bed? You literally banished her, man."

"I've got leverage she can't ignore," I said, shrugging.

"What about the widow thing? Doesn't that complicate things?"

"Who cares? As long as she plays the doting wife for the cameras, we're golden. Now, send me the damn coordinates."

Kael was our go-to guy. He was damn good, which is why my family kept him on retainer for years. We go way back, which is the only reason I didn't fire him for talking back.

"Before I send the file, there's... something else," Kael hesitated, shifting in his seat. I stared at him. The guy looked genuinely freaked out, like he was holding a live grenade.

"Just spit it out, Kael," I growled.

He didn't speak. He just slid a photo across the desk. I picked it up, squinting at the image. Then, it clicked. Those grey eyes. I knew them anywhere. Dad had them. Declan had them. I had them. Even Asher had them.

A slow smirk spread across my face.

I had Willow trapped. Right in the palm of my hand.
