Chapter 73

My brain was short-circuiting. For years, I swore Aurora was my forever. Turns out? Total lie. And that realization was currently wrecking my entire head.

I scrambled over and dropped onto the bench next to Asher. God, I needed this whole circus to end. Right now. My skin was practically itching to get out of there.

"What did you and Aurora even talk about?" Asher muttered the second my ass hit the seat.

He didn't even try to hide the disgust. No shocker there. Everyone knew he loathed my ex-fiancée.

Honestly, his hate was the main reason I hit the brakes on her anyway. How could I play happy family with a woman my own kid couldn't stand? Especially when she clearly couldn't stand him either.

She blamed him. I knew it. In her mind, Asher was either Lyra's kid or the anchor that forced me into that marriage. Probably both.

"Nothing, bud," I mumbled, staring off into space.

We sat in silence for a second. Then he opened his mouth again, and whatever came out made my knuckles turn white.

"So, I got a best friend now," he piped up. "Like how you and Uncle Orion have Uncle Corbin."

"Oh yeah? Cool."

I wasn't in the mood. My head was a dark place right now, but Asher didn't care. He just kept rolling.

"His name's Jasper. New neighbor. Moved in last week."

I gave a half-assed nod. My mind was spinning, mostly around his mother.

"And get this," Asher paused, leaning in. "His dad is seriously good-looking."

That got me. I snapped my head toward him. The kid was wearing this smug, little-shit grin.

"They came over," he said, dropping the bomb. "Jasper and I were upstairs, and Mom and Mr. Silas were downstairs eating cupcakes and talking. He's even gonna help her plant her garden."

What the actual fuck? Who the hell was Silas? And why was Lyra letting some random dude into her house when the Reapers were still out there hunting us?

He kept going, looking like he'd just won the lottery, completely ignoring the bomb he'd just dropped in my chest.

"He made her laugh. Like, a lot. When we came down for dinner, they were standing super close. He was helping her set the table."

My blood boiled. I clamped my jaw shut so I wouldn't scream at my own kid. My heart was hammering against my ribs.

"I used to worry about Mom when you married Aurora," Asher said, casual as hell. "But now? Go ahead. Marry her. Mom can just marry Silas. He actually makes her laugh. Plus, Jasper gets to be my brother. Win-win."

Then, the little bastard smirked. "Maybe they'll even have more kids. I'll ask them, but they'll probably say yes."

That was it. I snapped.

"Asher!" I roared, slamming my fist onto the wooden picnic table.

The whole yard went dead silent. Everyone stared. Necks snapped our way. I'd never raised my voice at him like that. Ever.

"What?" he asked, wide-eyed and fake-innocent, like he hadn't just lit a match and dropped it on my sanity.

He didn't even flinch. He just stood up, flashed me one last smug grin, and strolled over to my mom and Eleanor.

"Dude, what was that?" Orion asked, sliding onto the bench. Corbin dropped down right behind him.

I was shaking, staring at nothing, trying to scrub the image of Lyra laughing with some other guy out of my head. It was tearing me apart.

She never laughed with me. If she smiled, it was cold. Sharp. The thought of some stranger getting that warmth? It tasted like ash.

"Seriously, what did the kid say?" Corbin asked, nudging me.

"Nothing," I growled, my fists still clenched. "Just that some neighbor is playing house with Lyra."

Orion and Corbin locked eyes and burst out laughing. I wanted to break something. I was seeing straight red.

Every caveman instinct in me wanted to scream that she belonged to me. No one else touches her.

It shocked me. I'd never thought of Lyra as *mine*. She was just Lyra. The woman who ruined my life.

"He's totally playing you, man," Orion said, wiping a tear. "He wants you two back together. He wanted a reaction, and congrats, you just showed him how much you care."

I glared at my brother. I wanted to lie, but I couldn't. I was jealous. Out-of-my-mind jealous.

I wanted her smiles. I wanted her laughs. I wanted to be the guy setting her table, hell, I'd get on my knees and dig in the dirt for her garden. As long as I was the only man there.

The realization hit me like a freight train. I'm a Thorne. I don't beg. But right now? I'd do anything to be next to her.

It was terrifying. I never wanted Lyra. Now? I was a different man.

"You want her back?" Corbin asked, dead serious.

The question hung there. I had zero answers. How do I explain the absolute chaos in my chest? How do I tell him I'm drowning in feelings I don't even understand?

Why now? Why not years ago?

My head was pounding. I groaned, stood up, and walked away. I ignored my parents' looks, Asher's smug face, and Eleanor's confusion.

I needed air. I headed inside, hoping for some quiet, but luck wasn't on my side.

Aurora was sitting on the couch.

She jumped up the second I walked in. "Declan, please. Talk to me. How do we fix this?"

I hated hurting her, but leading her on was worse. The spark was gone.

Maybe she felt it too. It had been years. She was just clinging to a high school fantasy, same as I had been.

I ran a hand through my hair. "We can't, Aurora. I'm sorry, but we're done. It's over."

"Are you saying you don't love me?" she whispered, her face crumbling. "That you love Lyra?"

I didn't answer. I just walked past her.

Later, as the sun went down, the same question kept looping in my head.

If I didn't love Aurora... did that mean I loved my ex-wife?
