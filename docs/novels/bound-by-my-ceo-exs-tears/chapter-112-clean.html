NovelVibe

Chapter 113

Total disaster. Goddammit. Why her? Who targeted her? I must have missed the red flags. Did I overlook the threats? My brain spins with these calculations as Orion guns the engine out of the dark garage. If I failed to assess the risk and protect her, the cost is on me.

"Is she breathing?" The question sticks in my throat. She has to survive. The alternative is zero utility, total loss. Orion cuts his eyes at me. "I don't have details, man. But she's breathing."

*Barely.* He doesn't say it, but the math is obvious.

I watched the footage. The shooter wanted a confirmed kill. Zero margin for survival. I don't know the full damage report, but two rounds definitely found their mark.

"Where'd they take her?" My voice sounds like gravel. I was so focused on the immediate crisis I forgot to optimize the destination.

"Yeah, chill. I made some calls," Orion says. "They're routing her to Avenue Hospital."

Good. That facility has the highest recovery rate in the state.

I force air into my lungs, trying to stabilize my heart rate. Panic is useless right now; it burns energy I need. But damn, it's tough. I can't function properly until I get a status update.

"She'll pull through, Ro," Orion says, breaking the quiet.

I want to believe him, but the variables are too high. Even if she makes it, what about the fetus? Gunshots plus a hard fall equals extreme trauma for a pregnancy. If we lose the kid, the psychological damage will break her. I know her. The grief would ruin her entire life.

"Step on it, will you?" I snap.

The traffic feels frozen. Time is lagging when we need maximum speed.

"I'm flooring it, Ro."

"Not enough. Every second counts."

He doesn't get it. If she regains consciousness alone, her stress levels will spike, hurting her chances. I need to be there to stabilize her.

I should have acted on my gut feeling. I should have run a full security audit on everyone I care about. I ignored the warning signs, and now she's paying the bill.

"Any leads on who did this?" Orion asks.

He's trying to redirect my cognitive load. Keep me from spiraling.

"Kaelen," I spit. "He's the only variable with a clear motive."

If he pulled this off, his strategy worked. He maximized my pain. Nothing else could inflict this much damage.

"What about Asher? He needs to know," Orion says.

Shit. I didn't factor him into the equation yet. The kid worships his mother. This news will absolutely crush him.

"What do I say? Hey kid, your mom got shot because I failed to secure her?" The weight of it hits me hard. No. I can't break down. Asher and Lyra need me functional. I'll shelve my feelings for now; they have zero utility.

"It's not on you, Declan. You aren't psychic," Orion says.

I want to buy that, but the failure is mine. I should have trusted my gut.

I shut up. Words won't change the outcome. Minutes later, we slide up to the ER entrance. I don't wait for a full stop. I roll out of the moving passenger side and sprint through the sliding doors.

"Lyra!" I bark at the reception desk.

A nurse waves me over. "Follow me. She got here ten minutes ago. They've got her in Trauma Room One."

"Status? What about the baby?"

"I don't have those metrics, Mr. Woods. The surgical team is on it. I'm just supposed to get the family to the waiting area."

Screaming at her won't speed up the doctors. It's a waste of energy.

She drops me in the waiting room and vanishes. My mind is spinning, calculating worst-case scenarios. Then, arms wrap around my waist. I spin around. It's my mother.

"Mom," I mutter. My throat tightens, but crying won't fix her vitals. I hold it in.

"She'll make it. Keep the faith, honey."

I just nod. Words are useless right now.

"Any updates from the staff?" Piper's voice cuts through.

I look around. The whole crew is here: Corbin, Eleanor, Dad, Orion, Audrey, even Aurora. Only Beatrice and Edmund are missing from the headcount.

"Nothing yet," I say. "Did someone call her parents?"

"Yeah," Piper says. "They're out of town for work, but they're flying back now. ETA is four hours, minimum."

I look at my brother. A new risk factor hits me: Asher can't find out about this online or from some kid at school. I need to control the information flow.

"Orion, scrub that video off the internet. Use our tech guys."

"Got it," he says, pulling his phone out and walking away to make the call.

"Someone needs to fetch Asher," I mutter, staring at the double doors.

Audrey steps up. "Silas is on it. He's grabbing him from school now."

Aurora freezes at the name, but I don't give a damn. Her personal drama has zero priority today.

The ER doors swing open. A guy in scrubs steps out. We all stand up.

"Are you folks with Lyra?"

"Yes," I say, stepping forward. "Give us the breakdown."

"It's bad," the doctor says. "But the immediate threats are the fetus and a bullet lodged in her skull."

Behind me, the women gasp. I tune them out. I need the hard data.

"She's out cold, so we need immediate consent for an emergency C-section."

"But she's only at twenty-four weeks!" Audrey gasps.

"Correct. But a round penetrated her abdomen and ruptured the amniotic sac. No intervention means zero chance of survival for the baby."

My chest tightens. Damn. The odds are terrible. I have to make a call to maximize life.

"Do it," I mutter. If it keeps the kid alive, we take the shot.

Right as I say it, a siren blares. The light above the door flashes red. Code red. A nurse sprints out, whispering frantically to the doc. I see their eyes widen. Panic. The doctor turns back to us.

"Her heart stopped," the doctor says. "We're running CPR, but we're hitting a critical bottleneck. You need to make a call."

The nurse steps in, cold and direct. "With her trauma, saving both is statistically impossible. We can only save one. Who do we prioritize? The mother or the child?"

The question hits like a sledgehammer. But emotion is a luxury. I have to calculate the net outcome. If I choose the baby, Lyra dies—leaving Asher motherless and destroying our family's core. If I choose Lyra, the baby dies, causing her immense grief, but she lives to raise her existing son. Saving Lyra preserves the highest amount of life and minimizes long-term devastation. Yet the choice is a brutal equation.
