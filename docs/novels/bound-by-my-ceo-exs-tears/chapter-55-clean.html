NovelVibe

Chapter 56

"What the hell is he doing here?" Declan’s voice shook the walls. He glared at Adrian like he wanted to murder him.

I rolled my eyes. I didn't have the energy for his tantrums today. Sure, he helped me out yesterday, but that didn't give him a say in who got to hang out in my living room.

Edmund—uh, my dad—cleared his throat. God, it was going to take forever to get used to calling him that.

The sound cut through the tension, and everyone turned to look at him.

"Edmund Howell?" Declan blinked, trying to play it cool but failing. "What are you doing in this place?"

Declan’s eyes darted around the room. He looked at Edmund, then Beatrice, then locked onto me. I could practically see the gears grinding in his head.

"Been a minute, Declan," Edmund said. He flashed a smile that looked more like a threat. "Though I gotta say, I’m pretty pissed about how you treated my kid."

"Pissed is putting it lightly," Beatrice snapped. No smile from her. Just pure, icy rage. "We’re absolutely disgusted by what you and your family did to our daughter. Consider our business partnership dead."

"Wait, how is this even possible?" Corbin’s jaw hit the floor.

Yeah, everyone in the country knew the Howells. They kept to themselves, sure, but they were still filthy rich and famous.

"Oh, honey, anything is possible in this world," Beatrice said, her eyes practically shooting sparks at him.

I glanced at Aurora. She looked totally brain-dead, her mouth hanging wide open. Seriously, she was going to swallow a fly if she didn't shut it.

"Can we just get this over with? I want you guys out," I said, staring straight at Declan.

He nodded, pulled out his laptop, and flipped it open. He didn't waste any time opening Skype. A second later, his mom’s face popped up on the screen.

"We’re all here, Mom," Aurora muttered.

The woman on the screen nodded. "How are you holding up, Lyra?" she asked, her voice soft.

Why the hell did she care now? She hadn't given a crap about me for over twenty years.

"Cut the fake nice act," I said. "Just say what you came to say."

She flinched, looking hurt. I didn't care. I wasn't going to play her little game. It was way too late to fix things.

She took a deep breath to steady herself. "You came to us when you were just a few weeks old, but we didn't actually adopt you until you were fifteen months."

"What do you mean?" I frowned.

"We had this amazing housekeeper, Winnie. She worked for us for years. She wanted a kid of her own, but she couldn't bring herself to have a baby with another man. See, her husband died after seven years of marriage, and she never really got over it. They were going to start a family the year he passed—they finally had the money. But a drunk driver took him away, and that was that."

"Winnie wanted a baby, but she refused to remarry or do IVF. So, she decided to adopt. About a month later, her mother called her. She said the local preacher found a baby abandoned outside the church and wanted to know if anyone would take her in."

We all traded looks. A preacher? Was that my grandfather? No way that was a coincidence. It was too perfect.

His mom kept talking. "She took a week off and went back to her hometown to get the baby. We expected her back in seven days, but it took her two weeks. Finally, she came home with the kid. Nobody knew who the birth mother was. No ID, nothing. The preacher figured it was just some scared teenager who gave birth and panicked."

"Since there were no issues, the social worker let Winnie bring the baby home while the adoption papers went through. Winnie thought the baby was a miracle. She named her Lyra. That baby was you," she said, looking right at me. "I’d never seen her that happy. It was like you brought her back to life. She absolutely adored you. Loved you like her own blood."

She paused. I needed a second to process all of this. Winnie sounded like a saint.

"If she loved me so much, why didn't she raise me? How did I end up with the Sharps?"

"A year after she got you, she was diagnosed with stage four breast cancer. The doctors said it was terminal—she only had a few months left. Before she died, she begged us to take you. To adopt you. We didn't want to. We already had two kids and didn't want a third."

But I did end up with them. Something had to have changed.

"So why did you do it if you didn't want me?" I asked.

The room went dead silent.

"When Corbin was two, he snuck out of the house," she said. "By the time I noticed, he was running into the street. A car was coming fast. I knew I couldn't reach him in time, so I just screamed. Winnie heard me. I don't know how she did it, but she threw herself in front of that car and saved him. She ended up in a coma for two months. They had to amputate her right hand because it was crushed, and she walked with a limp for the rest of her life."

She took a long, shaky breath. "We took you in because we owed her everything. No amount of money could ever repay her for saving Corbin’s life, or for what she lost. So when she passed away, we adopted you."

I looked down at my hands. "I wish you hadn't," I muttered. "An orphanage would've been better. It would've been a hell of a lot kinder than the life you gave me."

I couldn't think of a single good memory with them. Just pain, misery, and screaming.

"Oh my god, you are so ungrateful," Aurora sneered, rolling her eyes at me. "Do you know how many people would kill for the life you had?"

"Shut your fucking mouth, Aurora, before I shut it for you," I snapped, my blood boiling. "I am so sick of your stupid mouth. I slept with your boyfriend. Get over it. Grow the hell up. It’s pathetic watching a thirty-year-old woman throw a tantrum like a toddler. Even Asher is more mature than you."

The room went dead silent. Aurora’s face turned bright red, and she looked down.

Suddenly, I felt a hand on my shoulder. It was Beatrice. She was grinning. I’d almost forgotten she was sitting right next to me.

"I’d listen to my daughter if I were you, girl," Beatrice said, her voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "It’s been years, and you’ve made her pay for that mistake a thousand times over. Let it go. You talk to my daughter like that again, and I will personally ruin you."

I stared at her, shocked. Nobody had ever stood up for me like that before. Well, except Piper. It felt weird, but really damn good.

Aurora looked up, then quickly looked down again. Beatrice’s threat had sent a visible shiver down her spine.

His mom cleared her throat, breaking the silence. "I know we treated you like garbage, Lyra. And I am so, so sorry. More than you'll ever know."

"Save it," I said flatly. "Like I told your son, it’s way too late for apologies. I don't want to hear it, and I don't want anything to do with any of you."

It was over. The damage was done. Maybe I’d forgive them someday, but right now, the scars were too fresh.

"Alright, we're done here," Edmund said, standing up. "I don't care what you think she did. You hated my daughter long before she ever slept with Declan. Mark my words—you will pay for every single tear she cried under your roof."

The air in the room turned heavy, almost suffocating. Everyone knew you didn't cross Edmund Howell. Ever.

"Please, Mr. Howell," his mom begged through the screen.

"How many times did my daughter beg you?" Beatrice hissed, her eyes full of venom. "You didn't care then. Why should we show you any mercy now?"

His mom didn't say a word. She just looked down, defeated.

"Get out," I told Corbin and Aurora.

They had what they came for. They stared at me for a second, then got up and walked out. Declan followed them, and then Adrian stood up too.

"You don't have to worry about a thing anymore," Beatrice said, hugging me. "Your enemies are our enemies. Anyone who hurt you is going to pay. No one will ever touch you again."

"We love you, sweetheart. Always remember that," she added before they left.

Once they were gone, the house was quiet. It felt amazing to have people in my corner. But did I really want revenge? And if they went after everyone who hurt me... would they go after Adrian too? Would Beatrice and Edmund really destroy their own son for me?
