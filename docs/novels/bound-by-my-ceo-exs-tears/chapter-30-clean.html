Chapter 30

"You sure about this, Lyra?" Piper’s voice through the phone is pure worry.

I’m tossing things into my bag, rushing to get ready. It’s the big annual parent-teacher sit-down at Asher’s school, and Declan is supposed to meet me there. Piper called out of the blue to grab coffee. We haven't caught up since she got back from Japan two days ago. She was over there closing some corporate deal with Corbin when my world blew up and I got attacked.

"I'm positive," I say, pacing the room. "If I’m ever going to get over Declan, I have to put myself out there. How else am I supposed to move on?"

I’d already spilled about the kiss with Adrian. Honestly, we’ve locked lips a few more times since then, though we haven't crossed any real lines yet.

"Yeah, okay, but aren't you moving at warp speed here?" Piper cuts in. "Like, how long have you even known the guy?"

"Three months. Maybe a bit more," I counter. "Wait, why the sudden attitude? A couple of weeks ago you were practically shoving me out the door to go see him. You were dying for details about our first kiss! What changed?"

I slide my feet into a pair of flats, waiting for her to make sense. It’s a warm afternoon, so I kept it simple—just a camisole and my favorite jeans.

"What changed is I realized you're doing this for all the wrong reasons," Piper sighs. "If you're going to date Adrian, do it because you actually want him. Not because you're using him as a human band-aid to forget your ex. You're on the rebound, babe. And trust me, rebounds always crash and burn, especially for girls like you."

I freeze, my hand hovering over my keys. "Excuse me? Girls like me?" My voice sharpens.

"You have zero game, Lyra. You’ve been locked down with one guy since you were eighteen, and let's face it, that relationship was a total trainwreck. I’m just terrified you're jumping into the deep end without knowing how to swim."

"I know exactly what—"

"Have you guys even talked about what this is?" she steamrolls right over me. "Are you guys even on the same page about the future?"

"Whoa, hold on. Who's rushing things now?" I scoff. "Tonight is literally our second official date, Piper. We’re still in the test-drive phase. It’s way too early for the 'where is this going' talk."

Adrian had invited me over to his place for dinner. After that first kiss, he promised he’d cook me the best meal of my life.

Piper lets out a heavy, exhausted sigh, like I'm a toddler refusing to eat her vegetables. "Just promise me you're being honest with yourself. Using a new guy to erase an old one? It never works. It just gets messy."

"Yeah, yeah, I hear you," I say, brushing her off. "Look, I gotta run or I'm going to be late."

"Fine. We're still on for Wednesday, right?"

"For sure."

I hang up and check the screen. 1:00 PM. I roll my eyes. Seriously, who schedules a parent-teacher conference on a Saturday afternoon? It’s brutal. I grab my purse, lock the front door, and head out to my car.

As I hit the road, Piper’s words start looping in my head. Damn it, she’s right about one thing—I’m clueless when it comes to dating. What does Adrian actually want from me? A real relationship, or is he just looking for a casual hookup?

And what do I want? Am I just using him? I don't want to play games, and I definitely don't want to mess with his head. I need to get Declan out of my system, but is this the right way to do it?

The truth is, I'm scared. I'm getting attached to Adrian, but maybe it's just because he’s a distraction. He makes the pain of losing Declan vanish. But is that really so terrible? We all lean on people to survive. Why is it wrong to want someone who makes the heartbreak stop hurting?

A loud honk behind me snaps me back to reality. I jerk the wheel, realizing I’ve already pulled into the school driveway.

I park the car, kill the engine, and step out. The lot is packed with shiny, high-end SUVs and sports cars. As I walk toward the entrance, a couple of moms give me the once-over, their noses practically touching the sky. Of course. My car is a cheap clunker, and I’m wearing basic denim, not designer labels.

God, I hate this crowd. To them, your worth is entirely tied to your bank account. They don't even try to hide their disgust for anyone they deem beneath them. I grew up around that kind of money, but I swore a long time ago I’d never let wealth turn me into a monster.

I find an empty chair in the hallway and sit down. I watch families drift in and out of the classrooms.

I check my phone. 3:00 PM. Still no sign of Declan.

I dial his number. Straight to voicemail. A hot spike of anger flares in my chest. Two hours drag by. By 5:00 PM, I’ve officially lost my patience. I call Orion.

"Yeah?" Orion grunts into the receiver.

"Hey, Orion. It's Lyra—"

"I know who it is. Your name popped up."

I blink, stunned into silence. I didn't think he’d even keep my number, considering he blamed me for ruining Declan's life and keeping him from Aurora.

"Right. Okay," I stammer, shaking it off. "Look, have you seen Declan? We have Asher’s school meeting right now. He’s a no-show, his phone is dead, and I’ve been sitting here for three hours. Penelope is packing up her classroom."

Penelope had told me she was ready to call it a day, but I’d practically begged her for five more minutes to track him down.

"He's not with me," Orion says, his voice dropping to a low, almost sympathetic tone. "He took Aurora out."

I don't want his pity. "Thanks." I click end before he can say another word.

My mind goes completely blank. I can't believe Declan would blow off something this important.

Penelope pokes her head out of the classroom, and I walk in. It’s obvious Declan isn't coming.

Since Asher is away, we set up a video call on my phone. His face lights up when the call connects, but then his smile completely vanishes when he realizes his dad isn't sitting next to me.

Penelope spends the next twenty minutes raving about how brilliant Asher is, but I can barely focus. My blood is boiling. Asher looks totally crushed, and seeing that look on his face makes me want to scream.

When the meeting finally wraps up, I force a tight smile, thank Penelope, and walk out.

"Why wasn't Dad there, Mommy?" Asher asks through the screen, his voice cracking. "He promised. I wanted him to hear how good I'm doing. I'm top of my class."

Tears well up in his eyes. I want to reach through the screen and pull him into a hug, but he’s thousands of miles away.

"I'm so sorry, sweetie," I choke out. "Maybe he got stuck at work."

"But he promised!" he cries, fat tears rolling down his cheeks. "He said he'd never break a promise to me!"

My heart shatters into a million pieces.

On the screen, my mom and Beatrice—Declan's mother—step into the frame to comfort him. I feel completely useless, and the distance is killing me.

"He'll make it up to you, baby. I promise. You know he loves you, and he never misses your stuff," I try to soothe him, but the words taste like ash.

Asher doesn't say anything. He just stares blankly at the camera, silent tears streaming down his face. He looks so small, so lost. Beatrice gives me a sad look and tells me they’ll take care of him before hanging up.

I storm out of the building and march toward my car. The parking lot is almost empty now.

Just as I reach my door, a sleek black Mercedes whips into the lot. Declan parks, kills the engine, and practically runs toward me.

"I am so incredibly sorry I'm late," he pants, looking disheveled. He's wearing casual clothes, which is weird for him. "Is it over?"

"Are you kidding me?" I yell, my voice echoing across the empty lot. "It's almost six-thirty! You're four damn hours late, Declan!"

I am so furious I could actually kill him.

"I know you're pissed, but—"

"Pissed doesn't even begin to cover it!"

"Lyra, please—"

"No! I had to call Orion, Declan! Imagine how great it felt to hear that you were out playing savior to Aurora. I don't give a damn what you do with her, but you made a promise to your son. I sat here like a complete idiot while you were off with her, ignoring the one thing Asher was looking forward to!" I shove my hair back, my hands shaking.

A teacher walks past us to her car, giving us a polite wave. I try to force my face into a normal expression, but I can't manage it.

"You're right. I messed up. I'm sorry," Declan says, looking genuinely wrecked.

"What is wrong with you?" I ask, my voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "You have never, ever forgotten about Asher. But now Aurora is back, and suddenly she's the priority? You just forget your own kid? Is this how it’s going to be from now on?"

Declan clenches his fists, his jaw tight. "It won't happen again."

"But it already did," I snap. "What's going to stop you next time?"

He doesn't answer. He just glares at me, his eyes burning with a mix of anger and frustration.
