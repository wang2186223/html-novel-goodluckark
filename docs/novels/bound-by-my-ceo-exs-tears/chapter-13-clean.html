Chapter 13

“So, Declan?” Adrian asked, steering us back.

Thirty minutes after that bathroom disaster, I couldn't stand being near Declan. I'd begged Adrian to get me the hell out of there.

“My ex,” I said, voice dead.

Silence hit the car.

I still couldn't shake it. The nerve of that guy, trapping me in the bathroom. And then? He almost kissed me. Like, actually kissed me. Declan never initiated. Never. I was completely thrown.

Honestly, I almost let him. It was all I ever wanted. But then—boom—reality check. Aurora. He was with her. He'd probably kissed her, slept with her, the whole deal. That thought gave me the push to shove him off. I wasn't going to be his toy. Not again. He had Aurora. To him, I was just the kid's mom.

Declan never did the whole jealous, protective thing with me. Back in high school, he did it with Aurora, and god, I thought it was so hot. I used to dream about him acting like that over me. But nope. Never. Not until tonight.

He was pissed. It was written all over him. He thought I didn't notice, but please. I saw the death stare he gave Adrian. The clenched jaw. The white knuckles.

It messed with my head. Why now? Sure, I lied and implied Adrian and I were hooking up, but that was just to get him to back off. I didn't think he'd actually lose his mind over it. Part of me wanted to throw a party because he finally showed some damn emotion, but I knew better than to trust it.

Adrian didn't love me, obviously. I wasn't going to play pretend.

“Wait, isn't he with Aurora?” Adrian asked, brow furrowed. “Your sister, right? According to the news?”

“Yeah,” I muttered.

“So what's his deal? He just swaps sisters?”

Ouch. That stung. Adrian was new in town, so he didn't know the history. And honestly, even if I liked the guy, I wasn't about to dump my baggage on him. I wasn't going to explain how my stupid mistakes wrecked three lives nine years ago.

“It's a mess,” I said. “I don't really want to get into it.”

Truth was, I didn't even want to think about it. I'd paid my dues. Reliving it just hurt.

“Fair enough. I won't pry,” Adrian said. “But hey, if you ever need to vent, I'm here.”

My chest loosened.

Nobody ever said stuff like that to me. Whenever things went south, I was on my own. No safety net. No shoulder to cry on. Just me, myself, and I.

“Thanks, Adrian,” I mumbled, a lump forming in my throat.

We turned onto my street and pulled up to my driveway. Like a total gentleman, Adrian hopped out, opened my door, and walked me to the porch.

It was dark out. I could see my nosy neighbor twitching her curtains.

“I had a blast, even if we had to cut it short,” Adrian grinned. “Next time, dinner's on me. Real food.”

His smile was contagious. I couldn't help but grin back. He had that same crazy confidence Declan had.

“Aren't we cocky?” I teased, Declan completely slipping my mind.

“Not cocky. Just hopeful. So, is that a yes?”

I nodded, feeling a little spark. He was direct. He wouldn't say it if he didn't mean it, right?

“Awesome. See you around, beautiful. Have a good night.” He leaned in and planted a soft kiss on my cheek.

My face instantly caught fire. He definitely saw me blush.

“You too, Adrian.”

“Go on, get inside. I want to hear the deadbolt click before I roll.”

He nudged me toward the door. I unlocked it, slipped inside, and threw the lock. I leaned against the wood, listening to his footsteps fade, then his engine cranking, and finally, the sound of him driving off.

Nothing left to do but crash. I crawled into bed, my brain spinning with thoughts of Declan and Adrian.

The doorbell wouldn't stop buzzing. I groaned, blinking at my clock. 7:20 AM. Seriously?

I wasn't supposed to be awake for another two hours. It was Sunday. I usually did the church thing. Look, I'm no saint, but church was a non-negotiable growing up, and the habit stuck.

Pissed off, I threw on my robe and marched downstairs. Whoever this was was getting chewed out.

I yanked the door open, ready to scream, but the words died in my throat. The absolute last person on earth was standing on my porch.

“What do you want, Aurora?” I snapped.

I did not have the energy for her.

“I'm here to tell you to back off,” she hissed, eyes blazing, brows pinched. “Declan is mine. You're not stealing him again.”

I let out a dry laugh. “You came to my house at seven in the morning to warn me off Declan? You're barking up the wrong tree, Aurora.”

I wasn't that naive little girl she left behind anymore. No way was I letting her push me around.

“Declan is mine, Lyra! He's always been mine! I lost nine freaking years with the love of my life because of you. I'm not letting you ruin this.”

“I don't want him!” I yelled back. “Keep him. Seriously. Just leave me the hell alone.”

I couldn't believe this. Standing on my porch, screaming over a guy who didn't even want me. Like, hello? Does she not realize Asher is the only reason Declan and I even speak?

“I saw how he looked at you yesterday,” she spat. “I saw him follow you to the bathroom, too.”

“And how is that my problem?”

She looked ready to slap me. Even furious, she looked flawless. How does someone look that perfect when they're losing their mind? What's the trick?

“You're trying to claw your way back into his bed.”

“We were married, Aurora. We've slept together a million times. I don't need to seduce him. Go ahead, take my sloppy seconds. Be my guest.”

The words tasted like poison the second they left my mouth. God, I shouldn't have said that.

“He would've been my husband if you weren't so damn obsessed with him!”

Great. Now I felt like absolute trash. She had no clue how much the past chewed me up inside. But she'd never believe me anyway.

“Aurora...”

“Save it. Just stay away. I mean, look at us. I'm a lawyer. You're a broke teacher. You have nothing, Lyra. No money, no status. I actually fit the part of Declan Woods' wife. You don't. You never did, and you never will.”

She spun on her heel, marched to her red Tesla, and sped off.

I sighed. Honestly, her words didn't hit. I'd heard the "you don't match him" speech a thousand times because he's a billionaire and I'm just a teacher. Whatever. I love my job.

I went back inside, finished grading papers, and got ready. After breakfast, I headed to church. My shoulder felt fine, so I drove myself instead of calling an Uber.

I couldn't focus on the sermon. My brain was absolute mush. First Adrian, then Declan, now Aurora.

The service ended in a blur. I mumbled some hellos to people I knew on my way out and walked to my car.

My phone buzzed. I answered, pressing it to my ear while rummaging through my purse for my keys.

“What the fuck did you say to Aurora?” Declan's voice was ice-cold, dripping with rage.

Of course. Of course she ran crying to him. And I bet she conveniently forgot to mention she ambushed me at my house.

I hit the unlock button on my key fob. I didn't even get to reply or open the door.

*BOOM.*

A massive blast of heat slammed into me. I was airborne, then I hit the asphalt hard. The last thing I registered was a high-pitched ringing in my ears and people screaming in terror.
