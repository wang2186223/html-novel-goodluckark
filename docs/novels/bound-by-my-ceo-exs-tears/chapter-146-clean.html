Chapter 148

The contracts on my desk are just black ink swimming on white paper. I can't read a damn word. My brain is miles away, locked up in the villa with Lyra. Sure, I got a small army guarding the perimeter, but my chest still feels tight. What if they slip up? What if I'm not there when the shit hits the fan? I screwed up once. She took a bullet because of me. I can't—won't—let that happen again.

My phone buzzes on the mahogany. I snatch it up, heart doing a stupid little flip. *Please be Lyra.* I bought her that new iPhone two days ago, hoping she'd call. No dice. Kaelen's name flashes on the screen. Dammit.

I drag a hand over my face and press accept. "What do you want?" I growl.

"Who pissed in your coffee?" his rough voice barks back.

God, I hate this guy. He hates me right back. But we're stuck in this shotgun marriage of convenience for Lyra and Aurora.

"You got actual news, or are you just trying to bust my balls?" I shove the folders away and slump back in my leather chair. "Because if it's the second one, I'm hanging up. I don't have the bandwidth for your crap today."

Silence. Just static. For a second, I think the asshole actually hung up on me. Wouldn't be the first time. I pinch the bridge of my nose, letting out a long, ragged breath. Four hours at my desk and my head is already splitting.

"Did you find anything on the shooting?" I ask, breaking the quiet. Better than listening to him breathe. He wouldn't call just to say hi.

He lets out this heavy, exhausted sigh. "Zip," he grunts. "And it's driving me nuts. Usually, I sniff out a target in forty-eight hours. It's been months, and we've got jack shit on who ordered the hit."

The cops are useless, running into brick walls left and right. I thought teaming up with Kaelen would actually get us somewhere.

"It's eating me alive," Kaelen snarls. There's a loud thud on his end—probably him punching a wall. "As long as this bastard is breathing, Lyra's got a target on her back."

He's right, and it terrifies me. It's why leaving her at the house makes me sick to my stomach.

"No leads? Nothing at all to point us somewhere?" I hate how desperate I sound, but I don't care. I need a break. I can't lose her. Not now, not when we finally got a second shot at this.

"Nothing," Kaelen mutters. "But... something's not adding up."

"Spit it out."

"How is this ghost staying off the grid?" he says. "You know who I am. I run the streets. Nobody hides from me. Nobody."

I chew on that. He's not wrong. Kaelen's a monster in the underworld. People hear his name and run. The fact that he can't find a single trace of the shooter or the mastermind is downright freaky.

"Maybe they've got a shield," I mutter, thinking out loud.

"What?"

"Think about it. We've thrown both our networks at this, and we've got zero. That means someone big is covering their tracks. Someone with serious pull."

The line goes dead quiet. I can hear the gears turning in his head. It's the only logic that fits. You don't vanish this well unless a heavy hitter is sweeping up behind you.

"So even if the shooter pulled the trigger or hired a hitman," Kaelen says slowly, "they aren't a lone wolf. Someone's playing keeper."

"Bingo," I snap.

"Which means they're hiding right under our noses."

I let out a frustrated groan. "Yeah, no shit. That's what I've been screaming at you for the last five minutes!"

I push out of my chair, walk over to the wet bar, and splash some whiskey into a glass. I knock it back in one go, then pour another double. Talking to Kaelen is a fast track to a migraine. My day was already crap, and he just dumped gasoline on the fire.

"Chill the hell out," Kaelen barks. "I'm trying to think here. Keep your bad vibes to yourself."

"Why you little—"

"Shut up and listen," he cuts me off.

I grip the crystal glass so hard my knuckles turn white. I swear it's gonna shatter in my hand. He's pushing every single one of my buttons.

"This points to one thing," Kaelen says, his voice dropping. "Whoever is shielding our guy has enough muscle to flip us both off."

A cold realization hits me. "Or," I say, the words tasting like ash, "they're family."

"Holy shit. You think?"

"Yeah," I growl, my voice turning to ice. "And if it's family, whoever is covering for them will burn the world down to keep them safe. Even if it means going to war with us."

Dammit. This just got a whole lot uglier. We have to hunt this bastard down before they get another shot at Lyra.
