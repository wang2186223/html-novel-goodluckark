Chapter 71

"Hey, Ash! Homework done?"

Nothing. Just silence.

Typical Friday. My feet are killing me, and my battery is at absolute zero. God, I forgot how pregnancy just completely drains your soul. Literally breathing feels like a workout right now.

Small favors, though—no barfing this time. With Asher, I was practically living on the bathroom floor. This kid is being way nicer to my stomach.

"Asher? You there, bud?"

Still nothing. What is he even doing up there? Usually, he yells back in two seconds flat. Unless he found some shiny new distraction, which is highly likely.

I’m about to drag my heavy ass up the stairs to investigate when the doorbell chimes.

*Ugh.* I let out a massive sigh. Look, I don’t hate people, but right now? I just want to zone out in a hot tub for a solid hour.

I’ve been drowning in paperwork at the Hope Foundation all day. My eyes feel like sandpaper, my brain is fried, and every muscle in my body is screaming.

I shuffle over and yank the door open. Well, talk about a plot twist. Silas and Jasper are standing right there. It’s been a full forty-eight hours since our little backyard encounter.

When Jasper didn't drop by after school, I figured Silas had blacklisted us. I mean, the guy was a total grump-lord during our first chat.

"Oh. Hey," I blurt out, my voice totally giving away how shocked I am.

"Hi, Miss Lyra!" Jasper beams, looking adorable. "Brought you these."

That’s when I spot the bundle in his hands. Pink roses. Gorgeous ones. I grab them, totally melting.

"Oh, wow, Jasper. They’re beautiful. Thank you so much." I lean down on instinct and plant a quick kiss on his cheek. He blushes, giving me this cute, shy grin. Yep, he’s a total sweetheart, just like my boy.

I look up at his dad. "Wanna come in?"

Silas hesitates, looking like he wants to bolt, but Jasper tugs his sleeve. That does the trick. He nods.

I wave them into the kitchen. The place smells like heaven because I’ve been baking cookies and cupcakes. Asher begged for them, and honestly, I’m a sucker who can’t say no to her kid, even when I'm running on empty.

"Perfect timing, guys. Fresh out of the oven," I say as the timer goes *ding*. I grab a vase from the cupboard and pop the roses in.

"Asher!" I yell, sliding a plate of warm cookies toward our guests.

"Coming, Mom!" his voice echoes.

*Thud.* His bedroom door slams. Then comes the thunderous stampede down the hall and the stairs. He bursts through the kitchen door like a hurricane, then freezes mid-step when he spots the company.

"Come here, sweetie. Meet our neighbors."

He edges over, suddenly acting all bashful. Classic Asher. He plays the shy card at first, but once he warms up? Forget it. The kid will literally talk your ear off until you beg for mercy.

"Asher, this is Jasper from next door. And Jasper, this is my boy, Asher. The one I told you about."

I brace myself for some awkward kid-silence, but nope.

Jasper flashes a grin. "Hey."

"Wanna see my room?" Asher blurts out instantly.

"Yeah, let's go."

Asher snatches a fistful of cookies, shoves some into Jasper's hands, and raids the pantry for juice boxes.

"Come on, I gotta show you this new game," Asher commands, leading the way.

I just stand there, blinking. Wow. I thought I’d have to play matchmaker, but they’re already best buds.

"Huh. That was easy," Silas mutters. "Usually, Jasper doesn't initiate. He's super quiet."

I spin around. Honestly, I’d totally forgotten he was standing there.

"Asher's the same," I say. "Takes him a minute to feel people out."

"Really? Well, Asher's got the edge then. When I say Jasper's quiet, I mean he's a total loner. No friends, hates meeting new people. At school, he’s basically a ghost."

"Wait, really?" My jaw drops a little.

"Yeah. Honestly, his only buddies are me and our dog, Koda."

That makes me pause. I’m no shrink, but I’ve taught enough kids to know that’s not normal. A kid that shut down is usually dealing with some heavy stuff. And it’s not Silas’s fault—you can see the love between them from a mile away. No, something else broke this kid. Something made him build those walls.

"Well, hopefully Asher can crack that shell open," I murmur, sliding a cupcake his way. I bypass the kitchen island and sink onto a barstool. *Oh, thank God.* Getting off my feet feels heavenly. I take a massive bite of a cupcake, letting my brain go completely numb.

"Hey, look," Silas starts after a beat. "I wanted to say sorry."

"For what?"

"For being an asshole the other day."

I wave it off. "Forget it. Honestly, I was being way too extra. We're good."

But mentioning that day brings back the raw pain I saw in his eyes. He’s masking it well right now, but I see right through it. Most people would buy his tough-guy act, but I know that soul-crushing vibe. It takes one broken person to recognize another.

"So, what's your deal? What do you do?" he asks, trying to break the ice.

"Teacher. But I'm taking a few months off." I’d toyed with the idea of going back, but decided against it. Sure, staying home alone is boring as hell, but I need this break. For the baby, yeah, but mostly for my own sanity.

"So, you and Declan, huh? Asher is literally his clone. It's wild."

I let out a bitter snort. "Please. The whole damn town knows that drama. We didn't 'get together.' It was just one messy, drunken mistake."

Sometimes I hate myself for that night. If I hadn't been so obsessed, my life would be totally different. But then I look at Asher. No drunken mistake, no Asher. It's this weird paradox—wishing it never happened, but so damn grateful it did.

"What actually went down?" Silas asks, leaning in.

My chest tightens. A cold sweat breaks out on my neck. "Yeah, that's a story for another time. Really don't want to relive that trainwreck right now."

Declan's been radio silent since he crashed my doctor's appointment three days ago. Thank God. And no psycho visits from Aurora telling me to back off her guy, either. Declan's probably drowning in damage control with her, which works perfectly for me. I don't want him near me. He just scrambles my brain, and I'm totally done trying to decode his mixed signals.

"What about you, though? Where's the wife?" I ask. He looks around Aurora's age, so I figure he's hitched.

"No wife," he says flatly.

"Okay... so where's Jasper's mom?"

Instant regret. This raw, agonizing flash of pain hits his eyes, and it's so intense I can feel it in my own chest. It's that heavy, suffocating kind of grief.
