Chapter 94

I stared at the sky, took a slow breath, and forced my heart rate down. No way was I letting this ruin my day.

"Hey, Ingrid. Aurora," I said, keeping my voice dead even. I wanted zero drama. Just play nice, then get the hell out of there.

Ingrid let out a nasty little laugh. I ignored her. My eyes locked onto Aurora. Her arm was still hooked in that black sling. Seeing it brought the whole scene rushing back—the gunshot, the blood, the way she literally threw herself in front of a bullet for me. I’d wanted to text her, to call, but I chickened out. To her, I was probably still public enemy number one.

I looked straight into her eyes. "I never got to say thank you for what you did," I said, gesturing toward her shoulder. "But seriously. I'm incredibly grateful."

I expected a lot of things, but not the look of pure disgust she threw my way. Okay, we weren't going to be braiding each other's hair, but I figured a near-death experience might have wiped the slate clean. Guess not.

"Keep your damn thanks," she spat, her face twisting up. "And what makes you think I did a single thing for you?"

I let out a heavy sigh and shook my head. Same old story. The toxic bitch was back in full force. "Whatever, Aurora. I'm still glad you did it."

Behind me, Audrey and Piper realized I’d lagged behind. They spun around, their faces instantly darkening into pure murder. God, I hadn't known them long, but I freaking loved these girls.

"What's the problem here?" Audrey snapped, stepping up with a voice like ice. "You better not be messing with Lyra, or I will literally end you."

"Oh, look at that," Ingrid sneered, her voice dripping with pure venom. "Our little mouse found herself a pack."

Piper clenched her fists, ready to throw down, but I grabbed her arm. I didn't have the energy for a parking lot brawl. I just wanted some damn peace. "Don't waste your time, Piper. She's just a bitter, pathetic bitch," I said, giving her a pleading look to just drop it.

"Me? Not worth it?" Ingrid gasped. She glanced at Aurora, then glared back at me. "You're the joke here. Nobody wants you. Your own family hates your guts. And let's not start on how easy you are. Do you even know who knocked you up?"

I threw my hands in the air and turned to my friends. "Seriously? Why does everyone think I'm some kind of hooker? I've slept with two guys. Two! If that makes me a whore, what does that make her?" I pointed a finger straight at Ingrid.

"You bitch!" Ingrid shrieked, her eyes shooting daggers.

"What? It's the truth," I shot back. "I'm pretty sure you've slept with half the guys in this city, married or not."

"Shut up!" she screamed, totally losing her mind. "You're the reason I got fired! You made Declan kick me out!"

I actually laughed. It was so ridiculous I couldn't help it. Was she seriously hearing herself right now? "I can't make Declan do anything," I said. "He fired you because he finally realized you're a garbage human being."

"I'm going to kill you!" she screamed. She lunged at me, but Aurora grabbed her arm, holding her back. I blinked, surprised, but didn't dwell on it. I was done trying to figure out these people.

I ignored Ingrid's screeching and looked right at Aurora. "You think she's your friend? She's not. Don't let her play you. Did she tell you she tried to throw herself at Declan? She literally offered to be his side-chick, but he turned her down flat."

Aurora froze, her eyes darting between me and Ingrid. Ingrid went pale, looking absolutely terrified. She knew she was about to lose her golden ticket. "She's lying, Aurora! Don't listen to her!"

"Am I?" I challenged. "Ask anyone we know. They all know the truth. Ever wonder why she hates me so much? Because I was married to the guy she wanted. She's been chasing him since day one of her secretary job. It's not a secret."

Aurora turned to Ingrid, her voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "Is this true?"

Ingrid bit her lip, sweating.

Hey, the girl took a bullet for me. The least I could do was show her who she was actually dealing with.

"I can explain..." Ingrid stammered.

But Aurora wasn't having it. She exploded.

I tuned out the screaming and looked at my girls. "Let's go. My work here is done."

We turned to leave, and right then, a sharp smack echoed across the asphalt. I whipped my head around. Ingrid was clutching her red cheek. Aurora had clocked her, hard. Good. Zero regrets. The bitch had it coming.

We walked until the shouting faded. I slumped against a parked car and let out a massive breath.

"Holy crap, that was epic," Piper squealed. "The way you shut her down!"

I managed a weak smile. "Thanks. But guys, I'm so sorry—I need to head home. I'm completely wiped out, and my feet are killing me."

"You okay?" Piper asked.

"Yeah. Sorry for ditching."

"Don't worry about it," Audrey said, squeezing my hand. "We get it. Dealing with those two is a total migraine. Go home, sleep. We can do this another day."

"What are you guys going to do?" I asked, feeling guilty.

"We're going to stick to Piper's crazy plans and eat some greasy junk food," Audrey said, making a face.

I hugged them goodbye, got into my car, and pulled out. *I seriously need to hire a driver,* I thought. I was short, and with this massive pregnant belly, squeezing behind the steering wheel was becoming a nightmare.

Forty minutes later, I pulled into the driveway and walked inside.

"Oh thank God you're back, Miss Lyra!" my nanny gasped, practically running at me.

I dropped my bag, a sudden spike of panic hitting my chest. I forced myself to stay calm. "What's wrong?"

Before she could answer, Asher flew down the stairs. He grabbed my hand, pulling me up. "Whoa, Asher, slow down. What's going on?"

He looked up at me, eyes swimming with tears. "It's Jasper. He won't stop crying, and he won't tell me what's wrong. I don't know what to do."

"I tried calling Silas, but he's not answering," the nanny yelled from the hallway.

I let Asher drag me upstairs to his room. We walked in, and my heart shattered. Jasper was curled up in the corner, head tucked between his knees, arms wrapped tight around his legs. He was sobbing—hard, chest-heaving cries. It physically hurt to see him like this. He'd become so important to me.

It was a struggle with my belly, but I managed to kneel down in front of him. "Hey, sweetie, what's wrong?" I murmured, gently touching his arm.

The second he heard my voice, he threw himself at me. His arms locked around my neck like a vice. I lost my balance and landed flat on my butt on the carpet, holding him tight.

"Baby, talk to me..." I whispered, rubbing his back.

"I... I don't get it," he sobbed, choking on his words. "You're such a good mom, and you live with Asher. He said you and his dad aren't together, but you still love him so much. So why doesn't my mom love me?"

My chest squeezed. I held him closer, wishing he could feel how much I cared.

"I only met her once, and she doesn't even want to see me," he cried. "Am I a bad kid? Does she hate me? Why doesn't she want me?"

Tears started spilling down my own face. My throat tight, I squeezed him tighter, wishing I could just absorb all his pain.

"Dad says I'm not bad, and I try so hard to be good, but she still doesn't want me. It hurts, Lyra. It hurts right here." He let go of me and started thumping his chest.

I grabbed his hand to stop him, kissing his wet cheeks and forehead, then wiping a tear away.

"I don't have all the answers, buddy," I said softly. "But I know you are an amazing kid. I love you, Jasper. Just as much as I love Asher. Honestly, if I had another son, I'd pray he turned out exactly like you." I took a breath. "Don't ever think you're the problem. We don't know your mom, but it's her loss. You're awesome, and we're so lucky to have you. Don't ever put yourself down, okay? Because so many people love you."

I poured my soul into those words, hoping they'd sink in. He stopped crying and looked up at me. "Really? You love me?" he whispered.

God, I wanted to find his mother and beat the living hell out of her.

"Of course I do. You're family now. How could I not?" I said, wiping his face.

He calmed down, leaned in, and kissed my cheek. "I love you guys too. I'm so glad our dog ran into your yard that day. If he hadn't, we wouldn't have met you and Asher. I've been way happier since then."

My eyes welled up again. Damn, this kid was too sweet.

"I'm glad we met you too, Jasper," Asher chimed in, and I nodded.

A huge smile broke across Jasper's face. He looked exactly like his dad, but there was something about that smile. I stared at him, a weird feeling tugging at the back of my mind. I couldn't put my finger on why it felt so familiar, or why I suddenly felt like everything was about to blow wide open.
