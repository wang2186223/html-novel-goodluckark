Chapter 99

My brain was about to short-circuit. Seriously, one more thought and my skull would literally pop. Zero peace. Just a non-stop, high-speed loop of anxiety playing on repeat in my head. It was driving me absolutely nuts.

"Alright, look," I said, tapping the textbook. Chloe, my tutoring kid, blinked up at me. "Focus on the key and the bugs. You want to nail this dichotomous key thing? Those two are your target."

I figured teaching would shut my brain up. Nope. Total fail. My focus kept drifting every two seconds.

Chloe gave me a quick nod. Got it.

"So, first trait: big wings versus tiny-to-no wings. What's the play here?"

She stared at the illustrations, chewed her lip, and looked at me. She hesitated, then cleared her throat. "Well, only one bug here has huge wings. So... the butterfly goes there?"

I beamed. Score one for the kid. "Spot on. Since the butterfly's the outlier, the rest go to the small-wing pile. It's basically a game of knock-out. Now, we've got a bunch left, so we split them. Look at the remaining bugs. What are our next two groups?"

Her eyes darted back to the page. Silence. She kept sneaking nervous side-eyes at me.

"Hey, no rush, Chloe. Take your time," I said, keeping my voice soft. Patience is the whole gig when you're teaching. Kids don't just magically get it instantly. Some take a minute, and honestly? That's totally fine.

"I might be totally wrong," she mumbled.

"Who cares? If you mess up, we'll just fix it."

She nodded, gaining a little traction. "Uh... long back legs versus short back legs?"

"Boom. Perfect. Now, what goes where?"

Her face lit up as the confidence clicked. I couldn't help but grin.

"Okay, so the long-legged group ends with mosquitoes. And for the short-legged ones, I'd split them again. Like, big eyes and tiny eyes?"

I clapped my hands, hyping her up. Positive reinforcement is everything. "Nailed it!"

"Thanks," she beamed, practically glowing.

The rest was a breeze. She crushed every practice problem I threw her way. I wrapped up the session by handing her some homework.

"Thanks, Lyra," she said, packing her bag at the door. "Seriously, you make bio actually make sense."

"Anytime, kiddo."

Once the door shut, I collapsed onto the sofa like a dead weight. My brain was still buzzing. I needed a distraction, fast, but my mind was a total blank.

Asher was over at Jasper's, leaving me completely solo.

I rubbed my stomach, pacing around, trying to find a project. Jasper's mom was still renting space in my head. Plus, whatever Silas had dropped on Declan. The whole damn puzzle felt off, like a splinter I couldn't reach.

I marched to my room, threw on some beat-up clothes, and headed out back. Maybe digging in the dirt would quiet the noise.

I grabbed some seeds from the shed and dropped to my knees in the dirt. Carrots and tomatoes were on today's agenda.

"You're miles away."

The voice snapped me out of it. I blinked up. Silas was standing right there. I had no clue how long I'd been digging, but I'd been so zoned out I hadn't even heard his footsteps.

"Yeah... just got a lot of crap to process. Thought this would clear my head," I muttered, brushing dirt off my knees.

"Need a sounding board?"

"Probably not."

The guy was a vault when it came to Jasper's mom. I had zero details. Were they married? Just hooking up? How long did they even last? He wasn't going to spill, and I didn't want to poke the bear anyway. Every time she came up, he either shut down or blew up.

"Fair enough. I'm a solo processor too," he said, pausing. "But hey, I can still help with the heavy lifting." He pointed at the dirt patch.

"Thanks." I smiled, tossing him a pair of work gloves as he knelt down beside me.

"What are the kids up to?" I asked.

"Gaming. Honestly, it's wild how fast they bonded. They're like glue now. Never in a million years did I see this coming, especially with how much I despise Asher's dad."

I scowled. He couldn't even say Declan's name. Seriously, why?

"Why the massive grudge? Whatever went down was ancient history. Shouldn't you be over it by now?" I asked, thinking about his old high school obsession with Aurora.

He'd been just as bad as me back then. I was hooked on Declan; he was obsessed with Aurora. He'd trailed her like a sad puppy, leaving love notes, stuffing her locker with flowers and random gifts. He went totally overboard, and it drove Declan absolutely ballistic.

"It's nothing," he muttered.

Bullshit. He was hiding something, but I decided not to push it.

"Look, you've got to bury the hatchet. Our boys are inseparable. You're going to run into him. You can't start a brawl every time you cross paths."

I needed to give Declan the exact same lecture. Both of them needed to grow the hell up.

"Think about Jasper," I added when he stayed quiet. "This isn't about your old high school drama. It's about the kids."

The thought of Jasper triggered an image. I tried to shake it off, but it stuck. My brain started flipping through old memories, scanning faces, until it locked onto a specific smile. A smile that looked exactly like Jasper's.

I froze. My muscles went rigid. My heart hammered against my ribs. No way. No freaking way. She would've said something. She would've told her family. I tried to reject the thought, but the pieces fit too perfectly.

"Lyra?" Silas's voice sounded miles away. I was completely paralyzed.

That's why his face felt so familiar. That's why it had been nagging at me. His smile was a carbon copy of hers from when we were kids. Identical.

"Aurora," I whispered, the name tearing out of my throat.

Beside me, Silas went dead still. If I had any doubts, his sudden rigor mortis confirmed it.

"I... I gotta go," I choked out, scrambling to my feet.

I stumbled up, but his hand clamped onto my forearm, stopping me cold. I turned. He stared into my eyes, reading the panic. I didn't care.

"You know," he breathed. "You put it together."

I nodded, tears blurring my vision. I didn't say a word. I yanked my arm free, knowing Asher was safe with him, and bolted inside. I grabbed my keys and ran for the car.

This was insane. Not her. I needed answers, fast. Because the Aurora I grew up with would never dump her own kid. She wasn't that heartless.
