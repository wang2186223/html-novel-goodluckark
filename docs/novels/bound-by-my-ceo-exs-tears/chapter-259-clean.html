NovelVibe

Chapter 265

"Seriously, Aurora, move. You're rotting in this dump," Mom snapped.

I didn't even blink. My eyes stayed glued to the trashy drama on my screen. I was a total mess—pajamas on, bed covered in empty chip bags, half-empty soda cans, and a melting pint of Rocky Road I’d been shoveling into my mouth for hours. The room was pitch black. Thank God for the blackout curtains I bought last spring; they kept every single ray of sunlight out.

"See? I told you. She’s completely tuned us out," Clara chimed in, her voice sharp.

I felt her glare burning a hole in my temple. Whatever. Let her stare. I just wanted them to get the hell out so I could wallow in peace. I deserved this misery anyway. It was my own damn fault.

"If Jasper walked in right now, he’d freak," Clara scoffed, crossing her arms. "Look at this place. Look at *you*. When was the last time you even touched a hairbrush? Or took a shower?"

That name hit me like a jolt. I whipped my head around to face Mom.

"Did he ask about me? Is he... does he want to talk?" The desperation in my voice was pathetic.

Mom and Corbin had been hanging out with him lately. Everything was going great between them, from what I could gather. They always whispered about him behind my back, trying to spare my feelings because they knew it absolutely shredded me that they got to see him while I was shut out. But I wasn't deaf; I’d caught their hushed conversations in the hallway.

Mom avoided my eyes. Silence.

Oof. Right in the chest. My heart shattered. The guilt was a physical weight, crushing my ribs. Back then, when he actually wanted me around, I couldn't be bothered. I treated him like some annoying mistake, a regret I wanted to sweep under the rug. Now? He wanted zero contact. It was killing me. But hey, karma’s a real bitch, and she was currently shoving a giant slice of humble pie down my throat. This was exactly how he must have felt every single time I blew him off.

Clara cleared her throat, breaking the heavy silence. "Alright, up. Hit the shower. We're grabbing lunch."

I looked away, staring back at the glowing TV screen. "Pass. I'm not going anywhere. Just leave me here."

*Click.*

Mom snatched the remote off the mattress and killed the power. I blinked in shock. She spun around, pointing a finger right at my face.

"Cut the crap, Aurora. I am done watching you rot because you're too stubborn to grow up and face the music. You screwed up. Fine. But instead of dying in this pathetic cave, you should be out there fixing things with Silas and Jasper!"

My jaw dropped. Mom never swore. Seriously, I could count on one hand the times she’d dropped an F-bomb, and every single time she was absolutely pushed to her limit.

Before I could process it, she grabbed my wrist. Hard. She literally hauled me out of bed toward the bathroom.

"Mom, stop! Let go!" I yelled, yanking back, but her grip was like iron.

She shoved me into the bathroom and slammed the door right in my face.

"Open the damn door, Mom!" I screamed, rattling the doorknob. Locked tight.

"First off, watch your mouth!" she shouted through the wood. "Second, you're not coming out until you take a goddamn shower!"

I stomped over to the sink, fuming. I caught my reflection and winced. God, I looked awful. I stared at the stranger in the glass for a long minute, hating every single thing about her. I wanted so badly to point the finger at someone else. Anyone else. But there was no scapegoat here. Just me. That was the worst part of this whole disaster—it would be so much easier if I could blame someone else.

I eyed the shower, stripped off my dirty clothes, and stepped in. I cranked the dial to scorching hot. As the steam rose, the boiling water hit my shoulders, slowly melting away the knots in my back. Finally, I let out a breath and just let the water wash over me.

"Aurora? You alive? It's been like half an hour," Clara called out, her voice muffled by the door.

Her voice snapped me out of it. Wow, I'd been standing there forever. I shut off the tap, grabbed a towel, and wrapped it tight. Back at the mirror, I looked slightly more human, but my eyes still looked totally dead. Empty.

I unlocked the door and stepped out. Mom was gone, but Clara was waiting, holding a cute blue sundress.

"Put this on," Clara said, tossing it onto the bed. "Like I said, we’re going out. Food. Now."

"Clara, please. I showered, okay? Can't that be enough?" I groaned, completely exhausted. My brain felt like mush, and my bed was calling my name.

"Not even close," Clara snapped. "Your mom is 100% right. You're acting like a spoiled brat throwing a fit because things didn't go your way. You're just sitting here, crying over spilled milk, feeling sorry for yourself. When are you going to stop wallowing in this pity party?"

That stung. Mostly because she was right, and I hated admitting it.

"You want a quick fix without doing any of the actual work," Clara continued, stepping closer. "Look at Declan. Lyra actually forgave him. Why? Because the guy busted his ass and is *still* busting his ass to prove he deserves her. Why aren't you doing that?"

"And what exactly am I supposed to do, Clara? Where do I even start?"

She sighed, walking over to grab my shoulders. "Start by cutting yourself some slack. Forgive yourself, and pull your head out of the sand."

I shut my mouth. She had me there.

"Now, get dressed. There's this new spot downtown I want to try," she said, shoving the dress into my hands.

I stared at the blue fabric, feeling completely lost. Clara made it sound so damn easy, like flipping a switch. But she was missing the biggest point: Lyra might have let Declan back in, but Silas wasn't Lyra. He’d probably never forgive me. Not after what I did to Jasper.
