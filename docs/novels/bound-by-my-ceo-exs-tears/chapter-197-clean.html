Chapter 200

Declan’s wrapped around me when my eyes open. Last night, he begged me to stay, and for some stupid reason, I couldn’t walk out. God knows I tried. I fought my own brain, but my feet wouldn’t move. By the time I finally gave in and crawled under the covers, he was already out cold.

His grip on my waist is tight, like he’s terrified I’ll vanish the second he lets go. Pressed against him like this, the noise in my head just... stops. I feel safe. Cherished, even. All the old scars fade to nothing. Then his warm breath hits my neck, and boom—instant goosebumps.

I slip out from under his arm, super slow, trying not to wake him. Asher needs to get his butt out of bed or he’s going to miss the bus. I creep across the carpet, slip out the door, and peek in on Aurora first. Then I head down the hall to Asher’s room.

"Hey, Asher—" I start, but the kid is already up. He glances at me, completely silent, and goes right back to fumbling with his uniform buttons. No "good morning," nothing.

"Still giving me the cold shoulder, huh?" I walk over and plop down on the edge of his mattress. He doesn't just look at me; he glares. Hard. Not a single word. I let out a heavy sigh. Great, he's freezing me out, probably learning from how I treat his dad. I start to push myself up to leave, but his voice cuts through the quiet, sounding totally crushed.

"I just... I don't get it," he mutters, barely audible. "You know I can't stand Piper. But you literally threatened to cancel my whole party unless she got an invite. Why? Why do you always back her up?"

Ugh, my chest aches. Hurting my own kid is the last thing I’d ever want, but I couldn't let him freeze that girl out. "Come here, sweetie." I expect him to blow me off, but he doesn't. He drags his feet a little, then slumps down right next to me.

"I'm not on her team, Asher. Promise," I say.

"Then why make me do it?"

I never, ever wanted to drag up this part of my past to him. But I'm out of options. If I want him to get it, I have to lay out the ugly truth of my own childhood.

I take a slow, deep breath. "Okay, so when I was your age, I was basically invisible. No friends. Everyone thought I was this weird, uncool kid. Nobody beat me up or anything, but I was totally shut out. They thought I was annoying, so I never got the invite. No sleepovers, no birthday parties, nothing. Every time the other kids handed out invitations in class and skipped me, it felt like a punch to the gut. I used to cry myself to sleep."

Honestly? It still stings. And it was worse because I was a total outcast everywhere—not just at school, but with my own family, too. When you're a kid, you don't get why people treat you like garbage. So you keep trying to fit in, and they just keep slamming the door in your face.

"Who were they? Tell me their names," Asher snaps, suddenly furious. "I'll find them and make them pay. Nobody messes with my mom!" His sudden anger snaps me right out of my pity party. I can't help but smile. He's ready to go to war for me.

I grab his hand and tug him close. "Hey, that was a lifetime ago, buddy. Look, I made you invite Piper because I know exactly how miserable it is to watch everyone else get invited while you're left out in the cold. I just can't stand the thought of any kid feeling that way."

He goes quiet, processing. I really hope I finally reached him. I love this kid to death, and I'm so over this stupid wall between us.
