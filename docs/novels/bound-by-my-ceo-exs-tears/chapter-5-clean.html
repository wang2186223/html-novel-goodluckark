Chapter 5

The weather was gorgeous. Total bait-and-switch. I cruised down the usual streets, thinking today would be just fine. Boy, was I wrong.

We pulled up to a packed house. The chapel was overflowing with people showing up for the final send-off.

I scanned the room. Everything looked perfect. Figures. The rest of the family didn't lift a finger for the funeral prep. The whole damn circus fell on my shoulders.

But hey, no complaints from me. It was my turn to pay him back. The man kept me fed, dressed, and gave me a roof when I had nothing.

People were sliding into pews. Showtime. I headed straight for the far side of the aisle. No way was I sitting with the main crew. And sitting next to Aurora? Yeah, hard pass.

"Hey, Mom," Asher whispered, tugging my sleeve and pointing across the aisle. "Why are we over here? Aren't we sitting with Grandma?"

Side-eyes started coming our way. Let them look. Everyone knew the family treated me like a black sheep after the whole mess anyway.

"Pews are full, bud. I don't want to make a scene," I lied.

He gave me that look but dropped it. The priest walked up, the sermon kicked off, and right then, a body slid onto the bench right beside me.

My muscles locked up. That cologne. I'd know it in my sleep. What the hell was he doing here? He belonged over there with his precious Aurora. Honestly, I wished he'd just go back to her.

God, I sounded so bitter. But I was. Mad, hurt, and totally pissed off.

"Dad!" Asher hissed-whispered. A couple of heads snapped toward us.

I shot them a death glare until they minded their own business.

"Hey, can I sit in the middle?" Asher whispered.

Thank God. A tiny miracle. Now I didn't have to choke on his heavy, suffocating vibe.

I quickly shuffled us around, putting Asher between us. Instantly, the air got a little easier to breathe.

"We're all going to die," the preacher droned from the pulpit. "But how will you go? Did you make an impact? Did you touch lives, or are you leaving behind a trail of regrets?"

The words hit home. If I kicked the bucket today, who'd show up? Would anyone even give a shit? Who was I kidding? Nope. They'd probably throw a party. Asher was the only one who'd actually cry. Just my boy. No one else.

It was a pathetic existence, really. Zero friends because I kept my walls up. Growing up in Aurora's perfect shadow meant I was never enough. I wasn't the hot one. Or the smart one. Or the loved one. Aurora was flawless. I was just... background noise.

Years later, nothing had changed. Still in her shadow. Nobody cared about my struggles. It was always the Aurora Show. Her heartbreak was more tragic. Her joy was more important. She got the spotlight; I got the scraps of love they threw on the floor.

"Mom?" Asher's voice snapped me back to reality.

I blinked. The service was done. People were already shuffling out.

"Lyra, you good?" That deep voice of his sent a shiver straight down my spine.

I didn't want to look at him, let alone talk. But we had ten more years of co-parenting ahead. I had to play nice.

I shrugged, stood up, and kept my eyes glued to the floor. Rude? Maybe. But I couldn't look at him. Not with the mental image of him staring adoringly at Aurora burned into my brain.

"Let's go, Asher. Move it."

He hopped up, and we headed for the exit. Outside, the crowd swarmed us with the usual condolences. I spotted a few coworkers in the mix and gave them a weak wave.

We hadn't even put Dad in the ground yet, and I was already running on empty.

"Look who finally decided to show up," Aurora's venomous voice hissed from behind.

I turned around. Even with puffy eyes and a blotchy face, she still looked like a damn supermodel.

I let out a heavy sigh. I really didn't have the energy for this.

"Not now, Aurora. Can we just get through the burial first?"

She smirked, leaning close so her words were for my ears only.

"Oh, we'll bury him. But get this—I'm not going anywhere. You stole my family back then, but game over. I'm taking back what's mine. Everything. Including the guy who was always supposed to be with me." She backed off and walked away just as the preacher waved us toward the gravesite.

Asher looked from me to her retreating back, staying quiet. I stood there, stunned but not shocked. Classic Aurora.

What she didn't get was that there was nothing to steal. None of it was ever mine. The family worshipped her. And Declan? Please. He was always hers, anyway.

I swallowed the lump in my throat and guided Asher toward the open grave.

I kept my distance from Mom, Aurora, and Corbin. They were all huddled up. To anyone else, I looked like a random guest, not family.

"Ashes to ashes, dust to dust..." the preacher intoned as the casket went down.

Dirt started hitting the wood. Mom lost it, screaming for him to come back. Aurora and Corbin just sobbed quietly, holding her tight.

I pulled Asher close, holding him while he sobbed. Seeing him hurt broke me. I blinked back my own tears. No, I had to be the strong one. He needed me.

More fake smiles and handshakes. I went on autopilot, totally zoning out. By the time I snapped back, the crowd had mostly cleared.

"Mom, look, Grandma and Grandpa," Asher said, pulling my arm toward Declan's parents.

They were standing with Declan and his twin, Orion.

I stood there like an idiot while Asher ran over. They gave me a cold glance but said nothing. No surprise there—they never wanted me for their son.

"Can I go grab snacks with them?" Asher asked. I nodded.

The kid was starving anyway. Once they walked off, Declan and I were left in awkward silence. With Asher gone, his eyes immediately drifted back to Aurora, who was standing just a few yards away.

I opened my mouth to leave, but then—*screeech*. Tires squealed. Everything went sideways in a second. Gunmen. Shots fired. And the instant the bullets flew, Declan dove straight for Aurora.

I froze, watching him shield her with his own body.

He left me to die to save her. Why was I even shocked? It was the ultimate proof: I was never going to be his number one. Watching him play hero for her broke whatever was left of my heart.

"Get down!" a guy in a tactical vest screamed.

He shoved me hard, but too slow. A sharp, hot pain ripped through me. I hit the dirt, the wind knocked right out of my lungs.

"We need a medic! Now!" the guy yelled, kneeling and slamming his hands over my wound.

My head spun. Everything hurt. I tried to say I was fine, but then I saw the red soaking my dress and his hands. God, I hated blood.

"Oh God... Asher," I choked out.

He was my very last thought before the world went completely black.
