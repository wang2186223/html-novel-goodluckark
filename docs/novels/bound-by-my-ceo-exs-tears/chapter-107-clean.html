Chapter 108

That damn note. It was stuck in my head like a bad song. I couldn't shake it.

Sure, I wanted to write it off as some stupid prank. But my gut kept screaming otherwise. Every single time I looked at those words, a cold shiver hit me.

Maybe I should call the cops? No, scratch that. I didn't want to make a whole scene over one stupid piece of paper. What if Cal was right? What if it really was just some dumb joke?

*Ring!*

The phone blared, making me jump out of my skin. I dropped the mop, grabbed the screen, and saw Declan’s name flashing. My thumb hovered over the decline button. I almost did it. But I didn't.

"Yeah?" I kept my voice flat. Dead.

"Hey. How's it going?" He sounded... hesitant. Weird.

Seriously, this new version of Declan freaked me out. It just wasn't him. Like he went to sleep and woke up as some totally different guy. If this makeover was legit, it was gonna take me a hot minute to get used to it.

"You need something?"

"Yeah. Just wanted to let you know I'm heading out on a business trip for a few days," he said.

I blinked. "Uh, okay. You want me to tell Asher?"

Asher was at school. The kid would be bummed—he hated when Declan traveled—but he'd get over it.

A beat of silence on the line. "He already knows. I wanted *you* to know."

My jaw dropped. Declan never told me crap about his trips before. Back then, he’d just vanish in the morning. I’d be wrapping up his leftovers for dinner, and only then would Asher mention his dad wasn't coming home. He told our kid, but ignored me.

It was a total power move. Pure disrespect. He didn't care, though. He knew it cut me deep, which is exactly why he kept doing it.

"Right. Not sure why that's my business, Declan. I'm not your wife anymore, and I'm definitely not your babysitter." I tried to keep the bite out of my voice, but damn, it was hard.

These days, whenever he did something decent, it just triggered memories of him being a jerk. You can't just burn down a house and expect someone to forget the fire.

"I get that," he said, his voice slow and careful, like he was talking down a wild animal. "Just thought it was a basic courtesy."

I let out a sharp, ugly laugh. "Courtesy? You didn't give a crap about courtesy when we were married. Why start now?"

The line went dead quiet. I broke the silence first.

"Whatever. Forget it. I don't have the energy to fight with you."

He let out a heavy sigh. "Me neither." He paused. "Look, my jet's on the tarmac. But we need to talk when I get back. Seriously. It's important."

My ears perked up. "Can't you just tell me now?"

"No. Needs to be face-to-face."

Ugh. I hate waiting. Tell me something like that and make me wait? My brain goes into overdrive. I'll spend the next few days spiraling, cooking up worst-case scenarios.

"Come on, just spit it out. You know I hate suspense."

"I know, but it has to wait." He paused, and I heard muffled chatter on his end. "Gotta run. Stay safe. We'll talk when I'm back."

*Click.*

He hung up. I groaned, staring at the screen. Dammit. What was that about?

"Lyra!"

The shout shattered my thoughts.

Great. Now I was going to be a nervous wreck until he got back.

"In the kitchen!" I yelled back.

Heavy footsteps thudded down the hall. Cal walked in. He looked... off. Totally rattled.

"What's up?" I asked, since he was just standing there, mute. "You screamed my name like the house was on fire."

His eyes were huge, like he'd just seen a ghost or cracked some massive secret. I stared at him. He was wearing a baggy sweatshirt and sweatpants. Right, no work today. It was his day off.

"Cal?" I nudged.

He blinked, shaking his head. "Oh. Sorry. Look, I don't know if this is too fast, but... I gotta ask you something."

First Declan, now Cal. Great. He was fidgeting, shifting his weight. My gut told me I wasn't going to like whatever was coming next.

"Okay. Shoot."

He hesitated, taking a massive gulp of air.

"Go out with me. Like, on a date."

"Wait, what?" I stuttered, my eyes popping.

Did I hear that right? No way. Absolutely no way. We were just friends.

"Let me take you out," he said, his voice steadier this time. "I know, it's probably too soon. But think about it. We could help each other heal. We both got wrecked in the past. We get each other. It makes sense."

I just stared. My brain short-circuited. Was he serious? This was crazy. And I wasn't trying to be mean, but come on. He was basically asking me to be his rebound. Rebounds are a trainwreck waiting to happen.

"Please, Lyra. Say something," he pleaded, watching my blank face.

I looked down at the tiles, then back up, scrambling for a nice way to shut this down without crushing him.

"You're a great guy, Silas. Seriously. Any girl would be lucky to have you..."

"But?" he cut in.

"But this is a terrible idea." I sighed. "Look, we're both fresh out of toxic situations. Neither of us is ready. The last time I tried dating after my divorce from Declan, I ended up pregnant by a guy who was using me and literally tried to have me killed. And honestly? You're pissed at Aurora, but you're still in love with her. I already fell for one guy who was obsessed with Aurora—Declan—and he nearly destroyed me. I'm not doing that again."

His shoulders slumped. He went totally quiet. I felt like a jerk for popping his balloon, but getting tangled up with a guy who was still hung up on Aurora? Disaster. I wanted peace, not another round of playing second best to her.

"Yeah. You're right. Sorry," he mumbled. He turned on his heel and walked out the back door.

"Silas!" I called out.

Nothing.

I walked to the door, watching him cross the yard back to his place. I sighed, turning back into the house.

*Ding-dong.*

The doorbell rang. Talk about deja vu.

I walked down the hallway, my stomach doing flips.

I pulled the door open. Empty porch. But there, on the mat, was another note.

I picked it up. My fingers caught on something sticky. Red. Blood? The words practically screamed off the paper:

*TIME IS TICKING AVA. HAVE YOU SAID GOODBYE TO YOUR LOVED ONES?*

I dropped it like it was on fire, my heart hammering against my ribs. I stared at the paper on the floor, wishing it would just dissolve. This wasn't a joke. Someone was coming for me. Again. But who? And why?
