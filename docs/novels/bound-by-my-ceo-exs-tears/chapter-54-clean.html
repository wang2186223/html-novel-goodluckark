Chapter 55

"Please come in," Piper's voice jumpscares me from behind.

I snap out of it, realizing I’ve been standing there staring at them like a total idiot. My brain was just too scrambled to speak.

I step aside, letting them pass. My mind is still short-circuiting. The Howells. In my living room. And they might actually be my real family.

We all sit down, and I lock eyes with Adrian. "How the hell are you even out?"

"Bail," he mumbles, staring at the floor, avoiding my gaze like the plague.

Yesterday, Garrett pushed me to press charges. He said it would lock our case down against him. I couldn't give him a straight answer because I was totally frozen. What Adrian did to me was messed up, and I honestly don't know if I can ever get past it.

But at the same time, he taught me a lot about who I am. I just didn't know if I had the guts to ruin him. Corbin was pressing charges anyway, and so were the cops. Adrian had used fake IDs and forged documents to get in. Garrett told me that even if I backed out, the state was still moving forward because they’d already opened a file on me when the murder attempts started.

"Lyra, I'm so sorry for what my son did to you," Beatrice cuts in, breaking the heavy silence.

I just blink at her. I don't even know what to feel. The fact that she still calls him "her son" means she's okay with his crap. If that's the case, I don't want her anywhere near my life, even if she is my biological mother.

"So you're saying sorry, but you're the one who paid his bail, right?" Piper fires off, asking the exact shit I'm too chicken to say. "If he's telling the truth, does that mean you're cool with him torturing your real daughter? The one you supposedly cried over for decades?"

"We don't condone a damn thing he did. It was sick. Pure evil," Edmund spits, his jaw tight as he glares daggers at Adrian.

Even with the rage, I can tell he still loves the guy. It's written all over his face, same as Stella.

"Then why the hell did you spring him?" Piper presses, relentless. "He needs to rot for what he did to Lyra. I don't care about your excuses. Messing with someone's head while planning to put them in a grave is just twisted."

Hearing it out loud just rips my scabs right off. It hurts like hell.

"Because he's our boy and we love him. I didn't give birth to him, but we adopted him at ten. He's ours," Beatrice says, her eyes welling up.

I want to loathe her for that, but I get it. I'd do the same for Asher. If he went off the rails, I'd be devastated, sure, but I wouldn't ditch him. I'd ride it out till the wheels fell off.

I look at Adrian. He's already watching me. Dead eyes. Same blank stare as yesterday. Is this the real him, or was that sweet, warm guy I knew the actual fake?

I shake it off and face the parents. "Look, you guys want something. What is it? If you're here to beg for his case, save your breath. I'm not involved. If that's the pitch, there's the door." No sugarcoating. Just straight facts.

They look blindsided, but I don't care. I'm just calling it like it is. Why else would they show up?

"We're here because Adrian told us the truth," Beatrice chokes out, tears spilling. "Because you're our daughter."

I want to buy it, but my brain won't let me. The people who raised me hated my guts for twenty-eight years. Why would these strangers love me? They don't even know me, and they clearly worship Adrian.

I stand up, crossing my arms. "How do you even know? Adrian could be wrong. Look at me. I don't look a thing like you guys."

They're like supermodels, and I'm just... plain old me.

Adrian slides an envelope across the table. "I snagged some of your hair. Ran a DNA test. It's a match."

I rip it open. Scan the paper. Damn. He's not lying. The numbers don't lie. I'm theirs.

"You've got my brown hair," Edmund says softly. "And your mother's eyes. But honestly? I didn't even need the test. I just knew. In my gut, I knew you were our baby girl who vanished twenty-eight years ago."

I spin around, the waterworks starting. It's too much. My entire life is a giant, steaming lie. How do I even process this? Every insult, every beating, every ounce of hate from the Sharps just floods back.

They knew. They always knew I wasn't theirs. Why keep me? Why not just dump me at an orphanage instead of keeping me around as a punching bag?

"It's okay, sweetheart," Beatrice whispers. Hearing the nickname I use for Asher breaks me. I sob. "Just let it go," she says, wrapping her arms around me.

I didn't even see her move. But her hug? It feels... right. Like coming home. I never got that from Eleanor. It's like this warm wave of actual motherly love just swallowing me up.

I'm ruining her expensive dress with my snot and tears, but she doesn't budge. Then, another pair of arms wraps around us both. Edmund. I just bawl until my tear ducts run completely dry.

"You okay?" she asks gently.

I pull back, wiping my face, and manage a weak smile. "Yeah. Better."

"You're our kid, Lyra. We love you," Edmund says, his eyes glassy. "We never stopped, not for a second, even when we thought you were dead. We're here now. Just let us in. Let us love you like we should've for the last twenty-eight years."

That's the exact speech I wanted from my adoptive parents. The contrast is insane. Edmund and Beatrice adopted a ten-year-old kid and gave him the world. Eleanor and Alistair took a baby and treated her like trash. Night and day.

The doorbell buzzes before I can say a word.

"I'll get it," Piper says, clearing her throat to break up the heavy vibe.

I hear the front door click, then some angry whispering. Sounds like Piper is fighting with someone. I start to get up, but Corbin barges right into the living room, Aurora and Declan hot on his heels.

My jaw drops. Seriously? Are these guys glued at the hip or what? The only one missing is Orion.

"Sorry, Lyra," Piper sighs, shooting Corbin a death glare. "I tried to block him, but he just shoved right past me."

"What do you guys want?" I groan. I seriously don't have the bandwidth for this drama right now.

"Mom wants to talk. To all of us," Aurora says. "She wants to lay it all out, but you're ghosting her calls."

I let out a heavy, exhausted sigh. I really don't want to hear her excuses, but deep down, I know I have to face the music.
