NovelVibe

Chapter 24

My head is still spinning from what Declan did two days ago. Seriously, what is his actual problem? Is he trying to trash his relationship with Aurora? Or is he just trying to drag me down with him?

Aurora already thinks I’m some husband-stealing villain. She’s convinced I’m clawing to get him back. But she doesn’t get it—I just want some peace. I’m done with Declan. Been there, done that, got the scars to prove it.

*“Oh, cut the crap,”* a tiny, annoying voice whispers in my head. *“You loved that kiss. Admit it. That’s exactly how you wanted him to kiss you. All hot and heavy.”*

I shake my head hard to clear the thought. No. That’s messed up. I’m moving on, finding my own life, my own guy. Sure, my body reacted, but that’s just biology. Pure chemistry. It doesn’t mean a thing.

*“Keep telling yourself that,”* the voice snorts.

I’m not lying. Okay, maybe a little. But whatever. I’m not overanalyzing his weird-ass behavior or that random kiss.

I shove Declan to the back of my brain and stare at the coffee shop door. Five o'clock. Just clocked out. Piper and I agreed to grab a quick bite before heading home.

I’m chewing on a piece of cake when the door swings open. Piper walks in. She scans the crowded room, spots me, and jogs over with a massive grin.

"Ugh, I am so incredibly sorry I'm late! Last-minute meeting at the office," she pants, sliding into the chair opposite me.

I smile. "Don't sweat it. All good."

"Ooh, you already ordered," she says, pointing at my plate.

"Yeah, skipped lunch. I was starving."

She nods, flags down the server, and orders. Once the waiter leaves, she leans in, locking her eyes on mine.

"Okay, spill. How was the date with Adrian?"

Honestly, Piper has become my rock in such a short time. She’s the ride-or-die I never had in high school because I was too busy chasing Declan like a pathetic puppy.

"It was actually really good," I say, feeling a blush creep up my neck.

Her eyes light up. "Give me the details. Every single one."

"There's not much to tell, honestly. We hit this fancy dinner spot, and then we got ice cream. Honestly? The ice cream was the best part."

"Did he kiss you?" She’s practically vibrating with excitement.

I laugh. Of course. She didn't hear a word about the food or the ice cream. She just wants the juicy stuff.

"Nope," I say. "I could tell he wanted to, but he held back. Honestly, I don't even know if I'm bummed about it or glad."

"Wait, why?"

"Because part of me wanted to know what it feels like to be kissed by a guy who actually wants me. But the other part? Totally relieved. I don't think I'm ready yet."

Piper stops, just staring at me. I can practically see the gears grinding in her head.

"Are you telling me Declan never kissed you?" Her brow furrows.

"I mean, he did. But never like he meant it. Back in the day, I saw him kiss Aurora. It was intense. Like he was starving for her. Like he couldn't stop if he tried." I take a shaky breath. "He never, ever kissed me like that."

I look down at my hands. The shame is heavy. I’ve never told anyone this. I kept the ugly, broken parts of my marriage locked away, hiding how much he destroyed my self-esteem.

*“He kissed you like that on Friday,”* that annoying inner voice chimes in again.

A fluke. It means nothing. It doesn't wipe out years of feeling completely unwanted. It hurt so bad, thinking my own husband found me repulsive. Even when we had sex, he’d touch me, but he’d kiss me anywhere except my lips.

"You deserve a kiss that makes the world stop spinning," Piper says, grabbing my hand. Her grip is warm, steady.

I look up, letting out a breath I didn't know I was holding. No pity in her eyes. Just pure support. Exactly what I needed.

"So, besides that, everything was perfect?" she asks.

"Yeah. Oh, except I saw Declan and Aurora. They looked like they were on a date."

"Seriously?"

"Yep." I take a sip of my drink, trying to drown out the memory of how perfect they looked together. Aurora was right. They just fit. Everyone saw it, and now, finally, I see it too.

"Well, I hope he saw how gorgeous you looked and realized he threw away a total queen."

I laugh. God, Piper is great for my ego. Finally, someone who isn't obsessed with Aurora's looks. Someone who doesn't compare me to my sister or rub her perfection in my face.

"So that's it? Nothing else juicy?" she asks.

"Nope." I shake my head.

I want to tell her about Declan showing up at my place, but I bite my tongue. I trust Piper, but secrets have a way of slipping out. The last thing I need is Aurora finding out Declan came over after their date. And definitely not that he kissed me.

We move on, chatting easily, but I notice a shift. Something is eating at her.

"Okay, what's up?" I push my empty plate and glass away.

"Nothing," she says, looking everywhere but at me.

"Spill it, Piper."

She hesitates, clearly fighting with herself. Instantly, my stomach drops. I’m not going to like this.

"It's Corbin," she mumbles. "He's really, really sorry."

Ugh. I should have kept my mouth shut.

"We are not doing this," I say, my jaw tight.

Piper sighs. "Please, Lyra. It's killing him that you won't talk to him. He hates that he wasn't there for you when you were hurting. He's a mess."

"He's a mess?" My voice rises. "What about my years of being a mess? Do you have any idea what he said to me? What he did? He wants forgiveness, but he never forgave me for hurting his precious sister. He told me I was dead to him. Said he only had one sister. Do you know how much that burned? Or when he mocked me, saying Declan would never love me because Aurora was his everything and I was nothing?"

I'm trying to move on, but they keep dragging me back into the mud. His pain always comes first. Nobody cared when they broke me, but now I'm supposed to just forget it and play nice? No way.

I stand up, grab my purse, my mood completely ruined.

"If he can undo the damage he did, maybe we can talk. Until then, he doesn't exist to me."

Piper's eyes well up with tears, but I don't care. I turn and walk out. She calls my name, but I keep moving.

I flag down a cab and slide into the backseat just as she runs out of the coffee shop. I stare straight ahead as the driver hits the gas.

I blink back the tears. No more crying. No more feeling sorry for myself. That pathetic version of me is dead. I’m building a new life.

The cab pulls up to my place. I pay the driver and get out, relieved to be back in my safe zone.

I walk up to the porch, key in hand. Suddenly, a cold shiver runs down my spine. Someone is watching me.

I spin around, scanning the street. Everything looks totally normal.

A neighbor down the block is walking her dog. Cars are driving by. A couple of joggers pass under the streetlights.

I turn back to the door, unlock it, and step inside. But that creepy, heavy feeling? It doesn't leave. It feels like eyes are burning holes right through my back.
