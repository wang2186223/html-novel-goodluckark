Chapter 311

Aurora.

I sat at the kitchen island, choking on my breakfast. Literally. My throat locked up every time I tried to swallow. Total anxiety attack. I slammed my fork and knife onto the plate.

"Hey. You okay?" Mom asked.

"I don't know, Mom. Just... super freaked out." My voice wobbled. God, I sounded pathetic. What the hell was I doing? Was this therapy thing a massive mistake? Am I actually ready, or am I just dragging my feet? My brain buzzed with doubts while I stared at my plate, totally grossed out. I hadn't had a real appetite in months, but today? Today, food felt like poison.

Mom reached across the table, squeezing my hands and rubbing her thumbs over my knuckles. Her expression melted.

"I get it, sweetie. It's terrifying," she said, offering a weak, reassuring smile. "But you gotta do this. For yourself. You can't start over until you fix what's broken inside."

Sure, she was right. Didn't make it suck any less, though. Clara had bailed two days ago, but not before locking me into this appointment. I gave her my word I'd go. No backing out. I couldn't let her down, too. God knows I'd already screwed up every other relationship in my life.

"I'm just... scared, I guess," I mumbled, blinking back the hot tears stinging my eyes. "What if the therapist tells me what I already know? That I'm just a crappy person?"

Mom pressed a soft kiss to my knuckles. It helped. A little. "She's not there to judge you, Aurora. She won't. Her job is to help you put the pieces back together. But you have to let her."

Suddenly, I felt like a little kid again. The scared little girl who always ran to her mommy whenever the world got too loud. I loved my dad—damn, I missed him so much it ached—but Mom was always my rock.

"Yeah. You're right. Just pre-game jitters," I sighed, pulling her hand up to press against my cheek.

She smiled, but it didn't reach her eyes. The guilt was still eating her alive. I knew she was drowning in regret over how she'd treated Lyra. Honestly, we all were—Mom, Corbin, and me. Lyra had completely iced them out. No calls, no texts, nothing. She wanted zero part of their lives, and it was killing them. I had no clue how to fix it. Lyra had run straight into the arms of her real parents, leaving Mom and Corbin in the dust.

"You know... maybe I'm not the only one who should be sitting on a couch," I muttered quietly.

Mom cleared her throat, instantly yanking her hand back. "I have no idea what you're talking about, Aurora."

I let out a heavy sigh. Classic. She was stubborn as hell—guess the apple didn't fall far from the tree. She'd rather die than admit she needed professional help.

"Come on, Mom. You know exactly what I mean. You need to talk to someone. You're carrying all this heavy guilt and regret, and you need to let it go. You need this therapy session just as much as me."

Her mouth tightened into a hard, flat line. Silence. I dropped it. I'd nudge her again in a few days, but not today. Guilt and regret—they're like quicksand. They lock you in place, keeping you stuck in the past. The Sharp family was basically built on that shit. We all needed a serious detox.

Since the eggs and toast were a hard pass, I choked down a banana and chugged my coffee. I stood up, grabbing my keys.

"Alright, I'm heading out," I said, leaning down to plant a quick kiss on her cheek. "Catch you later."

"Okay, sweetie. Call me after."

I nodded and walked out the door.

The drive was a total blur. I stared at the road, but my brain was spinning out. I felt completely wrecked, yet totally numb at the same time. Just a toxic cocktail of guilt and regret on loop in my head. No happiness, no sadness. Just those two goddamn feelings. My heart was trashed, my life was a dumpster fire, and I had zero clue how to crawl out of this hole.

"Hey, I have an appointment. Under Audrey," I told the receptionist when I walked in.

She flashed me a massive, cheerful smile. I couldn't even fake one back.

"Got it. Have a seat. Aura's just wrapping up with someone," she said warmly, tapping on her keyboard.

I gave a quick nod and sank into one of the plush white leather chairs. I sat there, waiting, wondering if this was actually going to fix me, or if I was already too far gone for saving.
