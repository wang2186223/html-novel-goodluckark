Chapter 25

Just finished scrubbing the place down. My phone started buzzing. Honestly, cleaning is my therapy. It’s the only way to shut my brain up when everything goes to hell.

I’d already sent Felicity packing. I was back on my feet, totally fine on my own. She was great, sure, but I didn't need a babysitter anymore. I like my space.

I walked over and grabbed the screen. Piper. My thumb hovered over the decline button. I was still pissed, yeah, but I got it. You do crazy shit for the guy you love. Even trying to patch things up with his estranged sister.

"Hey," I mumbled, heading up the stairs.

"Oh god, Lyra, I am so sorry. I totally crossed the line. I promised I wouldn't bring up Corbin, and then I did." Her voice cracked. She sounded wrecked.

It threw me. She actually sounded miserable. I stood there, totally lost. People in my world don't do the whole "sincere apology" thing. Usually, they just screw you over and walk away.

"Piper, look—"

"No, seriously," she cut in. "You're right. We can't just expect you to play nice. He treated you like garbage for years. A little guilt on his end doesn't wipe the slate clean. I love him to death, I really do, but I can't ignore how awful he was to his own sister. You didn't deserve that. It's not fair to ask you to just get over it. I really value our friendship, Lyra. I don't want to blow it."

I let out a long breath. God, I was exhausted. Sure, forgetting would be way easier. But pain doesn't work that way. It sticks.

People say time heals everything. What a load of crap. Some scars never fade. You just learn to live around them. You numb the ache and keep moving.

"Look, Piper, I get it. I know it's tough," I said. "I want to be friends, too. But I'm not trying to get between you and Corbin. I really don't need him hating my guts any more than he already does."

"He doesn't hate—"

"Don't," I cut her off. "Just don't. He's made his feelings perfectly clear, and I dealt with that a long time ago."

Honestly, you get used to it. When your own family, your husband, and your in-laws all treat you like poison, you build up a tolerance. It hurt like hell at first, but I got over it.

She sighed. "I'm not letting this friendship go. I'll just keep you two completely separate."

Right. Like that ever works. Eventually, she'd get sick of playing referee. It's exhausting trying to balance two people who despise each other.

I was about to tell her that when my phone buzzed in my hand. Another call. A real smile finally hit my face. The heavy vibe from earlier evaporated.

"Hey, I gotta run, Piper. Asher's calling. Want to catch him before bedtime."

"Oh, totally. Go," she paused. "We're good, though? Promise, no more Corbin talk. Ever."

"Yeah. We're good. Don't sweat it." I meant it.

"Awesome. Go talk to your boy. Tell him I say hi. Night!"

"Night, Piper."

I clicked off and took a breath. I'd missed Asher's call, so I hit redial.

"Hello?"

My blood ran cold. That wasn't Asher. It was my mother.

I hadn't heard her voice since the airport. Out of everyone who stabbed me in the back, she cut the deepest. Moms are supposed to have your back. Mine treated me like trash. She just threw me away.

Now that I have Asher, I can't even wrap my head around it. I could never, ever do that to my kid.

"Lyra? You there?" her voice was shaky, almost fragile.

I kept my mouth shut. Not because I had nothing to say, but because I had too much. None of it was nice. Better to stay silent than scream things I couldn't take back.

"Please, sweetie. Just say something. I just need to hear you," she whispered.

Still nothing. My throat felt tight. Years ago—hell, even months ago—I would've begged for this. Now? Too little, too late.

"Okay. I know you're mad. I'll put Asher on. Just... I love you, Lyra."
