Chapter 319

Caspian dragged his feet inside, letting out a heavy breath. "Lysander."

The sheer weight in his voice killed Lysander’s smirk instantly. Caspian looked off tonight—quieter, like he was carrying a heavy load. "Hey, man. I owe you an apology."

Lysander blinked. Well, that was a curveball. He sat up, totally hooked. He didn't push, just waved toward the lounge. "Caffeine? Or tea?"

Caspian eyed the couch. Lysander flopped into an armchair, nodding at the seat opposite. "Staff's out cold, but I can whip up a brew." He grinned, a wicked spark in his eyes. "Just like I make it for Sera."

Caspian let out a miserable groan. "Seriously? You gotta rub it in?"

Lysander barked out a laugh that echoed off the walls. "Hell yeah, I do." He wore his smugness like a badge of honor. "Dude, I never beat you. Let me enjoy this."

Caspian just shook his head. He had zero comeback. Because Lysander was right. He’d won. While Caspian had been pushing Elara away under that damn curse, Lysander had been her rock through the worst of it. Caspian couldn't deny that, and honestly, he didn't want to.

"Yeah, yeah," Caspian muttered, sinking into the sofa. "You win."

Lysander’s grin widened. "Damn straight."

He slipped into the kitchen and returned a minute later with two steaming mugs. The smell of dark roast hit the air. He handed one to Caspian and kicked back in his chair.

They sat in silence for a bit. It wasn't weird. Just two guys bracing for a heavy talk, letting the quiet settle.

Finally, Caspian set his mug on the table. He took a breath and just spilled. Everything. He laid out his whole history with Elara—the day they met, the spark, the real feelings, and the absolute mess of misunderstandings that tore them apart. Then came the big one: the curse. He didn't sugarcoat it. He gave Lysander the raw, ugly truth of the spell.

An hour flew by. Lysander didn't interrupt once. His jaw had dropped early on, and the shock stayed plastered on his face, slowly turning into realization.

"Well, shit," Lysander muttered, rubbing his chin. "That explains a lot. I always knew you two had this weird vibe. The tension was crazy, but so was the hate." He leaned back, chuckling. "Seriously, one day you looked like you wanted to rip each other's clothes off, and the next, you wanted to rip each other's throats out."

Caspian actually cracked a smile. Spot on.

Lysander sobered up. "Look, for the record, I had no clue. Sera and I weren't close back then. It only started when Thane took over as Chief and made me his Deputy. That's when we teamed up."

Caspian nodded. It checked out. "I know. I believe you." No hesitation. He meant it.

The quiet returned, easy and relaxed. Then Caspian threw another wrench in the gears. "Thanks, man."

Lysander blinked. "Huh? For what?"

"For having her back." Caspian looked him dead in the eye. No jealousy, no bitterness. Just pure appreciation. "You stood by her when she was drowning. You shielded her. You had her back." His voice dropped. "And yeah, she loves you for it."

He didn't mean romance, and they both knew it. It was that ride-or-die family bond forged in the trenches. Caspian smiled. "You earned that."

Lysander actually choked up a bit. No jokes, no quips. The raw honesty hit him right in the chest. He cleared his throat, taking a sudden interest in his coffee.

But Caspian wasn't finished. "Also, I need a favor."

Lysander’s eyes narrowed instantly. "Aha!" He pointed a finger at Caspian. "There's the catch. I knew it."

Caspian laughed, breaking the tension. "Look, when Sera and I head back to the pack for good, the businesses here still need to run. I need you to train some people. Solid, reliable humans who can handle the day-to-day without us babysitting them. Keep the machine running."

Lysander nodded slowly. "Okay. Fair enough."

"That's not the real reason I'm here, though." Lysander’s guard went right back up, but Caspian plowed ahead. "I want you as my Assistant Chief Commander."

Silence slammed into the room. Lysander stared. And stared. Five seconds passed before he shook his head. "Wait, what? Did you just say Assistant Chief Commander?"

Caspian nodded.

Lysander frowned. "Dude, that's not even a thing."

"It is now," Caspian said, deadpan.

Lysander snorted, then burst out laughing. "You can't just make up jobs, man!"

"Watch me."

Caspian’s smug confidence just made Lysander laugh harder. Once he quieted down, Caspian got serious. "Look, I’ve already got a Deputy, and I'm keeping him. I can't make you Gamma either—you're above that. So I'm making a new slot. One that keeps you right in my inner circle."

The humor drained from Lysander’s face. He got it now. This wasn't about some fancy title. It was about trust. Caspian was giving him a permanent spot next to him and Elara. A place where he belonged, where his voice mattered, and where his loyalty was locked in.

It hit him right in the feels. "Wow. That's..." He trailed off, shaking his head with a soft chuckle. "Honestly? I like the pack way better than the city anyway."

He hadn't even realized he felt that way until he said it. Sure, the city had the cash, the clubs, and the convenience, but the pack had soul. It had family.

Then reality crashed the party. "Caspian." Lysander’s face hardened. "I'm not in your pack. Even if I sign up, there's red tape. Rules. Centuries of tradition don't just vanish. Putting me that close to the top? People are gonna talk. Big time." He sighed, running a hand through his hair. "Last thing I want is to stir up drama. Especially for Elara."
