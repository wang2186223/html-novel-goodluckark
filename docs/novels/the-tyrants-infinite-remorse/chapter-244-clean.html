Chapter 247

"Hey, Chief! Don't pin this on Elara. It was all the Co-Director's mess, I swear!" Brenna didn't wait for an invite. She scrambled over, hands flailing like she could physically patch up the disaster. She skidded to a halt a few feet off, words spilling out in a panicked rush. She knew damn well she was about to get dragged into the blast zone if she didn't talk fast.

Roric trailed her, nodding like a bobblehead. "Yeah, she's the reason we're in deep shit. She kept spamming our mind-links, forcing us to push back against Elara. Look, just let her get medical help, and she'll be right back to take her punishment with us."

Thane stayed on his knees, holding the dead weight of Briar. He stared at the duo like they’d just dumped a live grenade in his lap.

Roric let out a heavy sigh and sank onto the dirt, moving like a guy who'd been carrying the weight of the world. Thane, still clutching Briar, felt a dark, heavy weight settle in his chest. Everything was going to hell. He was already maxed out, and now this idiot—who was supposed to be serving a sentence—just plops down like he gets to choose when to follow orders. Thane parted his lips to snap at him.

"Honestly, they've got a point. Let 'em wait," Rhys cut in, smooth as silk, shutting Thane down before he could start. His voice was deadpan, laced with a subtle, icy sarcasm. "Chief Thane, we all know you're a fair guy." He paused, letting the bait dangle, then added, "And as your Deputy, I'm right behind you on that."

The crowd traded looks. Then, boom—one by one, they dropped onto the grass. Total silence. They sat there, stubborn and united, turning passive resistance into a loud-ass statement.

Thane had run packs for years. He'd handled tight spots, bad crowds, and brutal calls. But right now? He felt completely knotted up, more than ever before.

He scanned the sit-in, glanced down at the limp girl in his arms, and then locked eyes with Elara. She stood there, face like a stone wall, giving away zero. He couldn't shake the words she'd dropped earlier. "You said there was stuff about Briar," he muttered, his anger draining into a quiet, tense whisper. "What kind of stuff?"

Elara just tapped her lips with a finger. No rush. No words. She had Thane mapped out. Without hard, cold facts, he'd just make excuses for Briar. He'd sweep it under the rug, giving her the benefit of the doubt—something he'd never, not once, given Elara during their entire marriage.

But the receipts were coming. She was stacking them up, block by block. The hitmen angle—who hired them to hunt her in the outlands—was the silver bullet. "I'm not throwing accusations around without receipts," she said coolly. "When I have the full picture, you'll be the first to know."

She was over this drama. She yanked open Caspian’s passenger door, slid inside, and checked the rearview to make sure Asher was good in the back. Then she stared straight ahead.

"Director!" Willow panted, jogging up to the window, her breath hitching. "Director, please, can't you just forgive us?"

Elara turned her head. Her stare wasn't mean, but it sure as hell wasn't warm. It was the kind of look that stripped you bare. "Tell me," Elara said. "If your little scheme worked, or if Thane bought the lies you guys spun, would you be begging for mercy right now?"

Crickets. The question hung there, heavy and damning. They all got it. They were only sorry because they got caught. Apologizing after you lose isn't remorse—it's just PR. And Elara wasn't buying the act. The dead silence said it all, guilt written all over their faces.

Elara looked back at the windshield. "Hey, Thane," she called out, not even bothering to turn around. "Why didn't Briar's wolf jump in to save her just now?"

The question hit like a silent bomb. It rattled Thane deep down, leaving him totally speechless. Honestly? He had no clue. He'd never run with Briar. Since they weren't fated mates, he'd never had a reason to bring it up.

And during drills? Briar always dodged the shifting part. She had a million excuses, and he'd never connected the dots until now. Holy shit. He didn't even know what her wolf looked like.

"We're moving," Caspian barked. Rhys hopped into the back, no questions asked. Thane stood up, cradling Briar, and marched to the passenger side to dump her in. Caspian just watched him, deadpan. Then, cold as ice, he dropped the hammer: "Thane. My car, my rules. No chicks allowed."

Elara bit her lip to keep from laughing out loud. Uh, hello? Wasn't she a woman? Still, she loved that Caspian was playing dirty for her without her even asking.

"Dude, it's an emergency!" Thane snapped, losing his cool. "And you let Sera in—"

"The name's Elara," she cut in from the front seat, sharp as a razor.

Thane ground his teeth. "Fine. Elara. She's my ex. Why does she get a pass and not—"

"Because she's sitting on my billions," Caspian said. No smirk, no brag. Just straight facts. He stared Thane down, then added, "My job is keeping her breathing. 24/7. You know damn well why."

Thane knew. He'd even promised to help protect her. But he figured Caspian would bend the rules for a medical emergency. Guess not. He needed a Plan B, and Elara was about to make it way worse.

"Better beat us to the ER," Elara called out the window, her voice breezy and casual. "Otherwise, she might not make it." She paused, letting the words hang. "And hey, who knows? Maybe her wolf is just broken."
