Chapter 153

"Hell no," Caspian snapped. He shut her down instantly. No debate, no second chances. His voice was pure steel.

Elara blinked. The sheer speed of his rejection threw her off.

"Look, you’re my best friend’s ex-wife," he said, his voice dropping into that annoying, logical tone. "How exactly do I explain you hanging out at my place?"

He paused for a beat, then softened just a fraction. "Relax. Once I’m done training Lysander, the guy will be a machine. Whatever you need, he’ll fetch it."

Elara kept her mouth shut. She hated getting info secondhand, especially with the clock ticking, but arguing was pointless. He wasn't changing his mind.

"Fine," she muttered, swallowing her annoyance.

She hung up and turned to Lysander. He was already staring, trying to read her face.

"Caspian wants you tomorrow," she said flatly. "Guns, hand-to-hand, the whole deal. He’s training you."

Lysander let out a slow breath and nodded. "Honestly? I was already thinking about doing that." He looked tense, though.

Elara let out a tiny, fake smile. "I’m crashing. Just don't tell him when he sends the address."

Lysander froze. He stared at her like she’d lost her mind, caught between playing nice with Caspian and keeping her happy. He knew Caspian would lose his shit, but he couldn't say no to her either.

"Man, you are putting me in a horrible spot," he muttered. But he didn't say no.

She didn't push it. She grabbed her gear and headed out the door, leaving Lysander to stress over a mess he hadn't asked for but was stuck with anyway.

Once she hit the hospital, she locked in.

She and Julian worked like a well-oiled machine, patching up a patient with textbook precision. On the outside, she was totally in control.

Then her phone buzzed.

An unknown number flashed on the screen. She excused herself and stepped into the hallway. "Hello?"

"Sera. It’s Dominic. Good to hear you."

His voice sent a jolt straight to her brain. Instantly, she saw the hidden cameras, the secret rooms, the sick shit she couldn't unsee.

She kept her face blank.

"Thanks for the flowers," she said, forcing a sweet, polite tone. "But seriously, stop sending them."

He chuckled on the other end. "Only if you let me take you out. I’m still waiting on that RSVP."

Elara took a quiet breath. She’d already decided to go—it was part of the plan—she just hadn't told him yet.

"Sorry," she lied smoothly. "Work’s been crazy."

"Don't sweat it," Dominic said. "Just name the night. I’ll handle the rest."

He sounded so smooth, so charming. But all she saw was that massive mansion, the hidden lab, and the people locked up like lab rats.

"Next week," she said, trying to buy some breathing room.

A pause. "Too long," Dominic said, his voice losing a bit of its warmth. "How about three days?"

Her chest tightened. Three days was a death sentence. She needed more time to prep, but she couldn't sound suspicious.

"Make it five," she countered. "I’ve got surgeries lined up."

Another silence. Then, a low chuckle of approval. "I like a woman who takes her job seriously. It’s... hot."

Elara forced a dry smile. "Thanks. Wish I knew enough about you to return the compliment."

"Oh, you will," he purred. "Seven p.m., my place. It’s out in the sticks, so I’ll have a car pick you up."

"No," she said, a little too fast. She caught herself and softened her voice. "No need. Lysander’s driving me. Just text the address."

The line went quiet. "Lysander," Dominic repeated, his tone suddenly sharp. "Yeah, we’ll talk about him when you get here. Have a good day."

He hung up. Elara spun around and ran right into Julian. He was glaring at her, his eyes dark.

"Tell me that wasn't him," he growled.

She wanted to lie. It would be so much easier, so much safer. But she knew she’d need him eventually. Keeping him in the dark would only backfire.

"And what if it was?" she challenged, looking him dead in the eye. "Don’t you want to get those women and kids out of there?"

The words hit him like a punch. Julian flinched, his eyes darting away as he fought with himself. He let out a rough sigh. "We’re talking about this later," he snapped. "We’ve got another emergency."

She threw herself back into the grind. The hours blurred, but the dread in her gut never really went away.

By the time Lysander pulled up to get her, she was running on fumes.

She slumped in the passenger seat, eyes closed, running through every possible disaster scenario until the car finally braked.

She opened her eyes. A familiar ride was blocking their driveway.

Before they could even park, the door swung open. Thane scrambled out, looking completely wrecked, and stood right in front of the iron gate.

"Sera!" he yelled, his voice cracking with pure desperation. "Please... I need your help."
