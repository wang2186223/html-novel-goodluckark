Chapter 25

When you storm out of a pack, you don't look back. Valerie sure as hell didn't. She made her choice, and it turned her spine to steel. Her filter? Completely gone. Why play nice with people she’d never lay eyes on again?

"Savor the VIP treatment while you can," Valerie spat, her voice cold and sharp as a scalpel. "And seriously, pray we don't bump into each other in the city. Oh, and your kid? He’s done. There is no cure. Elara finished the job alone after we packed up and walked. If you knew the first thing about how shifting actually works, you’d get that."

She stopped to breathe, watching Briar turn ghost-white. God, it felt good. So she twisted the knife.

"Nobody saw the whole thing. Literally nobody. So if you want your boy to breathe, go crawl to Elara. Beg her to fix it. Because honestly? She said there was never a cure in the first place."

Briar lost it. Pure, ugly rage boiled over, and she threw a wild, desperate punch. Valerie saw it coming a mile away. She slipped the blow like a pro. Smooth. Effortless.

"That all you got?" Valerie smirked. "You’re just a fake-ass bitch."

Boom. Right in the ego. Briar shook, caught between screaming and crying. The docs in the room suddenly found the floorboards fascinating. Nobody wanted any part of this. This wasn't medicine anymore. It was a straight-up turf war.

Briar’s hand twitched. She wanted to mindlink Thane, like, right now. But she froze.

If people started asking questions, the whole house of cards would fall. They’d find out she didn't have the degrees. They’d see the corners she cut, the stuff she faked.

She watched Valerie walk out. The heavy thud of her boots sounded like a final goodbye. Two top doctors, gone. All because of Elara.

Briar clenched her fists so hard her nails drew blood. She ground her teeth, hatred bubbling in her throat.

*Just wait till Asher is okay.* Then she’d hunt Elara down and drag her back to reality.

Meanwhile, the medical team got back to work, the air thick enough to cut with a knife.

Over at the pack house, Thane spent days cleaning up the mess. Fixing the ranks, patching up the cracks left in Elara’s wake.

But one massive problem remained: they needed a new Deputy Chief.

Thane called up the wolves who’d moved to the city. Guys who had built cozy, rich lives far away from the pack's toxic drama. One by one, they shut him down.

No way they were trading peace for a high-stress, cutthroat gig. Pack life was brutal. One screw-up and you're trash.

In the city, things made sense. They were safe.

But a few days later, one of them actually showed up.

Single guy, no kids, loaded but not tied down. Rhys.

"Rhys," Thane said, staring. He couldn't tell if he was shocked or just incredibly relieved. "You actually came?"

Rhys gave a respectful nod. "Yeah, Commander. I got to thinking. If we all just bail... what happens to the pack? We need this place. Just as much as it needs us."

Thane felt a massive weight slide off his shoulders.

"Thanks, man," Thane said, genuinely grateful. "It's a huge sacrifice. I won't forget this."

Rhys didn't miss a beat. "Right. And honestly, we should start charging those rich city wolves when they show up for the New Moon festivals. They want the perks, but they don't want to do the work."

Thane grinned. "I like how your brain works. Let's get you settled first, then we'll talk business. Follow me."

He took Rhys to Lysander’s old room. The place was spotless. Not a single trace of the guy who used to live there. Huge, fancy, and totally dead.

"Thanks, Commander," Rhys said. "Gonna hit the shower first."

"Cool. I'll get the maids to whip up some food. Then I'll introduce you to the co-Managing Director."

Rhys stopped. "Co-Managing Director? Why 'co'? If Elara's gone, shouldn't someone just take over?"

The question hit a nerve. Thane wanted Briar in that seat. That was always the plan. But while he was gone, she'd botched everything and got herself hurt.

It left a bad taste in his mouth. "Briar's still training," Thane said, keeping his voice flat. "She'll get the title when she's ready."

Rhys smiled, totally polite. "Got it, Commander."

But something about the guy felt... off. Just a tiny vibe. Thane couldn't put his finger on it, but his gut was screaming. He'd have to keep an eye on him.

Rhys used to be one of their best fighters. Then he went to the city, got a degree, and ran a massive company. Now he just gives all that up to be a deputy?

Thane was glad, sure, but he wasn't stupid.

That night, Rhys joined them for dinner. He hit it off with Asher immediately, joking around and being super warm. But Briar? That was a trainwreck.

The tension was thick. Briar cleared her throat, forcing a fake smile. "So, Rhys. You used to be a big fan of Elara, right? You bummed she's not here?"

The room went dead silent. Thane stared at Rhys.

Rhys just smiled, totally chill. "Hey, everyone loved Elara. I mean, who wouldn't?"

"So you must hate me now because—"

"Not at all," Rhys cut her off smoothly. "You're the boss's favorite girl."

Oof. That landed wrong. Briar's fake smile froze. He didn't say she was good for the pack. He just said Thane liked her. It felt cheap.

A few days later, Thane was showing Rhys the ropes of the pack's finances when his phone buzzed. The caller ID made his stomach drop.

"Sir," Roric panted, sounding panicked. "Our stocks are tanking. I tried everything to stop the bleeding, but nothing's working. We just slid to number six on Forbes."

Thane froze. "What?" he roared, his eyes flashing a dangerous red. "How the hell did you let this happen?"
