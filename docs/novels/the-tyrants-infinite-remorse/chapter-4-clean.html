NovelVibe

Chapter 4

Caspian wasn't just some big shot running the Grimroot Consortium. The guy was a god. His name alone made Wall Street sweat. He was the baddest boss to ever come out of the East Coast, loaded enough to buy entire countries, and hot enough to make people stop and stare. One word from him, and billion-dollar companies folded. One look, and tough-guy Alphas backed down. Power just stuck to him.

But there was a catch. Everyone knew Caspian couldn't stand women. Rumor had it he was either gay or literally allergic to them. That’s why people whispered. The packs couldn't figure out why the country’s most eligible bachelor didn't have a mate, or why Grimroot still didn't have a first lady. See, when Caspian wanted something, he took it. No hesitation. So why was his bed empty?

Elara’s boot caught on a jagged stone. Her bum leg gave out, and she started to go down. She would’ve hit the dirt hard, but a heavy hand locked onto her arm and yanked her back up. Rough, but steady.

"Watch your step," Caspian muttered. Same old voice—cold, sharp, like a switchblade.

Elara didn't look at him for long. "Yeah. Thanks for the save," she spat, her voice dripping with sweet poison.

Then she ripped her arm away. She bolted down the hall, gone like a ghost before he could even blink. Caspian stared after her way longer than he should have, his jaw tight as her perfume faded from the air.

Elara didn't slow down. She ran straight to the pack hospital. If there were any answers left, they’d be in there. The place was dead quiet. Under the buzzing fluorescent lights, she tore through rooms, ripping open cabinets and grabbing files with shaking hands. Medical charts, birth records, death certificates—anything to prove how her baby girl had supposedly died.

Her chest was hammering, but she knew she was on her own. Then she spotted Lysander hovering by the door. "Hey, Lysander," she said quietly. "Aren't you scared your big boss will catch you?"

Lysander went stiff. He opened his mouth, choked on his words, and swallowed hard. "Ma'am... what are you looking for?"

Elara spun around. Ever since she’d walked into this clinic, people had treated her like the plague. Nurses looked away. Researchers ducked into offices. Doctors who used to kiss her feet now acted like she was invisible. She was the one who ran their lab, cured their sick, and even made the drug that fixed her own paralysis. Now? She was trash. But she didn't give a damn. If she found one lead on her baby, she was out of here.

"Lysander," she said, keeping her voice dangerously soft. "Talk to me. What happened to my daughter? How did she die?"

Lysander’s shoulders slumped. He stared at his shoes, looking miserable. "They told me to kill her, ma'am."

Elara froze. The papers slipped right through her fingers and hit the floor.

"But I couldn't do it," he choked out.

Elara’s head snapped up. A sudden, painful jolt of hope hit her chest. "So she’s alive?" she whispered. "Oh my god, she’s alive, right? Where is she? Tell me!"

Lysander shook his head, looking like he wanted to cry. "I don't know. That night, I saw some woman crossing the border. I panicked and dumped the kid on her before the guards caught me. I didn't even get her name." He balled his fists. "I killed a wild animal instead, burned it, and handed the ashes to Thane as proof."

Elara finally let out the breath she’d been holding. Relief hit her like a truck. Her knees shook, but her heart was racing with real hope. Her baby was alive.

"What did the woman look like? Describe her," she demanded.

Lysander shook his head again. "It was pitch black. I couldn't see a thing, I'm sorry. Look, I hated you back then. I wanted to do what I was told. But the baby started crying..." His voice cracked. "I just couldn't."

A single tear rolled down Elara’s cheek, but she was smiling. "Thank you," she whispered, her voice shaking. "Man, if I’d known this, I would’ve signed those divorce papers years ago." She wouldn't have waited for him to screw her over seven times.

She turned back to the shelves, grabbing folders left and right. Lysander frowned. "What are you doing?"

"They tried to murder my baby," she said, her voice turning to ice. "Why the hell should they keep my life's work?"

She marched over to the medicine cabinet, popping open vial after vial and dumping the priceless liquids right down the sink. Years of research, miracle cures—gone.

"Let Briar figure it out herself," Elara muttered. "If she even has a brain."

The last glass vial clinked against the drain. She’d wanted to leave quietly, but they’d ripped her heart out. As of right now, she was done with the Centenary Syndicate.

By the time she got back to the pack house, it was dark. Laughter drifted from Thane’s office—loud, mocking, irritating. Elara ignored it.

She went straight to her room, took a fast shower to wash the pack’s stink off her skin, and threw on some clean clothes. The only thing she packed was a stack of her own research papers—seven years of her life in a folder.

She was almost out the door when a voice stopped her. "So, you finally figured out you don't belong here, huh?" It was Briar.

Elara turned around slowly, a smirk tugging at her lips. "Well, look who’s finally showing her real face."

Briar stood there, looking smug, eyeing the room like she already owned it. "You actually thought being some beta's kid made you special?" she sneered. "Wrong. I'm the boss now. I got your man, I got your kid killed, and you spent years raising mine."

Elara smiled, but her eyes were dead. "It's your show now," she said softly. "Have fun with it."

Briar’s face fell. This wasn't the reaction she wanted. She wanted Elara begging on her knees, broken. If Elara wouldn't do it herself, Briar would make her.

Briar looked around, spotted a knife in the fruit basket, and grabbed it. She sliced it hard across her own wrist, dropped the blade, and let out a bloody-murder scream.

"Help!" Briar shrieked. "Thane! She's trying to kill me!"

Heavy footsteps pounded up the stairs. Thane burst in first, with Caspian right behind him. Elara hadn't realized Caspian was still around.

Thane scooped Briar up like she was made of glass, glaring at Elara with pure hatred. "You... how could you do this?"

Elara’s lips curved into a slow, nasty grin. "You really believe every single thing she says, don't you?" she said easily. "Fine."

She stepped forward, snatched the knife off the floor, and drove it straight into Briar’s other wrist.

"There," Elara said, her eyes burning cold. "Now I actually did it. What are you gonna do about it?"

Both Alphas froze. But it was the look in Elara’s eyes—zero regrets, zero fear—that absolutely terrified Briar. The shock knocked the tears right out of her.

For the first time, Briar realized something. Elara was completely out of their control. And nobody, not even Thane, could touch her now.
