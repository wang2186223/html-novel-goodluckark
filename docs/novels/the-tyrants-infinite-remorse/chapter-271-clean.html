Chapter 274

Caspian hit the passenger side just as Lysander vanished into the woods. A neat pile of clothes sat on the back seat. The car sat idling on the shoulder, hazard lights flashing orange. Caspian tapped the glass.

Elara didn't budge. She counted to five, then buzzed the window down an inch or two. Her shades stayed on. Her face was pure, unadulterated irritation.

"What do you want, Caspian." Flat. No question mark.

"We weren't done talking." He threw an arm over the roof, leaning in like he had all day. "You bailed."

She let out a long, slow hiss through her teeth. Thank God for the dark lenses. They hid the panic. "Look, we kissed. Big deal. It was a wolf thing, totally out of our hands. It's over. Done. Let it go." She flicked her wrist. "Shoo."

A flash of pain crossed his face, then vanished. His fingers dug into the car's metal roof. "Seriously? That's how you're writing it off? Just like that?"

"Yep."

"Right. So anytime a guy touches you, it's just 'blame the wolves, doesn't count, who's next'?"

Her head whipped around. A dead-slow, lethal turn. She opened her mouth to rip him a new one—

"I didn't even come here for the kiss," Caspian snapped, cutting her off. "That's not why I chased you down." He stood up straighter, locking eyes with her. "I came to say that whatever you do to Thane, I've got your back. Period. No second-guessing. I won't get in your way or mess things up. Promise."

Elara froze.

She slid her glasses down her nose, peering over the rims to read his face, looking for the lie, the trap, the catch. Nothing. He just stared back, totally open, completely serious.

"Well." She pushed the shades back up. "If that's it... thanks."

Caspian stared. He wanted to say a million things, but the words got stuck in his throat. The girl was a walking weather system—bipolar as hell. Last night she was sobbing in his arms, letting him hold her tight. Today, he was just some annoying speed bump in her path.

*Don't act shocked,* Gideon's voice echoed, dry and smug. *You acted like a total jackass when you first met her. What did you think would happen? And don't ask me what she's thinking. Her wolf shut the door. I'm locked out.*

Caspian blew out a breath.

"You still here?" Elara muttered, staring straight ahead.

He backed off but didn't head to his ride. Instead, he shoved his hands in his pockets and waited on the gravel, keeping an eye on the woods for Lysander. A couple of minutes ticked by.

Lysander emerged from the trees, shaking leaves out of his hair and rolling his shoulders like he'd just shed a ton of weight. He took his sweet time pulling his clothes on, then spotted Caspian still lingering by the asphalt. Caspian watched him, his face shifting—not angry, just processing a thought that hadn't quite clicked yet.

"Honestly, it's impressive," Caspian said, looking back at Elara. "How you keep all these guys spinning around you like planets." He paused, a nasty little smirk playing on his lips. "Oh, right. Forgot. You've got that special wolf. No wonder you're so damn cocky."

He spun on his heel and walked away before she could even open her mouth.

Elara sat frozen, jaw slack, her comeback dying in her throat.

Lysander slid into the driver's seat, shot her a quick look, and kept his mouth shut. Smart move.

They hit the gas, and Caspian's car trailed them, keeping a steady distance all the way back to the inn. Lysander pulled up to the curb and parked.

The lobby was dead. Just a few guests lingering by the door, the faint hum of a vacuum upstairs, and dusty sunlight streaming through the glass. Iris was behind the front desk, working on autopilot, her hands moving while her brain was miles away. She looked up when the door chimed, her face twisting in pure shock.

"Director?" She stood up straight. "You weren't supposed to be here until tomorrow."

"Change of plans," Elara said, already marching past the desk. "We're leaving. Now."

"Wait, I don't have my bags, I—"

"You won't need them where we're going." Elara flashed a quick, real smile. "Let's go."

Iris didn't push it. She ripped off her apron, tossed it on the counter, and fell in line.

Iris stepped outside and froze.

Lysander was leaning against the hood, arms crossed, tracking them. Instantly, Iris shrank. Her shoulders slumped, her chin dropped, and she stared hard at the pavement as they walked up.

"Deputy Chief Lysander," she mumbled, her voice barely a whisper. "Uh, hello."

Lysander tilted his head. He stood up, studying her with a mix of curiosity and a smirk. "How do you know my name?"

She didn't look up.

He dropped his arms and took a step closer. "Hey. Look at me. Let me see your face."
