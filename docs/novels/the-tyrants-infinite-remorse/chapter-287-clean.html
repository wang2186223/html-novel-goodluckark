NovelVibe

Chapter 290

Elara’s hands shook like crazy.

She’d figured, *whatever, just a quick peek at his phone.* No big deal, right? Wrong. The longer she stared, the more her chest squeezed. Like some invisible hand was choking the air right out of her.

Look, she knew Caspian. The guy’s entire life revolved around Aurelia. His pack, his business, every single move he made—all for that kid. So, yeah, she’d expected a camera roll full of Aurelia. Baby steps, birthday cakes, goofy faces. Standard dad stuff.

Nope. Not even close.

It was her.

The lock screen alone made her gasp. It was this old, faded birthday shot. A tiny Elara, laughing her head off, hair flying everywhere, eyes squeezed shut like she was having the absolute best day of her life.

She couldn't even place the year. Six? Seven? Who knows.

And then, the real panic set in.

There was this massive, gaping black hole where those years should’ve been.

She sat there, poking at the blank spot in her brain like a fresh bruise. Nothing. She’d always had this nagging feeling that her childhood was trash.

Nothing specific, just a bad vibe in the background. She only had random scraps. Sitting on the cold bathroom floor, untangling her own messy curls because nobody else would. Standing on her tiptoes to blow-dry her hair in the mirror because she couldn't reach the counter. Those parts stuck.

But her parents? Total blanks. Briar’s face from back then? Pretty much erased.

She did remember the blood donations, though. Doing it over and over for Briar. The sharp pinch of the needle, closing her eyes, counting to ten. That part was crystal clear.

But everything else? Blurry as hell. Like someone took a wet sponge and wiped out the first decade of her life.

She’d always told herself it was normal. Everyone forgets stuff, right? No biggie. But looking at Caspian’s screen, she wasn't buying it anymore.

She kept scrolling.

Tens of photos turned into hundreds. Then she hit the videos. Something in her chest snapped, and she tapped play.

Her own voice—young, loud, totally happy—blared out of the speaker.

"Caspian! Bring me a moon pendant from the city. Oh, and a matching bracelet, too!"

There she was on screen. Wild brown curls bouncing all over the place, riding on Caspian’s back like he was a playground horse.

He had his arms hooked under her knees, wearing this massive, goofy grin she’d never seen on him. Not once. He carried her so easily, like they did this every single day. Like she belonged right there.

Then Caspian spoke. His voice was younger, lighter, totally different from the cold, guarded guy he was now.

"I'll get you whatever you want, darling. Just wait for me."

*Darling.*

He’d called her *darling*.

Elara hit replay. Once. Twice.

In the clip, Caspian looked like a teenager. Maybe sixteen or seventeen. His hair was shaggy, falling past his ears. She squinted.

Man, he looked just like Thane. Same jawline, same hair, same vibe. If she hadn't heard her kid-self scream his name, she would’ve sworn it was Thane.

She kept going.

The next video started up.

"Caspian... wait, no, V sounds cooler," her younger self said, her voice going all shy and serious. "When we grow up... are we gonna be mates?"

Elara stopped breathing.

On the screen, Caspian threw his head back and laughed. A real, loud, happy laugh.

"The mate bond is dead, kiddo," he said, still smiling. "But I love you, Sera. Let's grow up first, and we'll see what the Eternal Spirit says."

Little Elara went wild. She started jumping up and down, waving her arms.

"Caspian and Sera! Nobody else!"

He caught her mid-air, his face softening up completely.

"Yeah, darling. Nobody else."

Then he flipped her upside down. Her kid laughter, high and totally free, echoed through the woods as her hair swept the grass. She was screaming with joy, totally sure he wouldn't drop her.

Elara’s throat went tight.

She slammed the phone face-down on her lap. She pressed her heels into her eyes, trying to stop the tears.

That little girl had zero walls. She trusted Caspian with her life. The kind of deep, solid trust you only get from years of being together. And that girl was her.

Another video popped up. She was crouching by a lake, holding a jar of water. "V, look! I caught two goldfish. Let’s name them V and Sera," she giggled. Caspian smiled right next to her. "Alright, keep our goldfish safe till I get back, okay?"

"Yep, this is us," Elara agreed, hugging the jar tight.

And she remembered absolutely none of it. Now, she wondered where the hell that goldfish jar even went.

She picked the phone back up. Stopping hurt worse than watching. More photos. More videos. Each one warmer than the last, twisting her stomach into a knot of confusion and heat.

Every time she tried to force a memory, her brain fought back. A dull throb started pounding behind her eyes.

She’d spent years feeling weird around Caspian. She’d never got why. She’d blamed it on him being a cold, distant jerk. Told herself it was his fault.

But it wasn't just him, was it?

She dropped the phone on the mattress and spoke before she could think.

"Caspian."

Not loud. Just his name. Like her voice already knew he was right outside.

The door swung open instantly. He leaned against the frame, arms crossed, watching her. His face was totally blank, completely calm.

"Do you remember now?"

She shook her head slowly. "No." Her voice sounded tiny. "Caspian, what the hell is this? Why can't I remember any of it?"

Something flickered in his eyes. His brows pinched together as he thought about it, looking just as lost as she felt.

Whatever he’d expected—maybe that showing her the phone would magically fix her brain—it clearly hadn't worked.

He stood up straight.

"I'm calling my parents back at the pack," he said, his voice dropping to a quiet, careful tone. "If science can't explain this, our shaman is the only one who can."
