NovelVibe

Chapter 8

Lysander leaned against the living room wall, blending into the shadows. Nobody noticed him. Sure, he’d grabbed dinner with Thane’s folks a bunch of times—stiff, polite, country-club stuff. But this? This was a whole different ballgame.

They stared at Elara like she was made of glass. It wasn't the look you gave an ex-daughter-in-law. No, they were holding onto her like a lifeline. No pack bullshit, no pride. Just raw, desperate love.

A knot tightened in Lysander’s chest. He looked at Elara. Her face had this soft, genuine glow. He’d never seen that back when she was running the Centenary Syndicate. Back then, she was all business—stiff, guarded, always playing the part. But in this cozy, normal house? She actually looked human.

"Look, Mom... Dad, I'm sorry," Elara said. Her voice didn't shake, even though she saw how exhausted they looked. She had to be straight with them. "But I’ve gotta look out for number one now. I need to build my own life. I’ll crash here until the divorce papers are signed, but after that? I’m done living in the Syndicate’s shadow."

She didn't mean to hurt them, but the words still hit like a punch. The old couple flinched. Eleanor looked away first, rubbing her eyes as a tear slipped out.

Eleanor had fought everyone—tradition, the pack, you name it—just to get Elara and her son together. She’d been a fool, thinking Thane would eventually come around. She thought if they lived together, he’d learn to love her. Instead, he blew the whole thing up. When they were kids, Thane didn't complain about Elara being around, and everyone misread it. Eleanor took his silence for love. What a joke. The truth was staring her right in the face now.

Thane didn't give a shit about Elara. Actually, it was worse—he hated her. He hated her enough to burn every bridge, even enough to trash their own kid just to get his freedom. You can't ignore that kind of sick cruelty. Eleanor had to face the music, no matter how much it hurt.

"I’ve loved you since day one," Eleanor choked out, her voice cracking. "I still remember when your parents brought you home from the hospital. You were just this tiny baby next door. And then, God, you were this little girl, always barging into Thane’s room, telling everyone who’d listen that you guys were gonna get married."

She let out a weak, sad laugh, but it died instantly. "I really thought he’d soften up. I had no clue that the whole time, he’d knocked up your old nanny’s kid. Or that he’d actually... God, that he’d actually swap the babies."

She gasped for air. "When Asher’s teacher called me, I wanted to die. I knew you’d pack your bags the second you found out. And I was just... I was too damn ashamed, too much of a coward to call you myself."

Elara took a slow, deep breath to steady her feet. Losing her baby still felt like an open wound, but she’d learned to live with the pain. Her little girl was out there, alive. That was the one thing keeping her upright. She just prayed—every single day—that whoever had her baby was loving her to pieces. That they were treating her like the little princess she was.

"I'm over the pain," Elara said softly. "I’m going to rebuild. I want the career I actually wanted, and some peace and quiet in the city. I’m never coming back here."

That killed the last bit of hope in the room, but Eleanor squared her shoulders, fighting back the tears.

"Then take my savings," Eleanor demanded. "All of it. Call it an apology."

"No way," Elara shot back. No hesitation. If she made it, she was doing it on her own terms. No handouts, no pity money. People always used "favors" to lock you down, turning gifts into chains. She wasn't going to owe her future to anyone, even people she loved. "I don't need your cash, Mom. I'm good."

She kept her voice level, but she was holding back. The truth was, she was a genius hacker, a cyber prodigy. Only a few high-level firms knew she existed because she kept her work in the dark. For years, she’d turned down massive, high-paying gigs just to play the dutiful pack wife and study medicine. Her brain was her secret weapon. Even if she was flat broke, she’d be fine.

Eleanor stared at her, then gave a slow nod. "Fine. Take it as a loan then. An investment in your new business."

It was a classic guilt-trip wrapped in a bow, and Elara saw right through it.

"Then we put it on paper," Elara said. "A real contract. Lysander and Dad can witness it."

The old folks just stared, totally speechless. She wasn't giving them an out. That night, they scribbled out the paperwork and signed their names, the air thick with tension, before everyone finally turned in.

Elara stopped at the bottom of the stairs, taking one last look around the place. She could almost hear the old laughter echoing in the halls. There were good memories here, sure, but every single one of them was poisoned by Thane. She turned her back on it.

"Deputy Chief Lysander, your room’s all set," the maid whispered.

Lysander nodded, muttered a quick "night" to Elara, and followed her down the hall.

Nobody got much sleep anyway. A few hours later, a brutal pounding on the front door shook the whole house. The wood practically rattled in its frame. Thane was outside, and he was absolutely pissed.
