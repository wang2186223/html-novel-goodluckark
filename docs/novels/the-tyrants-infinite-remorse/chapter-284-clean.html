Chapter 287

"Sera—" Lysander started.

Elara cut him off with a sharp shake of her head. No. Not tonight. She wasn't in the mood for excuses. That instant spark she’d felt with Aurelia—the way their eyes locked—was the only thing keeping her sane right now. You couldn't just explain that away.

"Look, some things are just facts," she muttered, keeping her voice low but dead serious. "Eyes, hair. It’s genetic. You can't fake DNA."

Lysander’s shoulders slumped. The tension drained right out of him.

"Yeah. I actually told Thane to check her out while you were passed out," he sighed, rubbing the back of his neck like the memory still bugged him. "But he chickened out. Couldn't bring himself to do it. Honestly, she had this crazy thick head of hair. Just like his. I mean, she was a tiny newborn, but man, she was a hairy little thing."

A tiny smirk tugged at his mouth. "But those eyes? Pure Sera. Maybe even prettier, if you can believe that."

Elara’s chest went tight. She stopped breathing. "Caspian," she whispered, the realization hitting her like a physical blow. "He has a kid." Goosebumps broke out all over her arms.

Lysander slammed on the brakes, pulling the car onto the shoulder. "Whoa, hold on. The five-year-old? You’re telling me she’s Caspian’s kid?"

Elara nodded, her throat dry. "She calls me mom. Said she saw me in some dream."

Lysander stared at her, totally speechless. His jaw worked as he tried to process the whole mess. "Jesus," he muttered, dragging a hand over his face.

"I knew it. Damn, I knew something was off the second I saw you two together. I just couldn't put my finger on it." He leaned in, his eyes drilling into hers. "Sera, are you a hundred percent sure about that night? What if Thane was right? What if you got the guy wrong?"

Elara clenched her jaw. She’d run that loop in her head a million times. "Trust me, I wanted to believe that," she said, choosing her words carefully. "But Iris confirmed the whole thing. And Caspian? He wasn't even at the moon festival. I didn't see him after I went up that hill."

She trailed off, looking out the window. The memory hit her hard, a heavy weight pressing down on her chest. She swallowed the lump in her throat, refusing to break down in front of him.

Lysander let the silence hang for a second. He didn't push. "Hey, it's okay," he said, his voice softening. "Love makes us do stupid things. We've all been there."

He gave her a warm, zero-judgment look. "But that's in the past. Focus on what's right here. Go check for the birthmark. Or hell, just run a DNA test and get some real answers."

The knot in Elara's chest loosened a bit. She nodded. "Caspian invited me over. Day after tomorrow. I want to hang out with Aurelia. Really get to know her."

Lysander grinned, a genuine, eye-crinkling smile. "Well, look at that. He's letting you in."

He leaned back. "Think about it, Sera. The first day I brought you to training, he shut us down. Changed the whole venue just to keep us away. I thought he was just being a dick."

He snapped his fingers, pieces falling into place. "But damn, it makes sense now. He was shielding her. He didn't want you near his place until he figured out his next move."

Elara chewed on that. The more she thought about it, the messier it got. No clean answers, just a giant, tangled knot.

"But if she really is yours," Lysander said, his tone turning serious, "where does that leave him?"

That question hit a nerve. A dark, scary place she wasn't ready to look. She felt completely helpless.

"I don't know," she whispered. "I really don't. But I can't think about that yet. Not until I'm sure." She looked at him, raw and vulnerable. "Lysander, that little girl... she's just... perfect. Like, out of this world."

Lysander just nodded, letting it slide. He merged back onto the road.

The next morning hit her like a freight train before she even got out of bed.

By sunrise, she was already drowning. One of those days that just grabs you by the throat and doesn't ask permission.

The hospital didn't give a shit about her existential crisis or Aurelia's eyes. It just kept throwing patients at her. One OR after another, until the hours melted into a blur.

She ran on pure autopilot, cold coffee, and that hyper-focus you get when your hands are inside someone's chest and the rest of the world just vanishes.

Four back-to-back surgeries. The last one dragged on forever. By the end, her legs were jelly, her neck was locked up, and her eyes felt like they were full of sand.

She finally walked out, still in her scrubs, hair a total mess, smelling like antiseptic. Then her pocket buzzed. She didn't even need to look at the screen.

Caspian.

She pushed through the exit, and the cool night air hit her face. God, she needed that.

The parking lot was dead. Just a few cars sitting under the harsh orange glow of the streetlights. She spotted his ride immediately.

He was leaning against the driver's side, arms crossed, watching her approach. His eyes scanned her face, calculating, keeping his thoughts to himself.

"You look like hell." No "hey," no small talk. Just straight to the point. Classic Caspian.

Elara let out a dry laugh. He wasn't wrong. "Gee, thanks."

A tiny twitch at the corner of his mouth. Almost a smile. "You got another shift?"

She shook her head and grabbed the passenger door handle before he could move. "Nope. Julian's rule. I pull a night like this, I get tomorrow off."

She sank into the leather seat and let her head drop back. Every muscle in her body went limp.

Caspian got in and shut the door. The cabin was warm, dark, smelling of cedar and his clean, masculine scent.

Without saying a word, he leaned over. He didn't touch her, but his presence was heavy, close, and totally effortless. He grabbed her seatbelt, pulled it across her chest, and clicked it into the buckle. She watched him out of the corner of her eye, her breath hitching slightly.

He was already looking straight ahead, hands on the wheel like it was nothing.

"You're staying at my place tonight," he said. Casual. Like he was just suggesting a shortcut.
