Chapter 262

"Elara."

Thane called out from the landing. He leaned against the railing, totally chill, like he’d been eavesdropping the whole damn time. "Hope you make it to the moon festival. Next full moon. We're throwing it right here."

Elara looked up. Her face was a blank wall. "Yeah, good luck with that."

Thane cocked his head. "Sounds like you're skipping."

"Wow. You're actually smarter than you look."

Thane’s eyes flared hot. His jaw clenched, but he forced it down. Caspian was standing three feet away, dead silent, which had a way of killing any vibe.

"The bond ritual needs every single wolf," Thane said, keeping his voice flat. "We're calling in packs worldwide. Everyone has to show."

"Got it," Elara said. She didn't even look up. Zero shits given.

"Who knows, you might find your fated mate there." Thane took two steps down, aiming right for the throat. "Probably won't be a Chief Commander, but—"

"Shut it." Caspian’s voice dropped like a lead weight.

Thane froze on the stairs.

Rhys cleared his throat, forcing his grin back on. He turned to Elara, trying to save the mood. "Okay, screw the festival talk. Sera—hug me. Come on." He threw his arms wide, locking eyes with Caspian over her shoulder. Pure provocation. "For the road, babe."

The room went sub-zero the second Elara stepped up to hug him back.

Caspian’s eyes slid to Rhys. Dark. Lethal. The kind of look that promises murder without making a sound. He didn't blink. Everyone in the room got the message instantly.

Then, he moved. Or rather, nobody saw him move.

One second Caspian was five feet behind her, the next he was right there, wrapping an arm under her ribs and hoisting her off the floor like a sack of potatoes.

It happened in a blink. Rhys stood there, eyes wide, wondering if he’d missed a frame. He seriously started wondering if Caspian had some vampire in his family tree.

"Caspian—" Elara shrieked. Her legs kicked the air, totally useless. Her bag thrashed against her hip as she squirmed. "Put me down! Seriously, stop!"

"Car's this way," Caspian said, his voice totally casual. He marched toward the exit, Elara tucked under his arm like a rolled-up rug. He didn't even look back, just threw a single finger over his shoulder at Rhys.

The middle one.

Rhys bit his lip to keep from laughing.

"I didn't say goodbye!" Elara yelled, her voice echoing in the hallway as the front door swung open. Her heels chopped the air. "I owe him a hug, Caspian! He was nice to me!"

"You want a hug that bad?" Caspian stepped into the chilly night without slowing down. His voice dropped to a low, gravelly rumble. "Use me. I'm right here. I won't complain."

Elara stopped kicking.

Like, completely froze.

Her whole body went stiff, her brain short-circuiting. The only sound for three solid seconds was the gravel crunching under Caspian’s boots as he carried her to the car. He popped her into the passenger seat, handling her with a weird, gentle touch that totally clashed with his words.

He slammed the door, acting like nothing happened, and walked around the hood. He checked the spot where the dent used to be. Thane’s mechanic had fixed it fast. Real fast.

Back at the house, Rhys stood in the doorway, watching the taillights fade into the trees. He wore a complicated smirk—not happy, not sad, just somewhere in the middle.

Behind him, Thane stayed quiet.

Out on the lawn, the five wolves on punishment duty slowed to a crawl the second Caspian carried Elara out. Now they just stopped, huddling near the track, staring at the gate.

The rumors about Chief Commander Caspian carrying Elara through the rain had been buzzing all night. Most people thought it was BS.

Nobody thought it was BS now.

They exchanged looks, and with the silent agreement of people who realized the drama was over, they just plopped down in the wet grass.

"The punishment goes until tomorrow," Thane’s voice barked across the yard, sharp enough to cut glass. "All of it. Rhys, watch them."

Briar’s head snapped up. She wasn't the pack princess anymore, not after she got everyone's pay docked. Her face was ghost-pale, held together by pure spite. She was panting, wet hair plastered to her face, staring at Thane, begging for a look, a word, anything.

But he just turned and walked inside.

Rhys stepped out, rolling up his sleeves. "You heard the man. Up."

The road back to the outlands was dark, empty, and quiet. The wet asphalt mirrored the headlights in long, blurry streaks. The storm had cleared the air, leaving a sharp, biting cold that clung to the back of your neck.

Inside the car, it was warm and dead silent.

Elara sat with her bag on her lap, staring out the window at the passing trees. She replayed his words once, told herself she wouldn't think about it, and then spent the entire drive doing exactly that.

The second they pulled into the restaurant parking lot, she had her hand on the door handle. She hopped out before the engine even died, letting the cold air hit her face like a slap. But then, a creepy vibe filled the air, making her skin crawl...
