Chapter 59

Briar always figured Rhys had cash. Like, millions. Big money, sure, but nothing Thane couldn't flex on if he wanted to dick-measure. But billions? Jesus. That was a whole different league. Fifty billion. He’d just tossed that number out like loose change. Her brain did a hard reset right then and there.

She’d spent years learning how to ride the coattails of powerful guys. She played her part with Thane perfectly. Out in public, she was the gorgeous, classy trophy on his arm. At dinners, she worked the room, giggled at stupid jokes, and kept the money guys happy. But let's be real. Deep down, she knew the truth. She wasn't a queen. She was just a glorified, high-end escort orbiting his little throne.

But Rhys? He didn't play those cheap games. He didn't need to show off. The guy talked about massive wealth the way normal people talk about rain. It was creepy how chill he was. He smirked, just a little, looking pretty damn pleased with himself.

"Look, I'm not trying to brag or anything," he said, his voice smooth as hell. "But if our big boss keeps screwing up, I’m gonna end up richer than him. Don't worry, though. I'm not trying to steal his crown. Those titles are hereditary, right? Well... unless the whole family gets wiped out."

That last line hung there, heavy and cold. Briar’s eyes narrowed, a sharp, greedy spark flashing in her head before she slapped on her signature mysterious smile. They were testing each other. They both knew real power wasn't about a fancy title—it was about cash, leverage, and timing.

Rhys saw her look but didn't blink. He just shrugged, totally relaxed. "Anyway, let's go check on Asher," he said, instantly killing the vibe. "If he looks like crap, we'll just dump him in the hospital."

They headed back to the house, but Briar’s head was spinning. Rhys’s words kept looping in her brain. Thane was losing his grip on the money, and she knew it. Elara was bleeding him dry, piece by piece, no matter how much Thane tried to act tough. A guy with no cash has zero leverage. And in her world, a guy with no leverage is useless. But Rhys? Rhys was solid. He was climbing.

Asher’s room was hot and stuffy. The kid was passed out on the bed, face red, sweaty hair stuck to his forehead. Rhys walked right over, grabbed a wet cloth, and started wiping the kid's face. He actually looked like he cared, which was weird.

"He's burning up," Rhys said, frowning.

"Yeah, well," Briar lied smoothly, using her rehearsed pitch. "That means the serum is kicking in. He'll be fine."

Rhys didn't buy it. He remembered what Elara had warned him about. He stood up slowly, all his casual charm vanishing.

"You sure about that?" he asked, staring her down. "Honestly, I think that serum is garbage."

Briar flinched. Just for a microsecond, but he saw it. She braced her shoulders and forced another smile. "I made it myself. I'll fix it."

Rhys crossed his arms, locking eyes with her. "Briar, cut the crap. Charles made that stuff. Look, take the credit if you want. I just want to know what your game is."

The room turned ice-cold. Briar felt the walls closing in, her heart hammering against her ribs. She glanced at the door, then stepped right up to him. "Come with me," she whispered. "Let's go to Thane’s office."

The office was empty. No maids, no eavesdroppers. Safe. She shut the door, turned around, and glared. "Who told you I didn't make it?" she demanded, keeping her voice low but sharp.

Rhys didn't back down. He looked her up and down, taking his sweet time. "Come on. It’s obvious," he said. "You spend all day doing your makeup and hair. You don't train, you don't do paperwork. Sure, you love managing the money, and hey, that's fine. That's your job."

Briar’s fake smile faltered. For the first time, she felt totally trapped. If Rhys ratted her out to Thane, she was done. Her reputation, her control—gone. And she couldn't even threaten him. He was too rich, too powerful.

"I do work!" she snapped, trying to sound defensive. "I multitask. I work on the serum at night when everyone's asleep. It's quiet. I can think."

Rhys tilted his head, almost impressed by how fast she lied. He stepped closer, his voice dropping to a low, intimate murmur. "I don't get why you're lying to me, Briar. Seriously. I'm the best friend you've got in this place. If you won't trust me, I can't help you."
