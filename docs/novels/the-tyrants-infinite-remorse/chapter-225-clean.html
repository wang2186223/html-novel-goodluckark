Chapter 227

Elara had her hands full with Lysander. The guy was spiraling hard, and she’d spent the entire drive playing therapist, dragging him back from a total meltdown. She was so locked into the call that the outside world just vanished. The dark highway, the trees whipping past—all of it was just background noise she wasn't tracking.

Then she hung up, turned her head, and—holy hell. A massive, completely naked Chief Commander was sitting right there in the driver’s seat. The dim glow of the dashboard was the only thing keeping the cabin from pitch black. Thank god for small favors, but still. A naked guy. Right next to her. Her nervous system was short-circuiting, and she had absolutely no clue how to handle it.

"Funny thing," Caspian said. He kept his eyes on the road, totally chill, like sitting butt-naked next to a woman was just another Tuesday. "You completely missed the rogue attack."

Elara whipped her head toward the window. Nothing. Just dark asphalt and empty woods. The fight was already done. "A rogue attack?" Her voice went flat. "And nobody thought to mention this to me?"

Caspian’s jaw clenched, then he let out a dry chuckle. "Well, you were kind of occupied. But Thane was on it, so we took care of business." He paused, letting the engine hum. "We had to shift. No spare clothes in the trunk, so, yeah. You’re just gonna have to deal with the view for a bit."

Elara glued her eyes back to the passenger window. "Find a store," she muttered. "Gas station, strip mall, whatever. Just pull over. I’ll go in."

"Thanks," he said. No sarcasm this time. He actually meant it.

They drove in silence. Not the awkward kind, just two people staying on their own sides of the console, respecting the boundary. Then, headlights caught a cheap clothing store up ahead. Caspian tapped the brakes and pulled onto the gravel.

Elara grabbed the door handle.

"Hey," Caspian’s voice came from the dark, quiet but heavy. "Be careful out there."

She didn’t look back. "Yeah. Sure."

The store was a total dump. Cramped aisles, fluorescent lights buzzing, smelling like cheap polyester and plastic. She didn’t waste time. She marched in, grabbed shirts, pants, and boxers off the racks, and piled them over her arm. She was back at the car in under five minutes flat.

She tossed the pile to Caspian. He caught them, ran a thumb along the waistband of the boxers, and paused. His eyes narrowed, studying the fabric, then he looked up. "You didn’t ask for my size. But these are perfect. Even the underwear."

A sudden, hot flush crept up Elara’s neck. Damn it. She stared out the window, trying to keep her voice level. "Don't flatter yourself. I used to buy clothes for Thane. He never wore them, so I ended up throwing them all in the trash. Stopped buying after that." She cleared her throat. "I grabbed extras in case you have to shift again."

The silence in the car stretched out, heavy and thick. Caspian’s smug look vanished. He sat there, totally still, letting her words sink in. He’d heard the rumors, sure. He knew she and Thane had a messy, broken history. But hearing it laid out like that—wrapped in something as stupidly normal as knowing a guy's clothing size—made the reality of it hit him like a punch to the gut. No anger in her voice. Just cold, hard facts.

"I'm sorry," he said. Just like that. Simple. No strings attached.

"How much do I owe you?" he asked, reaching back to adjust his seat. He started pulling on the clothes, fast and easy, completely unbothered by his own skin. Elara kept her eyes locked on the dark road outside.

"Nothing. Forget about it."

He didn't push. He knew when a door was shut in his face. "Thanks," he murmured, softer this time.

She gave a tight nod.

"Okay, you can look now. I'm decent."

Elara let out a breath she didn't realize she was holding. She turned, her eyes scanning him, and her brain betraying her for a split second. The clothes fit him perfectly. Like they were custom-made for him—which, in a weird, twisted way, they basically were.

"Looks fine," she said, keeping her tone deadpan. She brushed it off and got straight to the point. "So. What did you want to talk about?"

A tiny smirk tugged at the corner of his mouth. "A lot of things. But let's get some food first."

Elara didn't argue. Her stomach was practically screaming anyway.

A few miles down, Caspian swung the car into the gravel lot of a diner sitting on the edge of town. Warm, yellow light spilled out of the windows. The place was half-empty, but the second they stepped inside, the atmosphere shifted.

It wasn't a loud reaction. No gasps or drama. Just a subtle, synchronized ripple through the crowd. Heads bowed, just a fraction, like an automatic reflex they couldn't control. It was quick, but to anyone paying attention, it was loud and clear.

Elara caught it instantly. She scanned the room, watching the subtle shifts in the crowd, then turned her sharp gaze back to Caspian. Her brows knit together. "You're not their Chief Commander," she whispered. "So why the hell are they bowing to you?"
