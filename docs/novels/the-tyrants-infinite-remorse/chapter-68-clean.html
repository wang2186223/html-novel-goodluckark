Chapter 68

Weeks flew by, and Rhys played his hand perfectly. No feelings, just pure tactics. He kept Briar close, making things look cozy and safe on the outside. But inside his head, he drew a thick red line. He wasn't crossing it. Period.

Sometimes she pushed that boundary without even trying. Like the afternoon she showed up at his door in a dress that hugged every single curve. She spun around, showing him her bare back. "Hey, can you get this? I can't reach," she murmured. Rhys stepped in. He kept his hands strictly on the metal zipper, ignoring her sweet perfume and the sudden growl of his inner wolf. He yanked it up, grunted a quick "there," and backed off before the room got too hot.

Then there was the bedroom invite. "Help me pick an outfit," she'd said, holding up different fabrics against her skin, giving him that wide-eyed, innocent look. Rhys just stood there, arms crossed. He kept his eyes locked on her face—nowhere lower. He gave her dry, professional feedback. Like a consultant, not a guy who was interested.

Because he didn't want her. Not like that. And even if he felt a tiny spark, he had a rule: you don't touch another guy's girl. That boundary was set in stone way before he met this pack, and way before Briar. That rule, plus his messy situation, kept him sharp. He was here for Elara. Period. The rest was just background noise.

Still, he couldn't play too distant if he wanted her right where he needed her. So he put in the work. He trained her. When her form slipped, he adjusted her stance and grip, pushing her to react faster while keeping his voice calm and supportive. When the paperwork piled up and she looked stressed, he sat down and walked her through the logistics, acting like he actually cared about her progress. He patched up her weak spots. Slowly, she started relying on him.

That reliance turned into something warmer. Gratitude got messy. Suddenly, Rhys wasn't just a friend; he was a backup plan, a "what if" she kept in her back pocket in case things with Thane went south. He caught her staring. Her voice got softer around him. He knew she was comparing them.

It helped that he was the one nursing Asher back to health. With everyone else too busy to breathe, Rhys stepped up and filled the void. People noticed. Plus, pack life out here had slowed down. No city chaos, just quiet routines. Rhys actually liked the change of pace.

Out here, away from the concrete jungle, he could finally breathe. His wolf wasn't just pacing under his skin anymore; it was free to run. He'd tear through the woods, wind ripping through his fur, paws pounding the dirt. It was pure, raw freedom. He hadn't realized how much he missed it.

That freedom—and Elara—were the only things keeping him from packing his bags. He had a mission here, even if it was built on a lie.

"Hey, Deputy Chief Rhys," the accountant said one afternoon, sounding pretty tense.

Rhys looked up from his paperwork. "Yeah, Finn? What's up?"

"Uh, did you know Briar just pulled five million out of the pack account? In three days?"

Rhys froze for a split second, his brain doing quick math. Five mil? In three days? Damn. The quiet, mousey girl he'd written off wasn't a mouse at all. She was a snake. Just waiting in the grass, ready to bite.

"Thanks for the heads-up, Finn," Rhys said, keeping his voice totally flat. No need to show his cards. "I'll handle it."

Finn nodded, but he didn't leave. He fidgeted, weighing his words. "Look, I know you will, but... back when Elara ran things, we were always in the green. With Briar? It's the opposite."

That didn't shock Rhys. It just proved his theory. Elara had been quietly pumping her own cash into the pack to keep them afloat without bragging about it. Briar, though? She was just bleeding the pack dry like it was a free ATM.

"Don't sweat it," Rhys said. "Next time, you run any transfers by me first. Got it?"

Finn let out a massive breath. "Yeah. Thank you, Deputy Chief."

In all his years, Finn had never seen someone blow through cash like this while their income dried up. Keep this up, and the pack was going broke. Fast.

Rhys didn't sit around. He marched straight to Briar's quarters. He knew she'd be inside, and he was right. He raised his hand to knock, but stopped. Her voice was coming through the door—sharp, quiet, and pissed off.

"I told you, stay the hell away from the pack," she hissed. "Or you're cut off. Not another cent." A pause, then some rustling. "Look, I can't wire more right now. Thane will notice. The only reason I got away with this batch is because he told me to spend whatever it takes to cure Asher."

Rhys put the pieces together. Was she talking to her folks? Made sense. But then she dropped a name.

"Orion, seriously, don't come here. Yeah, Thane went to the city, but we've got a new beta. You remember Rhys, right? Yeah, him. Look, I'll figure out a way to meet up, but stay away from the territory. I..."

A loud buzz shattered the quiet.

Rhys's heart jumped into his throat. Shit, it was his phone. He killed the ringer instantly, his chest pounding as he backed away from the door. Every alarm in his head was screaming *get out*. He slipped down the hall, keeping his footsteps dead silent, and ducked into his own room two doors down. He locked the door behind him, took a breath, and finally swiped to answer.

"Hey, Chief Commander," he muttered, keeping his voice down.

On the other end, Thane sounded completely wrecked. Desperate. "Just call me Thane," he rasped. "Look, Rhys... I need a massive favor."
