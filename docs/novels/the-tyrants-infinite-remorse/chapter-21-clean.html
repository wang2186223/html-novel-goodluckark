Chapter 21

The flight was dead quiet. Just the low drone of the jet and endless clouds outside. Elara stared out the window, her brain firing on all cylinders. She needed a plan. Who was safe? Where should she start? They’d kicked her out before she was ready, and now she had to rebuild from scratch.

Touchdown. The airport was a madhouse—wheels scraping tile, speakers blaring, people shouting over each other. Lysander navigated the chaos, pulling her toward the arrivals gate. An older couple stood there waiting. The dad, Felix, was tall and carried himself like a guy used to calling the shots. The mom, Lyra, melted the second she saw Elara.

Lysander had given them a heads-up, but Lyra still looked shocked. She stepped up, her voice soft. "Wow. It really is you. Managing Director Sera... Honestly, I thought Lysander was pulling my leg. You and Chief Commander Thane were so tight. We never saw a divorce coming."

Elara forced a tight, polite smile. It felt like poking a fresh bruise. "Yeah, well, that chapter’s closed," she said, keeping her voice steady. "Sorry to crash at your place, by the way. I’ll get out of your hair as soon as I find my own spot."

Felix and Lyra traded a quick look, then shook their heads.

"No way," Felix barked.

"You did a killer job as Managing Director," Lyra said, totally serious. "Look, we only left because—"

"Ahem. Ahem." Lysander hacked out a loud, fake cough.

Lyra went stiff. She quickly plastered on a fake grin and waved her hand. "Uh, forget I said anything."

Something was definitely off. A hidden story there. But Elara let it slide. Some secrets just need time to spill.

The vibe relaxed once they hit the road. Glass skyscrapers blurred past the windows. When the car pulled up to the house, Elara’s jaw nearly dropped. It wasn't flashy, but it was massive, classy, and screamed old money. They walked straight into the dining room, where a hot dinner was already waiting. The smell was incredible. Elara took a bite, looking around the room. Curiosity got the better of her. "Hey, not to pry," she said, setting her fork down, "but... what do you guys actually do for a living?"

Felix froze, fork halfway to his mouth. Another silent exchange with his wife.

"Wait, you didn't know?" Lyra asked.

Elara blinked. "Know what?"

Lysander took a slow, deep breath. "My folks were never broke, Sera. They only stayed with the pack for my sake. Once I grew up, they moved out here to run their companies. I just never came to visit after I took the Beta gig."

Elara raised an eyebrow. So Lysander wasn't just a loyal sidekick—he was loaded. "Well, then you shouldn't be wasting your time helping me run my life," she said softly. She missed the way Felix’s eyes went cold, and how Lyra’s smile completely vanished.

"Sera, cut it out," Lysander said, his voice hard. "I made a promise, and I'm keeping it. My parents are backing you. Five billion."

Elara’s face turned to stone. "Absolutely not."

"As a loan!" Lysander cut in before she could shut him down.

"Fine. Then we put it in writing," Elara snapped.

Felix didn't even blink. He reached into a folder on the table and slid over a stack of fresh paperwork. "He said you'd say that," Felix murmured. "So we got it ready."

Elara went through the contract line by line, laser-focused. Once she was sure there were no traps, she signed her name.

She capped the pen and looked up. "Since you guys know the local market, I could use some advice."

"Depends," Felix said. "What's the play?"

She paused, calculating. "I'm thinking tech to start."

"Tech?" The parents both frowned.

"Just so we're clear," Elara said, her voice cool, "I'm a beast with code. I'll build a base there, play the stock market for quick cash, and then pivot to medical."

Felix stared at her, then a slow, impressed nod spread across his face. "Man. Thane is going to kick himself for letting you walk."

Elara’s smile didn't reach her eyes. "Aside from ripping my initial investment back out of his company," she said, "the guy is dead to me."

That was exactly what they wanted to hear. They wrapped up the details, setting the wire transfer for the next morning.

Her guest room was massive—quiet, cozy, and totally private. She crashed hard, her body finally realizing she was safe.

The next morning, she and Lysander hit the stores. She bought only the basics, no splurging, tossed the bags in the trunk, and headed straight to her next meeting.

Jasper’s office was a penthouse suite overlooking the skyline. The second they walked in, Elara took charge. "I'm Seraphine," she said, deadpan. "But on paper, I'm Marjorie Steward."

Maddox’s jaw hit the floor. "Marjorie?" he gasped, staring. "But... you're a kid."

"Yeah, I'm young. So what?" she shot back.

Maddox laughed, throwing his hands up. "Alright, alright. My bad. Follow me."

They spent hours grinding through calls and verifying her returns. When the endless back-and-forth between Maddox and Thane's people started giving her a headache, Elara stood up. "Enough. I'll deal with this myself."

She walked out, but the second the elevator doors slid open, she froze. Augustine was standing right there.

"Sera?" he gasped, eyes wide. "What are you doing here? I thought you'd call."

She forced a quick, polite smile. "Just got into town. Had some business to run. What about you?"

"Oh, I'm in the legal department here," he said, his eyes scanning her face. "Hey, if you ever need a lawyer, you know who to call."

The way he stared made her skin crawl a bit, but she noted the offer. "Will do," she said, serious.

She headed up to Thane's office. His assistant, Roric, gave her a warm welcome and waved her right in. Thane was grabbing his coat to leave when he spotted her. He froze.

"Sera?" His brow furrowed. "What the hell are you doing here?"

She was wearing a sharp, tailored white suit, looking incredibly polished. She’d put on some light makeup, and the look threw him off completely. It had only been a few weeks, but she felt like a total stranger.

Then his eyes drifted to the two guys standing behind her. He locked onto Maddox, and the color drained from his face.

"Wait," Thane muttered, his voice cracking. "Don't tell me you're..."

The words died in his throat.
