Chapter 302

Vladimir was losing the fight, but his voice practically dripped with panic. The guy was a dad, pure and simple. Years ago, when Caspian first fell for Elara, Vladimir didn't freak out. He just said, "Hold your horses. Wait till she’s eighteen." If the spark was still there, fine. He actually liked the kid back then. She was sharp as a tack.

But the girl who lit up Caspian’s world also blew it to pieces, dragging him straight into hell when she left. Commanders don't mix with humans. Period. But Vladimir saw the obsession. Caspian was in too deep, throwing the rulebook out the window to date her, even fathering her kid. Now she was back. No wonder his dad was sweating bullets.

"Use your head, Caspian," Vladimir groaned. "An ex-Director running the show again? It’s a slap in the face to our people. The elders will lose their minds, and the other packs will laugh us out of the room."

He kept lecturing like Caspian was some rookie. But Caspian was the Commander. He knew the laws inside out. He also knew the agonizing hell of every single second spent without Elara.

"A Director who walks out on a Commander doesn't get to move on to someone else. That's how we keep our authority, remember? And what if you fix the bond, only to find out she’s still tied to Thane? What then?"

A cold chill hit Caspian's spine. He forced a breath, hardening his voice. "Drop it. I know the risks. I'm jumping back in with my eyes wide open."

"And don't forget, Grimroot only got this powerful because I took the wheel. Before you start trash-talking Elara, there's stuff you don't know. Mind-blowing stuff that changes the whole damn game."

He paused, chest tight. "Her mind was wiped. A memory block. She didn't just ditch me or stop loving me. Someone literally ripped those memories out of her head." He squared his shoulders. "And that night with Thane? She was roofied."

The line went dead quiet, save for the sudden hitch in his dad's breathing.

"So look," Caspian muttered, dropping his tone. "Just give her a break. The same break you'd want for me if I was the one hurting. Help me out here. Please." Begging tasted like ash, but watching Elara suffer was worse.

Silence dragged. Then came that familiar, gruff throat-clear. The classic *I'm-rethinking-this-but-I'll-never-admit-it* sound.

"You guys sleep together yet?" Vladimir blurted.

Caspian's face stayed stone-cold, but his eyes flickered. "Why the hell does that matter?"

"Just answer me," his dad snapped.

No way. Caspian didn't do bedroom talk with his father. Not then, not ever. "That's none of your business, and I'm not—"

"The cure," Vladimir interrupted, "isn't coming in some bottle, kid. No magic weeds, no potions, no Amazon package. Get it?" He paused for effect. "The cure is you."

Caspian froze, his brain short-circuiting. Vladimir didn't beat around the bush this time. He laid it right out.

"Triggering her pheromones kills the pain instantly—assuming there's still a spark. But that basically locks you in as—" his dad hesitated, fishing for the term, "her permanent guy. Because you'll have to do it on day one of every cycle."

"Your skin, your touch, just you being right there. That's the only thing that beats back the curse tearing her apart. That’s the fix. Always has been."

Dead air on both sides.

Caspian swallowed. "Gotta go." He yanked the phone down and killed the call before his dad could utter another word.

He stood glued to the floor, phone dangling from his fingers, staring blankly at the wall. Would she even let him? They were barely a thing yet, and triggering those hormones was heavy stuff. It was basically like getting down and dirty—like oral.

A rustle by the bathroom made him snap out of it. Elara clung to the doorframe, trying to stay upright. His oversized shirt was slipping off her shoulder.

She was barely standing. Her face was totally white, her eyes completely hollowed out by the kind of brutal pain that shuts down your entire brain.

"Did you... find the cure?" she whispered. Still trying to play the tough girl. Just like before—hide the agony, don't let them see you break.

Caspian closed the distance in two strides, silent. He scooped her up—one arm under her back, the other under her knees—lifting her like she was made of air. Smooth, easy, like he'd done it a thousand times.

He carried her over and tucked her into the pillows, then sank onto the edge of the mattress, his weight pulling her slightly toward him.

Elara stared up, waiting. Scanning his face. Even half-dead with pain, she was trying to read his mind. Classic Elara.

Caspian opened his mouth. Nothing. He shut it, tried again, his jaw locking. The words were there, but they wouldn't form. He loved her, sure, but he wasn't about to cross her boundaries.

"Look, it's..." he muttered. "I..." He slammed the heel of his palm against his forehead. "Damn it." The curse slipped out, raw and quiet. How the hell was he supposed to pitch this to her?
