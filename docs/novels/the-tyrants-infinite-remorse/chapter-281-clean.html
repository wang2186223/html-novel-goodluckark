Chapter 284

She figured he’d dodge. Or shut up. He was a master at playing dead, but this? No way. She stared, half-pissed, half-clueless, with a weird third feeling bubbling up deep down that she refused to label.

"What the hell is that supposed to mean?"

"Exactly what it sounds like." Caspian wasn't wearing his usual fake-calm mask. None of that annoying, rehearsed silence. He looked like a guy who was done putting on a show and just wanted to spit the truth.

"I had to drag every single detail out of you, Sera. You gave me zero. Nothing." He clamped his mouth shut, choosing his next words. "And hey, correct me if I'm wrong, but didn't you say we aren't friends?"

Elara froze. Not because her mind was blank. Her brain was actually short-circuiting trying to sort through a million thoughts at once.

Caspian didn't stop. He dropped each word like a slow, heavy brick. He'd rehearsed this, and he wasn't going to blow it. "I pushed because I care about you. I never wanted to keep you in the dark." He took a long drag of air, searching for the right pitch. "Look, I tried to get close. You built a wall. You said we're just business partners, remember? You hammered that home."

He locked eyes with her. No games, no pressure. Just raw honesty. "Even when we kissed, I thought—I don't know, I thought it would spark something. But it was nothing to you, right?"

Elara shut her mouth, letting the blow land. Before she could even process, he hit her again. "So what was I supposed to do, huh? Sit across a desk from my business partner and chat about my kid? My private life?" He leaned in, keeping his voice low but sharp. "Would you have done that?"

Around them, the hospital hallway didn't care. A monitor beeped down the hall. The vending machine buzzed. Squeaky sneakers faded into the distance.

Elara went dead silent. Not because she was on board. Not yet. But because his words hit a nerve she didn't know she had, and she needed a second to catch her breath. He was right. God, she hated it. And he was standing right there, watching her swallow the bitter pill.

Caspian got the hint. He didn't push. He let the awkward silence do the heavy lifting. "If you want the whole story," he said, his voice dropping, losing its bite. It was soft now. Cautious. "Then you gotta be my friend." He kept his eyes glued to hers. "So. Will you be my friend, Sera?"

Behind her, the elevator dinged. The doors slid open with a soft hiss, like a sigh. Neither of them budged.

She didn't answer right away. Elara just stood there, letting his question float between them while she actually chewed on it. Not the simple "sure, let's hang out" version, but the heavy, messy reality underneath.

She thought about Thane. Caspian was always in Thane's corner, solid as a rock, no matter which way the wind blew. Even when Arthur asked if Caspian knew Thane had killed their kid, Caspian didn't blink. He just stood by Thane like the accusation was nothing but a light breeze. No defense needed. That told Elara everything she needed to know. She'd filed that away, and it colored every single interaction she had with him after.

Then there was the city. When she first got here, totally wrecked and trying to rebuild, Caspian had blocked her at every turn. He shut down her opportunities, systematically trying to ruin her with the cold precision of a guy who had the cash and the power to do it. She survived. Not on luck, but on pure grit and talent. And she didn't forget. You don't forget the face of the bulldozer that tried to flatten you.

So why was she still standing here? She looked at his face, past the walls she'd built, past the neat little box she'd put him in. There was something else there. Something she'd been too busy fighting to notice. Not soft, exactly, but deep. After everything, he'd backed her up at the pack. Sure, their wolves were acting like idiots, but things had changed.

Caspian had her back. He'd even clocked Thane in the jaw for her. He stayed when she gave him every reason to walk away, sticking around until she got back to the city.

Elara didn't do emotional decisions. But standing here, staring at this puzzle of a man she couldn't stop thinking about, she made a quiet call. If she had to open a door to figure him out, fine. She could do that. Plus, she wanted to get close to Aurelia. The kid called her mommy, and the second she'd walked into that hospital room, she knew the girl was special.

"Friends," Elara said.

The word came out easier than she thought. Short, simple, but it hit.

Caspian's chest dropped as he let out a massive breath. His grip on her wrist loosened, his fingers letting go one by one until her arm was free. He didn't budge. He just stood there, staring at her like he was double-checking he hadn't misheard.

She was back in his life, and this time, he wasn't going to lose her. No way. "Hey, I didn't see Lysander's car out front," he said, fishing for an opening. "Maybe I should—"

"Caspian."

Linnea's voice cut through the air behind them. The relaxed look on Caspian's face instantly vanished. His jaw went rock hard.
