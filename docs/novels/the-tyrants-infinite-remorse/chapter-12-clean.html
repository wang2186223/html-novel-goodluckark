Chapter 12

You could hear a pin drop in that room, except for the click-clack of the chess pieces.

Elara and Eleanor stared each other down over the board. Weak sunlight hit the wooden knights and rooks, throwing long, ugly shadows across the table.

Sure, it looked like a friendly match. But Lysander and Arthur weren't stupid. They saw the real shit going down—a silent, brutal mind game.

Eleanor had been crushing it at first. She moved her pieces like a shark circling a meal, totally smug.

But Elara was clawing her way back. Every move she made felt heavy, like she was forcing the board to do exactly what she wanted.

This wasn't about a checkmate anymore. It was personal. The vibe in the room was electric. Lysander leaned in, chest thumping, totally clueless about who would win.

Then, heavy footsteps thudded down the hall. Slow. Deliberate. Everyone looked up. Thane wasn't alone.

Chief Commander Caspian walked in right behind him. The room went ice-cold. Thane looked like a cocky jerk, but Caspian? He just looked like a boss.

The guy had this crazy gravity. He didn't say a word, but you wanted to salute. The air got thick, and even the sunlight seemed to fade out.

Lysander gave a quick, nervous bow. Eleanor froze with her hand over a pawn. Arthur's shoulders went rigid.

But Elara stood her ground. She didn't budge, spine straight as a rod, just radiating pure defiance. She locked eyes with Caspian.

"Chief Commander Caspian," she said, her voice cutting through the silence like a knife. "Let me guess. You're here to force me to give my blood to Briar, too?"

Holy shit. The temperature plummeted. Arthur grabbed the edge of the table so hard his knuckles turned white. Eleanor sat up straight, totally on edge.

Even Thane blinked. He probably expected her to beg, bow, or at least play nice. But her sheer balls-out attitude threw everyone for a loop.

Caspian's stone-cold poker face actually cracked. Just for a split second, surprise flickered in his eyes before he shut it down.

"You've got a mouth on you," he said, his voice low and sharp as a razor.

Elara just tilted her head, looking completely unimpressed. "And I'd call you an idiot if you actually came all this way for Briar."

Arthur's stomach dropped. Did this girl have any damn clue who she was messing with?

Caspian's eyes flared. He was losing his grip, and he hated it.

Lysander shivered, squeezing his hands together like he was trying to hold Elara back. But she wasn't moving an inch.

She stood there like a lion staring down a wolf. "You were right there," Elara said, her voice flat but heavy with blame. "You saw the whole damn thing."

"Yeah. I did," Caspian spat, his voice dripping with icy rage.

You could hear the walls breathing. Nobody had ever dared to grill him like this, let alone some kid who refused to back down.

He stared at her, trying to find a word for her sheer nerve, but came up empty.

"You stabbed her first," he growled. "We ran downstairs. Then you stabbed her again, right in front of our faces."

Elara's eyes turned to flint. She looked cold enough to freeze hell over. The pain had gutted her, but it left her with a spine of solid steel. Losing everything had made her mean. Now, she wanted payback.

"What if I told you I didn't touch her the first time?" she whispered, but the words hit like ice.

Everything stopped. Caspian's jaw clenched. The truth was, he didn't have the full picture.

Thane had been sketchy and desperate, throwing business favors at him to get him to pull some strings. Caspian only showed up because someone's life was on the line.

But now this girl was standing her ground, totally fearless, demanding the truth. "Would you even believe me?" she challenged, her voice ringing out like a dare.

Caspian narrowed his eyes. "Got a witness?"

Elara bit her lip. The Syndicate House didn't have cameras. No paper trail. Nothing to prove she didn't do it.

She let out a slow, shaky breath, her mind racing. "And if I don't?" she shot back, keeping her voice steady.

"Then," Caspian said, not missing a beat, "you're cleaning up your own damn mess."

Elara's face fell, just for a second. Then she stood taller, cracking her neck like she was shaking off a heavy coat.

"Chief Commander Caspian," she said, her eyes dripping with pure contempt. "Forget what I said about respecting you. You're just another idiot."

The room went dead silent.

Caspian's face turned to stone. Thane's fists shook, white-knuckled. Arthur could feel his own pulse pounding in his ears.

Arthur had to stop her before she got herself killed. He knew exactly what Caspian was capable of. The guy ran things globally. People in both the boardroom and the pack whispered about his temper like he was the boogeyman.

But Elara didn't even blink. Arthur lunged forward, grabbing her arm tight.

"Sera, stop," he hissed, desperate. "You can't talk to him like that. He owns half of America, Asia, and Europe. You do not want this guy on your bad side."

"Too late," she whispered. Tears pooled in her eyes, but she refused to let them fall. "He's backing the wrong horse, Arthur. I'm telling the damn truth." Her voice broke, raw with pain.

Arthur let out a long breath. "That's enough. Let me talk."

He used to be the big dog himself, back when Caspian was just starting to take over the city's corporate ladder. When Caspian came back to claim his pack, he built an empire from scratch. He was a monster, wiping out anyone who dared to question him. Nobody knew his secrets, but rumor had it his rivals ended up six feet under, and his business tactics were straight-up savage.

And now, here he was, actually looking a little rattled by a girl's attitude.

Arthur couldn't let her pick a fight she couldn't win. She'd been through enough hell. She deserved a break, a shot at a real life.

"Chief Commander Caspian," Arthur said, playing nice to cool things down. "Excuse my daughter. She's stressed. Why don't we sit down and talk this out?"

Caspian stared at him, then slowly sank into the plush leather sofa like a king taking his throne. The room let out a collective, quiet breath.

Thane smirked to himself. Finally. He was getting that blood. Whether she liked it or not, the docs were already waiting outside in the van. He had this in the bag.

Arthur sat down opposite him, looking cool but ready to spring at any second.

"Chief Commander Caspian," Arthur started, his voice low and heavy. "You and Thane go way back. But did he happen to mention how he swapped and killed Elara's baby just because he wanted her out of his life?"

The room went dead cold. The question hung in the air like a loaded gun.

Elara kept her eyes glued to Caspian, her anger and pain boiling right under her skin. She wasn't some scared little girl begging for mercy anymore. She was a ticking time bomb, ready to blow.
