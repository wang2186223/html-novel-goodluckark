Chapter 280

"Caspian?" The name slipped out before she could lock her teeth. "What—what are you doing here?"

God, she sounded rattled. She hated the wobble in her voice, hated how it totally gave her away. Her gaze locked onto him, lingering, scanning his features like she was trying to crack some secret code she hadn't even written yet.

His forehead creased, those dark brows knitting together in that classic, tense scowl. He stared at her like he’d just walked into a closet and found a ghost. "I was about to ask you the exact same thing," he muttered.

He sounded genuinely thrown. To his knowledge, she was supposed to be clocked in at Julian's clinic, scrubbing into surgery, or running her own tight schedule. Her standing in this room? It didn't compute. Not even close.

Valerie slipped in right behind him, hovering by the doorframe. She didn't even try to play it cool—she was shamelessly staring. Her eyes darted back and forth between them, gears practically grinding in her head as she tried to force the puzzle pieces together. Nothing fit. The redhead had claimed the kid, called the dad her fiancé, but anyone with a brain knew Orion didn't do relationships. No fiancée. No girlfriend. Zero.

The itch to know was too strong. Valerie couldn't help herself. She jerked her chin toward the red-haired woman lurking in Caspian's shadow. "Hey, Caspian. Is she, like... your fiancée or something?"

The redhead cut in before Caspian could even part his lips. Her voice was slick, practiced, like she’d rehearsed this exact script in the mirror. "He's Mari's dad." She let the words hang there for a beat. "And I'm her mom."

Caspian didn't say a damn word. No nod, no denial. He just brushed past her words like they were clutter on the floor, walking straight to the bedside. His eyes locked onto the little girl propped up on the pillows.

Elara froze. The words sank in, heavy and slow, rewriting everything she thought she knew. She finally really looked at the kid—not as a doctor, but just *looking*. No clinical walls to hide behind this time. The thick, dark hair. The sharp curve of the jawline. Standing right next to Caspian, the match was undeniable. You couldn't ignore it if you tried. It was staring her right in the face, glaringly obvious.

"Linnea." Caspian's voice was sharp, a cold, hard edge cutting through the room. He glared across the mattress at the redhead. "You should've called me before dragging her here."

Linnea stared down, her fingers nervously smoothing the blanket over the girl's legs. "I know, okay? I just... I panicked. She started shaking, and my brain went totally blank. I just—" Her voice cracked, dropping to a whisper. "I'm sorry."

She sank into the chair opposite him. There they were: Caspian standing, Linnea sitting, the kid nestled right in the middle. A perfect, neat little picture. A real family.

A cold, heavy weight slammed into Elara's chest. She felt it, identified the ache, and immediately shut it down. *Nope. Not my business.* This had nothing to do with her.

Just another dead end. Another door slamming in her face. She’d known the odds were crap before she even parked her car. She’d literally spent the entire drive telling herself exactly that.

"I gotta run," she mumbled, already pivoting to grab the chart off the nightstand. "I have some stuff to—"

"Daddy?"

The voice was tiny, a sleepy, raspy mumble from a kid waking up in a strange room. But it froze the entire room.

Caspian reacted instantly. All his stiff, icy armor just melted away in a second, leaving him completely raw. It was almost shocking to see. He bent over the mattress, grabbing her tiny hand and scooping her up against his chest.

"Right here, baby," he murmured, his voice softer than Elara had ever thought possible. "I've got you. You're safe."

He kissed the crown of her dark head, holding her tight, his massive hand covering her tiny back as he rocked her. It was pure instinct. No manual needed.

Elara stared. She couldn't tear her eyes away, and she hated herself for it. It was that same gentle side she'd glimpsed back at the pack—the way he'd carried her on his back. Suddenly, her head started throbbing like crazy.

"Sera," Valerie whispered right by her ear. "She's up. Go check her out."

Elara hesitated. Caspian had endless cash—top-tier specialists, private clinics, doctors on speed dial 24/7. She didn't need to be here anymore.

"She looks okay to me," she said, though her voice lacked any real conviction.

The little girl pulled back from her dad's chest. Her bright blue eyes scanned the room, totally unfiltered, just drifting from one thing to the next. They swept past Valerie. Ignored Linnea. Then they locked onto Elara. And stayed there.

One second.

Two.

"Mommy?"
