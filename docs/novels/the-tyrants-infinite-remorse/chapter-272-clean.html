Chapter 275

Iris looked up. Just a slow tilt of her chin, but the second Lysander saw her face, his eyes went sharp. Bingo.

"You were there," he muttered, his voice deadpan, like he was talking to himself. "Serving drinks. That night."

Iris locked eyes with him for a beat, then looked away.

Lysander shut up, but his face gave him away. Not mad, just... annoyed. Like a guy rewinding a movie in his head, realizing he missed every single clue. If he’d known back then—if he’d even guessed Elara would dump Thane—he would’ve paid attention. He would’ve spotted her. Things would be totally different now.

"Alright," Elara cut in, sharp and clean, killing the vibe. "Let's roll."

Lysander snapped out of it, grabbed the wheel, and hit the gas. No more talking.

In the rearview, right where the outlands ended, Caspian’s ride took a hard turn and vanished. Elara kept her eyes glued to the road ahead.

The manager picked up on the second ring. She was downstairs in seconds, still shoving her arms into a jacket, totally caught off guard on a random afternoon. She looked from Elara to the quiet girl standing behind her, waiting.

"This is Iris," Elara said, skipping the small talk. "She's from the outlands. She's crashing with you for a bit." Straight to the point. No sugarcoating. "Tomorrow, take the day off. Go shopping. This weekend, show her the ropes—how we do things, what to expect. Monday, she starts at the office."

The manager stood frozen, letting the info-dump sink in. Then she looked at Iris. Really looked. Her face shifted. That "I can work with this" look.

"Got it," she said, nodding. "Plenty of potential. We'll make it happen." She glanced at Elara. "Thanks for the trust, boss."

Two days later, the hospital swallowed Elara alive the second she walked in. Honestly? Perfect. She had back-to-back surgeries lined up, and she knocked them out one by one like a machine.

Monday morning, she was back at MindNest for an all-hands meeting. The room was a mess of coffee cups, open laptops, and loud chatter. Everyone was packed into the conference room for once, grabbing chairs, nodding hellos.

Elara walked in and froze.

She stared at the girl sitting two seats down. Her brain literally glitched. Lysander, right behind her, went dead silent too.

Iris looked... insane. Not just a new outfit—she was a whole new person. Whatever the manager did to her over the weekend, it was a total glow-up. You had to double-take just to make sure it was her. The posture, the high chin—everything was different.

Neither of them spoke. They exchanged a look, then stared back at Iris. She was sitting there, looking super professional and cool, until she followed Elara’s gaze and spotted Orion.

She went white as a sheet.

Elara marched over before things kicked off and leaned down. "Hey, don't freak," she whispered. "He's not that guy anymore. Seriously, he's changed. Don't let his past self get in your head today."

Iris gave a tight, tiny nod. Message received, but she didn't buy it.

The meeting ended, and everyone started packing up, scraping chairs and grabbing phones. Orion tried to corner Iris by the door. He opened his mouth to speak, but she just swerved right past him like he was a misplaced chair. No look. No pause. Just ghosted.

Orion stood there, stunned, then whipped around to look at Elara.

Elara threw her hands up.

"Not my problem," she said, already backing out the door. "That's on you to fix. I'm not playing referee, and I'm definitely not giving you advice. You know what you did. Deal with it."

He looked ready to argue, but she bailed.

Back in her office, Elara pulled up the business plan she’d built around Caspian’s cash. She checked the stocks, the cash flow, the rollout timeline. It was a tight plan. Bulletproof. The kind of strategy that wouldn't break under pressure.

Lysander was across the desk, flipping through files, throwing out a question here and there. They worked in a nice, quiet groove.

Then she brought up the funding numbers, and the second Caspian’s name slipped out, her face flushed. Just a quick, warm blush. She tried to wipe it off her face instantly, but she was too late.

Lysander’s pen froze.

He looked up over his paperwork, staring at her with that smug, patient look of a guy who just solved a puzzle. He didn't say a word. He just let the silence get awkward.

"Sera."

She kept staring at her monitor. "What?"

"You never did tell me what went down with you and Caspian at the pack."

"Nothing went down."

Her voice was flat, eyes glued to the screen. But her cheeks were betraying her, turning bright red like they wanted to spill the tea even if she wouldn't.

Lysander dropped his pen.

"Sure," he said, sounding totally unconvinced. "Guess I'll just call Rhys. Get the real story."

Elara’s head snapped up so fast she almost killed her coffee cup. "Whatever. Fine." She sat up straight. "Fine. I'll tell you."

Lysander leaned back, crossed his arms, and kept a straight face. He was loving every second of this.

So she spilled. Everything. How Caspian carried her through the pouring rain, the whole bathroom mess, how she lost her mind crying and he just held her without making it weird. How she somehow ended up sleeping right on top of him all night. And the kiss. She laid out every single detail of that kiss in this dry, robotic voice, which only made it super obvious she hadn't stopped thinking about it.

Lysander didn't say a peep.

When she finally shut up, the room went dead quiet for a solid minute. He just sat there, arms crossed, face blank. Honestly, the silence was worse than a tease.

Then... "Sera."

"Don't start."

"Just one question."

"Lysander, seriously—"

"Do you like him?"
