Chapter 266

Freezing, pitch-black water. Caspian didn't hesitate. The second that foul, wrong scent hit his nose, he dove. Not up. Going to the surface was suicide; they’d be waiting. He went deep, kicking hard against the current, letting the dark swallow him. He knew these flooded tunnels better than his own bedroom. He grabbed a wet ledge, hauled his body through a tight crack in the stone, and scrambled onto solid ground.

He was way down the bank from where he’d jumped in. Then he heard it. Footsteps. Right freaking next to him. Not Elara’s. He slammed his back against the rock, held his breath, and stared into the dark. There. A bunch of shadows were closing in on Elara, tightening the circle.

His jaw locked. No time to think. He let the shift rip through him—bones snapping and reshaping as Gideon took over. He leaped straight into the branches. The hitmen never saw him coming. He dropped them one by one, hard and fast. No survivors, no arguments. When the last body hit the dirt, Gideon stood over them, chest rumbling, paws dug into the mud.

He spun toward Elara but froze mid-step. Her wolf. Gideon went stiff as a board, eyes glued to the beast in the clearing. *I knew it,* Gideon’s voice echoed in his head. *I knew she was different.* Caspian felt a jolt of shock. *Whoa, hold on. You sure? The gifted wolf is just a bedtime story.* *That’s no story,* Gideon snapped, staring. *Look at her. She’s wild. Zero fear. She loves the blood just as much as I do.*

And damn, he was right. Her wolf wasn't just fighting; she was having fun. Pure, raw joy. Caspian’s mind raced, putting the pieces together. Suddenly, it all made sense. Elara’s crazy instincts. The way she predicted every move. The weirdly ancient combat skills she shouldn't have. Her wolf was feeding her all of it. The gifted wolf was a freak of nature—it didn't just give its human speed or strength, it gave them instant, bone-deep knowledge. And it was the only wolf alive that could match Gideon’s psycho level of violence.

And Thane had just let her walk. The realization hit Caspian like a punch to the gut. Thane never even bothered to look. If he’d actually paid attention, he would’ve torn the world apart to keep her. Instead, he threw her out like trash. What an idiot. Seriously, his loss.

*Stop her,* Caspian ordered, cutting through Gideon’s trance. *She can't kill that last guy. We need him to talk. I want to know who sent them.* Gideon bolted, eating up the ground in massive leaps. One hitman was still breathing, kneeling in the dirt in front of Blaze, head down. Her wolf was hovering over him. Caspian knew that look—he’d had it himself. The guy had maybe three seconds to live. "Hey, it’s us," Gideon growled, low and rough. Blaze snapped her head around, coiling to spring. For a split second, it was a coin toss. Then the tension popped. Both wolves shrank back, bones shifting, leaving two naked humans shivering in the cold night air.

Elara walked off to find her clothes without saying a word. Caspian rolled a massive rock onto the surviving hitman—heavy enough to pin him, not enough to crush his skull. Then he darted into the trees. He knew where the emergency stash was hidden. He grabbed a set of clothes, grabbed another for Elara, and jogged back. But she was already fully dressed.

He stopped, frowning. "Wait, did you seriously strip naked right in front of them?" The words came out sharper than he meant, but whatever. "I wasn't about to fight with clothes on," she said, chin up, totally unbothered. He rubbed his neck, sighing. "There’s a stash right here, Elara." He shook the bundle of clothes. "You literally gave those guys a free show for zero reason." She blinked. Once. A weird look crossed her face—not quite embarrassed, maybe a little amused. "Hey, better safe than sorry." Caspian clenched his jaw. He threw the spare clothes onto a nearby branch and tried to pretend he didn't care. But he did. The image was stuck in his head now—Elara, totally bare, standing in front of a bunch of guys who had just tried to murder her. And the fact that those bastards got to see her and he didn't? Yeah, that stung like a bitch.

He forced his mind back to business and turned to the rock. Elara was already crouching over it, hands gripping the edges, trying to heave it off the guy. "Whoa, what are you doing?" Caspian took four quick steps and grabbed her arm. "He needs to die," she muttered. Her eyes were cold, still holding that icy wolf glare. She’d wanted to grill the guy herself, but not with Caspian watching. If this guy spilled his guts, Caspian might run straight to Thane before she was ready to handle it.
