Chapter 234

"Five hundred billion. That's my floor," Caspian said, deadpan. No bluffing, no games. "Not a penny less."

Elara shut up and sized him up, scanning his face like a puzzle she had to solve from three different sides.

"MindNest is solid," she finally muttered. "Sure, I've got other startups lined up down the road. But right now? I have one job: find my kid. Once I get her back, she's my whole world. The company, this town, all of it? Dead last."

She locked eyes with him. "That's why Lysander is running the show. We built the team, so he can handle the weight without me. So let's get real. I might pack my bags, skip the country, and never look back. You still want to dump five hundred billion into a ship I'm ready to jump?"

If Asher were actually Thane’s kid, this whole mess wouldn't have blown up. She knew it, deep down in the dark corner of her mind she hated visiting. But she couldn't dodge the truth anymore. It was leaking out, ready to blow her life to hell at the worst damn time. The panic squeezed her chest like a vice, making her desperate, sharp, and dangerous. Hiding wasn't enough anymore. She had to outrun the clock. She had to find her girl and tuck her away somewhere safe before Thane figured out Asher wasn't his. The crazy, reckless plan clicked into place with terrifying focus.

That word—*leave*—hit Caspian like a cheap shot. It crawled under his skin, uninvited and messy. He kept his face totally blank. "Where to?"

"Why do you care?" she shot back, cold and blunt. "The stock? Sorry, not a good enough excuse to give you my itinerary. I don't color inside the lines, and I'm not going to play nice just to make you feel safe."

Caspian stared. He'd dealt with enough big-shots to know who was faking it and who was real. She was the real deal. He flashed back to the Sovereign Circle meeting. She'd backed him into a corner so smoothly, so damn fast, he'd signed on the dotted line before he even realized he had a choice. She'd busted her ass to get him in the game. Now, she was telling him the game might end tomorrow. The irony was actually pretty amusing.

"You cornered me last time," he said, a faint trace of respect in his voice. "You were a bulldog. Now you're playing hard to get?" He let the silence hang, then made up his mind. "Screw it, I'm in. Give me a week to wire the cash. And hey, let's call ourselves friends."

"Partners," Elara snapped, shutting that down instantly. "Strictly business."

Caspian raised an eyebrow. "You sure?"

"What's that supposed to mean?" she asked, genuinely curious.

He shrugged, keeping his cards close. "Nothing. I had plans for us tonight, but you look like hell. Tomorrow, then." He said it like a done deal. No arguing.

Elara scowled. "Can't do tomorrow. I'm meeting Rhys." She hesitated. "Besides, how do I know you won't blab about all this?"

"We write it into the new contract," he said, already mapping out the legal jargon. "If I open my mouth to Thane or anyone else about your personal drama, I lose every single cent I put in. No refunds, no loopholes."

Elara let out a long, slow breath she hadn't realized she was holding. "Fine. That works." She tossed her napkin onto the table. "We're done here. Take me back."

"Let's go." Caspian whipped out his wallet and slapped a fat stack of cash on the table, ignoring the bill.

A waiter rushed over. "Commander Caspian, dinner is on us tonight. Compliments of the house."

Caspian gave him a hard, single shake of his head. "Tell your boss I don't do handouts." He frowned. This never happened when he rolled with Thane. Why now, with Elara?

He stood up and, totally on instinct, grabbed her hand as they headed for the exit. They took three steps before she yanked her hand back. The realization of what he'd just done hit him like a cold splash of water—way too late. Neither said a word. They just walked out into the night.

At the lobby, Caspian leaned over the front desk. His voice was low but carried that "don't mess with me" authority. "Give me the room right next to hers."

He grabbed the keys and walked her down the hall. "Lock your door. You hear so much as a squeak, you run straight to me."

"I can take care of myself, thanks," Elara muttered, trailing behind him.

"I know," he said, not even looking back. "I'm just protecting my money."

Elara literally ground her teeth. "I seriously hate your guts," she snapped, grabbing her doorknob. "Just so we're clear."

Caspian leaned against the doorframe, totally relaxed. "Right back at you, babe." A smirk tugged at his lips. "I'm taking you to that spot tomorrow. You're gonna love it."

"I told you, I'm—"

"Go take a shower," he cut her off. "Get some sleep. We'll fight about tomorrow when the sun's up."

She choked back her comeback. She was just too damn tired to argue. She slipped inside, and the lock clicked shut.

Caspian waited in the hall until he heard the deadbolt slide home. Then he walked into his own room, pulled out his phone, and hit dial.

Thane picked up on the second ring. "Caspian? Is Elara okay?"

He didn't sound sleepy at all. He sounded like a guy who'd been staring at his phone waiting for this exact call. That told Caspian everything he needed to know.

When Caspian spoke, his relaxed vibe was gone. His voice went dead cold, sharp as a razor. "How the hell did you know I was calling about her?" He paused, letting the chill sink in. "Thane. If I find out you had anything to do with those hitmen tonight, you're gonna regret every single year we've been friends."
