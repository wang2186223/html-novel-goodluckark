Chapter 278

"I already told her," Lysander said, totally shrug-it-off casual. "She looked me dead in the eye and said she doesn't see me that way. Period."

A tiny smirk tugged at his mouth, almost sweet. "And unlike some guys in this room, I actually listened. Took the L, moved on." He smiled, but his eyes stayed dead. "She’s my sister now. I'm good with that."

Something in Caspian’s chest finally let go.

He’d been dragging that stupid worry around forever, chewing on it, stressing.

Lysander was always *there*. First on her speed dial, always showing up, the only guy Elara actually let her guard down around. It had been rubbing him raw like a damn splinter.

Caspian kept his face like stone. "Cool. Nothing left to talk about then."

He headed for the door.

"Caspian."

Caspian stopped. Didn't turn.

"Don't go thinking you've got all day," Lysander said. No heat, just cold facts. It hit harder that way. "She’s not the type to sit around waiting for you to decide if she's worth the trouble." He let a solid ten seconds tick by. "I'm in your corner. But only if you promise me one thing. Don't pull the same crap your best friend did."

The words felt like ice water down Caspian's neck.

He froze. Lysander didn't know the half of it. If he knew the real dirt—the stuff Caspian kept locked down deep—would he still have his back? Would any of this be okay?

"It's not that simple, man." Caspian's voice dropped, barely a mumble. "She looks at me and sees a business partner. That's it."

Elara had the paperwork spread all over her desk when Caspian walked back in. She was already pages deep, scratching notes with a pen. She didn't even look up.

"Next of kin is blank." She tapped the paper with her pen. "Kind of a big deal for a contract this size."

Caspian grabbed the chair opposite her and dropped into it. He had that easy, boss energy, the kind of guy who owns the room just by breathing.

"We'll fill that in later. Left it blank on purpose." He scanned her face. "You eat yet? It’s past two. I know how your hospital shifts get—"

"Not hungry." Her voice was pure ice, totally professional. "Caspian."

The name hit like a slammed door.

Caspian shut up. He took a slow breath in through his nose, blew it out, locking his shit down.

Without Blaze messing with things, Elara was right back to her default settings. Cold. Distant. Polite in that way that says *stay the hell away from me*.

He knew she could be like this, but damn. He didn't think she'd freeze him out after everything they went through with the pack and out in the wild.

"If we're done here," he said, keeping his voice flat, "I'll get out of your hair."

Elara didn't try to stop him. Didn't even look up from her papers.

He was three steps from the exit when she threw it out there, totally casual, like it was nothing. "You don't need to hand-deliver this stuff. Send your assistant next time. That's what you pay them for."

Caspian ground his teeth together. "Got it." He grabbed the doorknob. "Whatever you want."

He walked straight to the elevator, face totally blank. Only when the metal doors slid shut and he was totally alone did he let out a massive sigh, staring at the ceiling.

And here he thought *he* was the one with issues. He'd spent years dealing with his own mood swings, going hot and cold, snapping for no reason.

But Elara? She blew him out of the water. The girl had dried tears on her face from twenty minutes ago, and now she was looking at him like he was a line on a spreadsheet.

That took some serious, terrifying discipline. It was almost hot, if it wasn't so damn frustrating.

Weeks flew by.

The busy kind. Surgeries, pulling all-nighters, grinding to build this new thing from scratch. Elara just kept her head down, ticking off boxes on her to-do list like a machine.

She was at her desk at the hospital, scribbling notes in a patient's chart, when her phone buzzed.

She saw the caller ID and froze.

Valerie.

She stared at the screen. Didn't grab it right away. The name brought back all the messy drama with Thane and Caspian at the coffee shop. But she swiped to answer anyway.

"Valerie, look, I—"

"Sera." Valerie cut her off, and whatever Elara was going to say died in her throat.

Valerie sounded panicked. Not fake drama, but the real deal. Fast, sharp, no time to waste.

"I need you. Now. We just got a little girl in. Rare condition. None of our docs have a clue what to do. I've tried everything. I'm tapped out."

Elara sat up straight, shoving the chart aside.

Then Valerie dropped her voice to a whisper, right against the mic.

"She’s one of us, Sera."
