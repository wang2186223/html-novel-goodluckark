NovelVibe

Chapter 260

"Are you kidding me right now?" Thane snapped, his voice cracking with a raw, defensive edge. "Man, we grew up together. You seriously think I’m that much of a pushover? That I'd just let some chick play me?"

Caspian kept his voice low. "Look, man. When you actually care about someone, you’re blind to the red flags. It happens."

Thane spun around, his jaw tight enough to snap. He swallowed hard, trying to keep his cool. "Yeah, okay, she’s got issues," he muttered, staring at the floor. "She lies. She gets crazy jealous. I get it. But she’s Asher’s mom, Caspian. She gave me my boy, my heir." He took a long, shaky breath. "She can change. I’ll make sure of it."

Caspian just stared. There was no point in arguing. Not right now, not when Thane was this deep in denial. You can't drag a guy out of a burning house if he wants to stay inside. He had to figure it out himself.

Caspian stood up straight, letting go of the balcony railing. He had to ask. The question had been eating at him since he first got to this place and watched Thane’s eyes constantly scan the crowd just to find her.

"Just one more thing," Caspian said, keeping his tone totally flat. "I'm only going to ask this once, so give it to me straight." He locked eyes with his best friend. "Do you actually want Elara back? Even a little bit?"

Thane went dead silent. It wasn't an awkward silence, though. He was actually processing it, weighing the words, looking for the real answer.

Then, his expression cleared. The truth had been right there the whole time.

"Caspian, seriously?" Thane sighed, sounding completely exhausted. He turned around to face him. "We’ve been boys since we were in diapers. You really have to ask me that?"

Caspian didn't flinch. He just waited.

"Remember our little trick?" Thane let out a dry, humorless chuckle. "My folks would force me to go hang out with her. So I’d drag you into a corner, throw my clothes on you—same shirt, same pants, the whole deal—and leave you stuck playing with her while I snuck out the back."

Caspian’s hands balled into fists.

Oh, he remembered. He’d spent years trying to bury those memories, but they came rushing back. Every single time. He remembered how, at the end of the day when the parents showed up, Elara would run straight past him to Thane.

She’d gush about Thane. She’d giggle about some joke "Thane" had made, completely clueless that the boy she’d spent the last four hours with wasn't him.

Neither of them ever set her straight.

"So what?" Caspian asked, his voice completely deadpan. "What's your point?"

"It tells you everything, man." Thane looked back out at the yard. "I hated being around her back then, and I still do. Look, she’s hot. Obviously. I’m not blind. But I’ve never liked her. Not in that way, not even for a second." He paused, letting it sink in. "With her, it’s strictly business. That’s why I let things slide. She’s an asset, and I’m a realist. End of story."

He paused, letting the words hang in the air. Then he turned back, his eyes narrowing. He looked sharp, focused, like he’d finally gotten to the real point of this conversation.

"But hey, since we’re playing twenty questions—" Thane locked his eyes onto Caspian’s face, reading him like only a lifelong friend could. "I’ve been watching you. You’ve broken every single rule you set for yourself since you got here. Every. Single. One." He leaned in. "Are you falling for her too? Just like the rest of those idiots?"

Caspian held his gaze, feeling a weird crack in his chest. This whole mess went way back, and it was a hell of a lot more complicated than Thane realized.

"Yeah, I broke my rules," Caspian said slowly. "But not my code. There's a difference."

Thane started to say something.

"A massive difference," Caspian cut him off. His tone was a brick wall. Subject closed.

Thane rubbed the back of his neck. Usually, Caspian would immediately shut down any talk about Elara. This time, he didn't deny it, but he didn't admit to it either.

Thane dropped it. Not because he was happy with the answer, but because he knew when he was hitting a stone wall. No point in bruising his knuckles.

"Whatever," Thane said, squaring his shoulders and switching back to his leader voice. "Look, I need your take on something else. I’m going to throw the next moon festival right here." He shot a quick look at Caspian. "It’s been ages since we hosted one. I figure we get everyone together, get them talking about the blood moon and fixing the bond. We need to build some hype before the actual ritual."

Caspian processed the idea.

His Shaman was still trying to pin down the exact date for the blood moon. Every time Caspian thought about the timeline, his stomach did a weird twist. Something felt off.

"Good call," Caspian said. "If we drag the city wolves out here and get them hyped on the full moon energy, they’ll buy into the ritual. It’s hard to back out when you’re already in the thick of it."

Thane nodded. "Exactly."

That bad feeling in Caspian’s chest flared up again, sharper this time. He stared hard at Thane. "Hey. What if Briar isn't your fated mate?"

The question hung in the air like lead.

Thane swallowed hard. He kept his eyes glued to the empty yard, staring at nothing. Before he could even open his mouth, Caspian hit him again. "What if it's Sera?"
