Chapter 317

Aurelia chewed on that for a beat. Then she nodded. "Silver hair. Super long. But, like, nobody ever sees her face."

Caspian’s brow furrowed hard. *No face?* He opened his mouth to press her, but she beat him to it, twisting under the covers with a massive, theatrical yawn. "Daddy, I'm wiped."

Classic Aurelia. When she checked out, she was out. You couldn't bribe her, beg her, or grill her. Game over.

Right on cue, she flipped onto her side and shut her eyes. Two minutes later, her breathing went soft and steady. Total peace. Meanwhile, she’d just dropped a nuclear truth-bomb that was going to trash his sleep all night.

He stood there, staring down at her in the dead silence of the bedroom. His brain was spinning at a million miles an hour. He backed away from the mattress. Only one guy could help him make sense of this.

He whipped out his phone and buzzed Lysander. One ring. Two. Three. He was just about to cut the call when the line clicked open.

"Chief... Caspian?" Lysander rasped, sounding like he’d been dragged out of a coma. He let out a heavy yawn, then snapped awake. "Wait. Is Sera okay? Did something happen?"

Caspian started to speak, but the words died in his throat. His gaze snapped to the door. He froze, every muscle locking up. A shadow stood in the doorway. Dead quiet. Just lurking.

Every soldier instinct screamed. His face turned to stone, eyes locked on the dark shape. "Hold on," he muttered into the phone, his voice dropping to a lethal whisper.

He dropped the phone to his chest and glared into the dark.

"Linnea."

He didn't yell, but the sheer weight in his voice could halt a train.

A beat. Then the door creaked open. The hallway light hit her. Linnea stood there, wild red bedhead spilling over her shoulders. She wore a slip of a nightgown—practically see-through, clinging to every curve.

Caspian’s jaw clenched. His eyes went black.

"Yo, Caspian? You there?" Lysander’s sleepy voice buzzed from the receiver.

Caspian ripped his eyes off her and shoved the phone back to his ear. "I need a favor."

Lysander groaned, a long, pathetic sound. "Ugh, please tell me you didn't call just because you have insomnia. Dude, I'm dead on my feet." Another yawn. "My desk is literally buried tomorrow, and with Sera out, I'm screwed."

Hearing her name actually made the knot in Caspian's shoulders loosen a bit. "She's fine."

That did it. Lysander snapped awake instantly. The sudden shift almost made Caspian laugh. "Wait, what? She's okay?"

"One hundred percent fine," Caspian said.

Lysander exhaled hard. "Oh, thank God."

Caspian leaned back. "She's coming in tomorrow. So don't dump extra crap on her desk before I show up."

Lysander completely tuned out the warning. He was already buzzing. "Hold on—the cure worked? Seriously? How? What'd you do? Dude, you gotta teach me!"

Caspian pinched his nose. He really should've expected this.

"Man, I gotta tell my folks," Lysander babbled, laughing. "Actually, wait, don't tell me now. Save it for tomorrow. I want the whole story face-to-face." He kept going, a mile a minute. "When I finally find a girl, I'll be a total pro."

Caspian actually laughed at that. "Trust me, kid. Your parents already know the trick."

Lysander paused, instantly suspicious. "Wait, what's that supposed to mean?"

"Exactly what I said," Caspian shrugged. "They know how it works."

Two seconds of dead air. Then: "Then why the hell didn't they tell me?!" The sheer outrage made Caspian grin. Now he got why Elara liked having this idiot around.

"Because watching you struggle is way more fun."

"That is so messed up."

"Life's tough, buddy."

Lysander grumbled something salty. Caspian glanced back at Linnea. Time to end this. "Keep your pants on. I'll lay it all out tomorrow."

Lysander started to protest, but Caspian hung up on him mid-sentence.

The second the screen went black, Caspian's smile died. His face turned back to ice. He locked eyes with Linnea.

His eyes dragged slowly down her body, taking in the sheer, skimpy fabric, then snapped back up to her face.

"We have rules here," he said, his voice dripping with disgust. "What the hell are you wearing?"

Linnea gripped the doorframe, her knuckles turning white. She ducked her head, playing the classic, fragile victim. It would've worked on anyone else. But Caspian knew her game.

"I'm sorry," she whispered, the words tumbling out way too fast, totally rehearsed. "I didn't know you were in here. I was just checking on Mari. The light was on, and I wanted to turn it off if she was asleep."

Caspian just stared. Cold. Silent. Panicking, she scrambled to fill the silence. "She seemed kind of off earlier. Wanted some space, so I left her alone."

On paper, it made sense. But Caspian knew her playbook. For years, she’d played the sweet, innocent girl whenever she got caught. People fell for it. He didn't. Not anymore. And honestly, that wasn't even the point.

He narrowed his eyes. How many times did he have to spell it out? How many warnings had he already wasted on her? He was sick of it.

"We're done with this. Right now."
