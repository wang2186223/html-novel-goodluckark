Chapter 249

It was just bad timing, really. Asher’s stomach started growling right between the lobby and the first ward. Elara shot Rhys that silent, desperate mom-look—the one that says *I can't be in two places at once, please take him.*

Rhys didn't complain. The plan was simple: Caspian would drop him at the packhouse, Rhys would grab his keys, feed the kid, and bring him back. Easy-peasy. Done.

Caspian walked back into the clinic expecting everything to be fine.

Instead, he heard Elara. Her voice was tight, sharp, screaming Rhys's name. That tone alone told him everything he needed to know before he even hit the door.

Inside his head, Gideon growled. *Kill him.*

No thinking. No planning. Caspian’s body just reacted.

He stormed in and clocked Thane right in the jaw. No holding back. He put every ounce of Gideon's rage into that fist. It was way worse than the hit Briar took on the track.

Thane’s head whipped back. He stumbled, clutching his face, his wolf scrambling to fix the damage.

The healing took a second. Thane stood there, eyes glassy, jaw throbbing, looking totally lost.

"Caspian," Thane choked out, totally shocked. "What the hell did you just do?"

It was a massive line crossed. Any Alpha knew that hitting another Commander on his own turf wasn't just rude—it was a declaration of war.

But Caspian didn't give a shit. Not even a little bit.

"What, I'm just supposed to stand here and watch you keep hurting her?"

In Elara's mind, Blaze snapped. *We're leaving. Now. And we are never coming back.*

Elara didn't argue. Whatever patience she had left was completely gone.

"I just wanted an apology," Thane muttered, sounding pathetic and empty. "That's all. Just—"

"An apology?" Elara’s voice exploded, raw and loud. "Then give me back my daughter!"

She slammed the medical tray. Vials and bottles shattered on the floor, glass and liquid spraying everywhere. She was already moving before the noise stopped.

"I'm done." She ripped off her white coat, threw it on the floor, standing there in her gym clothes.

The crash brought Charles running from the next room, his face tight with professional worry.

He froze in the doorway, eyes darting around. Caspian standing like a statue, Thane holding his jaw, Elara heading for the exit. He needed a second to process the mess.

"Chief Commander," Charles said carefully. "Director Sera."

Nobody looked at him. Charles glanced at the bed, saw Briar pale and out cold, and his face fell.

"Co-Director," he said, rushing over. "What happened to you?"

Elara let out a bitter, humorless laugh. Then, the only sound was her sneakers squeaking against the floor as she walked out.

She hadn't even changed. She'd rushed straight from the gym to save these people, and now the cure was in pieces on the floor. The whole morning was a total waste.

The truth finally hit Thane like a brick. He watched her walk away and realized exactly what he'd just thrown away.

"Sera," he called out, his tough-guy voice completely gone. He sounded small. Desperate. "Where are you going?"

She didn't even look back.

She reached the exit and stopped.

Outside, the sky had finally opened up. The wind was whipping the trees, and the rain was coming down in heavy, bouncing sheets.

Thunder rumbled across the sky, sounding like a final judgment.

"I'll drive," Caspian said, appearing next to her. No drama, just a quiet offer.

Elara stared at the rain, stepped back, and didn't argue.

Behind them, Thane scrambled up. "Caspian. Please. Tell her I'm sorry."

Caspian turned, his face completely blank. Any old friendship they had was dead and buried.

"She gave up everything to come back and help you. And you ruined it," Caspian said quietly. "This is on you."

Then he was gone, the door swinging shut behind them.

Charles knelt in the broken glass, carefully checking the shattered vials to see what he could save.

He tipped the last drops from a cracked bottle.

"Don't sweat it, Chief," Charles said without looking up. "I can work with this. I can piece enough together to copy the cure."

Thane stood there, silent. He knew it wouldn't be perfect. There would be mistakes, gaps.

A copy wasn't the real thing, and they both knew it. But he had no other choice.

"Do what you can," Thane said. "And check Briar. I have to go get Sera."

He ran out into the freezing downpour. It soaked him instantly, making everything feel heavy and real.

The storm was still ramping up, the wind whipping in circles.

Behind him, inside the clinic, one of the treated patients spoke. Whatever she said made Thane freeze dead in his tracks.
