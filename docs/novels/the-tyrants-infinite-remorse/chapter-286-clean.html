NovelVibe

Chapter 289

"Hey, can we go somewhere... less tight?" Elara scanned the walls. They felt like they were squeezing in.

Caspian just shook his head. "Nope. Can't talk about this out in the open." He spun on his heel, heading down the hallway. "My room's got more space. Let's go."

He reached the door before she could even think to argue. He swung it open, stepped back, and waved her in. No drama. She slipped past him and froze right past the threshold.

Not what she expected. At all.

Sure, the bed was a monster—took up a whole wall with room to spare. But she barely looked at it. Instead, her eyes went to the seating. Two big-ass couches faced each other over a low coffee table on one side. On the other, another pair of sofas did the same. It looked like a freaking living room, not a bedroom. The air felt heavy, but not in a bad way. More like someone spent a lot of late nights pacing around, thinking. It actually felt... cozy. Go figure.

"We can hit the study if this weirds you out," Caspian said behind her. "But it's too formal in there." He walked past her toward the couches. "And the garden is only good for small talk. Not this."

Elara walked over and dropped onto a couch. She sank way deeper into the cushions than she meant to. Caspian poured some water from a pitcher, handed her the glass, and sat down. Same couch, but he kept his distance. A few deliberate inches. Like he'd mapped out the exact gap beforehand.

She gripped the cold glass with both hands.

"Okay, first," she said. "Tell me about Linnea."

Caspian didn't blink. No hesitation. He promised he'd talk, and he was doing it. His voice stayed totally level.

"She saved my mom's life." He leaned forward, elbows on his knees, staring straight ahead. "My mom was heading back to the pack. A rogue jumped her. Linnea was just out there—lone wolf, no pack, totally on her own. She jumped in. The rogue beat the hell out of her, but it bought my mom enough time to get our fighters."

He let out a sharp breath. "Mom couldn't let it go. Felt like she owed her everything. So she brought her home. And she figured the best way to pay her back was to hand her my hand in marriage."

Elara squeezed her glass. "So... you guys are married." The words felt like ash in her mouth.

Caspian let out a dry laugh. "No. Absolutely not." He sounded dead serious. "If I were married, trust me, everyone would know. I don't do secrets. That's not my style."

Elara had her doubts about that, but she shut up and let him talk.

"Linnea's chill. Chill and practical," he said. "She was straight with me from day one. Said she liked me." He stopped, letting the silence hang so she'd hear what came next. "And I was straight right back. Told her I didn't feel it. Never would. Told her I wasn't marrying her." He leaned back, sinking into the cushions. "She was cool about it. Better than most. Then, somewhere down the line, she got super attached to Aurelia. That was like, three years back."

Elara chewed on that. "Three years under the same roof and you felt zero?"

"We didn't live together. But even if we did, wouldn't matter. Nothing would change." He looked right at her, totally unfazed by the accusation. "She lived with my folks. Spent almost all her time there. Then Aurelia's nanny quit. Her kid got into a bad accident, so she had to leave, no idea when she'd be back. That's when everything flipped."

"Aurelia needed stability, and Linnea just stepped up. Didn't even ask." He took a beat. "Before that, I was mostly living in California. When my parents came to town, they'd crash with me in Carli. During that last Sovereign club meeting, Aurelia had a massive episode. I couldn't leave her. That's why I dragged the whole meeting from here to the Bohemian Groove."

Caspian locked eyes with her. "But honestly? Me moving back here for good? Had nothing to do with any of that crap."

Elara froze. "Then why?"

"You."

Just like that. No big dramatic pause, no romantic music. He said it like he was stating the weather. Just a plain, simple fact.

Elara could only stare.

"We'll talk about that in a second," Caspian said, catching her expression. "But first, get this straight about Linnea. She's Aurelia's nanny. That's it. I pay her a ton, and she knows exactly where we stand." He didn't look away. "But if she makes you uncomfortable, or if you just don't like her vibe, I'll fire her. Done. Easy call."

Elara shook her head, a weird knot tightening in her chest. "Hold on." She stared at him. "Why the hell does my opinion dictate who you keep around?"

Caspian's face twitched. Not quite hurt, but damn close. A tiny, exhausted smirk touched his lips, never reaching his eyes.

"I think," he muttered, "we should talk about Aurelia's mom instead." He reached into his pocket, grabbed his phone, and shoved it toward her.

Elara grabbed it automatically. Locked. She stared at the dark screen, then looked up, raising an eyebrow.

"You know the passcode," he said. He stood up, heading for the door with that same casual, slow stride. "Gonna go check on Aurelia. And grab us some food."

Then he slipped out, the door clicking shut behind him.

Elara sat there in the dead silence, holding the phone. Her heart was suddenly hammering against her ribs like crazy. She stared at the lock screen, her thumb shaking slightly over the glass.

She didn't know the code. Except... her thumb moved anyway. Tap, tap, tap, tap.

The screen swiped open. Holy shit. Caspian's passcode was her passcode.

Before she could even process how weird that was, the home screen loaded. The wallpaper popped up, and the room literally tilted. Every drop of blood rushed out of her face.
