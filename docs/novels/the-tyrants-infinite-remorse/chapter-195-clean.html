Chapter 197

A massive, slow yawn crackled through the receiver. Arthur sounded half-dead and totally pissed off.

"Thane, seriously? Let me sleep for once," he groaned. "Your drama, your problem. Keep me out of it."

Thane squeezed his phone until his knuckles turned white. He locked his jaw, fighting the panic clawing at his throat. He had to play it cool, but time was running out fast.

"Dad, listen. A chemical bomb just went off in the territory," Thane spat out, keeping his voice level but tight. "I need you to call Sera. Now. She’s got the antidote, and we need her. Our guys are dropping, paralyzed. If we don't stop this, another blast could hit any second, and God knows how many more—"

Another loud yawn cut him off. Thane’s face went cold, eyes burning with pure frustration. Then Arthur’s voice snapped back, sharp as a knife.

"Hey, Thane," Arthur growled, fully awake now. "Elara was literally dying in bed from that exact same poison, and you didn't give a single shit."

The guilt hit Thane like a punch to the gut. But Arthur wasn't done.

"And now," his father sneered, his tone freezing over, "you expect her to pack her bags, leave the city, and play savior for your little club?"

Thane took a slow, shaky breath. He had to keep this professional, keep his emotions out of it.

"She doesn't need to travel," he said, forcing a calm, logical tone. "I'll handle the logistics. She just makes the meds, and I'll grab them myself. Name a price. I'll pay whatever she wants."

The line went quiet. Then came a heavy, disappointed sigh.

"Throwing money at it..." Arthur muttered, sounding completely disgusted. "And here I thought you actually felt bad for what you did."

Thane sucked in a breath. The air felt like lead.

"Look, I appreciate what she did," he muttered, the words tasting like ash. "She saved Asher. I'm not ungrateful."

Then his voice went hard again. "But let's be real here. Without Briar, there is no heir. She's the one who actually gave you a grandkid."

A sharp, brutal silence hung in the air.

"Wrong," Arthur said, his voice slicing right through Thane's bullshit. "Sera gave me my grandchild." He paused, letting the next words sink in. "And you let her die."

Thane froze, his heart skipping a beat.

"Thane," Arthur said, his voice dropping to a deadly whisper, "if your hands are so clean... why aren't you calling her yourself?"

"I tried!" Thane snapped, jumping on the defensive. He hesitated, frowning, trying to find a way to spin it. There wasn't one. "She trashed me," he mumbled, his voice cracking slightly. "Then she blocked my number." He let out a dry, pathetic laugh. "And... yeah. I had it coming."

The ugly truth hung between them.

"But if you don't want this entire pack turned to ash," Thane pleaded, panic creeping back into his voice, "then please, Dad. Help me out here."

Arthur let the silence drag on, punishing him. Finally, he spoke, his voice cold and business-like.

"You said you'd pay, right? Fine. Get your checkbook out."

Thane sat up straight, his shoulders tensing.

"I'll try to talk some sense into her," Arthur continued, "but get one thing straight. She's not the same girl anymore. Her price tag just went way up."

"I get it," Thane cut in, losing his patience. "But a doctor is a doctor. Pack, human, whatever. They have an oath. You don't let people die just because you're holding a grudge."

Arthur let out a low, mocking chuckle.

"Look, I don't know crap about medicine," he said, "but let me tell you something. Sera isn't some run-of-the-mill doctor." You could hear the pride dripping from his voice. "She's a prodigy. A genius chemist. What she does is straight-up magic."

He paused. "Just say it, Thane. You threw away a masterpiece."

Thane ground his teeth, a wave of hot anger washing over him. It wasn't just about the medicine. Elara had built a freaking empire, way bigger than he ever imagined, way bigger than he'd ever given her credit for. Like MindNest. Just thinking about it made his stomach turn. It was a goldmine. Everyone wanted a piece of it, including him. But she wouldn't even let him buy in. God, she was a stubborn, annoying bitch.

"Dad," Thane snapped, pulling himself together and focusing on what actually mattered. "Asher is my only priority." He didn't blink. "And Briar is the one who gave him to me." He made sure his voice left zero room for debate. "Now. Are you going to call her or what?"

"Yeah, I'll call," Arthur said, his tone turning dark. "But you better be ready to swallow whatever pill she makes you take."

Thane frowned, a cold sweat breaking out despite his bravado. Elara's terms were never easy. She always found a way to make you bleed more than you planned.

"Whatever it takes," he said anyway, not hesitating for a second.

The line went dead.

Thane slowly pulled the phone from his ear. He looked over at Rhys, a smug, cocky smirk creeping back onto his face.

"Told you," he muttered. "She won't say no to my old man."

Rhys raised an eyebrow, totally unconvinced. "So, she said yes?" he asked, trying to keep his voice flat but failing to hide the skepticism.

"Not yet," Thane said, but his eyes were dead confident. "But she's going to."

And for once, he wasn't full of shit. Half an hour later, his phone buzzed.

Arthur.

Thane snatched it up, sitting bolt upright, his chest tightening.

"Listen up," Arthur said, his voice dead serious, all the annoyance gone. "It took some serious begging, but here are her terms—"
