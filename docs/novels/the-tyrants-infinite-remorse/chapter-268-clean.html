Chapter 271

Caspian’s thumb hovered, tapping out the last digit. Then—*smack*. Something hard clipped his knuckles, sending the screen spinning across the hardwood. It hit with a sharp crack. Miracle of the day: no shattered glass.

"Caspian," Elara said, her voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "You gave me your word."

He spun on her, jaw locked tight. "Yeah? And you got your payoff, didn't you?" A muscle twitched in his cheek, his eyes turning pitch black.

"Briar literally wants you in a body bag," she snapped back. "You think she’s just gonna wake up tomorrow and play nice? Seriously?"

Honestly? He wanted to rip Briar apart. No quick, clean bullet. He wanted her to bleed slow, to feel every ounce of the hell she’d put Elara through.

The brutal image flashed in his mind, sweet and sharp. But then Elara stepped up, and that cold, chess-player look took over her face. She was already ten moves ahead. She always was.

"Briar and Thane deserve each other," Elara muttered, sounding like she was reading off a dry-cleaning receipt. Totally blank. "They’re a match made in hell."

The tone gave him chills. It wasn't the words; it was the dead, hollow vibe underneath. The kind of quiet that only comes when you bury your demons so deep they never see daylight. It settled in his chest like ice.

He stared at her. "You won, Sera," he muttered, stepping closer. "You’re on top. So why do you look like you just lost everything?" He hesitated, praying he was wrong. "Are you still hung up on him?"

He never saw the hand coming.

*Crack.*

Her palm slapped his cheek so hard his neck popped.

This wasn't like the slap back during the Dominic mess. This was pure, unadulterated grief. Rage. Desperation.

"Hung up on him?" Her voice cracked, rising to a screech that sucked the air right out of the room. Sharp, wicked claws slid from her fingertips. For a split second, he thought Blaze was going to burst through her skin and tear him to shreds.

But she forced it down. The wolf retreated. The claws slipped back under. Suddenly, she was just a broken girl shivering in the dim light.

"They tried to murder my baby," she whispered, the quietness of it way worse than the screaming. Tears finally spilled over, hot and fast. "And even if she’s alive, Thane stood there and said she should die." Her throat hitched. "Nobody even knows if she’s breathing out there, or if she’s—" She choked on the words, pressing her lips into a hard line. "And you’re standing there asking if I still love that monster? Are you insane?"

Caspian opened his mouth, but nothing came out.

Guilt hit him like a physical punch to the gut. Watching her shoulders shake, something locked deep inside his chest started rattling the cage.

Before his brain could veto the move, he stepped in.

He threw his arms around her. She shoved his chest, trying to back away, but he locked his grip. She pushed again, hard, but he didn't budge. Finally, like a dam bursting, she collapsed against him, sobbing right into his shirt.

"I'm sorry, Sera." Caspian leaned down, pressing a quick, soft kiss to her hair. He didn't even realize he'd done it until she went totally rigid.

She tried to pull back, but he held her tighter.

"Hey, easy," he murmured against her ear. "I'm not trying to pull anything while you're down. That's not what this is. Promise."

That did it. The last of her walls crumbled. She melted against him, letting her weight drop, finally resting for the first time in god knows how long.

He ran his fingers through her hair, gently untangling the strands. Slowly, her breathing hitched, then leveled out. The shaking stopped.

She didn't love Thane. Not even close.

The realization washed over him like a warm wave. He held onto it. He didn't need it for some grand plan. He just needed to know. For himself.

Her breathing went deep and slow as the tears dried up. When he tilted his head to check on her, her fingers gripped his shirt, bunching the fabric. She wasn't letting go. Just like the old days.

So he didn't make her.

He eased them both down onto the mattress, moving slow, treating her like glass.

She curled right into his side, head on his chest, and within two minutes, she was dead to the world.

He stayed awake, staring up at the dark ceiling. His mind drifted back fifteen years. That first trip to the city, leaving her behind. Everything went sideways when he came back a few months later. Did she hurt him on purpose, or was there some massive secret? She didn't even seem to remember him like that now. Not once had she said sorry.

That night, they both slept hard. No tossing and turning. Just deep, heavy sleep that dragged them under.

Elara dreamed of weird, vivid things. A kid. Tiny hands. A crying sound she kept running toward but could never reach.

Then her phone buzzed, and her eyes shot open.

Morning light was already streaking through the curtains, painting the floor yellow. She had no clue what time it was, but it was late.

Then she realized where she was.

Somehow, she’d ended up sprawled right on top of Caspian. Her cheek was pressed to his chest, her legs tangled up with his, and his arms were locked around her waist like she belonged there. Her heart started hammering so loud she was sure he’d hear it.

She tried to slide off him, super quiet, but his hand tightened on her hip, pinning her right back down.

"Sera," he mumbled, his voice thick and raspy with sleep. The low rumble of it sent a weird shiver straight down her spine.

"Caspian, god, I'm sorry, I didn't mean to—"

He didn't let her finish. He just pulled her down and shut her up with a kiss.
