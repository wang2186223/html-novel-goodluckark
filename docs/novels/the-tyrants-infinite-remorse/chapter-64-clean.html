Chapter 64

The question hit the room and just sat there. Boom. Like a rock thrown into a quiet pond, sending waves everywhere.

Nobody blinked. On paper, the guy did nothing wrong. No attitude, no insults. But he’d just stepped right over a massive red line.

The air turned thick and weird. Some things you just don't tell normal people. Our world has rules, period. You don't gossip about pack business. You don't chat about it over lattes or laugh it off. Those boundaries are set in concrete.

"Just family," Elara said. Her voice was flat. She didn't offer a single detail more.

Lysander glared at Julian. It wasn't a friendly look. It was heavy, sharp, and totally territorial. Julian's internal alarms went off. The threat hung in the air, loud and clear without a word: *Bring her back whole, or else.*

"Hey, we should probably get going," Elara blurted. She saw the blowup coming. Lysander was just doing his big-bad-wolf routine. It was in his DNA.

Julian moved fast. He grabbed the door, held it open, and let her slip past before clicking it shut. He trailed a half-step behind her. No show, just natural backup.

Lysander clenched his teeth. Behind him, his mom watched the whole thing. She didn't miss a beat. A soft throat-clear made him spin around.

"Just tell her, honey," she murmured. Gentle, but heavy. She was sick of watching her kid shoot himself in the foot. She had the scars to prove she knew how fast time runs out. But she was also done with his BS. He was letting his shot slip away while playing the cool guy.

"No way," Lysander snapped. He didn't even think. The word slammed down like a vault door. He wanted it to sound tough, not terrified. He puffed his chest out, trying to make his own body believe the lie.

His mom didn't push. She just looked him over. She saw the tight jaw, the white knuckles, the way he avoided looking down the driveway. "You think keeping your mouth shut keeps you safe?" she asked. No attitude, just stripping away his armor.

"I'm not her type, okay?" he mumbled, looking away. Like that solved everything. Like love had a checklist. He shoved his hands deep in his pockets, squinting, trying to look smart instead of scared.

"And that human guy is?" she pushed, her voice getting sharper. She’d seen Julian’s smile. She’d seen how easy they were together. And she’d seen her son’s face drop when the door shut.

Lysander’s jaw ticked. The pressure was building. "She likes them cold," he finally spat out, like pulling teeth. He sounded bitter, mostly at himself. "Guys who don't smile. Guys who play hard to get, who make her beg for a little warmth." He let out a dry laugh, his eyes going dark. "But Julian? He's funny. He talks, and it's easy. I'm scared he'll just smile his way into her head until she forgets I even exist."

His mom let out a long, heavy sigh. She stepped right into his space, blocking his view of the empty driveway.

"Then open your mouth before she's gone," she whispered, but she meant it. She saw right through his pride to the panic underneath. If he waited, he was done.

He shook his head, looking miserable. "I already screwed up. I should've protected her kid, not given the baby away. If I could find her child—fix that mess—maybe then I'd have the balls to tell her."

Across the driveway, Julian popped the passenger door for Elara. He waited till she settled in, then clicked it shut. He jogged around, slid behind the wheel, and cranked the engine. The engine purred.

"So," she said, trying to sound casual, though her eyes gave away her nerves. "Where are we going?"

Julian looked her over, then hit the gas. The car rolled forward. "Surprise," he said, keeping it mysterious.

Elara just nodded. Honestly, she didn't even know what to ask anyway. Dating wasn't her thing. She'd watched other people do it, sure, but she'd never actually done it. She felt totally naked, like an actor on stage who forgot her lines.

Julian hit the radio. Some low, chill tune started playing, taking the edge off the quiet. The hum of the tires, the easy sway of the turn, his cologne mixing with the leather seats—it actually felt pretty good.

They pulled up to the spot, and something warm bloomed in her chest. Just like that, everything she thought she knew about Julian flipped upside down.
