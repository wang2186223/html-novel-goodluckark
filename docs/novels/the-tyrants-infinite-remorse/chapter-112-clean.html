Chapter 113

Elara shut her eyes tight. *In, out.* She forced her lungs into a slow, steady rhythm. Just sleep. If she could pass out long enough, her body might actually heal. Sleeping for three straight days was gonna screw up everything—her stock portfolio, her freelance cyber gigs, the MindNest chaos, and her hospital shifts. But honestly? Screw it. The pain was a total bitch. She had no choice.

By day three, things got brutal. Waking up just to shove some food down her throat felt like running a marathon. Sharp, nasty cramps ripped through her gut. She gritted her teeth, fingers clawing the edge of the mattress. Every breath felt like inhaling lead. Moving was pure hell. But Lyra was right there every time Elara blinked open her eyes. Lyra kept her cool, handing over water and soft, easy-to-swallow bites while whispering sweet, comforting stuff.

"The last wave is always the worst, babe," Lyra said softly one afternoon. She helped Elara prop herself up against the pillows. Lyra had seen this shit a million times. "Once this peak passes, you'll start feeling human again." Elara gave a pathetic little nod. Her eyelids weighed a ton. She slid back under the duvet, ready to dive right back into the dark, quiet void. Then the door clicked open. Heavy footsteps thudded across the floor. She didn't even bother looking up.

"It’s Caspian," Lysander said, waving his phone. He sounded totally annoyed and over it. "I told him you’re sick, but the guy won't take no for an answer. He’s demanding to talk to you."

Boom. Adrenaline spiked. Caspian’s name cut right through Elara's brain fog like a shot of espresso. And then the memory hit her—the wolfsbane. Someone had spiked her drink. They tried to put her in the dirt. No way in hell was she letting that slide.

"Tell him I'll be at his office tomorrow," Elara mumbled into her pillow, eyes still shut.

Lysander scowled. He stared at the lump under the blankets, totally lost. What the hell did she need to see Caspian for? He still didn't know what went down at the club. Julian had muttered something about poisoning earlier, and the whole thing smelled incredibly fishy.

"You're in no shape to go anywhere, Elara," Lysander muttered.

Elara didn't answer. She just yanked the duvet over her head.

"Relax, she’ll be good to go by tomorrow," Lyra chimed in, tossing him a look. "You won't even tell she was dying today. Ah, the magic of the menstrual cycle."

Elara wanted to say something, but her brain shut down. She blacked out before she could even open her mouth.

Lysander sighed, walked out into the hallway, and put the phone back to his ear.

"She said she'll see you tomorrow at your office," he told the guy.

On the other end, Caspian scowled. He wasn't buying it. He was still paranoid about the wolfsbane.

"You literally just said she’s sick," Caspian barked. "If she’s that bad, maybe I should just drive over."

"Trust me, she doesn't want to see you right now," Lysander said, keeping his voice flat and firm. But Caspian wasn't having it.

"I don't care. I'm coming anyway."

Lysander pinched the bridge of his nose. He knew Caspian. The guy would show up on their doorstep in ten minutes if he didn't stop him. Time for some brutal honesty.

"Look, man. It’s a PMS thing," Lysander blurted out.

Dead silence on the line.

For like five seconds, nobody said a word.

Then Caspian cleared his throat, sounding suddenly very cautious. "Wait. Is that... like, a she-wolf thing?"

He let out a quiet breath of relief. Okay, so not wolfsbane. But he knew about this. Some of his female employees complained about it too. Apparently, the cramps hit them so hard they could only do one thing: sleep it off.

"Yep. That’s what my mom always said," Lysander replied dryly.

Caspian exhaled slowly, processing. "Right. Okay. Tell her I’ll be at my desk by eight sharp."

But when Caspian walked into his office the next morning, he got a massive shock. Elara was already sitting in the lobby. She looked flawless. Not a single trace of the girl who’d been writhing in pain a day ago.

But Elara wasn't there to chat. She was there for something Caspian was absolutely not ready to face.
