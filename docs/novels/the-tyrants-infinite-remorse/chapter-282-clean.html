Chapter 285

Caspian froze, back turned. He wasn't about to let some random interruption ruin his vibe.

"What," he grunted. Not even trying to sound polite.

Linnea crept up anyway, like she was walking on eggshells around a wild animal. "Aurelia won't eat a thing." She kept her voice flat, just giving him the facts.

"Yeah, I'll be right there," Caspian muttered, eyes still glued to Elara. He wanted that soft look back in her eyes. He needed to keep her hooked until he figured out why she'd ripped his heart out and what happened to their history.

His gaze drifted past Elara's shoulder. Linnea wasn't stupid; she got the hint. Time to bounce.

But before she turned around, Linnea locked eyes with Elara. It wasn't a glare, but it sure as hell wasn't friendly either. Just a weird, messy vibe Elara couldn't quite decode. Then Linnea slipped away, her quiet steps fading down the hall toward Aurelia's room.

Caspian parted his lips to speak.

"Who's the girl?" Elara beat him to the punch.

A flicker of regret crossed his face. Right. He'd offered to be friends, and friends ask questions. He couldn't just shut her out anymore.

"Look, it's a whole thing," he said, locking eyes with her. "And we can't do this here." He scanned the bright, sterile hallway. Nurses staring, zero privacy. "Tomorrow. The park with Aurelia, or my place. Your call."

Elara took a slow, deep breath. Tomorrow was a nightmare. Pitching new business ideas all morning, then back-to-back surgeries all night. "Tomorrow's a no-go. Let's do the day after."

"My place, then," Caspian said, shutting down any debate. "The park is Aurelia's weekly thing. Not the place for this talk anyway."

He stared her down, that heavy, unblinking look she couldn't ignore anymore. "You know how I operate. I can't talk about this stuff in public." He pursed his lips, thinking. "Want me to pick you up? Day after tomorrow?"

His voice softened, losing its sharp edge. For once, he looked almost... fragile. Elara wavered. "Uh, I should probably check with Lysander first."

Right on cue, her phone buzzed in her hand. Speak of the devil. Lysander.

She took a step back to get some space and swiped the screen. "Hey, Lysander. Yeah, I'm just leaving... No, don't worry, I'll just grab an Uber."

"I can drive you," Caspian offered from behind her.

At the exact same time, Lysander's dry voice came through the speaker. "Yeah, I can literally see you guys from here. I'm coming inside to get you." Classic Lysander—calm, but totally judging.

Elara squeezed her eyes shut. God give me strength. "Fine. I'll meet you at the front doors." She hung up.

Caspian was smirking. That classic, annoying look of his. "Still don't trust me, huh?" Just stating facts. No anger, just calling it like it was.

Elara kept her mouth shut. Silence was her answer.

"Whatever," he shrugged, letting it slide. "Since you've got time to kill, come wait in Aurelia's room."

"Sure," she said. She actually wanted to get a closer look at the kid anyway—maybe spot a birthmark or something. Caspian was supposed to be single, so this sudden kid was super sketchy.

They walked back down the corridor. Caspian pushed the door open, and Aurelia's head snapped up instantly.

The kid's face lit up like a Christmas tree. Just pure, unfiltered toddler joy.

"Mommy!" she squealed. "Are you coming home with us today?"

Elara opened her mouth to set her straight, but Caspian gave her a quick, subtle head shake. She shut her mouth.

She walked over and sat on the edge of the mattress. That's when she saw the untouched food tray on the bedside table.

Elara put on her best serious doctor face. "Wait, you didn't eat a single bite of this?"

Aurelia glanced at the plate and pouted, totally dug in.

"Man, I am absolutely starving," Elara sighed, laying the drama on thick.

That did the trick. Aurelia's eyes went wide. "Then I'll feed you, Mommy!"

"Hmm, here's the thing," Elara said, pretending to think hard. "Mommy's tummy hurts because Aurelia's tummy is empty. If you eat, then I'll feel full. Magic, right?"

Aurelia stared at her, totally baffled. Across the room, Caspian let out a soft laugh.

The little girl's brain was working overtime, trying to figure out how she'd just been outsmarted.

"Fine," she huffed, sitting up straight. "I'll eat. But you have to stay."

"I can stay for a few minutes—"

"She's gotta go tonight, kiddo," Caspian cut in, his voice warm and relaxed now. "But she's coming to our house the day after tomorrow. Sound like a plan?"

Aurelia perked right back up. She ran the numbers in her head and decided she liked the deal.

"Yay! That's gonna be so much fun," she cheered. Then she got a bright idea. "Daddy, we have to cook for Mommy!" She turned to Linnea, looking for backup. "Right, Aunt Linnea?"
