Chapter 32

A kid, maybe six, was going wild in the back of a black sedan. He thrashed against the leather like the car was trying to kill him. His face was bright red, tears soaking his eyelashes and running down his cheeks. He slammed his little fists against the seat, the door, whatever he could hit. His screams were raw, echoing through the concrete parking garage, pure panic.

A woman stood frozen by the open door. She wore expensive designer clothes—perfectly tailored, pricey—but she was a total mess. Her hands shook as she hovered there, uselessly mumbling things the kid couldn't hear. She looked exhausted, terrified.

Elara didn't hesitate. She moved. Her heels clicked fast on the concrete as she ran over, years of crisis training kicking in instantly. She kept her voice low, steady, totally grounded. "Is he hurt?"

The woman shook her head fast, pulling down her sunglasses. Her eyes were red and swollen. "No... no. It's a brain thing. A neurological injury. He gets these fits." Her voice cracked. "There's no cure. He just screams until he passes out. Nothing I do works. Nothing."

Elara felt that familiar ache in her chest, but she locked it down. She crouched next to the boy, giving him space. She spoke softly, slow and steady, matching her breathing to give his frantic body a rhythm to follow. Her hands moved with practiced ease, pressing gently on his pressure points, pulling his focus away from the storm in his head. No grabbing, just holding him steady.

Slowly, the screaming stopped. The kid's fists loosened, his fingers trembling before they finally let go. He slumped against the seat, hiccuping, his breathing finally slowing down. The panic cleared, leaving nothing but quiet exhaustion.

The woman stared, completely shocked. "How... how did you do that?"

Elara pulled a notepad from her bag and scribbled fast. "Look, I can't write prescriptions," she said, her voice firm but kind. "But take this to his doctor. It's sensory stuff, behavioral protocols, some extra meds. It won't cure him, but it'll cut down the meltdowns." She ripped the page out and handed it over.

The woman grabbed it like a lifeline, her fingers shaking. Her composure completely shattered. "Thank you," she whispered, tears spilling over. "You have no idea what this means."

Elara nodded, looking at the quiet kid one last time, then stepped back so the mom could shut the door.

Lysander watched the whole thing, a tiny, knowing smile on his face. He'd seen her do this a thousand times over the years. Mostly with kids, including Asher. Then the smile died. The memory of her lost kid hit him like a fist. If only he hadn't hated her back then. If only he'd made a different choice, found another way.

By the time he snapped out of it, Elara was already walking away. He followed, keeping his distance.

The second Elara walked into Ashkael Holdings, she felt dizzy. First time here, and the place was massive. A giant glass-and-steel monolith cutting into the Manhattan skyline. The tallest building around. Everyone inside was rushing—suits, phones glued to ears, eyes forward, driven by schedules and hustle.

She saw the front desk and walked straight up. "I'm here for Orion."

The receptionist—barely out of high school—froze. Her eyes went wide, shocked that someone just dropped his name like that. But she kept her professional cool. "Ninetieth floor," she said. "Talk to his assistant."

"Thanks," Elara said, already moving.

She slipped into the elevator right before the doors shut. Five other people stood inside, stiff and silent. Lysander squeezed in at the last second, keeping quiet and staying out of the way.

When the doors opened on ninety, the vibe changed instantly. The ground-floor chaos was gone, replaced by quiet, expensive power. A woman in a sharp suit and glasses looked up, smiling politely. "Can I help you, ma'am?"

"Orion," Elara said flatly. "Tell him Seraphine is here."

The assistant stared like Elara had two heads. She tapped her tablet. "Uh, I just checked his schedule. You don't have an appointment."

"Tell him anyway," Elara said, her voice hard.

The assistant wanted to say no, but Elara's stare made her sweat. She hesitated, then hit the intercom. "Okay... yeah... fine," she muttered, then hung up.

A second later, she looked up. "Sorry. He says he has no business with you."

Elara froze, her mind racing. Too many eyes here. This wasn't pack territory; it's the city. One wrong move and the cops show up. "Fine," she said, cool. "I'll wait."

The assistant thought about telling her Caspian was in a long meeting and wouldn't be out anytime soon, but decided to just let her sit there and tire herself out.

Elara sat on a lobby couch. Lysander sat next to her, grabbing her hand to keep her steady. He didn't say a word, terrified of setting off the anger bubbling under her skin.

An hour passed. A door clicked open. A guy walked out of Caspian's office, followed by Orion's voice: "We'll meet at the Sovereign Circle."

Elara was up instantly. She marched over, stopping right behind him. "Orion," she said, her voice cutting through the room. "How do you sleep at night, knowing you do nothing but wreck lives and cause chaos?"

The whole floor went dead silent. Conversations stopped. Nobody moved. Every single eye locked onto Elara, the air thick and buzzing, waiting to see how Orion would handle the woman crazy enough to call him out in his own castle.
