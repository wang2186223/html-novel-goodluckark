Chapter 11

"What do you want this time, Thane?" Arthur didn't even look up from the chessboard.

Wooden pieces sat messy between him and Elara. The game was basically over, but they hadn't finished.

They’d been having a rare quiet moment. Then Thane barged in. Three people trailed right behind him, instantly killing the vibe.

Arthur hovered his hand over a rook, then dropped it. His jaw tightened.

Thane stood by the door, stiff and pissed. "Dad, seriously? This is my house too. Am I banned now or what?"

Arthur ignored him. He slid one last piece across the board, then leaned back. Elara won. Again. But she wasn't smiling. Nobody was. The room felt toxic.

Their entry ruined everything.

"Sera, how can you be so damn cold?"

Celeste’s voice cut the quiet like a slap. She stood tall, chin up, glaring at Elara like she was some nasty stain on the carpet.

Before Elara could open her mouth, Eleanor stood up. "That’s enough, Celeste," she said, quiet but hard. "You don't yell at Sera. Not under my roof."

The room went dead silent.

"Managing Director," Celeste muttered, bowing her head fast. Old habits. Her husband used to be the Centenary pack’s beta, so she knew the drill.

Pack rules were simple: once a Chief Commander or Managing Director, always one. Unless you got divorced, stepped down, or committed a crime.

Eleanor didn't run things anymore, but she still had the juice.

Celeste stood up straight, forcing a fake smile. "Look, I’m just saying, Sera’s out of control. Honestly, we spoiled her rotten."

What a joke.

They had the same eyes and cheekbones, or else nobody would believe Celeste actually gave birth to her.

"Spoiling" meant ignoring her, shutting her out, and leaving her to rot when she needed them most.

Elara finally looked up. "What do you want, Celeste?" She didn't say 'Mom.' That word was dead to her.

Celeste blinked, caught off guard.

Before she could snap back, Silas cleared his throat and stepped up. "Did you seriously just call your mother by her first name?"

Elara turned her whole body to face him. This middle-aged guy had never had her back. Not when people threw dirt on her name, not when Briar cried her fake-ass tears.

Not even when Elara lay in a hospital bed, pale and drained of blood just to keep someone else breathing.

"Chuck," Elara said, keeping her voice flat to hide the sting in her chest. "Last I checked, Briar’s your only daughter. She’s the one you believe. The one you baby. Let’s cut the crap. You’re not here for me. You’re here for her."

Guilt washed over their faces. She nailed it.

The second Thane told them Briar was in the hospital, they ran over.

But when Elara spent three months in that exact same hospital? Not a single one of them showed up. Not once.

"So what if we did?" Chuck snapped. "Maybe if you acted right, we’d actually back you up."

Elara let out a cold, empty laugh. Her eyes were ice.

"If 'acting right' means letting Briar walk all over me," she whispered, "then yeah. I’m always going to be the bad guy."

"You ungrateful little—" Chuck swung his hand up.

Too slow.

Arthur was already up. He grabbed Chuck’s wrist mid-air, twisted it hard, and shoved him back.

"Get the hell out of my house," Arthur roared. "Now!"

Thane stepped in the way. "Dad, stop taking her side. She’s a snake. She’s always been a manipulator."

Arthur glared at his son. "I’ve known Sera since she was a baby. I trust what I see. If you want to play blind, that’s on you. Now get out. Don't come back until you're ready to apologize."

Thane clenched his fists, chest heaving. He was going to figure out how Elara brainwashed his parents. Whatever it took.

"This isn't over," Thane spat. "If anything happens to Briar, I’m done with you. You're not my parents."

"Do it now," Eleanor barked, walking down the stairs, her eyes blazing. "Because Sera is never helping Briar again. Not on my watch. I should’ve kicked that leech out of the pack years ago instead of letting her grow into a parasite."

That hurt. Thane’s face darkened. Hearing his mom trash the woman he loved made him sick.

"Fine," Thane said, cold as stone. "Consider yourself down a son."

He waited. Maybe they’d flinch. Maybe they’d take it back.

Arthur just sneered. "Works for us. Go let that trashy couple adopt you." He shrugged. "We can make it official."

Elara chuckled. Thane’s blood boiled.

In the corner, Lysander watched, arms crossed, totally silent. For seven years, Elara took all the hits alone. Even he couldn't defend her back then. Seeing Thane’s parents turn on him now? It felt damn good.

"I don't have a daughter anymore," Celeste declared.

Elara let out a breath, feeling lighter. "Finally. Let’s put that in writing."

She grabbed a pen and paper, slapping them on the table. "Sign it. You never treated me like a daughter anyway."

"You ungrateful bitch!" Celeste screamed. "We fed you, we clothed you, and you embarrass us in front of the Chief Commander and Managing Director? Fine. Briar is our only kid now. Don't come crying when you regret this."

Elara rolled her eyes. "She always was. You guys just forgot to sign the papers."

They scribbled their names. The bond was dead.

But Thane didn't sign anything with his parents. He still needed them, and he knew it.

A minute later, the three of them slammed the door. But the tension in the house didn't leave with them.

Arthur paced, face tight with anger. "He’s not going to stop, Sera. We might need to get you out of here."

Elara shook her head. "Not yet. I’m staying until the divorce is done." She looked him in the eye. "You’ll protect me. From now on, you’re my dad. Eleanor’s my mom. Officially."

The three of them hugged, holding on tight.

"You have no idea how long I’ve wanted a daughter like you," Eleanor whispered.

They got the paperwork done later. But the drama was far from over.

Two days later, Thane was back. And he brought backup. This time, he was going to drain Elara’s blood for Briar himself if he had to.

Too bad he had no idea who his ex-wife really was.
