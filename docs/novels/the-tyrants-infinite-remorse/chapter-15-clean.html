Chapter 15

Jonas knew the drill. Hooking Briar up to human blood was a massive gamble, and he’d known that way before she ever bled out on his operating table.

But now? He saw right through her. No more lies, no more cheap excuses. Not when she was supposed to step up and run the damn pack as Managing Director.

Lucky for him, he hadn’t tossed the blood he’d sucked out of her earlier. It was bagged, tagged, and locked in the fridge. But spilling the truth to Thane? That was a death wish.

He’d kept his mouth shut and played along with Briar’s little game. If he confessed now, Thane would brand him an accomplice.

Getting kicked out of a century-old pack wasn't just a slap on the wrist. It was social suicide. Thane owned this city; Jonas wouldn't even get a job flipping burgers.

Jonas played it cool. "Hey, look," he said, keeping his voice deadpan. "I found some of Elara’s old blood in the back of the vault. A leftover reserve."

Briar’s face twitched. Her eyes shot him a look that could kill. Jonas didn't blink. Thane was standing right there—she couldn't touch him. Or so he hoped.

"Babe," Briar purred, turning to Thane with her best fake-sweet smile. "You should go. I know you’ve got way bigger things to handle."

Thane nodded, pivoting toward the exit. With Lysander gone, finding a new Beta was a massive pain in the ass. It wasn't like the old days.

The modern world ruined everything. Every decent candidate had a life in the city now—real jobs, wives, kids, mortgages.

Dragging them back to the pack territory meant asking them to blow up their entire lives.

"Yeah, you're right..." Thane muttered.

"Actually, Chief," Jonas cut in, sharp and polite. "Stick around. I need you to watch this."

Briar opened her mouth to snap, but Jonas beat her to the punch.

"Plus," he said, cool as ice, "we need to talk. I’m thinking of quitting the pack. Want to try my luck in the city."

Briar gritted her teeth so hard they nearly cracked. Damn it. He was trying to slip through her fingers, and Thane wouldn't suspect a thing.

But Jonas wasn't out of the woods. He’d helped her lie without telling Thane first. He was locked in. Snitching on her meant dragging himself down, too.

Thane scowled. This sucked. First his Beta, now his top doc?

He had to keep him. "What do you want, Jonas?" Thane asked, his voice surprisingly soft. "Name your price."

"Thane, seriously, blood first," Briar snapped, cutting Jonas off. She needed a second to breathe, to figure out how to climb out of this grave she’d dug.

"Right on it," Jonas said.

He walked back in with the bag—the exact same blood he’d drained from her veins days ago. He hooked her up. Thane watched the line fill, his forehead wrinkling.

"Hold on," Thane said, squinting. "You said this was Elara's. Why the hell does the label say 'Briar'?"

Briar went white. Jonas’s stomach did a flip. Shit. He’d forgotten to swap the stickers. He didn't think Thane would actually pay attention.

Jonas cleared his throat, spinning a lie on the fly. "Uh, well, everyone knew Elara’s blood was reserved for her. We put Briar’s name on it so no one else grabbed it by mistake."

It made sense. Thane stared at the plastic bag for another second, then shrugged and let it go.

But Briar knew. Jonas hadn't thrown her blood away. He was pumping her own blood back into her.

She froze, totally conflicted. She wanted Elara's blood, but that was gone. Human blood would’ve bought her a free pass to slack off from pack duties.

Now she had to play the hero. Everyone in the pack—except clueless Thane—knew Elara was the one who actually ran this place.

Briar had spent years playing the martyr, painting Elara as some lazy mom who only cared about her kid, Asher.

That was why Thane had forced Elara to hand Asher's custody over to Briar, telling Elara to get her head back in the game.

God, it pissed her off. Why did Elara have to run away? Why couldn't she stay and watch Briar win? Her eyelids grew heavy, and she passed out.

That’s when Jonas opened his mouth.

"Do you really have to go?" Thane asked softly. "Seriously, what do you want?"

His voice was barely a whisper, but Briar’s ears twitched. Jonas gave a sad, messed-up little smile. He wanted to spill everything, but would Thane even believe him?

Screw it. He had to try. "Chief," Jonas said, taking a slow breath. "Briar is playing you."

Thane’s face went cold. The room turned to ice. "The hell does that mean?" his voice cracked. Jonas swallowed hard, his throat tight. "She’s a fraud. She didn’t bleed out. She made me drain her."

Thane lost it. His eyes turned sharp enough to slice throats. "Watch your mouth!" he growled. "I saw Elara stab her! She was covered in blood when I carried her in here—"

He cut himself off. A shadow crossed his face, then his jaw set. "Who paid you to say this crap?"

Right on cue, Briar stirred. Her eyes fluttered open, sharp as tacks despite her fake-weak act. "Rav?" she whimpered, playing the perfect victim. "What’s going on, babe?"

"Jonas says you faked the whole thing," Thane said, his eyes glued to the doctor.

Jonas let out a bitter laugh. What a joke. "My bad, Chief. I knew you wouldn't buy it." He stood up straight. "I'll leave my resignation on the desk, since Lysander isn't around to take it."

That was Lysander’s job, and Thane froze, totally torn. Briar saw the hesitation and pounced.

"Rav!" she gasped, grabbing his arm. "This is Elara. I swear it is. She’s trying to poison your people against you, forcing your best guys to quit one by one."
