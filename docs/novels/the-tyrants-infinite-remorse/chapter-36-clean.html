NovelVibe

Chapter 36

Elara didn't say a word. She just let a slow, nasty little smirk creep onto her face, letting the quiet stretch out until it got weird. Lysander fidgeted next to her. God, he hated when she did this silent-treatment crap. "Come on," he grunted, nudging her. "What are you gonna do to her?"

Elara let out a sharp, ugly laugh. "Briar wants to play games, right?" she said, shrugging like it was nothing. "Fine. I'll give her a front-row seat. Just... not quite yet." Her eyes flashed.

She kicked back in her seat and crossed her arms. "First, I've got a deadline. Caspian's twenty-four hours are ticking, and I owe him a little present."

Lysander kept his mouth shut. He was starting to get the picture: when Elara got this quiet and calm, people got hurt.

"Take me to the tech market," she said before they even hit the highway. He blinked, totally thrown off, but he just gripped the wheel and drove.

He trailed behind her as she marched through the stalls. She didn't hesitate once, grabbing gear like a pro who had a checklist in her head. High-end laptops, blacked-out drives, weird networking rigs—stuff Lysander couldn't even name.

He couldn't help himself. Once they got moving, he pulled out his phone and started typing the model numbers into Google. His jaw dropped a little lower with every hit. "Okay, seriously," he said, turning to her. "How the hell do you know how to run this stuff?"

Elara shot him a quick wink. "I've got secrets, Lysander. Lots of 'em." Her voice dropped an octave, losing the edge. "But watch me. I'm getting back every single dollar your folks lost because of my mess."

A knot formed in his throat. He didn't care about the cash, not like this. But the way she said it—no bragging, just cold, hard facts—scared the crap out of him. She'd done this before.

Who the hell was this girl? How could she look a guy like Caspian in the eye and not even flinch? He got his answer a few hours later.

When she finished unpacking, her bedroom looked like a damn command center. Blue light from massive monitors cut through the dark, thick black cords tangled across the rug, and the whole room vibrated with the low buzz of cooling fans. It was clean, high-tech, and terrifying.

Lysander just stood in the doorway, paralyzed. The reality hit him like a slap in the face. Elara wasn't just talking big. She didn't bluff. When she made a threat, she delivered.

"Alright," he said, his voice dropping all the hesitation. "What do you need me to do?"

She didn't even glance at him, her eyes glued to a terminal. "Watch your family's stock tickers. Keep an eye on the news feed for Ashkael Holdings. If anything moves, even a fraction, yell."

Not exactly what he thought he'd be doing tonight, but he nodded and pulled up the charts. Behind him, her voice cut through the hum of the fans—cool, sharp, and dead serious.

"Yeah, it's me. I'm back," she muttered into her headset. "And my baseline is twenty mil. No negotiations."

Lysander froze, his fingers hovering over his laptop. He had zero clue who was on the other end of that line, or what kind of underground world she'd just jumped back into. He kept his mouth shut.

Then, the clack-clack-clack of her keyboard started up like machine-gun fire. He spun around, totally mesmerized. Her fingers blurred across the keys, so fast and smooth it looked like she was plugged directly into the mainframe.

First he was just impressed. Then came pure respect. But deep down, a heavy weight settled in his chest. He wasn't just watching a smart girl; he was watching a hurricane in a tank top.

They pulled an all-nighter. Sometime around three, his eyes gave out and he crashed. When he blinked awake hours later, stiff and groggy, the room was exactly the same.

Elara hadn't budged. Still in the same chair, still locked in. No sleep, no breaks. One hand was flying over the keys while she held her phone to her ear, talking in that same icy, lethal tone.

"Jesus," he muttered, scrambling up to brew some coffee. He slid a mug next to her hand. She grabbed it, took a quick gulp, and whispered, "Thanks," without her eyes leaving the monitor for a second.

Suddenly, his phone buzzed. Then again. Then it started vibrating like crazy, blowing up with alert after alert.

"Holy crap," he muttered, scrolling fast. "Our stock is bouncing back. But... look at Ashkael. Their servers are down. Users are locked out of the apps, and everyone's losing their minds. They think it's a massive scam."

He stared at her, his heart hammering with a mix of terror and pure awe. "Did you do this?"

Elara finally looked up, a smug, brilliant spark in her eyes. "What do you think, genius? You think I'd just sit on my hands and watch your family's business go down the drain?"

His throat went dry. This was insane. Highly illegal, totally reckless, and absolutely brilliant. "What if they trace it back to you?" he whispered.

"Only if I make a mistake," she said, shrugging like they were talking about lunch. Then her face went cold. "Usually, I'm the one who gets paid to clean up messes. This time, I'm making the mess so they have to pay me to sweep it up."

A tiny, wicked grin touched her lips. "Besides, what he did to your family? Totally illegal in my book." That smile should've set off alarm bells, but instead, it flipped a switch inside him.

Elara wasn't just some smart chick. She was wild, completely untamable. No wonder Thane never got her—you couldn't own a girl like this, couldn't box her in. And the more Lysander realized what she was actually worth, the more his chest squeezed with sheer panic.

"So... what's the play now?" he asked, adrenaline pumping through his veins as he dragged a chair right next to hers. Green lines of code cascaded down her screen—total gibberish to him, but he couldn't look away.

"They're panicking, trying to patch the leaks," she said, her voice dropping to a low growl. "So I'm squeezing harder. I'm not stopping until he's on his knees begging, and down a few million for screwing with my family."

*Family.* The word hit him right in the chest, warm and heavy. Before he could even process it, his phone started buzzing again. "It's my dad," he said, showing her the screen. Elara grinned. "Put him on."

He tapped the speaker button. Felix's voice blasted through, tight and stressed out. "Lysander! My secretary just told me Caspian is downstairs in the lobby. Tell me you didn't have anything to do with this."

Lysander slowly turned his head to look at Elara. Her fingers were still tearing up the keyboard, but that grin on her face? Yeah, that was the look of a girl about to burn the whole place down.
