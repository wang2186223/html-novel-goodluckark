Chapter 18

Elara couldn't blow this off. Not with her divorce on the line. She glared at the random number lighting up her screen, gripping the plastic until her knuckles went white. She swallowed the lump in her throat and picked up, keeping her voice dead flat. "If we're talking about the papers, I'll text you my address."

The text barely sent before the guy buzzed back, sounding totally blown away. "Wait, you're crashing with the Chief's folks?" he blurted. "Are you seriously crawling back to him?" He sounded so let down, which was honestly none of his business. Elara pinched the bridge of her nose, killing her rising temper. "I'm never going back to him. Period." Zero doubt. She meant it.

Silence stretched over the line. Then, a heavy sigh. "Fine. I'm heading over."

A few hours later, Alden strolled up like he was just delivering the mail. "Hey, some guy's outside for you," he said. "Wouldn't give a name."

They were right in the middle of lunch. Arthur froze, fork halfway to his mouth. Eleanor's face fell. "We're coming with," she said, dropping her napkin. Before Elara could open her mouth, Lysander stood up. "I got this," he said, totally chill. "Just to be safe." Eleanor hesitated for a second, then nodded. "Okay. Go."

Elara walked out to the front gate with Lysander, her chest tight with nerves. But when she got past the driveway and saw the guy leaning against a ridiculously expensive sports car, her stomach dropped. All the color left her face. "Julian?" she breathed.

Tears hit her eyes, blurring everything. It was a total gut punch of old memories. Then, wait. Confusion kicked in. "Uh... your voice. It didn't sound like you on the phone."

Augustine flashed a smooth smile. He looked sharp as hell in a custom black suit that practically screamed "big city money." The goofy kid she used to know was gone, replaced by a guy who looked like he ran corporate boardrooms for breakfast.

"Yeah, I used a voice modulator," he said, rubbing the back of his neck. "Figured you wouldn't show up otherwise."

Back in the day, the guy had been totally crazy about her, zero shame. But Elara only had eyes for Thane. Then came the Moon Festival. Augustine lost his mind when he found out she'd slept with Thane. That was the night he packed his bags and left the pack forever.

"Look, I'm sorry," he said, looking her dead in the eye. "For not having your back back then." Elara let out a long breath. "Water under the bridge." Then she hit him with the hard truth. "But August, I was never into you anyway." She'd shot down his proposal hard and in front of everyone right before the Moon Festival. He skipped her wedding to Thane, and honestly, she didn't blame him.

"I get it," Augustine said, nodding. "Anyway, I'm doing some part-time work for the council now. Plus, I'm a city lawyer." He shot a quick look at Lysander, then looked back at her. "I gotta say, I was shocked when your divorce papers landed on my desk."

"Yeah, well, life happens," Elara said, her voice tight. Augustine checked Lysander out, not even trying to hide it. "So," he drawled, "you dumped the Chief Commander for... a beta?"

Lysander just laughed before Elara could say a word. "Hey, I'm flattered," he said, totally relaxed. "But Sera is way out of my league. Seriously." Augustine visibly relaxed, his shoulders dropping. "Good," he muttered. "Glad you know where you stand."

He turned back to Elara. "I'll get the papers stamped today and sent over by tomorrow morning. You staying here for good?" Elara shook her head. "Nope. The second I get those papers, I'm hitting the road for the city."

"I'll see you there, then," Augustine said softly. "I can wait, even if you need time. Just call me if you need a hand." "Sure, thanks," Elara said, playing nice, though she knew damn well she'd never call. She had bigger fish to fry—getting her life back, restarting her career, and tracking down her daughter, who was still out there somewhere, completely lost to her.

Back inside, she spilled everything to Arthur and Eleanor. They just listened, looking a mix of relieved and totally heartbroken. "Sera, honey," her dad said softly, "just tell us where you're going. We won't breathe a word to Thane, swear." So she laid out the whole plan. Their eyes got watery, but they just nodded. "It's the right move," Eleanor whispered. "We'll come see you whenever we're in town." The whole house felt heavy with the goodbye they weren't saying out loud.

Next morning, right on cue, the mail carrier showed up. The second Elara held that stamped divorce paper, a massive weight lifted off her chest.

She dialed a number right away. "Jasper," she said when he picked up. "I'm pulling every single share under 'Marjorie Steward' out of Walker Global. Do it now." The line went quiet for a second. "Copy that, ma'am," Maddox said, sounding cautious. "But... uh, what's the plan for all that cash?"

"I'm heading to the city," she said. "I'm putting it somewhere else." He let out a massive sigh of relief. "Finally. I actually get to meet you face-to-face." "You and me both," Elara said. "Can't wait."

Arthur and Eleanor were a mess about her leaving, but they didn't try to hold her back. But then, out of nowhere, Thane barged in.

"Mom. Dad." He sounded pissed and in a rush. "Where's Sera? She needs to get back to the pack. I've got urgent stuff to handle in the city." Arthur just looked at him, looking incredibly sad. "Sera's never coming back, son."

Thane snorted, a cocky smirk on his face. "I can just freeze the divorce if she wants to play games. This is a big deal, and I have to get to the city." "Then let Briar do it," Eleanor said, shrugging. Thane took a sharp breath, clearly losing his patience. "Mom, seriously. I'm running late. Just call Sera. Let me talk to her."

The two of them just smiled—a sad, almost mocking little look. "The divorce papers came yesterday," Arthur said flatly. "Sera packed her bags and left the second they arrived." He locked eyes with his son. "You regretting this yet?"

Thane's smirk vanished. "She left?" he muttered. "Where the hell did she go?" He figured she had zero options. He thought she'd just rot here for the rest of her miserable life.

"Why would we tell you?" Eleanor said, cold as ice. "You guys are done." "I haven't seen any papers," Thane snapped. "We aren't divorced." Eleanor smirked. "Well, Sera got hers yesterday. Maybe yours is sitting in your mail. Call your people and check."

Gritting his teeth, Thane whipped out his phone and buzzed Briar. "Did we get any mail for me?" he demanded. The silence on the other end went on way too long. Something dark flashed in Thane's eyes.
