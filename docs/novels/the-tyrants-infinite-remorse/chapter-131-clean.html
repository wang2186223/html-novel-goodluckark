Chapter 132

Elara was drowning. Her calendar was a total trainwreck, leaving her zero room to even catch her breath. But that chaos? It actually gave Lysander the perfect cover. With Elara completely out of the loop, he had all the time in the world to dig into things she couldn't handle.

While she was busy grinding, building her startup brick by brick and killing herself to hit impossible deadlines, Lysander went off-grid. He was out there shaking hands, making calls, and quietly plugging back into networks way outside their city limits.

He didn't just stick to the local centenary pack, either. Nope. He reached out to every pack he’d ever done business with, waking up old contacts and restarting chats that had gone cold months ago.

And boom. The intel started rolling in.

It wasn't clean, organized data. No spreadsheets here. It was the usual wolf-world telephone game—messy, random, passed down from guy to guy. Some of it popped up in encrypted pack group chats. Some of it was just whispered in dark corners, stuff that was supposed to be off the record but leaked anyway.

Just rumors, sure. Half-truths wrapped in gossip. But in their world, rumors didn't just appear out of thin air. There was always fire under the smoke.

And what Lysander was hearing? It wasn't the kind of casual gossip you bring up over beers. This was heavy. Game-changing heavy.

So when he finally brought it up, his voice went dead serious. It instantly snapped Elara out of her work-brain.

"Hey, so... I’ve been hearing things," he said, picking his words like he was walking through a minefield. "Word is, some of the Alphas are planning a ritual. They want to bring back the mate bond. Apparently, they're aiming for the next moon festival."

Elara froze.

No drama, no screaming. She just went totally rigid. But under that frozen exterior, a dark, ugly feeling started clawing its way up—something she’d spent a long time trying to bury.

After the absolute hell Thane put her through, the mere mention of a "mate bond" felt like a noose tightening around her neck. It was suffocating. She never wanted to deal with that crap again.

Right now, she was free. She had a clear head, she was crushing her goals, and no stupid mate-bond emotions were dragging her down. She wasn't about to give that up.

"Why the hell would they even want that?" she muttered, her voice dropping, sharp with disbelief. It made zero sense to her. Not after what she’d lived through.

Lysander forced a tight, humorless smile.

Honestly? A part of him used to wish for this. He used to fantasize that if the bond was still a thing, maybe Elara would end up being his fated mate. Because, God, he loved her. Secretly, of course.

It would have made everything so much easier, so much cleaner. Destined. But yeah, life doesn't care about "what-ifs."

"Look, it’s mostly just talk right now," he said, keeping his voice flat. "But from what I gather, they can only pull off a ritual like this during a blood moon. And get this... a ton of people are actually down for it. Like, sixty percent. Maybe more."

He paused, letting that sink in. "Guess free will isn't working out so hot for our kind."

He watched her closely. The color was draining from her face, her jaw locking as she tried to keep her composure.

"Don't you think?" he pushed, keeping his eyes locked on her. "I mean, if the bond was still active, maybe you never would've ended up with Thane in the first place."

He hesitated, then decided to just say it.

"Or maybe he wouldn't have treated you like garbage," he added, his voice softening, turning heavy. "He would’ve felt a connection to your daughter, too. A bond so strong that... even if things went south, he wouldn't have tried to kill her."

"Exactly!" Elara snapped, cutting him off. She needed logic, fast, before she lost her mind. "The fact that rejection is even an option proves the bond is flawed. People still walk away. They still cheat, or they just hold out for someone better."

The words spilled out of her, fast and sharp, like she was building a brick wall between them. "It doesn't guarantee a damn thing," she said, her eyes turning to ice.

Lysander didn't push back, but he didn't buy it either. "Sure, but walking away has a massive price tag," he murmured.

That was the kicker.

Elara felt a physical weight press down on her chest. The walls were closing in. She wasn't ready for marriage—not now, maybe never. And a resurrected mate bond? That was a direct threat to her freedom.

Because she knew the truth: you don't just ignore a real mate bond.

It claws at you.

It messes with your head, your gut, your choices, until fighting it feels like trying to stop a freight train.

And the people who tried? They ended up broken.

"Keep me posted," she said, her voice steady but quiet. She was shutting this down. She couldn't deal with it right now.

Lysander just nodded. He got the hint.

They dropped it, but the air in the room stayed thick, heavy with everything they hadn't said.

Two days later, just as she was getting back into her groove, her phone buzzed. Augustine.

He was back in town.

Elara jumped at the chance. She locked in a date immediately. She needed a distraction, bad. She wanted something normal, something that had absolutely nothing to do with the heavy wolf drama suffocating her.

But the universe had other plans. Because right when she thought she could take a breather, everything went sideways.

Just like that, her fragile little peace shattered all over again.
