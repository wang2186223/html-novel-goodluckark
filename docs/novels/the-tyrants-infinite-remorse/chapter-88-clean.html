Chapter 88

Julian knew it. Thane hated Elara too much. No amount of talking or playing nice would fix this. Still, a tiny, stupid part of him hoped Caspian would grow a spine, play fair, and shut down whatever nasty game Thane was planning.

The air in the room turned to lead. It was hard to breathe. Julian kept his eyes on Thane. The guy looked smug as hell, like he’d been waiting all night for this exact moment.

Elara caught Julian’s eye and flashed him a real, warm smile. That simple look took the edge off the panic clawing at her chest since Thane threw down the gauntlet.

Julian actually cared. It was a solid reminder that not every rich guy in this room was a heartless, arrogant prick. Feeling that kind of safety here? Yeah, she didn't see that coming.

"Thanks, Julian," she said, keeping her voice smooth and steady. "But seriously, I’ve got this." She didn't sound rattled at all, even though the whole room was about to blow.

Julian stared at her, trying to spot a bluff. Was she just acting tough? He wanted to believe her, he really did, but his eyes practically screamed that she was walking straight into a trap.

"Sera, please," a voice cut in from behind.

Both of them jumped. Victor had slipped over without making a sound. He looked stressed, like he felt personally responsible for this mess.

"Julian’s right," Victor said, stepping up. His voice was quiet but dead serious. "Let me talk to Caspian. Look, when Walker wants something, he gets it. And Caspian always has his back."

Elara listened to the father and son. She couldn't just brush them off. They weren't just being nosy; they brought her to this party, so they felt like her safety was on them.

If they were wolves, she could just tell them the truth. It’d be so much easier. But they were humans.

And the ancient wall between wolves and humans? You don't just break that. It's been locked tight for centuries.

To guys like Julian and Victor, werewolves belonged in cheap horror movies. Messing with that boundary was playing with fire, and everyone knew it.

Back in the day, wolves kept to themselves. They stuck to the pack and ignored the human world completely.

But then, some started blending in. They lived like humans, worked like humans, and realized something crazy.

They wanted what humans had.

Not the money or the muscle. They wanted the freedom to love. Humans got to choose. No fated mates, no cosmic chains—just pure, messy, beautiful choice.

Some wolves actually prayed to the Moon Goddess, begging to break their destiny just to feel that human spark.

Looking at Julian’s loyalty and Victor’s worried face, Elara finally got it. She understood why her ancestors were jealous.

These two humans were showing her more real kindness than she’d ever seen from her own kind. It hit her hard, especially in a room full of corporate sharks.

For once, someone actually cared about her. No politics, no pack ranks, no favors. Just her.

"Mr. Hawthorne," she said softly, looking Victor right in the eye. "Trust me. I have a plan. Just watch, it’ll all make sense in a minute."

Victor opened his mouth to argue. He wasn't about to let her ruin herself. But Julian put a hand on his shoulder.

"It's fine, Dad," Julian said, sighing. "I trust her."

He looked back at Elara, his eyes still tight with worry. "But seriously, Walker looks like he wants to rip your throat out."

"I know," Elara said, not even blinking. Her calm was almost creepy. "But he can't touch me. So relax and enjoy the show."

She turned around and started heading back to the center of the room.

Before she could take three steps, Julian grabbed her wrist and yanked her back.

She stumbled right into his chest. Before she could gasp, his arms wrapped around her. It was a quick, tight hug—totally instinct.

Elara didn't feel any sparks. It wasn't romantic. But the sudden warmth still caught her off guard.

He smelled like clean cologne and fresh air. It was so normal, so human. For a split second, she forgot about revenge, power plays, and the whole messy game.

She pulled back and turned around. The room’s vibe had completely shifted.

Caspian’s face was pitch black. Thane’s eyes were burning holes through her.

Both of them glared at her like they owned her, clearly reading way too much into that quick hug.

Elara didn't give a damn. She didn't owe these guys an explanation for anything.

She was single. Divorced. She could hug whoever the hell she wanted, and nobody could say a thing.

She walked back into the middle of the circle, feeling every eye on her.

"I'm ready," she said, her voice ringing out clear and cold. "Let's hear the next option."

Thane stared at her. A slow, nasty grin spread across his face. It wasn't a friendly smile; it was the look of a guy who wanted to break her.

"Well, then," he said, letting the silence drag out until the room felt ready to snap. "Glad you're ready."

He leaned in, his eyes locked on hers, dripping with malice. "Option three. Strip down and give me a lap dance."
