Chapter 340

Caspian took a long, dragging breath and blew it out. His muscles hummed like a live wire. He wanted to tear this whole thing open, find every single bastard who hurt Elara, and rip their lies to shreds. But he wasn't stupid. He’d paid in blood to learn the difference between itching for a fight and actually winning one. Kick the hornets' nest now, and innocent people get stung.

Gossip in this town spread faster than a grease fire. Thane had spies in every corner. If that sick piece of shit got even a sniff of what they were planning, Elara’s whole play would go up in smoke before they even lit the fuse. No way in hell was Caspian letting that happen. Not a chance.

"Look, you guys want to help?" Caspian’s voice went dead quiet, solid as concrete. "Then make sure every single person shows up to the full moon festival. Thane’s hosting the damn thing."

Arthur’s jaw clenched so hard his teeth clicked. Pure, black rage flared in his eyes—the kind of anger he’d been swallowing for years with nowhere to spit it out.

"That son of a bitch," he spat, shaking his head like he’d just swallowed battery acid. "Elara’s letting him off too easy. She’s being way too soft on him."

"She’s not soft," Caspian snapped, cutting him off clean. "She’s playing chess, man. Every single move is designed to ruin him. Just get there, and you’ll see the trap spring. It’ll make perfect sense."

He let that sink in for a second. Then he leaned forward. "But hey, that’s not the only reason I dropped by. I wanted to invite you guys to our wedding. Personally."

He said it totally casual, like he was talking about the weather, even though he’d just dropped a nuke right on their coffee table.

"Keep it under wraps for now. She doesn’t want a soul knowing about us until she blows Briar sky-high. Once she pulls that trigger, everything goes boom at once."

The room went dead silent. You could hear a pin drop.

Arthur and Eleanor froze like statues, just staring at him. They didn't gasp, but their brains were clearly short-circuiting trying to process the news.

Caspian had dropped some heavy shit since he walked in, but this wedding thing? It hit them like a Mack truck. Thane, the festival, Elara’s payback—all of it just evaporated. Only two words mattered now.

*Our wedding.*

"Hold on, wait a minute." Eleanor smacked her hand against her chest, her eyes popping. "You’re marrying Sera? Our Sera?" She said the name twice, like she had to hear it out loud to believe it.

Something warm bloomed in Caspian’s chest. *Our Sera.* The way they claimed her, that instant, protective fire in Eleanor's voice—it showed how much they actually loved her. No fake, polite bullshit here. This was the real deal.

"Yeah," Caspian said. "And honestly, that’s why I’m here. I need to apologize for how I acted the last time I saw you guys. I was out of line."

He looked Arthur dead in the eye, then turned to Eleanor, letting them see he meant every word.

"I spent a long time pissed off at her. I actually believed she just packed up and walked out on us, like she chose to leave and I was just too proud to accept it."

His jaw tightened. "But that was a lie. They stole her memories of me. Of us. Some sick spell locked them away. She didn't run. They made her forget."

Eleanor gasped, clapping a hand over her mouth as tears welled up.

"She still doesn't remember," Caspian said, his throat tightening up. "Not yet. But honestly? It doesn't kill me anymore. Because what we have right now, what we built from scratch? It's real as hell."

"Whatever magic they used, whatever they ripped out of her head, they couldn't touch our connection. And I'm gonna fight like hell to get it all back. The memories, the mate bond, every single scrap of it. I'm not stopping until she's whole again."

The words had barely left his mouth when Eleanor lunged. She threw her arms around him, slamming into his chest with a hug that was pure, unfiltered love. No polite hesitation, just a bone-crushing squeeze. Caspian froze for a split second, then relaxed, letting her relief wash over him.

She pulled back, tears shining in her eyes, and cupped his face with both hands like a proud mom.

"Finally," she whispered, her voice cracking under the weight of years of worrying. "Finally, Sera has a man strong enough to stand in front of her, not hide behind her. God, I've been praying for this forever."

She let out a watery laugh, totally overwhelmed. "This is the best damn thing I've heard in years."

She let her hands drop but didn't step back, her expression shifting from relief to something serious. "But Caspian... what about your mother?"

Eleanor watched his face closely, looking for a reaction. "She lost her mind when Elara forgot you. She was completely broken. And when she gets like that, she acts. She actually showed up here at our pack, ready to kick down doors and shake the truth out of Elara."

"We had to block her. No way were we letting anyone touch our girl, I don't care who they were." She stared at him, begging him to understand. "I just hope she gets it now. Elara didn't choose this. It wasn't her fault."

Caspian frowned, a mix of shock and old, sharp pain hitting him in the gut.

He had absolutely no idea. His folks had never said a word about visiting the centenary pack. While he was drowning in misery, starving himself of sleep, and thinking the love of his life had just erased him like trash, his mom had been out there fighting.

His mother had actually marched into enemy territory, driven by that same pain, ready to demand answers Elara couldn't even give.

He locked that piece of information away. He'd have a serious talk with his mom later. Not today, though.

"Don't sweat it," he said, trying to sound calm while his brain spun. "She'll get over it. We'll all be at the wedding, and by then, there won't be any anger left. Lysander will text you guys with the dates and details soon."

"You should've called first, kiddo," Eleanor said, instantly switching into full-on mom mode, already looking around the kitchen. "I would've cooked a real dinner, not just this—"

"I gotta run," Caspian interrupted gently, giving her a warm, genuine smile. "I'm taking Elara to see my shaman tonight, and I'm on a tight schedule. Plus, I've got one more stop to make."

He nodded at the pile of gifts on the table. "Please, keep those. Least I could do."

Eleanor opened her mouth to argue, but Arthur put a hand on her arm, and she quieted down.

Caspian said his goodbyes, giving Arthur a firm handshake. The older man squeezed his hand, holding on for an extra second—a silent nod that meant more than any speech. Then Caspian slipped out the door into the cool air.

The house felt different now, warmer, like his visit had finally broken the ice.

Inside, Eleanor stood by the table, absently running her fingers over the edge of a gift box. Arthur walked over and stood next to her. They both stayed quiet, letting the heavy truths they'd just learned settle in.

"Look at this," she whispered, her voice cracking. It wasn't about the expensive gifts. It was proof. Real, solid proof that the hope she'd held onto wasn't crazy.

Arthur picked up one of the boxes, turned it over, and set it back down gently. Eleanor looked up at him, her eyes still wet. "Hey... where do you think his other stop is?"
