Chapter 178

Elara put her hands up. Slow. Easy. Her body said *I give up*, but her brain was firing on all cylinders, running the numbers, refusing to freeze with that barrel staring her down.

She spun around to face him. "Hey, did you grab those tampons?" A tiny smirk played on her lips. She treated the loaded gun like a bad joke at a dinner party, not the trigger that could blow her life to pieces.

Dominic didn't crack a smile. The nice-guy act was gone. In its place was a cold, heavy silence that sucked the air right out of the room.

"Never figured the Feds would send a girl like you," he muttered, his voice low and dangerous. "So, come on. How'd you pull off the scam?"

Elara let out a soft snort, her smirk turning sharp. "Cut the questions, Dominic. You need to figure out your next move. Because right here? This is where your ride ends."

She locked eyes with him, dead serious. "So, how's it feel knowing we just cleared out all those people?" she asked, her voice casual, like they were talking about the weather.

Dominic went quiet for a beat. Then, he grinned. A creepy, knowing kind of grin.

Before she could move, two goons slammed into her from behind. They grabbed her arms, pinning them back before she could even try to twist out of their grip.

They dragged her back to the dining room—the same fancy place from earlier, now feeling like a tomb—and tossed her onto the floor like trash.

Her ribs hit the hardwood hard. Pain shot up her side, but she didn't make a sound. No tears, no shaking.

She raised her chin, slow and steady, looking up at them like she was still the one holding all the cards.

Dominic walked over, looming over her. "Let's try this again," he said, his voice smooth but freezing cold. "You said I'm done?"

He dropped down, staring straight into her eyes. "Nobody puts an end to me, sweetheart."

Elara propped herself up, staring right back. "You ended yourself the second you asked me out on a date."

That got to him. A slow smirk crept onto his face. "You really think a little bug like you can squash me?" He tilted his head, looking at her like she was a funny pet.

His eyes swept down her body, totally writing her off because of her size. Big mistake. He knew he should just shoot her, but damn, that dress was doing things to him. He wanted to tear it off her and take whatever she was hiding underneath.

Elara exhaled, her lips twitching. "You'd be shocked what a little bug can do," she whispered. "Especially when it has teeth."

One guard snickered. Another rolled his eyes. But Dominic didn't laugh. He just stared. "You know," he said, standing up and looking down at her, "I actually liked you."

For a second, he sounded almost real. "I thought, hey, maybe this is it. Maybe I can settle down, have a normal life, a real home."

Then his face went dark. "But now... you're starting to feel familiar." He paused. "Like a female Caspian."

The name hit hard, but Elara kept her face blank. She knew the history there. "Well," she said, "at least he learned not to mess with me again."

A couple of the guards laughed, but Dominic's jaw tightened. Just a fraction.

"Again?" he barked, catching the slip instantly. "He crossed you before?"

Perfect. There was her opening. Elara jumped on it, keeping her voice cool and breezy, even though her heart was hammering. She needed to buy time for Caspian and Lysander to track her down. "He paid his dues," she said smoothly. "Trust me, he won't make that mistake twice."

Dominic frowned. The math wasn't adding up in his head. He stood there, putting the pieces together, doubting himself, getting pissed off. Elara watched the gears turn. Good. Keep thinking, asshole. Keep hesitating.

If he focused too much, he'd realize everything outside this room was already falling apart. She thought about the timer ticking down in her head, the servers she'd crashed, the tiny window they had left.

Dominic's voice snapped her back. "Tell me," he purred, stepping closer, his eyes narrowing. "How'd you handle Caspian? Maybe I can take some notes."

Elara looked up, a cold, knowing smile on her face. "Don't sweat it," she murmured. "You're about to get your own personal demonstration."

She pushed herself up higher, ignoring the ache in her ribs, focusing entirely on the exit. "I'm going to show you exactly what happens when you try to play god," she said, her voice low and steady. "By tomorrow, regret is going to be your shadow."

One guard scoffed. Another chuckled. Dominic just let out a dark laugh. "Man, the balls on you," he said, shaking his head. "You really think you've already won this game."

He stepped right into her space, his shadow blotting out the light. "Alright," he said, his voice dropping back into that casual, friendly tone. "Let's try this one last time." He tilted his head, watching her face. "You know how I run things. You've seen my empire."

He paused. "Marry me. Partner up."

Then the ice came back. "Or I put a bullet right between your eyes."

The room went dead silent.
