Chapter 108

Thane jerked his head back. A sharp, pissed-off shake. His jaw clenched.

"No way," he snapped, waving a hand like the whole thing was a joke. "You don't know her. Not like I do."

He let out a nasty laugh.

"Please. All she wants is a fat check to fund that dumb little business of hers. Just to rub my nose in it," he sneered, his voice turning ice-cold. "Throw a couple billion her way, and watch. She’ll grab the cash and run."

Thane actually believed this crap. To him, Elara was just some desperate girl begging for a spotlight, ready to sell out the second she got a chance to climb.

Caspian stared at him. For the first time, he didn't see a brother-in-arms or a fellow Commander. He just saw a total idiot. A guy completely blind to the real world.

"Oh, really?" Caspian’s voice was dead quiet. The other two Alphas shifted, watching them. "Then tell me why she threw their money back in their faces the second they started acting like sexist pigs?"

Thane’s chin dropped. He stared at the floor, his brain scrambling to twist the facts to fit his own delusional version of Elara.

Caspian didn't let him off the hook.

"Isn't that why we're in this shitshow right now?" Caspian’s voice lost its calm, frustration finally breaking through. "Because none of you bastards could swallow your pride and treat her like a human being?"

He took a step in, eyes locking onto Thane's.

"And now you think you can just pay her off?"

Gravy and Riven shut their mouths. They knew Caspian was spitting facts. But Thane? Thane was too wrapped up in his own bitter ego to ever see Elara clearly.

"Look, just let me deal with it," Thane muttered, his voice dropping, almost begging. "If she wants to sue them or whatever, she has to go through me first."

Caspian just looked at him. The silence stretched, thick and suffocating.

Then, Caspian let out a long, heavy breath.

The sword in his grip felt like lead. He let go. *Clang.* The blade hit the floor, ringing through the room.

He was done. Over it. He’d spent too long cleaning up after idiots who didn't care who they hurt. The only thing keeping him here was the Commander's code. Whatever went down tonight stayed in this room. Period.

"I don't care what she did. Nobody touches a woman in this place," Caspian said, his voice flat with disgust. "This is our circle, Thane. We built this house. We wrote the damn rules."

Thane looked down, a flash of actual guilt crossing his face.

"They lost their heads," he mumbled, the tough-guy act totally gone. "But hey... blame me. Just don't blow this up."

Caspian’s eyes stayed ice-cold.

"Whatever Elara does next? I'm out. Figure it out yourself."

He turned on his heel and walked toward the club.

Honestly, cutting off his own arm sounded better than dealing with the hell Elara was about to raise. But since those two dumbass Alphas were too proud to see the storm coming, Caspian decided he didn't give a shit anymore.

First, he needed the guy who actually handed her the glass. The waiter.

He marched down the quiet hallway and cornered another staff member.

"Where's Francis?"

The kid paled, looking down. "Uh, Francis clocked out. He's gone."

Caspian scoffed. Lucky for Francis he was a human. If he were one of them, Caspian would've ripped him apart. But humans were tricky. The circle had strict rules about keeping their world secret. Hurting a human meant too much heat.

Still, Caspian had other ways to ruin a life.

He waved the kid off, pulled out his phone, and dialed a number. The agency picked up fast.

"Yeah, I need to report one of your guys," Caspian said, his voice smooth, hiding the rage boiling inside. "Felix More."

He paused, letting the name sink in. "He served a guest a spiked drink."

The line went dead silent. "I assume you know what to do."

"Don't worry, Caspian. He's blacklisted. He'll never work in this town again."

Caspian hung up. Done. In this business, a rumor like that was a death sentence. Francis was finished. He'd have to pack his bags and move to some middle-of-nowhere town just to get a job washing dishes.

A few days later, Caspian was back in Manhattan, the city skyline looming outside his car window.

The second he got to his desk, he called Lysander.

"Put Elara on."

Lysander’s voice came through, cold and flat. "She's sick."

Caspian’s jaw clenched. Sick? Was there still wolfsbane in her system?

"What do you mean, sick?" he demanded. "Give her the phone."

He wasn't playing around, but the next words out of Lysander's mouth made his stomach drop.
