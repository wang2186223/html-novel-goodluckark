Chapter 16

"Sera..."

Thane spat the name. It hit the room like a gunshot. Jonas froze, instantly cursing himself for bringing up Briar. The air turned to ice. Thane stood there, towering, his eyes dead and pitch-black.

"Look, I didn't even talk to the Managing Dir—" Jonas stammered, desperate.

"Shut up!" Thane snapped. He wasn't asking. He was the boss, and he'd already made up his mind. "You think I'm stupid? You throw a tantrum, quit, and then try to frame Briar? Nice try, man." Thane sneered. "Get your resignation letter. Now."

Briar let out a tiny, smug smirk. Just a twitch, but Jonas caught it.

Something cracked inside him. He balled his fists, shaking. "Chief," he spat, "I hope to God you realize one day that she is the poison in this entire pack."

*Crack.*

Thane’s hand whipped across Jonas’s face. Jonas hit the floor hard, his head spinning as his cheek flared with white-hot pain. That wasn't just a slap; it was an Alpha putting a dog in its place. The shame hurt way worse than the bruise. Jonas dragged himself up, gave a cold, forced nod, and walked out. Five minutes later, he walked back in and slapped the paper onto the desk. His face was totally blank.

Thane scribbled his signature. The doctors and nurses started whispering, begging Thane to reconsider.

Thane drowned them out. "Anyone else want to walk? Go ahead. Follow him out," he barked. "Otherwise, shut your mouths."

Nobody breathed. The room went dead silent. Everyone just stared at the floor, terrified, until Briar chimed in from her hospital bed.

"Hey, guys, you don't need to go," she said, her voice all sweet and fake. "Let's just work together and make this place better."

Classic Briar. She knew exactly what to say to brainwash them. Jonas watched the staff soften, their anger melting away into submission. He shook his head, disgusted, and walked out for good.

Guilt ate at his gut on the drive out. He’d actually helped her get that co-Managing Director title. He’d been a sucker, just like the rest of them. If he’d seen through her act earlier, he would’ve warned everyone. But it was too late. Most of the pack had already headed back to the city, totally clueless about the snake in their grass. That was her trick. She was dangerous. Still, as he passed the gates, he prayed they’d wake up eventually, realize they’d been played, and bring Elara back.

Briar got out of the hospital a few days later. Back at the mansion, she got the royal treatment. People bowed, smiled, and held doors open like she was a queen. She loved every second of it. She didn't waste any time moving her stuff straight into Thane’s master suite—the exact rooms Elara used to own.

When Briar walked in and saw Elara’s things, her face darkened. The massive walk-in closet was full, and she could still smell Elara’s perfume. She squeezed Thane’s arm, putting on her best worried face. "Rav, babe... she left all her clothes. Does that mean she’s coming back?"

Thane looked at the closet, his jaw clenching. "Tell the maids to burn the whole lot," he said.

Briar stiffened. "Burn them?"

She stared at the racks. It was all high-end designer stuff, brands she’d only seen on Instagram. Thane had never bought her anything like this. Whenever he gave her cash, she hoarded it or sent it to her folks. Half of these clothes still had the tags on.

"Wait, no," she said, trying not to sound greedy. "They’re brand new. I can just wear them. But... what if she comes back for them?"

God, she hoped Elara never came back.

"Relax," Thane grunted. "She’s gone for good."

Briar smiled, totally satisfied. "Okay, then. I’ll go check on Asher."

"Yeah," Thane said. "And I need to find a new Deputy."

Weeks flew by. Nobody wanted to leave the city to work in the middle of nowhere. Thane was drowning in paperwork, exhausted and pissed off, when his phone rang with more bad news.

It was his city assistant. "Sir, Marjorie Steward wants out. She’s dumping her shares immediately. Needs the cash now, and we don't have a buyer."

Thane’s stomach dropped. If those shares sold, he’d slide from number two on the Forbes list down to three, maybe four.

"No way," Thane barked. "Stall them. Make a deal."

"I tried, boss," the assistant sighed. "Her lawyer isn't playing ball. They're pointing to the contract. Either we buy her out right now, or we pay the twenty percent monthly penalty."

Thane cursed. "That’ll drag us down to sixth."

"Yep," the assistant said. "So, do I write the check, or are you coming down to talk to her guy? Just so you know, Marjorie is a ghost. Nobody’s ever actually seen her. Only her lawyer shows up."

Right. Marjorie Steward was a shadow player. "I’m coming," Thane said. "Don't do anything till I get there."

An eight-hour drive or a two-hour flight. He chose his jet, which meant leaving the territory. He buzzed Briar into his office.

She walked in, smiling, and kissed him. "Miss me?"

"We have a problem," Thane said. "I have to fly to the city. Can you run things here while I'm gone?"

Briar froze. Even Elara had Lysander to back her up. Doing this solo was a nightmare. "Uh... I’m gonna need some help," she muttered. "Like, a Deputy or something?"

Thane sighed, looking stressed. "I know, I’m trying to find one. But I have to go now."

Panic hit her. She hated being left behind, especially with Thane going to the city alone. City girls were vultures. What if some bitch stole him?

"But I have to watch Asher," she whined. "Why don't you make Elara do it? The divorce isn't official yet. This is still her job."

Thane thought about it. "Good point. I'll go find her. She can't say no to this."

If she tried to refuse, he'd just hold up the divorce papers until she gave in. Easy.

But right after Thane drove off, a courier showed up at the gate. Briar took the package.

She ripped it open. Her stomach dropped, her face turning pale with pure rage and terror. Everything was blowing up in her face.

Thane pulled up to his parents' place, completely unprepared for what his dad was about to say.

"The divorce went through yesterday," his dad said, smirking. "Sera packed her bags and left the second she got the papers. Regretting it yet, son?"
