Chapter 80

Thane stared at the stock charts until the glowing numbers stopped looking like data and started looking like a personal attack. Ever since Rhys signed off on the transfer, he’d been glued to the screen. Every tick up, every dip down—he tracked the percentages like a surgeon sweating over a flatlining EKG.

On paper, he was back in the black. The lines on the graph were climbing steady enough to fool any outsider into thinking he was winning. But Thane wasn’t just some guy. He knew the margins. He knew his target. And staring at those damn figures, the brutal truth hit him: he was still short ten billion.

Give him three days, maybe a week, and the market’s natural momentum would’ve carried him across the finish line. The projections looked solid. The trends lined up. In any normal world, he’d just sit back and let the math do its thing.

But nothing about his life was normal right now. Time was a noose tightening around his neck. Patience? Not an option.

He sat in his Manhattan penthouse, boxed in by floor-to-ceiling glass. Outside, the skyline stretched out—a view most guys would kill for. Inside, he felt absolutely zero thrill.

The place was just a massive, empty shell. Briar’s absence hung in the air like a bad hangover, turning all this high-end luxury into a ghost town.

His phone buzzed on the polished table, cutting through the numbers and the memories. Caspian’s name flashed on the screen. Thane’s chest tightened. Caspian didn’t do casual calls.

Thane snatched it up. "Caspian. Talk to me. Everything good?"

Caspian sounded like death warmed over. "Not even close, man. I’m stuck in California. No way I make it back to Manhattan in time. I’m trying to drag the meeting out here instead. Suggested the Bohemian Grove, and honestly? Like, seventy percent of them already signed off on it."

The Grove. Just the name carried weight. It wasn’t just a venue; it was a playground for the ultra-rich, hidden deep in giant redwoods that had buried secrets for a century. Thane could picture it already—the dark, empty forest roads, the total isolation. Perfect for keeping things quiet.

For guys who spent their lives in glass towers dodging paparazzi, the place offered the one thing Manhattan couldn't: the illusion of absolute freedom.

Thane ran the numbers in his head. You don't move a circus like this on a whim. "Moving the whole setup is gonna cost a fortune," he said. "We already paid for everything in New York."

"Yeah, I know," Caspian sighed, and Thane could practically hear him rubbing his tired face. "But they’re down to split the bill. Actually, get this—David Sterling offered to write a check for the whole damn thing."

Thane froze. Guys like David Sterling didn't do charity. Being number two on the global billionaire list meant the guy played life like a grandmaster. Thane knew better than to trust a free lunch.

"He’s paying for all of it?" Thane let the question hang.

"Yep," Caspian said. "I guess he wants to buy himself a night with zero hassle. Look, I still don’t know if I can make it. Which means you’re up. I need you to chair this thing for me."

That hit hard. Thane had literally written the club’s bylaws, and rule number one was a strict net-worth floor. He let out a slow breath. "You know I haven't hit the number yet, Caspian. I wrote the rule. Technically, I’m disqualified."

Caspian let out a dry, exhausted laugh. "Man, you forgot about the golden ticket, didn't you? I never used mine, and I still have my plus-one. We stack those together, and boom—you’re the chairman tonight."

A wave of relief washed over him, slow and warm. He really had forgotten. Mostly because in his head, that plus-one had always belonged to Briar. Without her, the invite was just trash.

"Thanks, man," Thane said, his voice softening. "I won't mess this up. Just... try to get here if you can."

"I'll try," Caspian muttered, sounding completely spent. "Haven't slept a full night in days."

Thane didn't push. Whatever Caspian was dealing with in California was heavy, personal, and off-limits. "Go crash," Thane said. "I’ve got everything handled on this end."

Thane rolled up to the Grove early. He liked checking the details himself rather than trusting some hired help. The club looked like a high-tech fortress carved out of the redwoods. Sleek glass, steel, and massive timber beams—nature meets cutting-edge tech.

As the sun dipped, the reflective glass panels mirrored the forest so perfectly the building practically vanished into the trees. No doormen here—just biometric scanners at the gate. In the hidden lot, high-end electric rides hummed quietly.

Inside was a whole different vibe. Huge redwood pillars held up the ceiling, while smart lights shifted colors to set the mood. Digital art installations flickered on the walls, changing with the season.

A minimalist glass fireplace glowed in the center, controlled by touch pads built right into the seats. Modular couches wrapped in eco-fabrics formed tight little VIP circles, complete with hidden chargers and holographic screens for quick pitches.

By the time Thane finished his walkthrough, the heavy hitters had arrived. Every guy had a gorgeous woman on his arm—half eye candy, half strategic play. Whispers filled the room, but the air was thick with tension. Deals made in this room could break industries or buy politicians. It was a rustic cabin turned high-tech temple of power.

"Jaxson Vance," a voice called out behind him.

Thane turned. David Sterling was strolling over, a wine glass in hand and a gorgeous woman on each arm. Mark had that smug, untouchable look of a guy who always got his way. "Didn't think I'd see you here tonight," he said, smooth as silk.

"I'm filling in for Caspian," Thane said, flashing a tight, polite smile. "He should've sent out the memo by now."

Mark checked his phone, saw the alert, and shrugged. "Fine by me. Just hope Caspian actually shows up. Things get pretty wild around midnight, and he’s the only one who knows how to keep a lid on it."

Thane nodded. He’d seen what happens when you mix unlimited cash with too much booze. "Yeah. Hope so too."

Before they could say more, the heavy entrance doors swung open. The whole room went quiet as the vibe shifted instantly.

Augustus walked in first, his wife Aurelia on his arm, looking smug as hell as he scanned the crowd.

Beside them walked Caspian, looking sharp, effortless, and totally ready for the cameras. And right next to him was a woman who made Thane freeze.

She looked different. Polished, high-end, styled to perfection. For a split second, his brain didn't register her—and then her perfume hit him. That exact, unmistakable scent.

They walked straight toward him. "Mr. Walker," Victor said, all formal and stiff. "Honored to introduce my son, Caspian."

Julian stepped up, extending his hand with total confidence. "Mr. Walker. Heard a lot about you. Seen you on the news."

Thane gripped his hand, but his eyes were locked on the woman. His chest squeezed tight. Before he could stop himself, the name slipped out. "Sera? What the hell are you doing here?"

Julian’s smile tightened. He pulled his hand back and slid his arm around her waist, pulling her close. "Oh, so you two know each other?" Julian said, casual as hell. "Meet my girlfriend, Seraphine."

The words hit Thane like a physical punch. The color drained from his face. He stared at Elara, desperately searching her eyes for some sign, some hint that this was a mistake. His voice came out cracked and raw.

"This... this is a joke, right?"
