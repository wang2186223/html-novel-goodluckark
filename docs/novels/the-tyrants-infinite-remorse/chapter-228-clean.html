Chapter 231

Sleep was a joke. Briar stared at the black ceiling, her brain redlining. She kept chewing on the same damn thoughts, over and over, like a tongue poking at a cracked tooth. It hurt, but she couldn't stop.

Rhys’s intel about Elara killing it in the city cut deep. It wasn't a shock, but it sucked. Success meant spotlight. Spotlight meant eyes on them. And eyes on them? That was a death sentence for Briar.

But the real kicker keeping her awake? Elara had stood right there in front of Thane and kept her mouth shut. She had the perfect chance to blow Briar's cover, but she didn't say a damn word. Why? It wasn't mercy. No way. It was a setup. Elara was playing chess, moving pieces Briar couldn't even see yet. That kind of quiet patience? It was way scarier than a screaming match.

Bottom line: Elara had to go. Now. Before the trap snapped shut.

Briar dialed up a name in her head. A pack member who owed her big time and didn't ask stupid questions. She fired off the mind-link before she could chicken out, her nails digging hard into the bedsheets.

*Get the rogue assassins. Elara dies tonight.*

The reply hit her brain fast, dripping with hesitation. Briar clenched her fists. *Tonight? Wait, if she's dead, who cures the paralyzed guys?*

*She already made the damn cure,* Briar snapped back, cold as ice. She’d done the math. *She’s useless now. Felix can copy her homework. But listen to me—if the truth about that night leaks, I'm not the only one going down. You're coming with me.*

She paused, letting the threat breathe. *Exile. We lose everything.*

Then she twisted the knife. *And think about Asher. Thane is grooming him for Chief Commander. You want to watch him lose that dream?*

The silence in her head stretched, heavy and tense. Then, the snap.

*Fine. We’ve been waiting on Asher to grow up anyway. Once he's in, Thane is history. I’ll handle Elara. Consider it done.*

A slow, nasty grin spread across Briar's face. God, she loved this. She’d done it before—turning a total disaster into the very thing that made Thane hate the woman he was supposed to protect. She’d clean this up, too. No loose ends.

But she missed one tiny detail: Elara wasn't stupid. Since she got back, she hadn't spent a single second alone.

Hours dragged by. When the mind-link finally buzzed, it wasn't the good news she wanted.

*Elara’s got backup. Caspian and Thane are hovering. The first hit flopped. But I’ve got the real pros on it now—the rogue assassins. They’re tracking her. The second she’s alone, they’ll end her. Clean.*

The news hit Briar like a punch to the gut.

She bit her lip, boiling inside. How the hell did Elara always stay one step ahead? It was like the bitch had better software in her brain.

*Make sure this doesn't bounce back to me,* she ordered, trying to hide the shake in her mental voice.

*Relax. They’re paid a fortune. They’d rather take a bullet than squeal. Once they catch her away from those Alphas, she’s history.*

Briar let out a shaky breath, feeling the panic ease up. Just then, heavy, familiar footsteps echoed down the hall.

*Gotta go,* she cut the link.

The door swung open. Thane walked in, looking like a thundercloud. He was stark naked, ignoring her completely as he marched straight toward the bathroom.

"Rav." She slid out of bed and touched his bare arm. "Hey, you look stressed."

"Not now." He brushed past her.

She stood there, watching the bathroom door click shut. The silence in the room felt heavy, like a permanent weight on her chest.

There was a time Thane actually talked to her. Not about everything, but enough to make her feel like she was in his life, not just some bystander watching from the sidelines. Now? That guy felt like a ghost.

She waited. When he finally stepped out, hair damp, shoulders tight with stress, she slipped up behind him. Without a word, she dug her fingers into his tense shoulders, working the knots.

He stiffened, then slowly, his muscles gave in. A low, involuntary groan of relief escaped his throat.

She gently guided him down onto his back. For a little while, the world shrank. No Elara, no schemes, just the two of them.

Their lips crashed together, hands roaming over bare skin, their heavy breathing and soft moans filling the dark room.

Hours later, a sharp ring shattered the quiet. Thane jerked awake, squinting at the screen. Caspian’s name was flashing. The heavy dread of the night rushed back instantly.

"Caspian," Thane muttered, his voice thick with sleep. "Is Elara okay?"

That name hit the dark room like a bucket of ice water. Briar’s eyes snapped open.

On the other end, Caspian’s voice was so cold it practically froze the speaker, sending a chill straight down Thane’s spine before the words even registered.
