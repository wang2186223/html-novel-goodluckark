Chapter 279

A sharp pain hit Elara right in the ribs. It twisted hard.

"How old?" The words slipped out before she could stop them. Her voice went dead flat, that forced, steady tone she used when her whole world was about to crack open.

Her brain went into overdrive, piecing clues together, fighting the urge to hope. Every single day, she’d begged the moon goddess for just one thing—find her little girl before the next full moon. It was a heavy weight she carried everywhere. But she’d learned her lesson with the kids from the Dominic mess. She couldn't lose her mind over a random call. Not again. No way.

"Paperwork says five," the voice on the line said. "But Sera, seriously, her hair... it’s a dead ringer for Thane’s."

Elara let out a long, slow breath. Her kid would be seven soon, but hey, paperwork gets messed up all the time. She needed to check for that birthmark Eleanor mentioned. Plus, she didn't walk away from sick kids. Ever.

"Fine," she said, her voice locking back into place. "I'll clear it with my boss and head over. On my way." She was already out of her chair before she even hung up.

Julian snapped his head up when she burst into his office. One look at her face and he knew exactly what was up.

"Leo," she cut straight to the point. "There’s a kid over at Whitfield Private. I need to go. Let me take off an hour early? I’ll cover the shift first thing tomorrow, I swear."

He glanced at the packed surgery schedule on his monitor, sighed, and looked back up. Whatever he saw in her eyes shut down any argument before it started.

"Go," he said, waving her off. "But tomorrow means tomorrow, got it?"

"Thanks," she threw back, already halfway out the door.

She dialed Lysander as she hit the stairs. He picked up on the second ring.

"We have a situation," she muttered, pushing through the exit into the warm afternoon. "A kid needs help. It's messy. I'm heading to Valerie's place. It's nearby, so I'm just grabbing an Uber."

A beat of pure silence. Then, Lysander’s tone went cold and hard. "Valerie? Are you kidding me, Sera? You remember what happened last time she dragged us into her drama—"

"It's a five-year-old girl, Lysander. Come on."

Another pause, shorter. "Fine. Call me when you're done. I'll pick you up."

She hung up, waved down a ride, and slid into the back seat.

The twelve-minute drive was a blur. She stared out the window, seeing absolutely nothing. Twice, she had to force herself to stop hoping. No matter what, she had to play the doctor here. Just a doctor. Nothing else.

Valerie was pacing the entrance like she'd been there for hours. The second she saw Elara, she bolted over, practically vibrating with nervous energy. "Thank God you're here," she said, grabbing Elara's arm and pulling her toward a group of white coats down the hall. "I need you to meet—"

"Everyone, this is our miracle worker," Valerie announced, waving her hands dramatically. Elara really didn't have the patience for the show. She scanned the crowd, then froze. "Jonas?"

He looked up, giving her this shy, goofy grin that looked totally ridiculous on a guy his size. He looked good—broader, more relaxed, none of that old stress in his eyes.

"Hey," he said softly. "Yeah. We'll catch up later."

Got it. Pack stuff. Too many humans around. Later. She gave him a quick nod and pushed past the doors into the ward.

The room was quiet, but the tension was thick enough to cut. A redhead was hunched over in a chair by the bed, clutching her phone, her voice shaking violently. "I don't know what to do. Get over here. Please, I'm freaking out, I—"

Elara looked past her at the bed. The little girl was tiny, totally still. She was out cold, but she looked so peaceful, like she was just taking a nap instead of fighting for her life. Dark eyelashes, hands relaxed at her sides.

And the hair—Valerie wasn't lying. It was a wild, gorgeous mess of thick, jet-black curls. It made her look way older than five. It was Thane's hair, 100%.

A weird, magnetic pull tugged at Elara's chest, getting stronger with every step she took toward the bed. She'd never laid eyes on this kid, but she had to check for that birthmark. Now.

"Get her out of here," Elara whispered to Valerie.

But the redhead was already up before Valerie could even speak. She just walked out, phone still glued to her ear, not saying a word. Valerie glared at her back. "Unbelievable, the attitude on her," she muttered. Elara tuned her out.

She snapped on latex gloves, grabbed her stethoscope, and got to work. Pulse? Strong and steady. Lungs? Clear. She ran through the standard checks like a machine, but nothing added up. She stood up, frowning. "She's totally fine."

Valerie blinked. "Are you serious? She was having full-blown seizures when she got here. It took two nurses just to pin her down. Then she just blacked out when we tried to drug her."

"I'm not saying you're lying," Elara said, staring at the kid. "But right now, her vitals are perfect. I'm staying until she wakes up. Hey, can you give us the room for a second?" She wasn't going to feel guilty for hoping. Every kid could be her kid.

Valerie waved the other doctors out, and the door clicked shut. Silence.

Elara picked up the chart from the nightstand. The name at the top read: *Aurelia A.* Just an initial for the last name. She stared at it, set it down, and looked back at the girl. That weird, familiar feeling wasn't fading. It was screaming at her now.

Her fingers trembled as she reached out, slowly sliding her hand under the hem of the girl's shirt, desperate to find that mark. Just as her fingers brushed the soft skin, the door clicked open.

Elara snapped her head up.

The blood rushed out of her face, leaving her completely numb and freezing cold. She froze, her hand hovering right over the kid's stomach, staring blankly at the person standing in the doorway.
