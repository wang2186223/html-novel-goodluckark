Chapter 301

The call didn't drop. Caspian heard the faint, steady rasp of his dad's breath through the speaker. But man, the silence that followed? It felt like a physical weight, dropping onto the room and sucking all the air out.

Caspian didn't flinch. His face just froze. Completely blank.

He glanced over at Elara. She was hurting, bad, but her eyes were locked on him, trying to piece together the one-sided talk. She didn't need to hear both sides to know the game had just changed.

His knuckles went white around the phone. He took a step back, keeping his eyes glued to her, his voice dropping to a hard, quiet rumble.

"Dad," he said, grinding his molars until his jaw literally ached. "I called for the cure. I don't need a sermon."

The old man was a brick wall. Once his mind was set, that was it. Caspian had spent his entire life slamming into that wall, so he knew the drill. Right now, his dad was digging his heels into the worst possible stance, and he wasn't going to budge. Not a millimeter.

"If this is for her," his dad said—using that shut-down, final-word tone old Alphas love to pull—"then forget it. You're not getting it."

Caspian spun around, hiding his face from Elara. He slammed his palm against the drywall, taking slow, deep breaths to keep the rising rage from boiling over.

He remembered how wrecked his dad had been back when they almost lost him. Caspian himself had spiraled hard after that heartbreak—starving himself, chugging booze like water.

He got it. His dad was just terrified of watching him drown in that dark place again. And Caspian respected him for it—that messy, complicated father-son dynamic where your old man is your mentor and your biggest roadblock all at once. But right now? That respect was wearing paper-thin.

"Who the hell did you think I was calling about, anyway?" He kept his tone dead level.

A beat of silence. Then his dad spoke up, a little less confident but still trying to sound tough. "Your mother and I... we assumed it was for Linnea."

A dry laugh bubbled up, but Caspian choked it down. "Linnea’s a rogue, Dad. She never had a pack, so she never broke a bond. The curse doesn't touch her like it does a she-wolf who walked away from her pack."

"You know how this works. If this was about Linnea, would I really have waited this damn long to call you?"

The silence stretched, getting awkward as his dad chewed on that. Caspian used the pause to look back over his shoulder.

Elara was gone. He spotted her through the frosted glass of the bathroom door. She'd slipped inside.

God, he needed to wrap this call up. Fast.

"Dad, she's in agony. Right now. While we're standing here bickering. So let me ask you one last time—are you giving me the cure or what?"

He didn't get an answer.

"She's with you?" His dad's voice dropped ten degrees. "Caspian. What the hell are you doing? I didn't raise my son to go picking up some other guy's trash—"

"Don't." The word slipped out of Caspian, soft but loaded. Like a valve releasing years of built-up pressure.

He didn't yell. It was worse than yelling—a dead, chilling calm that meant he was ready to burn it all down. "Don't you dare finish that sentence."

"Caspian—"

"She's not trash." Caspian's voice finally cracked open, a low, fierce growl vibrating from his chest. "She has never been trash. You say that word again, and I don't care if you're my father. We're done."

Total silence on the other end.

Caspian exhaled slowly, shutting his eyes for a beat. When he opened them, he had his voice back under control. "I've loved Sera since we were kids. Never stopped. Not for a second. Not through any of this shit. If you won't help, fine. I'll find another way."

His voice hitched right at the end. Just a tiny crack, but enough for anyone listening closely to catch.

And his dad was definitely listening.

The old man had spent decades reading people—especially his own son. He knew the difference between a stubborn tantrum and something real.

The silence dragged on. When his dad finally spoke, the hard edge was gone. Instead, he sounded worried, though he tried to mask it with a cautious tone.

"You have no idea what you're getting yourself into," his dad said softly.

"Then tell me," Caspian snapped back. "Make me understand. Because right now, your excuses make zero sense."

More silence. Longer this time. The kind where someone is weighing exactly how much truth to drop.

Finally, his dad spoke.

"This cure," he began, choosing his words like he was walking on glass. "It's not normal medicine. It only works under specific conditions. It only activates if it's given by someone who actually, truly loves the person suffering. Someone they can mate with." A pause. "You get what I'm telling you?"

Caspian stayed quiet. He got the message, loud and clear.

"Elara is your best friend's ex. There's too much history there, for both of you. You're a Chief Commander, Caspian. The code exists for a reason. It's not just some rules we made up. This path you're choosing... it's gonna cost you. The code isn't going to let this slide just because you're in love."

"I'll pay it," Caspian said. Zero hesitation.

"Caspian—" his dad started, but Caspian cut him off.

"She's worth it, Dad." He said it flat out. Like a guy who'd run the numbers a million times and always got the same answer.

"And unless you want my wolf to tear me apart from the inside out, you better cough up the cure. Because if this pack is ever going to have a Managing Director, Elara is the only one."
