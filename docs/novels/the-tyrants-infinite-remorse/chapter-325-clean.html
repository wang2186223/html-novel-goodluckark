Chapter 329

"Because if you mess up..." Elara swallowed, her throat tight. "I'm taking Esperanza and running."

That hit him like a physical blow. She wasn't playing games. No threats, no drama. Just sheer, naked terror. The kind of fear that comes from opening your chest to someone and waiting for them to rip your heart out.

He flashed back to last month. She'd rejected his cash, flat-out telling him she'd bolt the second she found her kid. She was ready to play solo, building a wall around herself and her baby. She had no clue the kid she was hunting for was literally sleeping down the hall in his house. God, it hurt to even think about.

Caspian reached out, framing her face in his hands. His thumb traced her cheekbone. "Sera," he breathed, his voice cracking. "If I had any damn clue back then..." He looked sick with regret. "I would've stopped it all. No crying. No cold, empty beds. No struggling. I would've been your shield, every single day."

He meant it. It was written all over his face, burning in his chest. "That's why we need to break this block. You need to remember."

Her heart did a hard thump at the sheer weight in his voice.

"I need you to remember us." He let out a dry, sad laugh. "Photos don't do it justice. Videos don't cut it. And my stories? They're nothing compared to the real thing."

Elara just watched him, speechless. Then she shook her head. "That's not the problem."

Caspian's brows knit together. She placed her hand over his. "I believe you, Caspian."

He blinked, caught off guard. She wasn't lying. She actually bought it. She believed they were crazy about each other, that they had a real history, that every word out of his mouth was true.

Trust wasn't the issue. Her eyes dropped, a dark, heavy shadow crossing her face—something way worse than just missing memories. She squeezed his fingers. "I'm just terrified that one morning, I'll wake up and—"

Caspian cut her off. He saw the panic in her eyes and knew exactly where her head was going. No way in hell was he letting her finish that thought.

"Not going to happen," he said, hard and fast. He squeezed back. "Zero chance, Sera. Nobody is tearing us apart again. Period."

She blinked, stunned by how dead-set he was. Caspian leaned in, locking eyes with her. "I'm never letting you go," he murmured, his face softening as he tucked a loose hair behind her ear. "Even if you bolt, I'm chasing."

She let out a breathless little laugh, but he wasn't playing. The past was still eating him alive. Years ago, he'd backed off because she chose Thane. He'd played the nice guy, telling himself it was the right move. What a joke. That mistake had haunted him for years. His jaw clenched. "I screwed up once," he muttered. "I thought loving you meant letting you walk."

His eyes went dark. "But it's not that simple. Not anymore."

Elara kept quiet, letting him rub slow circles on the back of her hand. "What you said to me back then... it wrecked me." He gave a sharp, bitter smirk. Yeah, it still hurt. "But once I got the whole story, it clicked. You weren't you. You weren't in your right head." He shook his head, his voice turning to steel. "So, yeah. Never again."

Her chest ached, but in a good way. Before she could even open her mouth, Caspian leaned in and kissed her. It wasn't frantic or desperate. Just soft, warm, and incredibly real. Suddenly, the noise in her head stopped. The medical tests, the blank spots in her brain, the wedding stress—it all just vanished.

"Daddy loves Mommy!"

A tiny, hyped-up voice broke the silence. They froze. Then came the frantic clapping.

Elara yanked herself back, her face instantly catching fire. Across the room, Esperanza was grinning like she'd just won the lottery. Lysander was smirking, and Caspian rubbed his neck, trying to keep a straight face.

"Uh..." Caspian started, but Esperanza pointed a finger at them.

"Daddy loves Mommy!" she cheered, totally proud of herself.

Elara cleared her throat, desperate for a distraction. "Hey, Espe, did you eat your pancakes?"

She hurried over to the kitchen island and stared at the plate. Her jaw dropped. Clean. Not a single crumb. A rush of warmth hit her chest. It felt way better than it should have. Guess Asher wasn't the only pancake fan in the house.

The kid nodded, beaming. "All gone!"

Before Elara could even smile, Lysander snorted. "Oh, that's not the half of it." They all looked at him as he pointed at the little girl. "She polished off the extras you left on the counter, too."

Elara blinked. "Seriously?"

Lysander nodded. "Every last bite."

Esperanza looked like she deserved a medal. Caspian stared at his daughter, stunned, then dropped to his knees to look her in the eye. He looked thoughtful. "Wait a second. So Elara was the trick? You just wanted her here, and that's why you've been skipping meals?"
