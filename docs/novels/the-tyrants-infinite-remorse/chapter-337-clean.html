Chapter 341

Arthur rubbed his temples, shaking his head. Caspian was a total puzzle, and honestly, Arthur didn't have the cheat code. The guy operated on some crazy, high-level frequency that would take a whiteboard and a ton of therapy to decode. "Hell if I know," Arthur muttered, his throat tight. "But I have to call Sera. Right now."

He whipped out his phone, tapped her contact, and dialed before Eleanor could even protest. He tossed it onto the center of the table and hit speaker.

Two rings, then a click. "Hey?"

"Congrats, Sera," Arthur said. Dead silence on the other end.

Sera was totally thrown. You could practically hear her gears grinding, trying to figure out what the hell he meant. Eleanor couldn't resist. She leaned right over the screen.

"Seriously, sweetie, you nailed it this time," Eleanor said, her voice thick with pure, unfiltered pride. "You got a real one."

"God, if we had any clue Thane was such a psycho, we would've forced the issue with your memory. We would've fought dirty. But whatever, that's over." She let out a shaky, emotional laugh. "You have Caspian now. Game over. He changes the whole playing field."

The line went dead quiet. Sera was clearly putting the puzzle pieces together, fast.

Her parents were hours away. You don't just make that drive on a whim. And Caspian was supposed to grab her soon for the pack trip. So when the hell did he find the time to—

"Wait, is Caspian with you guys?" Sera asked, her voice cracking with a mix of shock and this sudden, sweet warmth.

"Nope, just missed him," Eleanor beamed. "He literally flew in on a chopper, said he had to rush back to get you to some shaman tonight. Oh my god, Sera." She softened her voice, getting all mushy.

"I saw how he looked when he talked about you. Seriously, no wonder he let you slap him—twice, was it?—when he was acting like an arrogant prick. A guy who's over you doesn't look at you like that. He never stopped loving you, sweetie. Not for a single second."

Those words punched Sera right in the gut, sending her emotions into a tailspin.

She could still feel those two slaps. The first was pure panic and rage, just trying to survive. The second was basically the same. And Caspian just stood there and took it. No grabbing, no screaming, none of the toxic garbage other guys would've pulled.

He let her get away with it. Nobody else on the planet could've done that to him.

Right there, she made a quiet vow to herself. Never again. Not because she was scared, but because he didn't deserve it. The guessing game was over. He loved her, period. And he wasn't the type to lash out or play mind games. She didn't have to test him anymore.

"Thanks, guys," she choked out, her voice thick with tears. "Mom, Dad... thank you. Seriously. You're coming, right?"

Arthur snorted, laughing. "Try stopping us. We're the parents of the bride, kiddo. We're showing up early, crashing the place, the whole nine yards." He was grinning ear to ear, and he didn't care who knew it.

Eleanor nodded vigorously, forgetting Sera couldn't see her. "Yeah, we're staying over. You better have a guest room prepped."

"I'd love that so much," Sera whispered, her voice cracking.

The line went dead. Arthur stood there, hand resting near his phone, just riding the wave of relief. No drama. Just a rare, genuinely good moment.

Sera flipped her phone face-down on the table. She sat there, letting the silence settle around her.

She never saw that call coming. Honestly, she hadn't expected any of Caspian's behind-the-scenes moves today—quietly checking in on everyone she cared about before she even knew what he was up to.

No bragging, no looking for a pat on the back. He just handled it. Quiet, solid, and protective.

She was still processing all of this when Lysander strolled into the room, holding a thick, glossy magazine open to a massive spread of wedding dresses.

"Hey, Sera," he said, shoving it toward her. "Pick a dress before we hit the road. I want to get the designer started tonight so they can work on it while we're away." He shrugged like it was no big deal. "Oh, and I already grabbed your measurements."

Sera stared at the glossy pages, her brain short-circuiting. The dress. She'd completely blanked on it. With all the chaos, a wedding gown was the last thing on her mind. But looking at it now, she didn't feel like grabbing it.

Her first wedding had been a total joke compared to this.

Thane didn't give a shit. He just showed up, did the bare minimum, and checked out. She'd picked out every single detail herself—the cut, the color, everything—because why ask a guy who wasn't even looking? She dressed for herself because nobody else cared.

But Caspian actually cared.

He noticed the little things, the way only someone who truly loved you would. Sera wanted to wear a dress that would make his jaw drop.

"Hey, do you know what kind of style he likes?" she asked, looking up at Lysander. "He's done so much for me. I want this wedding to be about him, too, not just me picking random stuff."

Lysander paused, thinking. Then his face lit up and he snapped the magazine shut. "Actually, scratch that. I'll just ask him. He styled that whole Dominic look himself, so the guy's got taste." He nodded. "I'll corner him tonight, get his vision, and we'll build the dress around that."

The knot in Sera's chest finally unraveled. "Perfect. Thank you."

The rest of the afternoon was a total blur. She was a ball of nerves and excitement, her heart racing at the thought of finally seeing her little girl back home.

And then there was the shaman. She tried not to overthink it. Would it work? Would she wake up tomorrow as a completely different person? She forced the thoughts down, focused on packing, her chest tight.

When she finally walked out to the driveway, the cool night air hit her, and she stopped dead in her tracks.

Something felt seriously wrong.

She stood there, trying to figure out the vibe. It wasn't a threat, exactly. Just this weird, nagging pull. Like she had unfinished business she needed to wrap up before she could leave in peace.

She looked over at Lysander, who was waiting by the door. "Hey, can we make a quick detour to the Stone mansion first?" She locked eyes with him. "I need to see Uncle Felix and Aunt Lyra."

His eyes flickered, but he didn't ask questions or push back. "Yeah," he said after a second. "Sure. Let's do it."

Neither of them said it out loud, but Lysander needed this stop just as much. His parents deserved to hear it directly from him, face-to-face, that he was moving out. He owed them that much respect.

He popped the passenger door open, and Sera slid inside. They drove off in silence.

But what neither of them realized was that someone had already beaten them to the house.
