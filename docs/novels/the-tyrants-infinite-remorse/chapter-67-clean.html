NovelVibe

Chapter 67

Elara had kept this thing locked down for months. Years, honestly. It was her baby, too fragile to let anyone touch, let alone trash. Not even Lysander knew, and the guy basically ran her life. Some dreams just die the second you let other people squint at them with that "are you crazy?" look. Better to keep it quiet.

But then she was in Julian’s passenger seat, golden hour hitting the dash, and the words just spilled out before she could stop them. "Eclipse Entertainment," she said. Her voice was level, but her chest buzzed. "That’s the umbrella. But the first launch? Phantom Games."

Julian cut his eyes toward her, then back to the asphalt. His forehead bunched up. "Wait, you?" he asked. "Like, video games?" He wasn't laughing, it just threw him off. To him, gaming meant sweaty dudes screaming at monitors. It didn't fit the cool, calculated woman sitting next to him.

She caught the vibe but didn't snap. Instead, she leaned back and painted the picture. "Think bigger. Not just wasting time, but actual rewiring," she said, staring out the window at a future only she could see. "What if a game actually chilled your nervous system out instead of frying it?"

That got him. He looked over, actually hooked now. She started talking with her hands, sharp and fast. "Look, entertainment is just escapism, right? But what if we bake bio-hacks right into the code? Tiny cues that slow your breathing, drop your heart rate, and reset your brain. No one even notices it happening. I'm not trying to build some addictive, soul-sucking time-sink. No way. Fifteen, maybe thirty minutes max. Hard limits."

The doubt melted right off his face. "So... it's therapy disguised as play."

"It's both," she said, locking eyes with him. "But designed with a brain. Immersive, yeah, but it won't ruin your life. It actually gives you something back."

He whistled, genuinely impressed. "Damn. That’s a massive lift. You're talking heavy dev work, neuro-tech, crazy UI, and biometric sensors if you want real-time bio-feedback."

Elara smirked. She'd already said way too much for a first date, but she felt good. "Relax," she said. "I'll have the ducks in a row before we launch." She kept the rest to herself—the black-hat cyber contacts, the massive trading stash she'd built up, and the devs already locked down under NDAs. He didn't need the whole blueprint yet.

They pulled up to her place just as the sky turned bruised purple. The mood shifted. Julian hopped out, walked around, and popped her door open. Smooth as always. Inside, the smell of home-cooked food hit her immediately. Lysander was right there in the entryway.

"Hey, mind if Julian stays for dinner?" she asked, super casual.

Lysander’s gut screamed *hell no*. The urge to shut it down was instant. But how could he say no to her in her own damn house? He swallowed the salt. "Yeah, sure," he said, keeping his voice flat. "Your place, your rules."

Elara jogged out to the driveway before Julian could start his engine. "Hey! Want to grab a bite with us?"

Julian didn't hesitate. "Sure." Just like that, he was at the table, fitting right in.

The guy was a natural. He laughed, chatted, and hung on every word as Elara talked about the kids at the rehab park. Her face lit up, and honestly, it softened the whole room. The thick tension from earlier just kind of evaporated.

Then she dropped the bomb. "Oh, by the way, I'm starting part-time at Julian's hospital next week."

The room went dead silent.

"What kind of hours?" Lysander asked. He tried to sound chill, but his chest felt like it was in a vice. He hated her getting closer to Julian.

She flashed him a reassuring grin. "Three hours max. I've got too much other stuff going on. Plus, we have our own company to build, right?" She looked right at Lysander.

Lysander froze. *Our*. Not hers, not his. *Ours*.

Julian caught it too. His smile twitched. Why did Lysander have to be part of her dream? Why couldn't she just do it solo? Julian wanted to ask, but yeah, dinner wasn't the place to start a turf war.

Julian finally said his goodbyes and headed out into the cool night. The second the front door clicked shut, Lysander turned on her. "This gaming gig," he said, trying to sound casual but failing. "Did he put that in your head?"

She snorted, genuinely amused. "Are you kidding? Hell no. I've had this idea forever. I was just too busy dealing with the whole Thane and Caspian shitshow to actually do it."

Lysander let out a breath he didn't realize he was holding. He didn't even try to hide the relief.

The next few days were a total blur. Pure, organized chaos. She was up at dawn trading stocks, doing cyber consulting by noon, and coding her first game prototype late into the night. Her laptop was basically glued to her hands. Her eyes burned from staring at the glowing screen for hours.

Lysander saw she was running on empty way before she'd ever admit it. He’d catch her rubbing her temples, staring blankly at walls of code. He kept her alive—bringing her snacks, telling her to take a breath, keeping her grounded. Since she was home more, he took over the heavy lifting at Stone Group so she could focus on her new baby.

When Monday rolled around and her hospital gig started, he insisted on driving her. Drop her off in the morning, hit his own office, then grab her before lunch. It became their little routine. Everything was clicking. Work, life, goals—all of it.

Until her phone buzzed.

She swiped the screen, seeing the caller ID.

"Sera," Rhys’s voice cut through the line, sharp and tense. "We got a situation."
