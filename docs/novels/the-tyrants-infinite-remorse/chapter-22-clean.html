Chapter 22

"Marjorie Steward."

Elara dropped the name like a stone. She slid into the leather seat right across from Thane, totally chill, but her eyes were dead zones. Cold. Nothing like the girl he used to know. Two massive guys in black suits flanked her. No clipboards, no phones. Just heavy-duty muscle waiting for an excuse to break some bones.

Maddox looked from Thane to Elara, his forehead wrinkling. "Wait. You guys know each other?"

Nobody said a word. The room went dead silent, heavy enough to choke on. Thane felt a hard punch to his gut. His cool facade slipped, just for a second.

"Nope," Thane said, shaking his head to shake off the shock. "Briar’s hurt. I’m out of here. Got no time for this crap." He stood up, practically vibrating with anger. He took one step before Lysander cut him off, blocking the exit like a brick wall.

"Sorry, Thane," Lysander said, deadpan. "You’re signing these papers before you take another step."

Hearing his bare name without a title made Thane’s blood boil. Back in the day, Lysander wouldn't have dared use that tone.

But they weren't with the pack. No sacred ground, no backup. Thane reached for that familiar spark of power inside him, but got nothing. The city didn't care about his Chief Commander status. Out here, the rules were different. They were just two guys in a room, and in a straight fistfight, Thane had zero edge.

Thane spun back slowly, glaring at Elara. "Prove it," he spat, his mind racing. "Prove you're Marjorie Steward."

Elara didn't even blink. She just gave Maddox a look.

Maddox got the hint. He pulled a file from his briefcase and slapped it on the desk. "ID papers. 'Marjorie Steward' is just a shell name. The client wanted to stay off the grid, which is totally legal. We keep her real name private, but the paperwork makes the deal legit."

Thane dropped back into his chair and ripped the folder open. His knuckles turned white as he scanned the pages. Boom. There it was. Elara's actual name, black on white, right next to the alias clause. His brain short-circuited. He’d come to play hardball with some random investor, not deal with her.

He snapped his head up. "Where the hell did you get this kind of cash?"

Elara looked at Maddox. "Give us a minute, will you?"

Maddox hesitated, then nodded. "Sure." He slipped out and clicked the door shut.

Elara kicked back, crossing her legs. "My bank account is none of your business. You got the proof. Now sign the damn transfer."

"No way," Thane snapped. "You ripped off the pack. There's no other way you got this rich."

Elara’s jaw clenched so hard her teeth clicked. "You want to throw accusations? Bring receipts," she said, her voice like ice. "Otherwise, my lawyers will sue you for every dime you have."

Thane sucked in a breath, forcing his temper down. She was right. No pack rules here. Out here, the law ruled, and baseless threats would only bite him in the ass.

He glared harder. "If this is some sick game to get me back," he sneered, "you're delusional."

Elara let out a dry laugh, though her eyes stayed dead. "Thane, I used to be stupid," she said. "But I grew up. I wouldn't take you back if you were the last man on earth."

Lysander smirked. Thane caught it and turned on him, his voice dropping an octave. "So you're her little lapdog now? Lysander, you still have a spot in the pack. Come home, or you're going down with her."

Lysander snorted. "Go back? To get bossed around by your little bitch?"

That did it. Thane lost it. He swung a fist, but Lysander caught his wrist mid-air. With a brutal twist, Lysander shoved Thane's arm down, staring him dead in the eye. "Don't even think about it."

Thane ripped his arm free, chest heaving. "Don't you ever show your face in our territory again."

"Wouldn't dream of it," Lysander shot back. "Now sign."

Elara watched them, highly amused. A tiny smile touched her lips. Lysander was turning out to be a great asset. She'd have to pay him back for this later.

Thane stared at the papers, torn. Finally, he looked up. "Fine. I'll sign. But only if she gives back Briar's research."

Elara and Lysander both cracked up. "Briar's research?" Elara scoffed. "Please. Has that girl even seen the inside of a lab?"

Thane went rigid. "What's that supposed to mean?" Briar wouldn't lie to him. Elara was just bitter.

"What I burned," Elara said, cool as a cucumber, "was my own work."

"Bullshit," Thane barked. "It was Briar's. She told me. Bring the files out so we can check."

Elara looked at him like he was an idiot. "Can't. Like I said, I trashed them. But hey, if they're really hers, what's the big deal? She can just write them again."

"You used pack funding for that tech," Thane growled.

Elara's eyes turned sharp enough to cut. "I put billions into your company. Why the hell would I need your cheap pack resources? Got any proof of that, brother?" She spat the last word. Thane just stood there, looking completely lost. It was hilarious.

"I'm checking the records myself," Thane said after a beat. "No signing until then. You want to push it? We'll let the lawyers handle it."

Elara shrugged, totally unfazed. "Works for me. Let's go to court. I'll have Julian serve you the papers."

Thane's eyes went wide. "Augustine?" His voice cracked. "How the hell can you afford him?" Julian Augustine was the top shark in the country—undefeated, brutal, and insanely expensive.

"None of your business," Elara said. "And you're not walking out of here until I get my money back."

Thane opened his mouth to snap back, but the intercom buzzed. He hit the button. "What?"

"Sir," the receptionist said, "Mr. Orion is here to see you."

A slow, nasty grin spread across Thane's face. A plan clicked into place. "Send him in."
