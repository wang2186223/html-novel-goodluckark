Chapter 255

"Look at you, actually acting like a grown-up," Gideon’s voice echoed inside Caspian’s skull.

The packhouse glowed ahead. Warm yellow light sliced through the gray downpour, still a blurry smudge in the distance. Caspian dragged his feet. His boots were soaked, caked in heavy mud that pulled at every step.

"It’s what needs to happen," Caspian thought back.

Gideon went quiet. A beat passed. "Then let's put a ring on it. Claim her."

Caspian missed his footing.

"Are you out of your mind?" he snapped, his mental voice sharp with irritation.

"Am I, though?"

"She’s Thane’s ex, dude." Caspian stared straight ahead, locking his jaw. "We wait for our fated mate. Period. The Commander code doesn't just bend because we feel like it. Not even for a mate bond."

If he had to go to war for a woman, it was going to be for his actual fated mate. No exceptions.

"But what if she’s the one?" Gideon’s voice dropped, getting soft. It annoyed Caspian even more.

"She isn't."

"Right. You sound super convinced for a guy who freezes up every single time she laughs."

Caspian shut his mouth. The rain poured harder, slamming the leaves, his shoulders, the mud. A dark shadow crossed his face, gone before the storm could swallow it.

"Lysander told me the exact same crap in the city," Caspian finally replied, keeping his mental tone dead flat. "And he was wrong. Look, Sera’s great. She’s tough, she’s real, and yeah, she’s probably one of the best people I know. But I don’t love her. Not like that. So stop pushing a fantasy."

Gideon let out a rough sound—half-laugh, half-growl. "Keep telling yourself that, man. Say it enough, maybe you'll actually believe your own BS."

"I’m serious—"

"I just hope," Gideon cut him off, his voice suddenly heavy, almost sad, "you don't drag your feet so long that you lose her. Life doesn't pause while you try to figure out your feelings."

Then, boom. Gideon shut down the link.

A mental wall slammed shut so fast Caspian nearly tripped. The sudden silence in his head was deafening, like a door slamming in an empty house.

Caspian stopped. Rain dripped down his neck. He stared into the dark.

He’d blocked Gideon plenty of times, but getting shut out himself? That was different. It felt weird. Like reaching into his pocket and finding a hole.

He shook his head and kept moving.

"Sera!"

Rhys yelled from the porch the second they cleared the trees. Elara slid off Caspian’s back before he even came to a full stop.

Her boots hit the mud. She stood up straight, quickly smoothing her hair, trying to look like she hadn't just hitched a piggyback ride through a storm like a toddler.

"Thanks," she mumbled, avoiding his eyes.

Her cheeks flushed bright pink—and it wasn't from the freezing wind. Then the heavy front doors swung open. A blast of cold AC hit her face, and she shuddered, a hard, involuntary shiver racking her body.

She hugged herself and stepped inside.

"I need a shower." She looked between the two men. "Rhys, can I crash in your room?"

Rhys opened his mouth to answer.

"Take mine," Caspian cut in. His voice was flat, leaving zero room for debate.

Rhys shut his mouth. Elara’s eyes snapped to Caspian. She knew about that room. Thane had kept a private room reserved for Caspian at the Centenary packhouse for years.

And Caspian had done the exact same thing for Thane back at Grimroot. No one had to ask.

People always talked about their bromance like it was some legendary thing. Caspian knew Thane could be a brutal bastard, but it never touched their bond. That kind of loyalty was rare.

She shook herself out of it.

"I’d rather use Rhys’s," she said, her voice sharp. She stared right at Caspian, trying to read his face.

Caspian didn't flinch. He didn't even blink. "I said take mine. I’ll take his."

"You won't even let me touch your phone," Elara scoffed, throwing an eyebrow up. "But now I can use your room?"

The corner of his mouth twitched. Not a smile, just a tight, controlled tick. "No guy with half a brain leaves his phone around a hacker."

Her mouth popped open, then snapped shut.

Damn it. He was right, and she hated it. She’d cracked his server once—bypassed every firewall and password just to show off—and Caspian had never let her live it down.

She didn't know whether to laugh or punch him. That was the most annoying thing about him: he always had a comeback ready, even when he was clearly hiding something.

She sighed, giving up.

"Fine." She turned to Rhys, tossing her wet hair over her shoulder. "I know it’s pouring, but my bag—"

"Got it." Rhys was already jingling his car keys. He glanced at Caspian on his way to the door. "Clean towels are in the bathroom cabinet. Top shelf."

Then he bolted into the rain, his headlights cutting through the dark as he started the car.

Elara took Caspian’s room. Caspian took Rhys’s.

Caspian finished first. He threw on clean clothes, his hair still damp, and walked out into the hall. He heard Rhys’s car pull up outside and headed downstairs.

He was halfway across the lobby when he saw Rhys through the glass door, clutching the bag and shaking water off his coat.

"I’ll take that up to her," Caspian said, reaching for the door.

Rhys froze. Just for a second, but Caspian caught it. Rhys’s shoulders went rigid, his jaw tightening. "A maid can do it."

The air in the room turned ice-cold. Caspian’s eyes darkened. A low, warning growl rumbled in his chest, loud enough to make Rhys stiffen.

"Nobody goes in my room," Caspian said, holding out his hand. "Give me the bag, Rhys."
