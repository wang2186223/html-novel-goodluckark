Chapter 160

Elara let out a long, slow breath and sank into the seat. The sheer weight of the mess finally caught up to her. But looking at Lysander, she didn't hesitate. Not anymore. She wasn't just trusting him with her business; she was trusting him to keep her alive.

"Thane keeps swearing he didn't assault me," she said, her voice flat, though anger simmered right under the surface. "He claims I begged for it. I need hard proof to shut that lie down. If I don't get it, he's going to use that exact spin to drag me back the second I say no."

Lysander smirked, but there was no humor in it. He got it. She wasn't guessing; she was playing chess, always three moves ahead. She knew how these bastards operated better than they did. That was why she was still breathing.

If Thane ever got wind of the truth about Asher, he'd pull out every dirty trick to lock her down again. He'd twist the past, rewrite the facts, and never let go. Lysander felt a weird wave of relief that she was cutting off his options before he could even start.

Honestly, Lysander’s memory of that night was a total blank. He’d been too busy partying like everyone else, completely missing any red flags.

He opened his mouth to ask more, but she beat him to it. Her voice dropped, losing its sharp edge.

"Once I get my girl back," she murmured, staring out the window like she could already see the getaway car, "I'm vanishing. Somewhere he can never track me."

She locked eyes with him, dead serious. "That's the real reason I handed you the CEO keys."

The words hit like a physical punch.

Lysander went rigid, his jaw tightening. He couldn't hide the reaction. The subtext was loud and clear: her grand escape plan didn't have room for him. She wasn't trying to be mean about it; it was just a cold, hard fact.

And damn, for some reason he didn't want to dig into, that really pissed him off.

Before he could say a word, his phone buzzed. A text from Caspian. Lysander frowned at the screen. "Wait, he just sent the address," he said, totally confused. "But... this is his office. He said we were training at his private place. Why send me the office address if I already know it?"

Elara let out a dry chuckle, totally unsurprised. "You seriously think he'd trust you to keep me away? Especially after I literally told him I was coming?"

Lysander sighed, shaking his head. "Man, why do you two always think the exact same way?"

Elara ignored the comment. She flipped open her laptop, her fingers already flying across the keys as she dove right back into trading.

"Hey, can you set up some interviews for those top-tier tech guys once we're done dealing with Dominic?" she asked, completely back in boss mode.

"Way ahead of you," Lysander said. "Got a few lined up. I'm using a headhunter agency for now until we land a solid HR manager to handle the heavy lifting."

That got him a quick, approving smile. "Perfect. That’s exactly what we need."

By the time the car rolled into the underground garage at Ashkael Holdings, they were both locked in. But they didn't expect to see Caspian already standing there by the parking spots, looking like he'd been waiting for hours.

Lysander climbed out first. The second Caspian spotted Elara stepping out behind him, his face went dark.

"I knew I couldn't trust you," Caspian spat, glaring right at Lysander. Nobody had ever blown off his orders before, and the loss of control clearly stung.

Elara stepped right between them, closing the gap. "Don't blame him," she said, her voice cool and steady. "This was my call. I'm not sitting around waiting for secondhand training tips."

She held his glare. "I'm the one who asked for your help, remember? Not him."

Caspian's jaw clenched. He hated it, but he couldn't argue. Instead, he snapped, "When's this damn date?"

He was clearly pissed, but Elara didn't flinch. She knew he wouldn't have agreed if she'd begged, and sneaking around had obviously rubbed him the wrong way for some reason she couldn't care less about.

"Five days," she said, then tilted her head. "Actually, make that four. We already wasted a day."

Caspian took a slow, deep breath, his eyes narrowing as he ran the numbers in his head. "Fine. I'll get someone else to train you both."

The petty move didn't slip past them. He was clearly throwing a tantrum because they didn't listen.

Elara and Lysander exchanged a look. He wasn't just delegating; he was backing off, trying to put distance between them. It was sketchy as hell.

Lysander eyed him, keeping his voice casual but sharp. "Why do I get the feeling you're running from something?" he asked. "Or, let's be real... running from her?"

He didn't yell, but the hit landed clean. Caspian's cool, calm mask slipped instantly.
